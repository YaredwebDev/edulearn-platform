2.3 Maize (Zea mays)

◌ Not started
  • Origin: Central America. High yield/unit area; seeds covered by husk (protects from rain/birds); does not shatter (no seed loss on late harvest). Used as staple food, animal feed (green or dry), residues as firewood, and for starch & oil in industry.
  • Production: altitude ~500–2400 m; yields/unit area highest of Ethiopian cereals; ranks second in area. Better on loam soils without excess moisture. Grown as mono crop or mixed with legumes (haricot bean) or cereals (teff).
  • Land prep: plow 2–3×.
  • Planting: row planting 25 cm between seeds, 75 cm between rows; depth 5–7 cm; usually planted at start of rains (March–April).
  • Fertilisation: ~41 kg N + 20 kg P per ha.
  • Weed/pest/disease: manual weeding 2–3× or herbicides — pre-emergence (e.g. Atrazine, before weeds emerge) and post-emergence (e.g. 2,4-D). Insects: maize stalk borer, aphids, army worm, weevil. Fungal (rust, gray leaf spot, blight) and viral (strike virus) diseases — control by crop rotation (not mono) and adjusting planting time. Earthing up ~1 month after planting reduces lodging (plants falling over in wind).
  • Harvesting: maturity 3–5 months (early vs late varieties); harvest when seeds develop a black layer at the bottom tip; grain moisture ~25% at harvest → dry to ≤13% for safe storage. National average yield ≈ 32.5 quintals (3.25 t)/ha — the highest.
