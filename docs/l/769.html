6.4 Laws of Reflection & Refraction

◌ Not started

Reflection

When a ray of light bounces off a smooth polished surface: - Incident ray = incoming ray; reflected ray = outgoing ray. - Normal = imaginary line perpendicular to the surface. - Angle of incidence (θᵢ) between incident ray and normal; angle of reflection (θᵣ) between reflected ray and normal.

Laws of reflection: 1. θᵢ = θᵣ (angle of incidence = angle of reflection). 2. The incident ray, the normal, and the reflected ray all lie in the same plane.

  • Specular reflection: from a smooth shiny surface; all reflected light moves in the same direction (e.g., wet road).
  • Diffuse reflection: from a rough surface; reflected light scatters in many directions.

Refraction

Refraction = the bending of light as it moves from one optical medium to another, because light travels at different speeds in different materials. - Light speed in water < air; in glass < water. - Moving from a faster to a slower medium (e.g., air → glass): ray bends toward the normal. - Moving from a slower to a faster medium: ray bends away from the normal. - If the ray is along the normal, no bending occurs.

Laws of refraction: 1. Incident ray, refracted ray, and normal lie in the same plane. 2. Snell's law: the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant: sin θ₁ / sin θ₂ = constant

Refractive index (absolute) = ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in the medium: n = c/v (no units; n ≥ 1). - Snell's law in terms of refractive indices: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.

Refractive indices: Air 1.0003; Water 1.33; Glass (crown) 1.52; Diamond 2.42 (highest); Kerosene 1.44.

Example 6.3: Light from water (n=1.33) to air, θᵢ = 35°. n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂ → 1.33 × sin 35° = 1 × sin θ₂ → sin θ₂ = 0.763 → θ₂ = 49.7° (bent away from normal).

Total Internal Reflection

Occurs when light travels from a denser to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle: - Critical angle (θ_c): the angle of incidence for which the angle of refraction = 90°. - Two conditions: 1. Light travels from an optically denser (high n) to an optically rarer (low n) medium. 2. Angle of incidence in the denser medium > critical angle. - sin θ_c = n₂/n₁ → θ_c = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁).

Applications: optical fibres (telecommunications transmit data as laser pulses with minimal loss; endoscopes in medicine to examine the stomach).

Example 6.4: Glass n=1.52 to air. θ_c = sin⁻¹(1/1.52) = 42°.

Dispersion (Prisms and Rainbows)

  • A prism splits white light into a band of colors: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red (VIBGYOR).
  • Dispersion = the splitting of light into its component colors.
  • Red refracts the least; violet refracts the most.
  • A rainbow = dispersion of sunlight by tiny water droplets (which act like small prisms: refract, disperse, internally reflect, and refract again). It always forms opposite to the Sun.

✍️ Must-know: Reflection: θᵢ = θᵣ, same plane. Snell's law: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂. Total internal reflection needs dense→rare and θ > θ_c (sin θ_c = n₂/n₁). Dispersion: VIBGYOR; red least, violet most.
