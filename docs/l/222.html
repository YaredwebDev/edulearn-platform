1.4 Contributions of Biological Discoveries to Society and the Environment

◌ Not started

1. The Microscope

Magnifies objects too small for the naked eye. Uses: - Medical field — since Pasteur's germ theory (1860s), identify microbes causing infections, diagnose cancer. - Ecosystem study — study health of habitats by identifying microscopic organisms in samples. - Forensic science — examine crime-scene evidence (e.g., bullet striations). - Atomic study — electron microscopes study cells/viruses at atomic level. - Genetic study — examine genetic makeup and abnormalities. - Tissue analysis — histologists detect cancer in tissue samples.

3. Inheritance (Application of Genetics)

  • History: Robert Hooke (cork cells) → Brown (nucleus, 1831) → Schleiden & Schwann (cell theory, 1836–1840) → 1884–85 four scientists located heredity matter in chromosomes → Mendel's laws revolutionized genetics.
  • Genetics now serves agriculture, medicine, biological diversity, immunity, and inheritance.
