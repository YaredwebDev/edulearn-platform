5.10 Mechanism of action of contraceptives

◌ Not started

Contraception = intentional prevention of pregnancy (hormonal drugs, devices, practices, chemicals, surgery). Reasons: plan family size, reduce pregnancy-related risk, reduce teenage pregnancy, have healthy babies, prevent STIs like HIV/AIDS, and balance population growth with economic growth. - Barrier contraceptives — a physical barrier between sperm and egg. Examples: male condom, female condom, diaphragm, cervical cap. Condoms are the only contraceptive that also prevents STIs. - Diaphragm — small soft silicon dome placed in the vagina, blocking sperm from the uterus. - Non-barrier contraceptives — chemical barriers/spermicides, oral pills, IUDs, hormonal implants, injections, post-pill, contraceptive ring. - Spermicides — sperm-killing substances (foams, creams, gels, films, suppositories), often with barrier devices. - Oral contraceptive pills (COC) — combined estrogen + progesterone; prevent ovulation, thicken cervical mucus, thin uterine lining. Progesterone-only pill (POP) thickens cervical mucus & can stop ovulation; must be taken daily. - Intrauterine device (IUD) — small T-shaped (copper or hormonal); prevents fertilization/implantation; thickens mucus & thins lining. - Contraceptive implant — small plastic rod under the upper-arm skin, lasts ~3 years releasing progesterone. - Contraceptive injection (Depo-Provera = medroxyprogesterone acetate) — synthetic progesterone; prevents ovulation; ~6% may still get pregnant. - Post pill (emergency contraceptive pill, ECP) — levonorgestrel (a progestin), used within 72 hours after unprotected sex. - Contraceptive (vaginal) ring — releases continuous estrogen + progesterone. - Sterilization — permanent: women = tubal ligation; men = vasectomy (cuts/blocks each vas deferens).

Table 5.6 — contraceptive summary (key points): oral contraceptives inhibit ovulation (regulate cycle; not for women over 35 who smoke); injectables (Depo) ~1% failure, inhibit ovulation; IUD prevents implantation, continuous protection; spermicides chemically kill sperm; diaphragm mechanically blocks the cervix; condom mechanically prevents sperm entering the vagina + STI protection; sterilization (tubal ligation 0.04%, vasectomy 0.15%) most reliable but permanent.

✍️ Must-know (review): male condoms are best for STI protection; diaphragm is another barrier method besides condom; IUD types = copper & hormonal (not titanium); vaginal ring left ~3 weeks; natural family planning = basal temperature, cervical-mucus tracking, calendar tracking.
