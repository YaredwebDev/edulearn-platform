6.5 Superposition of Waves

◌ Not started
  • Superposition principle: when two or more waves pass through a single point at the same time, the resultant instantaneous displacement at that point is the sum of the displacements that would be created separately by each wave (taking signs into account).
  • Interference = superposition. Two types:
  • Constructive interference: waves in phase (corresponding points reach max/min at same time) → resultant wave is more amplified.
  • Destructive interference: waves out of phase → resultant wave is diminished (may be zero).

Standing (stationary) waves: - Formed by the superposition of two identical waves travelling in opposite directions in the same medium. Oscillates with time but remains fixed in location. - Antinodes (A): positions of maximum displacement. Nodes (N): positions of zero displacement. - Distance between adjacent antinodes = λ/2; between adjacent nodes = λ/2; between a node and an adjacent antinode = λ/4.
