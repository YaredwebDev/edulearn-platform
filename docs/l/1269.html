7.1 Temperature and Our Life

  • Temperature: the degree of hotness or coldness of a body; or the measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles in a sample of matter.
  • Human feeling of hot/cold is relative and not reliable (the warm-water activity: warm water feels cool to a hand from hot water, and warm to a hand from cold water).
  • Room temperature (comfortable for humans): 18 °C to 22 °C. Human body normal temperature ≈ 98.6 °F (37 °C).
  • Temperature affects clothing, eating habits, health, and even economy.
  • Global temperature variation: polar regions are very cold, temperate regions moderate, tropical regions hot. The equator receives the most direct sunlight.
  • Tropical rain forest yearly average: 21–31 °C.
  • Temperate climates average: −3 to 18 °C.
  • Polar zone average: −14 °C (summer), −50 °C (winter).
  • High-temperature bodies: Sun's outer surface ≈ 5600 K (≈5327 °C), fire, electric heaters/stoves, candle flame. Low-temperature bodies: ice (below 0 °C), solid CO₂ (−78 °C), liquid nitrogen (−196 °C).
