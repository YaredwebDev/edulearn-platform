3.5 The Solid State

◌ Not started

3.5.1 Phase changes — melting, freezing, sublimation, deposition

  • Melting (fusion): solid → liquid. Melting point = temperature at which a crystalline solid melts (solid & liquid in equilibrium). Ice melts at 0 °C; water freezes at 0 °C (melting point = freezing point).
  • Freezing (solidification): liquid → solid, at the freezing point.
  • Unlike boiling, melting points are affected only by large pressure changes.
  • Sublimation: solid → vapour directly (without passing through the liquid), e.g. iodine crystals warming directly to vapour. Deposition: vapour → solid directly (reverse of sublimation).
  • Molar heat of fusion (ΔH_fus): heat to melt 1 mol of a solid at its melting point. Ice = 6.01 kJ at 0 °C. Melting is endothermic.
  • Molar heat of crystallisation (ΔH_cryst) = −ΔH_fus (heat released on freezing).
  • Enthalpy of sublimation ΔH_sub = ΔH_fus + ΔH_vap; ΔH_sub = −ΔH_dep. Sublimation is endothermic; deposition releases heat.

✍️ Must-know: melting point = freezing point (both = temperature where solid & liquid coexist); ΔH_sub = ΔH_fus + ΔH_vap; ΔH_cryst = −ΔH_fus.

3.5.2 Heating curves

A heating curve plots temperature vs heat added. Sloped regions: temperature rises (heating one phase). Plateaus (flat regions): temperature stays constant while a phase change absorbs heat (melting at ~0 °C, boiling at ~100 °C for water). A cooling curve is the reverse (heat removed; plateaus where heat is released during condensation and freezing).

Phase-change energy table (memorise): | Phase change | Process | Energy change | |---|---|---| | Fusion (melting) | Solid → Liquid | absorbed (endothermic) | | Vaporisation | Liquid → Gas | absorbed (endothermic) | | Sublimation | Solid → Gas | absorbed (endothermic) | | Freezing | Liquid → Solid | released (exothermic) | | Condensation | Gas → Liquid | released (exothermic) | | Deposition | Gas → Solid | released (exothermic) |

✍️ Must-know: energy is absorbed to go from a more ordered to a less ordered phase (S→L→G); energy is released the other way (G→L→S).
