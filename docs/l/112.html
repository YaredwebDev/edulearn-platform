2.1 Characteristics of Animals

◌ Not started

Animals are eukaryotic, multicellular, heterotrophic, and sensitive to stimuli; they reproduce, protect themselves, move, respire, excrete, grow, and show different body symmetries.

The two major groups (based on presence/absence of a backbone): - Vertebrates — with a backbone; further split into homeothermic (warm-blooded: mammals & birds) and poikilothermic (cold-blooded: fish, reptiles, amphibians). - Invertebrates — without a backbone; phyla include Porifera, Platyhelminthes, Cnidaria, Arthropoda, Annelida, Echinodermata, Mollusca. Arthropods are split into crustaceans, spiders/arachnids, insects, and many-legged (myriapods).

✍️ Must-know: a trait that is NOT an animal characteristic is being autotrophs (animals are heterotrophs). If the left & right sides are mirror images it is bilateral symmetry.

This unit focuses on the general characteristics and reproductive cycles of the two major groups using examples from mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians and insects.
