7.4 Grammar: Relative (Adjectival) Clauses

◌ Not started

A relative clause gives information about a noun. It begins with a relative pronoun.

Pronoun Used for Function Example
who people subject The nurse who helped us was kind.
whom people object (formal) The doctor whom we met was friendly.
whose people/things possession The boy whose father is ill stayed home.
which things/animals subject or object The service which we did was useful.
that people or things restrictive The tasks that we finished took time.
where places place The hospital where we volunteered is in Bahir Dar.
when time time The day when we served was special.

Defining vs Non-defining clauses

  • Defining (essential) clause — gives essential information needed to identify the noun; NO commas; often uses that.
  • "The volunteers who visited the elderly felt proud." (Only those who visited.)
  • Non-defining (extra) clause — gives extra, non-essential information; set off by commas; cannot use that.
  • "Felege Hiwot Hospital, which is in Bahir Dar, has many patients."

✋ Trap: Use commas only in non-defining clauses. Don't write "My brother, who lives in Addis Ababa, is a doctor" as a defining clause without commas. whose always shows possession and is followed by a noun.
