6.2 Electric Fields

◌ Not started

Electric field: a region around a charge where another charge experiences a force. Electric field strength E = force per unit charge: E = F/q (unit N/C, also V/m).

Electric field lines (lines of force) — properties: 1. Field lines do not cross. 2. Lines begin on positive charges and end on negative charges. 3. Lines are perpendicular to the surface of the charged body. 4. Closer lines ⇒ stronger field; farther apart ⇒ weaker. 5. Equally spaced parallel lines ⇒ uniform field (e.g., between two oppositely charged parallel plates).

✋ Trap: for a positive source charge, the field points away from it; for a negative source charge the field points toward it. Field lines never cross.

Field of a point charge: E = k q / r² (magnitude). Direction: away from a positive charge, toward a negative charge. For multiple charges, add field vectors (superposition).

Worked examples: Example 6.4 (field 5 mm from a 2.0 nC charge); Example 6.5 (field between two charges).

Electric flux (Φ): the number of field lines passing through a surface. For a uniform field through a plane area A whose normal makes angle θ with the field: Φ = E A cos θ. Unit N·m²/C. When A is perpendicular to E, Φ = EA. Example 6.6: flux through a 2×2 cm square in the xy-plane.

✍️ Must-know: E = F/q = kq/r²; flux Φ = E A cos θ.
