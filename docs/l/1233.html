2.3 Classification of Physical Quantities

  • Physical quantity: anything that you can measure (e.g., length, temperature, distance, time). Measured values are described in terms of a number and a unit. Each quantity and unit has a symbol.

A. Fundamental vs Derived:

  • Fundamental (basic) physical quantities: measured directly; cannot be described in terms of other physical quantities. Their units are fundamental units. There are seven fundamental quantities.

B. Scalar vs Vector:

  • Scalar quantity: has only magnitude (a number with a unit), no direction. Examples: distance, mass, time, temperature, energy.
  • Vector quantity: has both magnitude and direction. Examples: displacement, acceleration, force, velocity.
  • A vector is represented by a bold letter or a letter with an arrow (e.g., displacement = S or ⃗S).
✍️ Must-know: Fundamental quantities = measured directly (7 of them); derived quantities depend on fundamentals. Scalar = magnitude only; vector = magnitude + direction.
