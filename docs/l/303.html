5.4 Equilibrium in Industry — applying Le Chatelier

◌ Not started

5.4.1 The Haber process (production of ammonia)

Fritz Haber (1909) found how to make NH₃ from N₂ and H₂; Carl Bosch developed it industrially.

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹ (exothermic).

  • High pressure favours NH₃ (Δn = −2). At 200 °C and >750 atm the yield is ~100%.
  • Low temperature favours NH₃ (exothermic) — but the rate is then too slow.
  • Compromise used industrially: pressure between 200–400 atm and temperature ~500 °C, plus an iron catalyst to speed the reaction.
  • As temperature rises, KC falls: KC = 6.4×10² (25 °C) → 4.4×10⁻¹ (200) → 4.3×10⁻³ (300) → 1.6×10⁻⁴ (400) → 1.5×10⁻⁵ (500 °C). So higher T gives lower equilibrium yield, but the catalyst + higher T make the reaction fast enough.
  • Continuous removal of NH₃ shifts equilibrium right (Le Chatelier) so the process never actually reaches equilibrium; unreacted N₂/H₂ is recycled; reaction heat is removed and used to preheat the incoming gas.

✍️ Must-know: Haber raw materials = N₂ (air) + H₂; catalyst = iron; product = ammonia. Favoured by high pressure and low temperature, but run ~500 °C / 200–400 atm as a compromise between yield and rate.

5.4.2 The Contact process (production of H₂SO₄)

Production of sulphuric acid by catalytic oxidation of SO₂ to SO₃: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH = −196 kJ (exothermic).

Steps: 1. Burn sulphur in excess air → SO₂: S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g). Impurities are removed (they would poison the catalyst). 2. Oxidise SO₂ → SO₃ at ~450 °C with V₂O₅ catalyst: 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃. 3. Dissolve SO₃ in concentrated (98%) H₂SO₄ → oleum/disulphuric acid H₂S₂O₇: SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇. (SO₃ is NOT added directly to water — that reaction is violently uncontrollable and makes a fog of H₂SO₄.) 4. Dilute oleum with the correct amount of water → H₂SO₄ of desired strength: H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄.

Le Chatelier in the Contact process: oxidation is exothermic → low temperature favours SO₃ but is too slow; high temperature is fast but lowers yield. ~450 °C is the compromise. Higher pressure favours SO₃ (fewer moles of gas on the right: Δn = 2−3 = −1), so moderate/atmospheric pressures are used; V₂O₅ speeds up the reaction.

✍️ Must-know: oleum = fuming sulphuric acid, a mixture of H₂SO₄ and free SO₃ (H₂S₂O₇). SO₃ is first dissolved in concentrated H₂SO₄, not water. Contact-process raw materials: S + O₂(air).
