6.3 Light as a Wave

◌ Not started
  • Light is a form of EM wave; visible light is what humans can see.
  • Light can travel through a vacuum (that's why we see the moon, stars, galaxies) as well as through matter.
  • Speed of light in vacuum: c = 2.99792458×10⁸ m/s ≈ 3.00×10⁸ m/s. Nothing travels faster.
  • Light travels fastest in empty space and slowest in solids (e.g., ~197,000 km/s in glass).

Wave equation: c = λ × f (wavelength × frequency = speed). - Greater frequency → smaller wavelength.

Propagation: light travels in straight lines from its source in all directions. A ray is an arrow-headed straight line showing the direction of propagation; a collection of rays is a beam; a drawing of light paths is a ray diagram. You see an object when light from it (emitted or reflected) enters your eyes.

Example 6.1: Red light λ = 700 nm. f = c/λ = 3×10⁸/700×10⁻⁹ = 4.29×10¹⁴ Hz.

Example 6.2: FM at 103.4 MHz. λ = c/f = 3×10⁸/103.4×10⁶ = 2.9 m.

✍️ Must-know: c = 3.00×10⁸ m/s in vacuum; c = λf. Light needs no medium and travels in straight lines.
