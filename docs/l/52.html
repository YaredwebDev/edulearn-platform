9.2 Dairy Cattle Breeds and Their Selection

◌ Not started

All domestic cattle belong to the family Bovidae, split into Bos indicus (indigenous/tropical) and Bos taurus (exotic/temperate). They differ in conformation, fitness and production.

Characteristic Bos indicus (e.g. Sahiwal, Boran, Fogera) Bos taurus (e.g. Holstein Friesian, Jersey)
Conformation much narrower body, longer legs, very prominent dewlap, humped wider body, shorter legs, less prominent dewlap, humpless
Environment adapted to tropical climate (Africa & Asia), natural selection adapted to temperate (Europe & USA), artificial selection
Production delayed maturity, long calving interval, slow growth, short lactation, low milk yield early maturing, short calving interval, fast growth, milked ~305 days, high milk yield

9.2.1 Ethiopian cattle breeds

~27 recognised breeds of Ethiopian origin, usually named after place of origin, all indigenous are multi-purpose (milk, meat, draft). There is no specialised local dairy breed. - Fogera — north-west (Fogera, Gondar); white with black spots/patches; small; ~915 L/lactation; butter fat 5.8%. - Barca/Begait — origin west Eritrea, abundant in Tigray & Gonder; variable colour, usually two-tone/pied incl. black; good milk cattle; ~682 L/lactation extensive (better if intensive). - Arsi-Bale — highlands of central Arsi & Bale; variable coat; small, compact body; selected cows up to 516 L/lactation; high butterfat 5.4–5.8%. - Horro — west Ethiopia (Horro Guduru, Wollega); dominant brown; medium; mainly meat & draft; selected cows up to 543 L/lactation.

9.2.2 Exotic dairy cattle breeds

Major world dairy breeds: Holstein-Friesian, Ayrshire, Brown Swiss, Guernsey, Jersey — all of European origin. Most popular in Ethiopia: Holstein Friesian & Jersey. - Holstein Friesian (HF) — originated Netherlands; Black-and-White or Red-and-White; large, angular; needs more feed than other breeds; outstanding producer >6,600 L/lactation; but milk low in butterfat (~3.6%) and protein (~3.2%). - Jersey — originated Island of Jersey, UK; small, light-grey to dark fawn; well-shaped udders, strong attachments, ease of calving; richest milk — butterfat ~5%, protein 3.8%; needs less feed than HF; good foragers; most heat-tolerant of the exotics.

9.2.3 Dairy cattle selection

9.2.3.1 Choice of breed

  • Climatic conditions: choose breeds hardy/tolerant of the tropics; pick the breed most popular locally (well suited to ambient temperature, poor feeds, parasites/diseases). Cross-bred animals with ~50% exotic inheritance are preferable — they keep the adaptability, heat tolerance & disease resistance of local animals.
  • Production objective: if market for cheese/butter → choose high-cream cow (Jersey or local breeds); if market for fresh milk → high yielder (Holstein Friesian).
  • Feeding system: grazing/forage systems → high grazing ability; cut-and-carry systems → less grazing ability acceptable.
  • Age of maturity: 4–5 months difference among major breeds. Small breeds (Jersey, Guernsey) start production soon after 24 months; large breeds only at 28–30 months; local breeds generally slow.
  • Cost: last consideration within reason; locally grown equal-quality cattle should be cheaper (lower transport costs).

9.2.3.2 Choice of individual animals

  • Physical characteristics: high-yielding dairy cows usually not large — lean, angular form, well-developed mammary system, wedge-shaped body (front less deep than rear), bright eyes, lean neck; udder healthy & large, all four quarters well demarcated with well-placed teats, attached high to the abdomen to prevent teat injury. Indicators = general appearance, dairy character, body capacity, mammary system.
  • Performance records: more reliable than visual appraisal; contain milk yield, milk composition, feeding efficiency, age at calving, condition at calving; also consider the breeding ability of sire/dam.
  • Free from diseases: contagious reproductive diseases — tuberculosis, brucellosis, leptospirosis, vibriosis, mastitis (females); trichomoniasis (bulls). Minimise by demanding negative test results at purchase.

✍️ Memorise the review point: HF = highest milk producer; breed-choice is not based on "attractive appearance".
