4.4 Work, Energy and Power

◌ Not started

Work done by a constant force = (component of force along displacement) × displacement: - W = F·S = F S cos θ (θ = angle between F and S). - SI unit: joule (J). 1 J = 1 N·m. (cgs: 1 erg = 1 dyne·cm; 1 J = 10⁷ erg.) - Work is a scalar.

✋ Sign of work: work can be positive (force along motion, θ<90°), negative (θ>90°, e.g. friction/opposing force), or zero (force perpendicular to motion, θ=90°). Carrying a bag horizontally while walking: the (vertical) support does no work on it; carrying it upstairs does positive work.

Total work: W_tot = algebraic sum of work done by each force = F_net S.

Work by gravity: W_g = mgd cos θ. Raising a mass a height h: applied force does +mgh and gravity does −mgh.

Work done by a variable force = area under the Force–displacement (F–S) graph. For a spring obeying Hooke's law, F = kx and work to stretch from x1 to x2 is W = ½k(x2²−x1²).

✍️ Must-know formulas: - Work: W = F S cos θ; total work = net force × displacement. - Work of gravity = −mgh when lifting, +mgh when lowering. - Work of a variable force = area under F–S graph; spring: W = ½kx².

Kinetic energy = energy due to motion; KE = ½mv² (scalar).

Work–Energy Theorem: the total (net) work done on a body changes its kinetic energy: W_net = ΔKE = ½mv_f² − ½mv_i².

Potential energy = energy due to position or shape. - Gravitational PE = mgh. - Elastic PE (spring) = ½kx².

Mechanical energy ME = KE + PE.

Conservative vs non-conservative forces: - A conservative force does work independent of the path; work over any closed path is zero. (Gravity, spring/elastic are conservative.) - A non-conservative force does work that is path dependent (e.g. friction dissipates energy). Work done by friction: W = −fk d. - When non-conservative forces act, W_nc = ΔME (mechanical energy not conserved).

Power = the rate of doing work / rate of converting energy. - Average power P = Work/time = W/t. - P = F v (power = force × velocity); instantaneous P = F·v (dot product). - SI unit: watt (W); 1 W = 1 J/s.

✍️ Must-know (power): P = W/t = F v. Example 4.15: an elevator + load (1000 kg car + 800 kg load) lifted at constant 2.5 m/s against 4000 N friction ⇒ motor must supply P = (mg + Ff)·v. Example 4.16: two forces on a sliding box — compute power of each force from P = Fv cos θ; net power = algebraic sum; perpendicular forces (weight, normal) contribute zero power.

Worked examples to master (energy): - Example 4.10: 10 kg block, 20 N force at 30° above horizontal displaces 5 m ⇒ W = 20 cos30° × 5. - Example 4.11: package pulled 400 N at 30°, friction 100 N — compute work by each force and total. - Example 4.12: 5 kg bag raised 2.5 m — applied force +mgh, gravity −mgh. - Example 4.13: block pushed up a rough incline — work by applied (+Fd), gravity (−mg sinθ d), normal (0), friction (negative). - Example 4.15/4.16 (power).
