3.3 The Logarithmic Functions and Their Graphs

3.3.1 The Logarithmic Function

The inverse of y=aˣ (a>0,a≠1) is the logarithmic function g(x)=logₐx, defined by:

y = logₐx ⟺ x = aʸ.

Since aˣ has domain ℝ and range (0,∞), logₐx has domain (0,∞) and range ℝ.

Evaluating: log₂4=2 (2²=4); log₄1=0; log₃27=3; log₁₀(1/10)=−1.

3.3.2 Graphs of Logarithmic Functions

f(x)=log₂x: domain (0,∞), range ℝ, x-intercept (1,0), vertical asymptote x=0 (y-axis), increasing.

Graph of f(x)=log₂x

f(x)=log(½)x: domain (0,∞), range ℝ, x-int (1,0), vertical asymptote x=0, decreasing.

Graph of f(x)=log(½)x

General shapes of y=logₐx:

  • a>1: increasing.
  • 0<a<1: decreasing.
  • Both: x-intercept (1,0), vertical asymptote x=0.
Both logarithmic shapes

Characteristics of y=logₐx (a>0,a≠1):

  • Domain ℝ⁺ (positive reals); range ℝ.
  • x-intercept (1,0); NO y-intercept.
  • Vertical asymptote: the y-axis (x=0).
  • a>1 increasing; 0<a<1 decreasing.

Reflections:

  • Graph of −f(x) = reflection of f in the x-axis.
  • Graph of f(−x) = reflection of f in the y-axis.
Reflections of y=log₂x
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: logₐx passes through (1,0), never touches the y-axis (x=0 is a vertical asymptote), is increasing for a>1 and decreasing for 0<a<1.

3.3.3 Natural Logarithms

Definition (Def 3.10): log_e x = ln x (logarithm to base e).

  • ln 1=0; ln e=1; ln e³=3; ln(e^(1/3))=⅓; ln(1/e)=−1.
  • The graphs of y=eˣ and y=ln x are reflections of each other across the line y=x.
