4.3 Phrasal Verbs: "get" (continued)

◌ Not started
  • get on — make progress/succeed: "Using a dictionary helps you get on with your English."
  • get over — recover from illness/distress: "He is getting over his heart attack."
  • get off — leave (a bus/train): "Get off the bus at Piazza."
  • get off on — be excited by / enjoy.
  • get in — enter; arrive: "The train got in on time."
  • get out — leave; exit: "Get out of the building."
  • get out on — (emphasise leaving).
  • get over with — finish something unpleasant quickly: "Let's get the exam over with."
  • get through — finish; survive; pass: "We got through the traffic."
  • get to — reach; begin: "We got to the park early."

✍️ Must-know: Unit 4's list: get on, get off, get off on, get in, get out, get out on, get over, get over with, get through, get to. "get over with" = finish an unpleasant task; "get over" = recover.
