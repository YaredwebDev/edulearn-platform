5.5 Feed Conservation and Compound Feed Manufacturing

◌ Not started

5.5.1 Feed conservation

Forage is conserved to feed livestock during dry-season shortage. Methods: hay and silage.

Hay making: forage harvested in the growing period and preserved by drying. Purpose: reduce moisture from 70–90% to 15–20% (this drying = curing, by sun & wind). Preserves grasses, legumes, fodders for later feeding. Stored in bales (small/large squares, rectangular/round) or a tripod system (three-legged stand 2–3 m tall that drains rainwater). Hay is the oldest & most important way to conserve feed; low cost, simple equipment, easy transport/storage, little wastage, can be marketed. Shortcomings: nutrient content & palatability vary greatly; very dependent on weather; late harvest lowers quality.

Silage making (ensilage): preserved material from the controlled (anaerobic) fermentation of green crops. Purpose: preserve succulent feeds for times of scarcity. Good silage from grasses/grass–legume mixtures/fodders (maize, sorghum). Harvest at the right stage (grasses at head-forming; legumes at early blooming; fodders at dough/milky stage). Silage process: wilt (~30% DM), chop, pack tightly in a silo, seal tightly. During packing: carbohydrates → organic acids; anaerobic fermentation → CO₂ + water + heat; lactic acid lowers pH to 4 or lower; aerobic bacteria die, anaerobic multiply; takes 2–4 weeks. Feeding: feed only to ruminants, not on empty rumen, not to late-pregnant cows or calves <6 months, not to sick/hungry/weak animals; start gradually. - Making a silo (above/below ground), add molasses/urea/salt/limestone while filling; measure ~1% molasses, 1–3 L water, 1 kg urea per 50–70 kg silage; harvest at 30–35% moisture; chop; seal to exclude air; after 2–3 weeks feed as appropriate.

✋ Trap (silage): silage is anaerobic fermentation in a sealed silo; lactic acid lowers pH to ≤4. Open only one end of the pit at a time to stop air entry/spoilage. Do not feed silage to calves <6 months or late-pregnant cows.

5.5.2 Compound-feed manufacturing

  • Manufacturing animal feed = blending several raw ingredients of different physical/chemical/nutritional composition into a homogeneous mixture meeting the target species' nutrient requirements.
  • Main ingredients in Ethiopia: cereals and agro-industrial by-products; may also contain salt, limestone and premixes. Premixes = complex mixtures of vitamins, minerals, trace elements & feed additives added at small levels.
  • Quality control is essential at all stages; the sequence/sophistication of operations (grinding, mixing, pelleting, etc. — see flow chart) varies with output.
