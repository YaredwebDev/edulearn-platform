6C Reading Skills: Effects of Global Warming on Economic Development

◌ Not started

Key facts: - "The most expensive thing we can do is nothing." If we fail to reduce global carbon emissions quickly & deeply, the economic/environmental consequences are serious. - Damage to property & infrastructure: sea-level rise, floods, droughts, wildfires, extreme storms require extensive repair of homes, roads, bridges, rail, airports, power lines, dams, seawalls. - Lost productivity: disruptions mean loss of work & school days; harm trade, transport, agriculture, fisheries, energy, tourism; severe rain/snow delay planting & harvest, cause outages; climate-related health risks (extreme heat, allergies, pollution) cut productivity. - Mass migration & security threats: more "climate refugees" (people forced to leave home due to drought/flood/climate disaster); mass movement & social disruption may cause civil unrest and even military intervention. - Coping costs: societies can prepare for some impacts (irrigating rain-fed areas, cooling livestock, managing pests; energy-efficient houses, early-warning systems, more responders; building seawalls, containing sewer overflows, strengthening bridges/subways) — but coping is costlier than reducing carbon emissions. - Post-disaster management: rebuilding after disasters is even more costly than preventive measures — and these costs don't include lives lost & irreversible consequences of unchecked greenhouse gases.

✍️ Reading skills practised: match paragraph main ideas (post-disaster management, coping costs, bad result, lost productivity, damage to property/infrastructure, mass migration); true/false/"Not Given"; pronoun reference (these); multiple choice on the less-costly measure (reducing global carbon emissions). Vocabulary from context: grapple, snarl, curtail, potent, unabated, irreversible.
