3.5 Uniform Circular Motion

◌ Not started

Uniform circular motion = circular motion with constant speed but continuously changing direction (so the velocity is changing — it is accelerated motion).

Angular and tangential displacement: Angular displacement θ = angle swept by the rotating object (radians). Tangential displacement s = tangential distance along the circumference, s = rθ. 1 radian = angle subtended when arc length = radius.

Angular and tangential velocity: - Angular velocity ω = rate of change of angular displacement; SI unit rad/s. Direction given by the right-hand rule. - Tangential velocity v = velocity tangent to the circle; SI unit m/s. Relationship: v = rω.

Centripetal (radial) acceleration: an acceleration in circular motion caused by change in the direction of velocity; always directed toward the centre of the circle. a_c = v²/r = rω².

Centripetal force: the force that causes centripetal acceleration; always perpendicular to the direction of motion, directed toward the centre. F_c = ma_c = mv²/r = mrω².

✍️ Must-know (circular motion equations): - v = rω (tangential = radius × angular velocity) - a_c = v²/r = ω²r - F_c = m v²/r = m ω²r Period T = time for one revolution; f = 1/T; ω = 2π/T = 2πf. A body moving with constant speed in a circle is accelerating because its direction changes.

Uniform circular motion in a horizontal plane: examples include a mass on a string whirled in a horizontal circle (tension provides centripetal force; Example 3.13: string can withstand 25 N, find max speed for 1 kg body), a car going round a roundabout (friction provides the centripetal force — Example 3.14: max safe speed around a curve of radius 40 m given coefficient of friction), the conical pendulum, and motion of a car round a banked road (the horizontal component of the normal force provides centripetal force; tan θ = v²/rg).

✋ Trap / driving note: cars skid on roundabouts when the required centripetal force exceeds the friction available (e.g. too fast, or wet road = less friction). The remedy is to reduce speed. On a banked road the needed force can come from the normal reaction, letting the car go faster safely.

Applications / examples in text: - Example 3.9: wheel ω = 40 rad/s, diameter 60 cm ⇒ tangential velocity v = rω = 0.3 × 40 = 12 m/s. - Example 3.10: car 50.4 km/h around a 40 m-diameter track — find angular speed & period. - Example 3.12: centrifuge — a point 7.5 cm from axis spinning at a given rpm; find centripetal acceleration and compare to g. - Example 3.11: 100 g ball whirled in a horizontal circle radius 40 cm; max speed if rod breaks at 50 N. - Exercise 3.8: merry-go-round kids feeling radial acceleration.

✍️ Must-know numerical relations for circular motion: ω = v/r; a_c = v²/r = ω²r; F_c = mv²/r. Convert km/h to m/s (÷3.6). Convert rev/min to rad/s via 2π and 60 s.
