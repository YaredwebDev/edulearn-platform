3.1 What Are Enzymes?

◌ Not started
  • Enzymes = protein molecules that act as biological catalysts (biocatalysts) and accelerate the rate of chemical reactions by lowering activation energy.
  • Activation energy = the minimum amount of energy required for reactants to be converted into products.
  • All enzymes are proteins made of chains of amino acids linked by peptide bonds.
  • Cells carry out metabolism = the chemical & physical changes including breakdown (catabolism) and synthesis (anabolism) of molecules.
  • Enzymes act on molecules called substrates, convert them into products, and remain unchanged after the reaction.

✍️ Must-know (True/False): enzymes are biocatalyst proteins (✓); "–ase" suffix indicates an enzyme (✓); all enzymes are proteins but not all proteins are enzymes (✓); our saliva contains the enzyme amylase (✓). Enzymes are NOT inorganic catalysts (they are organic/biochemical). The first discovered enzyme = diastase.

Lab activity 3.1 (salivary amylase): chewing bread slowly makes it taste sweet because salivary amylase converts starch into maltose (a sugar).
