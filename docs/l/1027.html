3.7 Writing a Narrative Paragraph

  • A paragraph = a group of sentences developing one main idea.
  • Topic sentence = carries the main idea (best at the start for beginners).
  • Supporting details = sentences that support the topic.
  • Outline = raw ideas in phrases/short sentences produced before the final paragraph.
  • Order the ideas correctly; avoid ideas unrelated to the topic.
  • A narrative paragraph tells a story — it needs characters (people/animals/things), a main point (theme/moral), and a setting (time & place).
  • Example outline (from the book): "My Lateness is My Enemy" — setting: Addis Zemen Primary School, summer; events: late arrival, missed a 10-point test, rank dropped first→second, lost the school reward.
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Narrative paragraph = story with characters, theme, setting, arranged in time order, with a clear topic sentence.
