4.5 Primary and Secondary Sexual Characteristics

◌ Not started
  • Primary sexual characteristics: present at birth.
  • Males: penis, scrotum, testicles.
  • Females: vagina, uterus, ovaries.
  • Puberty (adolescence): begins in the early teen years; the time when secondary sex characteristics begin to develop; sexual maturity is reached (potential for sexual reproduction).
  • Secondary sexual characteristics in males: growth/maintenance of male sex organs, increase in body hair, increase in muscle mass, increased growth of long bones of arms/legs, deepening of the voice.
  • Secondary sexual characteristics in females: increased growth of long bones, more hair (underarms, pubic area), hips broaden, more fat deposited in breasts/buttocks/thighs, menstrual cycle begins.

Hormonal control of puberty: - The hypothalamus produces hormones that cause the pituitary gland to release FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) and LH (luteinizing hormone). - In males: FSH → production of sperm cells; LH → production of testosterone in the testes (responsible for secondary sex characteristics). - In females: LH → causes eggs to be released into the oviduct (ovulation); FSH → stimulates follicle development and causes estrogen to be released (responsible for female secondary sex characteristics).

Psychological/social changes during puberty: body image changes; early/late puberty can cause low self-esteem and psychological pain; adolescents should seek medical/psychological advice if needed. - Adolescence = the period between puberty and adulthood, when a good deal of social learning takes place. - Biologically adult = reached puberty (capable of having children); economically adult = can support themselves; morally adult = responsible for actions.
