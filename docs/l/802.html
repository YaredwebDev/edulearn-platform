6.3 Exponential and logistic growth in populations

◌ Not started

Population growth rate (r) = percentage rate of change in the number of individuals over a specified period; the intrinsic rate of natural increase (r = B – D, birth rate minus death rate). Basic equation: dN/dt = rN (N = population size, t = time, r = per-capita rate). r can be positive (>0 = increase), zero (=0 = no change → "zero population growth"), or negative (<0 = decrease).

Exponential growth model — growth without environmental constraints (ideal, unlimited resources); per-capita rate stays constant positive. Graph = J-shaped curve. Equation: dN/dt = rmax·N (rmax = maximum per-capita rate under ideal conditions). Doubles each generation at r=1: 100→200→400→800…102400.

Logistic growth model — growth limited by resources; per-capita rate decreases as the population approaches a maximum. Graph = S-shaped curve. The maximum sustainable population = carrying capacity (K). Equation: dN/dt = rN((K−N)/K). When N reaches K, growth levels off (ΔN → 0). Example (r=1, K=250): 100→160→218→246→250→250 (level off at K).

✍️ Must-know: exponential = J-shaped, no limits; logistic = S-shaped, includes carrying capacity K. Logistic growth levels off at carrying capacity (NOT J-shaped). Density-dependent regulation (food, territory, predation) vs density-independent (air pollution is NOT density-dependent).
