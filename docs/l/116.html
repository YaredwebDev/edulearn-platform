2.10 Animal Behavior

◌ Not started

Animal behavior = all the ways animals interact with other organisms and the physical environment (movement, interactions within/with the environment, and learning about it).

2.5.1 Types of behavior

Innate (inherent) behavior — inborn, determined by genes, independent of experience, specific to a species. Three types: 1. Instinctive: web-making in spiders; nest-building in birds; chicks opening mouths when the mother returns; honeybee dance after finding food. 2. Reflex (reflexive): sudden, involuntary response to stimuli (pull hand from hot object, blink). Reflex messages travel only to the spinal cord (not the brain) for a fast response. 3. Orientation: - Taxis — directed movement relative to a stimulus (towards = positive taxis, away = negative taxis). Example: cockroaches moving away from light (negative phototaxis). Types: phototaxis (light), chemotaxis (chemicals), thigmotaxis (touch), geotaxis (gravity). - Kinesis — undirected, random movement in response to intensity of a stimulus (not its direction/source). Example: woodlice movement with temperature.

Learned (acquired) behavior — not inherited/not gene-determined; acquired during the individual's lifetime; allows adaptation to environmental change. Types: 1. Habituation — stops responding to a repeated, irrelevant stimulus (non-associative; no punishment/reward link). Example: you stop noticing a TV you first found annoying. 2. Classical conditioning — associative learning; discovered by Ivan Pavlov. Three stages: (1) before — UCS produces UCR; (2) during — a neutral stimulus becomes the conditioned stimulus (CS) paired with UCS over trials; (3) after — CS alone produces a conditioned response. 3. Operant conditioning — associative learning; discovered by B. F. Skinner. Uses rewards/punishments; no existing stimulus–response pair needed. Reinforced (rewarded) behavior is repeated; punished behavior occurs less. Three response types: neutral operants (neither increase nor decrease repetition), reinforcers (increase repetition; positive or negative), punishers (decrease repetition). 4. Sensitization (reverse tolerance) — non-associative; repeated stimulus produces a progressively amplified response (e.g. repetition of a painful stimulus makes one more sensitive). 5. Insight learning — based on past experience + reasoning; a hallmark of human behavior (problem solving: fire-starting to space travel). Latent learning also mentioned.

✍️ Must-know scientists: classical conditioning → Pavlov; operant conditioning → Skinner. Behavior with 24-hr rhythm even in constant darkness = circadian rhythm. Behavior changing drastically over a lifetime = learned. A female insect returning to its larval host plant = innate/instinctive (not learned).

2.5.2 Patterns of behavior

  1. Behavioral cycles — responses to periodic environmental changes; daily or seasonal. - Seasonal migration — movement between habitats with seasons (food/sunlight/temperature/breeding). Example: whales/birds moving Arctic/Antarctic → tropics. - Circadian rhythms (biological clocks) — 24-hour cycles, part of the body's internal clock; e.g. the sleep–wake cycle.
  2. Reproductive behavior — coordinates timing & patterning of reproduction; vital for locating/selecting mates, producing & rearing offspring. Example: courtship (sounds, visual displays, chemicals).
  3. Social behavior — seen in animals living in groups; division of labor (workers & soldiers). Best developed in ants, termites, bees, wasps; also elephants, penguins, humans, other primates.
  4. Competition — for space, territory, water, mates, food between organisms coexisting in the same environment. Two types: intraspecific (within same species) and interspecific (between different species).
  5. Territoriality — protecting a space (territory) from others; territories contain needed resources. Often defended by display behavior (safer & less energy than fighting). Examples: male dogs/lions mark territory with pheromones in urine; male gorillas pound chests; robins display red breast.
  6. Communication behavior — via sight, sound, tactile (touch), and chemical cues (pheromones). Examples: birds sing, frogs croak; ants use pheromones to mark trails to food.

✍️ Must-know: communication chemicals = pheromones; territorial marking by male dogs/lions uses pheromones in urine; ants mark food trails with pheromones.
