3D.1 Contextual guessing

Use sense relationships (synonyms, antonyms, hyponyms) and word structure to guess meanings without a dictionary. Practised examples: nebulous (unclear — clue "make himself clear"), pseudonym (false name), austere (plain — clue "clothing and homes are plain"), franchise, hyperactive, celestial (sun, moon, stars). Passage "Peace of Mind" uses elusive (hard to capture), attainable/practically achievable, unrest, invigorating.

3D.2 Word formation (affixes: prefixes & suffixes)

  • Affixation = adding prefixes, infixes, suffixes to root words to form new words (e.g. pass → passable → impassable).
  • Derivational affixes create new words and can change word class (verb → adjective via -able: pass→passable) or change meaning (prefix im- flips positive→negative without changing class).
  • Inflectional affixes only change grammar (number, tense, degree), not class/meaning: -er (strong→stronger), -ed (ask→asked), -s (book→books).

3D.3 Phrasal verbs with "let"

Common & used for effective communication. Meanings: - let in (allow to enter) · let off (not punish; let someone leave a vehicle/bus) · let on (reveal a secret/pretend; e.g. she never let on that she had a boyfriend) · let out (release; allow to leave; enlarge clothes) · let down (disappoint; lower) · let up (stop/decrease, e.g. the rain is letting up) · let by (allow to pass) · let the cat out (of the bag) (reveal a secret).
