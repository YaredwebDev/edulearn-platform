10.4 Grammar

10.4.1 Connectors of Compare-Contrast

  • Compare (similarities): also, just as, as well as, like/likewise, both, comparatively, similarly/similar, identical, equivalent, the same as, too, have in common, share the same, in the same manner, in the same way.
  • Contrast (differences): although, yet, while, unlike, however, in contrast to, but, on the other hand, contrarily, whereas, nevertheless, despite, in spite of.
  • Examples: "The economy is strong, but/yet there are frequent strikes." "Although/while there are strikes, the economy is strong." "In spite of / despite the strikes, the economy is strong." "There are strikes; however/nevertheless, the economy is strong." "Football and volleyball have many things in common." "Television gives information; similarly, radio provides information."
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: comparison words signal similarity; contrast words signal difference. Note the position patterns: although/while/despite can start; however/nevertheless usually after a semicolon/full stop; "in contrast to" + noun.

10.4.2 Modal auxiliaries (should, ought to, must, have to, need to, can, will)

should / ought to — advice:

  • should + bare infinitive; ought to + infinitive. "You should take more exercise." "You ought to ask for time off."
  • Negative: shouldn't / oughtn't to. Question: "Should I take the tablets?" (word order with "Should I…").
  • ✋ Errors: "She should to get it" ✋ → "She should get it." "He should going" ✋ → "He should go." Don't change form for person.

must / have to — rules & necessity:

  • "Children must start school when they are five." "All students have to wear a uniform."
  • must = the speaker's opinion/necessity; have to = external rule (but both OK for rules). For rules usually "Do … have to…?"
  • Past of must/have to = had to: "I had to go to a wedding last week."
  • ✋ Errors: "She musts do her homework" ✋ → "She must do." "She must to improve" ✋ → "She must improve."

can / could — requests & permission:

  • "Can I have a return ticket, please?" "Could I have a sandwich, please?" "Can/Could you help me, please?"
  • Add "please" when asking; "yes, of course" / "no problem" to accept; "I'm afraid I can't" (+ reason) to refuse.

will — future arrangements, promises, offers, refusals:

  • will + bare infinitive; doesn't change by person. "The doctor will see you in a few minutes." "I'll make sure she leaves on time." "I won't let him drive my car." Negative = won't; speech contraction = 'll.
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: should/ought to (advice); must/have to (obligation, past = had to); can/could (requests/permission); will/won't (future/promise/refusal). Modals take bare infinitive (except ought to, have to). Common errors: "must to," "should to," "she musts."

10.4.3 Direct and Indirect Objects

  • Direct object = the thing the action is done to: "I bought some flowers." "She closed the window." "Some of the boys noticed me."
  • Indirect object = the person who benefits/receives: "I bought Tirhas some flowers." "I gave Ali the book."
  • Position: indirect object can come before the direct object ("I bought Tirhas some flowers") OR in a prepositional phrase after it ("I bought some flowers to Tirhas"). "Bacha told me the truth." "She handed the book to the librarian."

10.4.4 Transitive and Intransitive Verbs

  • Intransitive verbs take no object: "Solomon Barega runs fast." "Her tooth ached." (runs, ached)
  • Transitive verbs take an object: "He is washing clothes." "She read a novel." (is washing, read)
  • ✋ Must-know: If the verb needs something to complete its meaning (an object), it's transitive; if the action doesn't involve anyone/anything other than the subject, it's intransitive.
