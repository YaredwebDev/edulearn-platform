1F.1 Types of sentences by function/purpose

  • Declarative — gives information: My father is an engineer.
  • Interrogative — asks a question: What is your father's job?
  • Imperative — gives a command/request: Keep quiet. Be an active participant.
  • Exclamatory — expresses strong emotion/surprise: What hot weather!

1F.2 Structural types of sentences (by clause)

  • Simple — one principal clause: My uncle is a teacher.
  • Complex — one principal + one/more dependent clauses: My uncle, who lives in Ambo, is a teacher.
  • Compound — two/more principal clauses joined by a coordinating connector (and, but, or, nor, for, yet), a conjunctive adverb or semicolon: My uncle is a teacher, but my aunt is a merchant.
  • Compound-complex — two/more principal clauses + one/more dependent clauses: My uncle is very polite, but he loses his temper when he gets angry.

1F.3 Punctuation & mechanics

  • Period (.) — end of declarative sentences and after many abbreviations.
  • Question mark (?) — a direct question.
  • Exclamation point (!) — an outcry/emphasis.
  • Comma (,) — separation of ideas/elements within a sentence; before coordinating connectors joining clauses.
  • Semicolon (;) — connects two independent clauses (closer than a period): Selam was hurt; she knew she only said it to upset him.
  • Colon (:) — before a list/series: He studied four subjects: politics, philosophy, sociology and economics.
  • Other punctuation noted: dash, hyphen, parentheses, brackets, braces, apostrophe, quotation marks, ellipsis.

1F.4 Errors in sentence structure (three common kinds)

  1. Fragments — incomplete sentences (often a subject with no predicate): ✗ The child who has a rash. / ✗ Since the drugs have many side effects.
  2. Run-ons — two independent clauses not joined grammatically: ✗ The doctor performed the operation the patient died.
  3. Comma splices (CS) — two independent clauses joined only by a comma (not acceptable): ✗ The doctor performed the operation, the patient died.

1F.5 Report writing

  • A report = a written presentation of factual information based on an investigation/inquiry, used for solving problems or making decisions.
  • Qualities of a good report: clarity (in idea & structure), conciseness, and appropriateness.
  • Report structure: introduction → body → conclusion. (Write on "causes, effects & solutions of deforestation".)
