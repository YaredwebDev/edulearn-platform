4.6 Impulse and Linear Momentum

◌ Not started

Linear momentum P is the product of mass and velocity: P = mv. It is a vector in the direction of velocity. SI unit kg·m/s. - Impulse J = change of momentum = F Δt (force × time). Impulse is a vector; its direction is the change in momentum. - Newton's second law in momentum form: F_net = ΔP/Δt (net force = rate of change of momentum).

✋ Trap: momentum is a vector; direction matters. A ball bouncing back with the same speed has momentum change ΔP = m(v_f − v_i); if it rebounds (reverses direction), ΔP = m(v + v_i) type. Example 4.22: 1.5 kg ball at 5 m/s East rebounds at 5 m/s West ⇒ ΔP = 1.5×(−5 − 5) = −15 kg·m/s West (magnitude 15). Impulse = ΔP.

Impulse–momentum theorem: Impulse = change in momentum: J = F Δt = ΔP = m v_f − m v_i.

✋ Trap / safety: a rapid change in momentum (short Δt) requires a large net force. Airbags, crumple zones, and following through lengthen Δt and thus reduce the force → less injury. Example 4.23: ball rebounds, contact 0.20 s — find ΔP, impulse, and net force.

Law of conservation of linear momentum: for an isolated system (no external force), total momentum is conserved: m1u1 + m2u2 = m1v1 + m2v2 (before = after). Collisions conserve total momentum always (isolated); KE is conserved only in elastic collisions.

Collisions in one dimension: - Elastic collision: both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved. - Inelastic collision: momentum conserved but kinetic energy not conserved. - Perfectly inelastic collision: the objects stick together and move with a common final velocity v: m1v1i + m2v2i = (m1+m2)v.

✍️ Must-know formulas: - P = mv; Impulse J = FΔt = ΔP; F_net = ΔP/Δt. - Conservation: m1u1+m2u2 = m1v1+m2v2. - Perfectly inelastic: m1v1i+m2v2i = (m1+m2)v.

Worked examples to master: - Example 4.20: 0.5 kg matchbox at 6 m/s ⇒ P = 3 kg·m/s +x. - Example 4.21: change in momentum when speed goes 6→15 m/s. - Example 4.24: football penalty — F Δt = ΔP ⇒ v from a kick; 1200 N × 0.02 s on 0.4 kg ball. - Exercise 4.8 (Venus Williams' tennis serve): F from impulse. - Example 4.25: elastic collision of 4 kg (2 m/s) and 6 kg (1 m/s) moving opposite — solve two simultaneous equations for final velocities. - Example 4.26: gliders on an air track — find final velocity of glider A from momentum conservation. - Example 4.27: recoil — pistol+bullet system: bullet goes off ⇒ pistol recoils backward (opposite direction). - Example 4.28: dump truck (25000 kg at 72 km/h) hits bajaj (150 kg at 54 km/h opposite) ⇒ perfectly inelastic, final common velocity ≈ 19.7 m/s. - Example 4.29/4.30: ballistic pendulum — bullet embeds in a suspended block that rises to height h; use conservation of momentum for the impact then conservation of energy for the swing to find bullet speed.

Centre of mass (CM): a point where the total mass of a body/system is assumed concentrated. Coordinates: - x_cm = Σ mi xi / Σ mi and y_cm = Σ mi yi / Σ mi (weighted average). - Example 4.31: three masses at given (x,y) — find x_cm and y_cm. - The CM of an isolated system moves with constant velocity (motion of a flipped baseball bat: CM follows a parabola while the bat spins). Centre of gravity may lie outside an object (e.g. a ring).

Rockets: conservation of momentum explains rocket propulsion — rocket pushes exhaust gas backward, gas pushes the rocket forward (ΔP_rocket = −ΔP_gas).
