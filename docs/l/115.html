2.9 The Economic Importance of Animals (Insects)

◌ Not started

Insects have both positive (beneficial) and negative (harmful) impacts on economy, life and the ecosystem.

2.4.1 Beneficial aspects of insects

  • A. Agriculture:
  • Pollination — flower-visiting insects transfer male gametes (in pollen) to female gametes. Essential for reproduction of most flowering plants; ~35% of world crop production results from insect pollination.
  • Pest regulation (natural biological control) — predatory/parasitoid insects attack insect pests. Examples: mantis (Stagmomantis) eats grasshoppers & caterpillars; ladybird beetle / Chilomenes eats aphids (cotton) and scale insects (orange/lemon); blister beetle (Epicauta) eats locust eggs.
  • Soil aeration & nutrient cycling (feeding on weeds, channels for water/air/roots; decomposers enhance nutrient cycles).
  • B. Food: edible insects are a sustainable protein source; over 1,462 recorded species of edible insects worldwide (mostly in Asia & Central America); crickets, grasshoppers, beetle/moth larvae, termites are eaten. Insect farming addresses food/feed security (high protein/fat/minerals, high growth & feed-conversion, low environmental footprint). Insects also feed birds, amphibians, reptiles, fish, mammals.
  • C. Industry (commercial products):
  • Honey & beeswax — honeybees (Apis mellifera).
  • Silk — silkworms (Bombyx mori) produce silk fibres for textiles.
  • Shellac — a resin secreted by lac insects (Laccifer lacca); used in dyes, inks, polishes, sealing waxes, stiffening felt hats.
  • Cochineal — red pigment from scale insects (Dactylopius coccus); used in foods, beverages, cosmetics (lipstick), art.
  • Tannic acid — from gall tissues (formed when tiny wasps of family Cynipidae stimulate trees); used in dyeing/tanning leather and making inks.
  • D. Health and medicine:
  • Honey treats burns, chronic & post-surgical wounds.
  • Bee & ant venom treat joint pain; bee products promote immune health, improve circulation, decrease inflammation.
  • Blister beetles secrete cantharidin, a powerful protein blocker; effective against severe viral infections and possibly useful for treating cancer tumours resistant to radiation/chemotherapy (reacts with genetic material of hostile cells).
  • Ground grasshoppers used as pain relievers (especially migraines) in some African cultures.

2.4.2 Harmful aspects of insects

  • Crop/store pests feeding on leaves, stems, buds, flowers, fruits, seeds → damage crops & stored grain. Examples: locusts, caterpillars, bugs, hoppers, aphids.
  • Locusts are among the most destructive pests (desert locusts), destructive in hot/dry regions; migrate in huge swarms devouring plants.
  • Stored cereal grain pests: Rice Weevil (Sitophilus oryzae), Lesser Grain Borer (Rhyzopertha dominica), Rust Red Flour Beetle (Tribolium spp.), Sawtooth Grain Beetle (Oryzaephilus surinamensis), Flat Grain Beetle (Cryptolestes spp.).
  • Disease vectors / intermediate hosts:
  • Anopheles mosquito → malaria (Plasmodium).
  • Culex mosquito → filariasis (filarial worms).
  • Tsetse fly → African sleeping sickness (Trypanosoma).
  • Housefly (Musca domestica) → food- and water-borne diseases.

✍️ Must-know vector–disease matches: Anopheles→malaria; Culex→filariasis; tsetse fly→African sleeping sickness (trypanosomiasis); housefly→food/water-borne diseases. Beneficial: mantis eats grasshoppers, ladybird eats aphids, blister beetle eats locust eggs.
