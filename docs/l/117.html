2.6 Homeostasis in Animals

◌ Not started

Homeostasis = the self-regulatory process by which animals maintain stable internal conditions regardless of external conditions, keeping body/cell conditions at a set point.

The four components of homeostasis: stimulus (a change forcing a response, e.g. body temperature/glucose/water), receptor (detects change & sends signal; e.g. thermoreceptors), control center (receives messages & commands; the hypothalamus of the brain), effector (acts to reverse the change; organs/tissues such as kidney, liver, heart; muscles/glands).

2.6.1 Thermoregulation

Thermoregulation = maintaining constant internal body temperature (via behaviour, physiology, morphology). Two animal groups: 1. Poikilothermic (ectothermic, cold-blooded) — body temperature ≈ environment, varies with it; lack internal control. Use behaviour (burrowing, sunning on rocks, swimming to cool, group activity like bees in a hive). 2. Homeothermic (endothermic, warm-blooded) — generate internal heat to keep a constant body temperature; use morphological, physiological & behavioural methods.

Homeotherms keeping warm in cold: - Insulation: fur, fat, feathers (arctic fox fluffy tail; birds puff up feathers; humans' hair raises). - Vasoconstriction — narrowing of skin blood vessels (contraction of smooth muscles) → reduces peripheral blood flow, retains heat. - Shivering — involuntary muscle contractions; respiration releases heat to warm the body.

Homeotherms losing heat when hot: - Vasodilation — widening of skin-surface blood vessels → more blood & heat to the surface → heat lost by radiation & evaporation. - Sweating — sweat from sweat glands travels up the duct & out the pore; evaporates to cool.

Coordination: the hypothalamus coordinates these. Four mechanisms of heat exchange with the environment: radiation, evaporation, convection, conduction.

Morphological effects: - Body structure: large ears in hot areas lose heat; small ears & fur in cold areas minimize heat loss. - Surface-area-to-volume (SA:V) ratio: smaller animals have higher SA:V → more heat loss (and higher metabolic rate); larger animals have lower SA:V → less heat loss. Rabbit (small) loses heat readily; elephant (large) retains heat.

Behavioral regulation: seeking hot/cold habitats, making nests/digging burrows, huddling together, wearing clothes/using air conditioning (humans).

✍️ Must-know: hairs standing up when cold = to trap air under them to keep warmer; if core temperature too high → blood vessels to skin dilate (and sweating increases). An animal that sweats when hot and increases muscular activity when cold is homeothermic. Smaller mammals have higher metabolic rates because of higher SA:V ratios.

2.6.2 Osmoregulation

Osmoregulation = the process that regulates the osmotic pressure of fluids and electrolytic balance to maintain homeostasis. ~60% of the human body is fluid: ~2/3 intracellular, ~1/3 extracellular (interstitial fluid + blood plasma).

  • Detected by osmoreceptors (in the hypothalamus and the kidneys).
  • Two types:
  • Osmoconformers — match their body's osmolarity to their surroundings (starfish, jellyfish, lobsters).
  • Osmoregulators — actively regulate osmotic pressure independent of the environment (many vertebrates incl. humans).
  • Kidney is the main osmoregulatory organ in humans: when body water is high → releases a large amount of hypotonic urine; when water is low → retains water and produces a small amount of hypertonic urine.
  • Controlled by the hypothalamus and Antidiuretic hormone (ADH) secreted by the pituitary gland.

✍️ Must-know: regulation of solutes + loss/gain of water = osmoregulation. ADH (from pituitary) + hypothalamus control it; kidney produces hypotonic urine when water is high.

2.6.3 Blood sugar regulation

  • Glucose is the main energy source (including for the brain).
  • Two pancreatic hormones control blood glucose:
  • Insulin — released when blood glucose is high (and glucagon low); promotes conversion of glucose → glycogen (storage) in the liver → lowers blood glucose.
  • Glucagon — released when blood glucose is low (glucagon high); promotes conversion of glycogen → glucose in the liver → raises blood glucose.
  • Glycogen is stored in the liver.

✍️ Must-know: high blood sugar → insulin → glucose→glycogen (negative feedback lowering glucose). Glucagon promotes hydrolysis of glycogen to glucose (raising glucose). Diabetes = blood glucose not regulated properly.

2.6.4 Control of homeostasis — feedback mechanisms

Feedback mechanism = a physiological regulation system that returns the body to its normal internal state (receptor senses → control center → effector).

  • Negative feedback — a change triggers a response that reverses the change; returns the body to the set point (homeostasis). Three components: sensor/receptor, control center, effector. Example: temperature regulation (too warm → vasodilation + sweating; too cold → vasoconstriction + shivering), and insulin lowering high blood glucose. Most homeostatic control is negative feedback.
  • Positive feedback — a change triggers a response that amplifies the change in the same direction, taking the body away from homeostasis (rare in homeostasis).

✍️ Must-know: negative feedback = reverses deviation back to set point (e.g., high glucose → insulin → lower glucose); positive feedback accelerates the change. Correct negative-feedback order = stimulus → receptor → control center → effector → response.
