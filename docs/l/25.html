UNIT 4 · LESSON 6 OF 6 · GRADE 11 AGRICULTURE

🧮 Key facts to memorise

◌ Not started
  • Domestication mostly 8000–2500 BC.
  • Species = interbreeding group; breed = visibly similar subgroup of a species.
  • Ethiopian population (2021): cattle ~70 M, sheep ~43 M, goats ~52 M, camels ~8 M, chicken ~57 M, ~7 M hives; mostly local breeds.
  • Cattle Bovid/genus Bos (indicus humped vs taurus); sheep Ovis; goat Capra (mohair from Angora); camel Camelidae (dromedary 1 hump, bactrian 2); poultry Gallus (layers/broilers, omnivorous); bees (worker/queen/drone).
  • Ruminant = 4-chambered stomach (rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum). Pseudo-ruminant = 3-chambered, no rumen (camel). Monogastric = single stomach (poultry, pig, horse). No rumen in monogastrics.
  • Rumen: fermentation, cellulose digestion, cud; omasum "many plies" squeezes water; abomasum = true stomach.
  • Poultry: crop (storage) → proventriculus (acid/enzymes) → gizzard (muscular) → ceca (bacteria) → cloaca/vent.
  • Livestock: >34% global food protein; livelihood of 65% Ethiopians; 45–48% ag GDP; 16–19% export earnings.
  • Major constraints: feed shortage, genetic, diseases/parasites, poor knowledge/traditional system, socio-economic (power, transport, credit), taboos.
  • Feed: natural pasture + crop residues supply >90% feed; crude protein only 3–6%.
  • Systems: extensive (pastoralism → nomads/transhumance, agro-pastoral, ranching, mixed) — low input; semi-intensive — near towns; intensive (pig, broiler, feedlot, commercial dairy) — high input, market-oriented.
  • Zero grazing = cut-and-carry; balanced ration; feedlot = fattening ~3–4 months.

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) distinguish species vs breed and local/exotic/hybrid breeds; (2) name the mammalian/avian/bee species in Ethiopia and give population/breed facts; (3) explain and give an example of ruminant (cow/sheep/goat), pseudo-ruminant (camel) and monogastric (poultry/pig/horse) digestive systems, including the four chambers and the poultry pathway (crop→proventriculus→gizzard→ceca→cloaca); (4) state the importance of animal production (food, economy/livelihood numbers, work, raw materials, feed, clothing, socio-cultural); (5) list the main constraints and mitigation strategies; and (6) compare extensive, semi-intensive and intensive production systems and the sub-forms (pastoralism/nomad/transhumance, agro-pastoral, ranching, mixed farming)? Re-answer the review too (which is not a monogastric component = rumen; chicken is omnivorous; disease is NOT a socio-economic constraint — it's a health constraint; intensive system is market-oriented; among extensive forms, ranching involves relatively better livestock management).
