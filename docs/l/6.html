1.6 Indigenous Knowledge in Crop Production

◌ Not started
  • Indigenous knowledge (IK) = traditional/local "home-grown" knowledge that local communities accumulate over generations in a particular place; unique to a culture and usually develops independently of scientific knowledge (includes beliefs/traditions about culture & landscape).
  • IK often arises from local production problems: land degradation, soil erosion, climate change, topography, small landholdings, and the community's social-cultural setting.
  • Examples given in the textbook:
  • Gumuz society: natural resources viewed as a gift/blessing/creation of the Yamba (God), sustaining past, present and future generations → they conserve resources (improving soil fertility, rainfall, soil moisture/temperature, crop productivity).
  • Konso people: extensive terracing against soil erosion plus traditional irrigation to cope with variable rainfall.
  • Bale farmers: dig trenches around potato plots to stop porcupine attacks.
  • Gondar farmers: shift barns between fields to use cow dung as fertiliser.
  • Highland farmers: use kitchen ash to fertilise and reduce soil acidity, and smoke to reduce frost damage.

✍️ Must-know: indigenous knowledge is local, generationally accumulated and independent of (complementary to) scientific knowledge. Konso terracing + irrigation; Gumuz conservation beliefs; Bale trenches vs porcupines; Gondar cow-dung barn rotation; highland ash (fertiliser, acidity) and smoke (frost).
