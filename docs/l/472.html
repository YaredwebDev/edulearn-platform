10F Writing Skills

◌ Not started

10F.1 Describing place, people & things (adjectives for descriptive writing)

  • Use descriptive words (adjectives/adverbs) to describe places/people/things: Guna is a very high and cold mountain; In Awash National Park there are many wild asses and beautiful birds (high, cold, wild, beautiful).
  • List colour & quality adjectives and write sentences; describe people/animals with intelligent, innocent, well-mannered, little, white.

10F.2 Review of reported speech for report writing

  • Reported/indirect speech is common in report writing. Identify indirect statements: the current Secretary-General said Kofi was one-of-a-kind; IOM said it urgently needs $27 million; WFP said it has delivered food to more than a million people. Convert between indirect and direct speech.

10F.3 Writing an article/report for a radio broadcast

  • Guidelines: write conversationally (like talking to an audience); paint a picture in the audience's mind; know your audience; write in active voice & present tense.
  • How are inventions invented? — inventors identify a need/problem, think of a creative solution, work hard. Match inventor → inspiration: Blaise Pascal (needing something unavailable — a mechanical adding machine, the Pascaline, to help his tax-collector father calculate faster); Margaret Knight (wanting to help — a stop-motion safety device for textile looms, and later the flat-bottomed paper bag; 25+ patents).
  • Write a report on your own invented technology for a radio programme (describe it well with descriptive words, following the radio-report rules).

10F.4 Extensive writing

  • Develop your innovation report into a descriptive essay with: background info followed by a thesis statement, supporting paragraphs, and a concluding paragraph.

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) summarise the AI and robotics (Sophia/iCog) listening texts and report robot use in industries; (2) express dissatisfaction, debate the importance of AI to Ethiopia, and give a speech proposing a solution to AI's labour threat; (3) explain the reading's two opposing views on new technology and the labour market and write a one-paragraph summary; (4) distinguish homophones & homographs and use the rule/run phrasal verbs; (5) form all three/four conditionals (incl. adverbial clauses of condition with if/unless/whether/provided/in the event), choose simple present vs present progressive, and classify the eight types of adjectives; and (6) write a descriptive essay (background + thesis + supporting + conclusion) and use reported speech in a radio report? Try the conditional-correction, adjective-type, and homophone/homograph exercises.
