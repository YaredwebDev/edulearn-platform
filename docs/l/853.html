2.8 Transport in Plants

Vascular bundles = xylem + phloem.

  • Xylem: transports water and dissolved minerals from roots to leaves; made of elongated dead cells joined end-to-end.
  • Phloem: transports food (organic matter, sucrose, amino acids) from leaves to the rest of the plant (translocation); made of living cells; transport up and down (source → sink).

2.8.1 Uptake of water and minerals

  • Water enters the root through root hairs (elongated single cells with large surface area) — this is absorption.
  • Water passes passively (by osmosis across the membrane/cytoplasm and diffusion along porous cell walls) to the root xylem.
  • Water is pulled upward by transpiration pull (the main force); cohesion (hydrogen bonds between water molecules) holds the water column together; adhesion (water to cell walls) resists gravity.

2.8.2 Translocation of organic matter (food)

  • Translocation = transport of food from source (leaf) to sink (stem/root).
  • Occurs through phloem; it is active transport (living cells use energy).
  • Ringing/girdling experiment: removing a ring of bark (with phloem) causes a swelling above the girdle due to accumulation of food. Ringing ultimately kills the plant because food can't reach the root; the root dies first.
✍️ Must-know: Xylem = water & minerals (dead cells, one-way up); Phloem = food (living cells, active, two-way).
