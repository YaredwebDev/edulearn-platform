5.2 Circulatory System

5.2.1 The Heart

  • A muscular organ located in the chest; about the size of a fist; pumps blood.
  • Four chambers: two upper atria (right and left) and two lower ventricles (right and left).
  • Septum: muscular wall separating right and left halves.
  • Valves (prevent backflow):
  • Tricuspid valve (right atrium ↔ right ventricle)
  • Bicuspid (mitral) valve (left atrium ↔ left ventricle)
  • Semilunar valves (base of pulmonary artery and aorta)
  • Pericardium: protective membrane around the heart.

Blood circulation

  • Pulmonary circulation: right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium (deoxygenated blood goes to lungs to pick up O₂).
  • Systemic circulation: left ventricle → aorta → body → vena cavae → right atrium (oxygenated blood to body).

Cardiac cycle

  • Systole: heart muscle contracts (pumps blood out).
  • Diastole: heart muscle relaxes (fills with blood).
  • Heart sounds: "Lub" (AV valves closing) and "Dup" (semilunar valves closing).
  • Pulse: the expansion and recoil of arteries with each heartbeat.
  • Blood pressure: force of blood against artery walls; normal ~120/80 mmHg (systolic/diastolic).

5.2.3 Blood

  • Plasma: liquid part (~55%), contains water, proteins, nutrients, wastes, hormones.
  • Red blood cells (erythrocytes): carry O₂/CO₂ via haemoglobin; no nucleus; biconcave discs; made in bone marrow.
  • White blood cells (leucocytes): defend against infection (phagocytes: neutrophils, macrophages; lymphocytes: T and B cells).
  • Platelets (thrombocytes): help blood clotting.

Blood groups (ABO + Rh):

  • Antigens on red cells: A, B, AB, O. Antibodies in plasma: anti-A, anti-B.
  • Universal donor: O (no antigens). Universal recipient: AB (no antibodies).
  • Rh factor (D antigen): Rh⁺ or Rh⁻. If an Rh⁻ mother carries an Rh⁺ baby, her antibodies may attack the baby's red cells → erythroblastosis fetalis (haemolytic disease of the newborn).

5.2.4 Lymphatic System

  • Lymph: fluid from tissue spaces collected into lymph vessels.
  • Lymph nodes, tonsils, spleen, thymus are part of it.
  • Functions: returns tissue fluid to blood; transports fats (via lacteals); filters pathogens; produces lymphocytes; immunity.
  • Elephantiasis: caused by filarial worms (e.g., Wuchereria bancrofti, Brugia malayi) blocking lymphatic vessels → severe swelling of limbs.

5.2.5 Circulatory System Diseases

  • Leukemia: cancer of blood-forming tissues (bone marrow) → abnormal white cells.
  • Varicose veins: swollen, twisted veins due to faulty valves.
  • Anaemia: low haemoglobin/red cells → fatigue (iron deficiency common).
  • Cardiovascular diseases: hypertension (high BP), coronary heart disease (blocked heart arteries → angina, heart attack), stroke (blocked/ruptured brain blood vessel).
  • Bradycardia: heart rate below 60 beats/min.
  • Tachycardia: heart rate above 100 beats/min.
  • Arrhythmia: irregular heart rhythm.
  • Atherosclerosis: hardening/narrowing of arteries by fatty plaques.
  • Heart failure: heart can't pump enough blood.
✍️ Must-know: Universal donor = O; universal recipient = AB. 120/80 mmHg normal BP. Tachycardia >100, bradycardia <60 bpm.
