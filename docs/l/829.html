6.4 Physical properties of alcohols

◌ Not started
  • The –OH bond is polar (highly electronegative O), so alcohols show significant hydrogen bonding → higher melting/boiling points than hydrocarbons of comparable mass and even the low members are liquids at room temperature.
  • Solubility: lower alcohols (methanol, ethanol) are miscible with water in all proportions (H-bond with water); solubility decreases as carbon number increases.
  • Physical constants of the first six monohydric alcohols (memorise pattern): | Alcohol | mp °C | bp °C | density g/mL | |---|---|---|---| | Methanol | −97 | 64.7 | 0.792 | | Ethanol | −117 | 78.3 | 0.789 | | 1-Propanol | −126 | 97.2 | 0.804 | | 1-Butanol | −90 | 117.7 | 0.810 | | 1-Pentanol | −78.5 | 138 | 0.817 | | 1-Hexanol | −52 | 156.5 | 0.819 |

💡 Trends (Group Assignment 6.1): di/tri-hydric alcohols boil higher than mono-hydric of similar mass (more H-bonding); solubility falls with chain length; branched isomers boil lower than straight-chain isomers (less contact → weaker dispersion).
