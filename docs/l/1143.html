2.5 Graphs of Polynomial Functions

Key properties (from observations):

  • Graph of a polynomial is smooth (no sharp corners) and continuous (no holes or jumps).
  • f(x)=|x| is NOT a polynomial (sharp corner at (0,0)); a graph with a hole/jump is not a polynomial.
Absolute value is not a polynomial (sharp corner)
  • A polynomial of degree n has at most n−1 turning points and intersects the x-axis at most n times.

Quadratic graph (degree 2):

Turning point of y=x²+2x−3

Odd degree (1,3,5,…): ends go in opposite directions.

  • Leading coefficient positive: up to the right, down to the left.
  • Leading coefficient negative: down to the right, up to the left.

Even degree (2,4,…): ends go in the same direction.

  • Leading coefficient positive: both ends up.
  • Leading coefficient negative: both ends down.
Cubic graphs y=x³−1 and y=−x³+1
Cubic end behaviour y=x³ and y=−x³
Quartic even-degree graphs

Example: f(x)=x³−1=(x−1)(x²+x+1): x-intercept 1, y-intercept −1, up right/down left (LC>0, odd).

f(x)=−x³+1: x-intercept 1, y-intercept 1, down right/up left.

✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Determine end behaviour from the degree (odd/even) and the sign of the leading coefficient. Find x-intercepts via factorization/rational zero test; use the location theorem to find where zeros sit.
