6.6 Human Eye and Optical Instruments

◌ Not started

Human Eye

The eye is like a camera; its lens system forms an image on the retina (light-sensitive screen). - Cornea: transparent bulge on the front; most refraction occurs here. - Iris: dark muscular diaphragm controlling the size of the pupil (which controls light entering). - Crystalline lens: provides fine focal-length adjustment. - Retina: delicate membrane with light-sensitive cells; forms an inverted real image; sends signals via the optic nerve to the brain. - Eyeball ~2.3 cm diameter.

Accommodation: the ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length. Muscles relaxed → lens thin → focal length increases (see distant objects); muscles contract → lens thickens → focal length decreases (see near objects). - Least distance of distinct vision (near point): ~25 cm for a young adult with normal vision. - Far point: infinity for a normal eye. Normal eye sees clearly between 25 cm and infinity.

Defects of vision: 1. Myopia (near-sightedness): sees near objects clearly but not distant ones; far point nearer than infinity; image forms in front of the retina. Caused by excessive lens curvature or elongated eyeball. Corrected with a concave (diverging) lens. 2. Hypermetropia (far-sightedness): sees distant objects clearly but not near ones; near point farther than 25 cm; image forms behind the retina. Caused by too-long focal length or too-small eyeball. Corrected with a convex (converging) lens. 3. Presbyopia: age-related loss of accommodation (weakening of ciliary muscles, loss of lens flexibility). Sometimes needs bi-focal lenses (concave upper for distance, convex lower for near).

Optical Instruments

  • Simple microscope (magnifying glass): a convex lens of small focal length; gives an erect, magnified, virtual image.
  • Compound microscope: uses an objective lens (forms an enlarged image) + eyepiece lens (further magnifies it). Used for very small objects.
  • Refracting telescope: uses a large objective lens + eyepiece; purpose is to gather light from distant objects (larger objective → brighter, more detailed images).
  • Reflecting telescope: uses a large concave mirror to gather light, focusing onto a secondary mirror that directs it to the eyepiece. Mirrors are cheaper to make/maintain and can be made much larger without sagging.

✍️ Must-know: Near point 25 cm, far point infinity. Myopia → concave lens (image in front of retina); hypermetropia → convex lens (image behind retina); presbyopia → bi-focal. Telescope purpose = gather light.
