2.4 Reproduction in Plants

2.4.1 Reproductive structure & life cycle of non-flowering plants (Gymnosperms — pine tree)

  • Pine has male and female cones on one plant.
  • Pollination (by wind) transfers pollen from male cone to female cone.
  • Pollen germinates forming a pollen tube; the generative cell divides into two sperm cells.
  • Each female-cone scale has two ovules; each ovule undergoes meiosis producing 4 cells, of which only one survives → a gametophyte with an archegonium (contains a single large egg).
  • Fertilization: one sperm fuses with the egg → zygote → seed embryo.
  • Mature seed is released when the cone dries and opens; dispersed and germinates into a seedling.

Key cycle: Sporophyte (2n, mature tree) → cones → gametophytes → egg + sperm → zygote (2n) → seed → seedling → mature tree.

Note: 📌 Ethiopian gymnosperms: Podocarpus falcatus ("Zigba") and Juniperus procera ("Tid").

2.4.2 Reproductive structure & life cycle of flowering plants (Angiosperms)

Four floral parts:

  • Sepals (calyx): green leaf-like, protect the flower.
  • Petals (corolla): brightly coloured, attract pollinators.
  • Stamen (androecium): the male part = filament + anther (bilobed, produces pollen).
  • Pistil (gynoecium/carpel): the female part = stigma + style + ovary (with ovules and egg).

Complete vs incomplete flower: complete has all four parts; incomplete lacks one or more.

Perfect vs imperfect flower: perfect has both stamen and pistil; imperfect has only one (staminate or pistillate).

Life cycle stages (angiosperm):

  1. Pollination → 2. Pollen tube formation → 3. Fertilization (union of male and female gametes in the ovule → zygote; angiosperms show double fertilization forming the endosperm) → 4. Seed and fruit formation (ovule → seed; ovary → fruit) → 5. Seed dispersal → 6. Seed dormancy / germination.
✍️ Must-know: Seed = matured ovule; Fruit = matured ovary. Angiosperms show double fertilization (zygote + endosperm).

2.4.3 Pollination

  • Pollination = transfer of pollen from anther to stigma.
  • Self-pollination = between anther and pistil on the same flower or between flowers on one plant.
  • Cross-pollination = between flowers on two different plants.
  • Agents of pollination: insects, wind, animals, water. Flower structure (colour, scent) strongly relates to the pollinating agent.
