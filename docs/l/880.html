📖 GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS (quick definitions for revision)

Absorption – passage of digested nutrients through the intestinal wall into blood/lymph.

Alveoli – tiny air sacs in the lungs where gas exchange occurs by diffusion.

Antibody – Y-shaped protein (immunoglobulin) produced by B cells that binds a specific antigen.

Antigen – foreign molecule that triggers an immune response.

Assimilation – use of absorbed nutrients by cells to build new materials.

Atom/ion – an atom with an unequal number of protons and electrons (charged entity).

Biomass – total mass of living organisms at a trophic level.

Biuret test – food test for proteins (mauve/purple colour).

Bolus – ball of chewed food formed in the mouth.

Cancer – uncontrolled, unregulated cell division ("cell madness").

Capillary – thin-walled vessel (one cell thick) where exchange with cells occurs.

Cell cycle – sequence of events distributing genetic material to form daughter cells.

Cellulose – structural polysaccharide of plant cell walls (dietary fibre).

Chyme – semi-liquid food mixture leaving the stomach.

Cohesion – attraction between molecules of the same substance (water).

Crossing over – exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes in Prophase I.

Cytokinesis – division of the cytoplasm into daughter cells.

Denitrification – conversion of nitrates back to N₂ gas by anaerobic bacteria.

Deoxyribose – 5-carbon sugar in DNA.

Diaphragm – muscle sheet below the lungs driving breathing.

Diastole – relaxation phase of the heart (filling with blood).

Diploid (2n) – cell with two sets of chromosomes (46 in humans).

Disaccharide – sugar of two monosaccharides (maltose, lactose, sucrose).

Egestion – removal of undigested waste (faeces).

Electrolyte – ion that carries an electric charge when dissolved.

Emulsification – bile breaking large fat globules into small droplets.

Erythroblastosis fetalis – Rh⁻ mother's antibodies attacking Rh⁺ baby's red cells.

Eutrophication – nutrient (phosphate/nitrate) enrichment of water → algal bloom → O₂ depletion.

Excretion – removal of metabolic wastes (urea, CO₂).

Expiration – breathing out (diaphragm relaxes, pressure increases).

Food chain – linear sequence of who-eats-whom.

Food web – interwoven network of food chains.

Gamete – sex cell (sperm/egg) with haploid (n) chromosomes.

Globular protein – ball-shaped, soluble protein (enzymes, haemoglobin).

Glycogen – animal storage polysaccharide (liver, muscle).

Goiter – enlarged thyroid from iodine deficiency.

Haemoglobin – O₂-carrying red protein in red blood cells.

Haploid (n) – cell with one set of chromosomes (23 in human gametes).

Homeostasis – maintenance of a stable internal environment.

Hydrogen bond – weak bond between water molecules (gives water its properties).

Hydrolysis – breaking molecules by adding water (during digestion).

Innate immunity – non-specific, from-birth defenses (skin, phagocytes).

Inspiration – breathing in (diaphragm contracts, volume increases).

Interphase – growth + DNA synthesis phase of the cell cycle (G₁, S, G₂).

Karyokinesis – nuclear division (mitosis or meiosis).

Lacteal – lymphatic capillary in a villus absorbing fats.

Lipid – water-insoluble organic molecule (fats, oils, phospholipids).

Lymph – tissue fluid collected into lymph vessels.

Lymphocyte – white blood cell (T or B cell) responsible for specific immunity.

Macronutrient – mineral needed in larger amounts (Na, Mg, K, Cl, P, S).

Meiosis – reduction division producing 4 haploid gametes.

Micronutrient – mineral needed in trace amounts (Fe, Cu, I, Mn).

Mitosis – division producing 2 identical diploid somatic cells.

Monomer – single molecular unit that forms polymers.

Monosaccharide – single sugar unit (glucose, galactose, fructose).

Nephron – functional unit of the kidney.

Nitrification – conversion of ammonia to nitrite then nitrate.

Nitrogen fixation – conversion of N₂ to ammonia by bacteria.

Nucleic acid – DNA or RNA (polynucleotides of nucleotides).

Nucleotide – base + pentose sugar + phosphate.

Organic molecule – compound containing both carbon and hydrogen.

Peptide bond – covalent bond joining amino acids (formed by condensation).

Phagocyte – white cell that engulfs pathogens (neutrophil, macrophage).

Phospholipid – lipid with hydrophilic head + hydrophobic tails (membrane bilayer).

Plasma – liquid part of blood (~55%).

Platelet – blood cell fragment helping clotting.

Polymer – long chain of many monomers joined by covalent bonds.

Polypeptide – chain of many amino acids.

Precipitation – water returning to earth as rain/snow/hail.

Producer – organism making its own food (autotroph, green plant).

Pulmonary circulation – heart → lungs → heart (O₂ pickup).

Pyramid of energy – always-upright diagram of energy through trophic levels.

Reduction division – meiosis (halving chromosome number to n).

Ribose – 5-carbon sugar in RNA.

Saliva – fluid with salivary amylase starting starch digestion.

Sister chromatids – two identical copies of one chromosome.

Spindle fibre – protein fibre pulling chromosomes apart in anaphase.

Starch – plant storage polysaccharide.

Systole – contraction phase of the heart (pumping out blood).

Tachycardia – heart rate above 100 beats/min.

Trophic level – feeding position in a food chain.

Tubular reabsorption – return of useful substances to blood in the nephron.

Vaccination – introducing weakened antigen to build immunity/memory.

Villus – finger-like projection of small-intestine wall increasing absorption area.

Water potential – tendency of water to move (drives plant transport).
