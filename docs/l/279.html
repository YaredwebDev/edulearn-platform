2.5 Intermolecular Forces

◌ Not started

Intramolecular forces hold atoms together inside a molecule (ionic/covalent/metallic bonds). Intermolecular forces act between molecules and are much weaker.

van der Waals forces = dipole–dipole forces + London (dispersion) forces.

  1. London/dispersion forces — the weakest; instantaneous/induced dipoles. They increase with increasing molecular mass (bigger electron cloud) and with greater surface area/contact between molecules. Example: n-pentane boils higher than its branched isomer because straight chains pack with more contact.
  2. Dipole–dipole forces — between the δ+/δ− ends of polar molecules.
  3. Hydrogen bonding — a strong dipole–dipole force when H is bonded to N, O, or F and is attracted to a lone pair of an N, O, or F on a neighbour. Responsible for water's high boiling point.

✋ Trap: Hydrogen bonding needs H bonded to N, O, or F (very electronegative small atom). CHCl₃ dipole–dipole is weaker than H-bonding; only species with H–N/O/F can H-bond.

Boiling-point comparisons (textbook examples): polar/hydrogen-bonding compounds boil far higher than comparable non-polar molecules; among covalent molecules with similar mass, the ones that can hydrogen-bond (or are more polar) have the highest boiling points.
