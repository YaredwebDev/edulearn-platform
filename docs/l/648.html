5.5 Change of Phase (Phase Change and Latent Heat)

◌ Not started

When a substance changes phase (solid↔liquid↔gas), heat is absorbed/released without changing temperature — this is latent heat. - Latent heat of fusion (Lf): heat absorbed by 1 kg to change solid → liquid at the melting point (temperature unchanged). - Latent heat of vaporization (Lv): heat absorbed by 1 kg to change liquid → gas at the boiling point. - Q = m L (L = latent heat; unit J/kg).

✍️ Must-know: Q = mL during a phase change (no temperature change); Q = mcΔT during heating within a phase (temperature changes). Melting/boiling proceed at constant temperature while heat is being added.

Example 5.6 (four-stage problem): raise 4 kg of water from −5°C (ice) to steam at 100°C — compute (1) heating ice to 0°C: Q = mc_iceΔT; (2) melting ice at 0°C: Q = mLf; (3) heating water 0→100°C: Q = mc_wΔT; (4) vaporizing at 100°C: Q = mLv; total = sum. Draw the temperature-vs-heat graph (flat regions during phase changes).

Phase diagram: a graph of the physical states of a substance under different conditions of temperature and pressure. - Triple point: the combination of pressure & temperature where all three phases (solid, liquid, vapour) coexist in equilibrium. - Critical point: the point where liquid and gaseous phases merge into a single phase. - Sublimation: direct change from solid to vapour without passing through the liquid state (e.g. dry ice).

✋ Trap: boiling point increases with pressure; you can't condense a gas into liquid above the critical temperature (for water, ~374°C). Melting point of ice is lowered at higher pressure.
