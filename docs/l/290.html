3.4 The Liquid State

◌ Not started
  • Liquids have definite volume, indefinite shape; particles closer than gases; held by intermolecular forces (dipole–dipole, London, H-bonding).

3.4.1 Evaporation / Vaporisation

  • Evaporation (vaporisation): the process by which molecules at the surface of a liquid break away into the gas phase. Only the most energetic surface molecules escape.
  • Condensation: the return of vapour to liquid; evaporation and condensation are opposing processes.
  • In an open container evaporation continues until all liquid disappears (equilibrium cannot be set up). In a closed container, evaporation slows as condensation rises until dynamic equilibrium — rates of evaporation and condensation equal, so the amount of vapour above the liquid is constant.
  • Vapour pressure = the partial pressure of the vapour in dynamic equilibrium with its liquid.

Factors affecting rate of evaporation: 1. Temperature — raising T raises average KE → more molecules escape. 2. Intermolecular forces — stronger forces → slower evaporation & lower vapour pressure. 3. Surface area — larger surface area → faster evaporation.

  • Volatile liquids evaporate readily at room temperature (weak forces): formaldehyde, ethyl alcohol, mercury, benzene, diethyl ether. Non-volatile liquids evaporate poorly (strong forces): motor oil, edible oil, glycerine, water, molten ionic compounds.
  • Vapour pressure increases with temperature (rate of evaporation rises); at a given T it is constant for a pure liquid. Water/ethanol have low vapour pressure (strong H-bonding); diethyl ether (weak dispersion) has high vapour pressure.

3.4.2 Boiling and Boiling Point

  • Boiling: the formation of bubbles of vapour within the liquid itself (not just at the surface) — this distinguishes boiling from evaporation. Bubbles persist and rise only when the vapour pressure inside them reaches the applied (external) pressure.
  • Boiling point (bp): the temperature at which a liquid's vapour pressure equals the external (atmospheric) pressure.
  • Normal boiling point: bp when external pressure = exactly 1 atm. Water: 100 °C at 1 atm, but 95 °C at 0.83 atm (lower pressure lowers bp; boiling point decreases at higher altitude).
  • While a pure liquid boils, its temperature stays constant until all liquid has vaporised.

3.4.3 Heat of vaporisation & condensation

  • Molar heat of vaporisation (ΔH_vap): heat needed to convert 1 mol of liquid at its boiling point into vapour. Water: 44.0 kJ/mol at 298 K and 1 atm.
  • Molar heat of condensation (ΔH_cond): heat released when 1 mol of vapour condenses to liquid.
  • ΔH_vap = −ΔH_cond. Vaporisation is endothermic; condensation is exothermic.

✍️ Must-know: ΔH_vap = −ΔH_cond; water's molar heat of vaporisation ≈ 44 kJ/mol. Boiling vs evaporation: boiling forms vapour bubbles throughout the liquid; evaporation only at the surface.
