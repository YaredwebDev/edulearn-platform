7C Reading Skills: Adwa — A Symbol of National Unity

◌ Not started

Key facts (chronological): 1. Battle of Adwa (1896) = a stunning Ethiopian victory, a rout/disaster for Italy. Ethiopia was the only African country to successfully resist European colonization before 1914; it resonated in post-emancipation America too. 2. Italian interest in East Africa dates from 1869 (opening of the Suez Canal raised the strategic value of the Red Sea coast); Italy established itself at Massawa (1885) and moved into the Eritrean highlands; Italians played on rivalry among Ethiopian leaders. 3. Italy pushed west into Sudan and south toward Tigray; by 1890 Italy controlled territory and created the colony of Eritrea (capital Asmara). In late 1894 Ras Mangasha of Tigray mobilised against Italian incursions but was defeated in early 1895. 4. In September 1895, Menelik (king of Shoa) called Ethiopia to arms and led ~100,000 men north; forced the overextended Italians to fight on his terms. General Baratieri was reluctant to attack in the open field and considered retreat, but officers (citing spy reports that Menelik's forces were demoralized/depleted) convinced him to advance at night. 5. By outflanking the Italians, Menelik exposed their supply lines to a population turning against the occupiers. In March 1896 the Ethiopian army routed the invading force and ended Italy's war of conquest in Africa — an event that opened a breach leading to Africa's later struggle for freedom from colonial rule. 6. Europeans interpreted Adwa differently (to discredit Italy militarily, or claiming Ethiopians "were not black"). The victory sealed Ethiopian unification, solidified Menelik's claim to Emperor, secured independence for a generation, and made Ethiopia a beacon throughout the African Diaspora.

✍️ Reading skill: true/false; find words from context (stunning, unrelenting, halt, rivalry, pretext, reluctant, depleted, massive, well-disciplined/solid, secured); pronoun reference; paragraph main-idea + supporting-detail summary table; identify writer's purpose, main idea, and organisation (chronological narration).
