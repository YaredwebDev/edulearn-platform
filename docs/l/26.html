5.1 Feed Resources in Ethiopia

◌ Not started
  • Animal nutrition = the science of feed preparation and feeding. Feed is the most important factor determining animal performance & profitability; feed cost is the largest operating cost (≈50–80%). Both overfeeding (wasteful/costly) and underfeeding (lower performance/profit) must be avoided.
  • Main Ethiopian feed resources: natural pastures, crop residues, improved forages, agro-industrial by-products.

5.1.1 Natural pasture

  • Naturally occurring grasses, shrubs and tree forages. Supplies >60% of livestock feed. Grazing is the cheapest way to deliver feed. Good quality in the wet season; diminishes in the dry season. Management: grazing-land management, fertiliser (urea, DAP), oversowing legumes; prevent weeds/pests/diseases; correct stocking rate (overgrazing does not increase pasture productivity).

5.1.2 Crop residues

  • Remaining portion after harvesting the main crop for human use; supply the greatest volume of feed. Forms: straws, stovers, cobs, hulls, chaffs. Generally low in crude protein, energy and micronutrients; fibrous, low palatability & digestibility. Improve by supplementing with improved forages/legumes/grains/concentrates, and physically (chopping) or chemically (urea treatment). Not suitable for pigs & poultry.

5.1.3 Forage crops

  • Plants cultivated for edible vegetative portions, fed fresh or preserved. Two types: grasses and legumes. Grasses = best/cheapest bulk feed for ruminants, more yield/unit area than legumes, higher fibre; examples Rhodes, Sudan, Elephant grass. Legumes = more protein, vitamins, minerals; cheap protein supplement for crop residues/natural pasture; examples Alfalfa, Vetch, Sesbania sesban. Nutritive value & digestibility decline as forage matures. Forage production is a profitable business (grass–legume mixtures best for yield & quality).

5.1.4 Agro-industrial by-products

  • By-products of agro-industries that supplement crop residues; rich in energy or protein.
  • Flour-milling by-products: palatable, readily eaten; bran (wheat, maize, rice) is pleasant-tasting, laxative, good for thiamine & niacin; cereal middlings & rice polish are lower in fibre, higher in energy than bran. Bran = outer layers (cuticle, pericarp, seed coat) + some starchy endosperm.
  • Oilseed cakes/meals: residue after oil extraction; high protein, carbohydrate, minerals, nitrogen; e.g. soybean meal, noug-seed cake, cotton-seed cake, peanut/groundnut cake.
  • Molasses: sugar-industry by-product; good energy source (≈54% TDN), low protein (3%) & minerals; an appetizer and dust settler.
  • Brewery by-products: spent grain (good protein, fibre, energy; higher fibre/protein/minerals than the original grains) and spent yeast (cheap protein, minerals, vitamin-B complex).

✋ Trap: straw/stover are crop residues, NOT agro-industrial by-products. Cereal grains & roots/tubers are concentrates, not roughages. Molasses is an energy-rich concentrate.
