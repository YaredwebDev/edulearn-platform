3.7 The Cell and its Environment

◌ Not started

The cell membrane is selectively (semi) permeable — it controls what enters and leaves. Materials pass through by passive transport (diffusion, osmosis) or active transport.

3.7.1 Passive Transport

Movement of substances down a concentration gradient (high → low concentration) without input of energy.

Diffusion: net movement of molecules/ions from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration. Important for movement of gases (e.g., oxygen for respiration enters by diffusion).

Factors influencing the rate of diffusion: - Concentration gradient: higher difference → faster diffusion. - Temperature: higher temperature → more kinetic energy → faster movement. - Mass of the molecule: heavier molecules diffuse more slowly; lighter diffuse faster. - Distance travelled: farther → slower. - Surface area: greater surface area → faster total diffusion (e.g., microvilli on cells of the kidney/intestine increase absorbing surface).

Osmosis: a special case of diffusion — the diffusion of water molecules from a region of their higher concentration (dilute solution) to a region of their lower concentration (concentrated solution) across a partially permeable membrane.

Tonicity terms: - Hypertonic solution: high solute concentration, low water concentration → water moves out of the cell → cell shrinks/plasmolyses. - Isotonic solution: equal solute and water → no net movement, no change in cell size. - Hypotonic solution: low solute concentration, high water concentration → water moves into the cell → cell becomes turgid (plant cells resist bursting due to cell wall; animal cells may burst = hemolysis).

Osmosis in plant cells: brings strength/rigidity (turgor pressure). In a hypotonic solution water enters the vacuole, presses on the cell wall → turgid. In a hypertonic solution the plant cell loses water, the plasma membrane shrinks away (plasmolysis), the cell wilts and dies.

Osmosis in animal cells: the concentration of blood water is monitored by the brain and adjusted by the kidneys. In a hypotonic solution, water enters and animal cells (no cell wall) may burst (hemolysis) — e.g., red blood cells. In a hypertonic solution, water leaves and the cell shrinks.

Osmosis in amoeba: freshwater unicellular protozoans have contractile vacuoles that accumulate and expel excess water (osmoregulation), preventing the cell from bursting.

✍️ Must-know: Diffusion = high → low concentration (any substance). Osmosis = water moving through a membrane. Passive = no energy. Hypertonic → cell loses water (shrink/plasmolyse); Hypotonic → cell gains water (turgid/burst); Isotonic → no net change.

3.7.2 Active Transport

Movement of molecules/ions across the cell membrane against the concentration gradient (low → high concentration), using energy released during respiration. - Enables cells to concentrate molecules inside or outside the cell. - Examples: ion uptake by root hairs, uptake of glucose by epithelial cells of villi. - Example: the sodium-potassium pump — nerve cells pump sodium out and potassium in (vital to the nervous system).
