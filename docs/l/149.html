5.1 Human Musculoskeletal System

◌ Not started

The musculoskeletal system gives humans the ability to move, gives the body shape and form, and protects vital organs. It binds organs together, supports stability, allows bodily movements, produces blood cells (in the bone marrow) and stores minerals.

  • Musculoskeletal system = bones of the skeleton + muscles + cartilage + tendons + ligaments + joints + connective tissues.
  • Its parts and their jobs: 1. Muscles keep bones in place and enable movement. 2. Joints and cartilages connect bones to bones and prevent bones rubbing against each other. 3. Tendons connect muscles to bones. 4. Ligaments are fibrous connective tissues that attach bone to bone. 5. Connective tissue keeps internal skeletal parts in position and favours movement.

✍️ Must-know: tendons = muscle→bone; ligaments = bone→bone. (Review: "fibrous connective tissue that attaches bone to bone" = ligament; "muscles attached to bones by" = tendon.)

5.1.1 Types of muscles

The muscular system has ~700 muscles (about half of body weight). Three types: 1. Cardiac muscle — the heart muscle; forms the thick middle layer (myocardium) between the outer pericardium and inner endocardium; supplied via the coronary circulation. It is involuntary, striated; individual cells joined by intercalated discs. 2. Skeletal muscle — attached to bones, arranged in opposing pairs around joints; ~30–40% of total body mass; voluntary (striated). Skeletal muscle fibers innervated by a single motor axon = a motor unit. 3. Smooth muscle — involuntary, non-striated, unconsciously controlled; found in the walls of hollow organs (stomach, intestine, bladder, uterus), blood & lymph vessels, respiratory/urinary/reproductive tracts, eyes (ciliary muscle). The erector pili of the skin make hair stand erect (cold/heat/fear).

✍️ Must-know: cardiac = involuntary + striated + only in heart (intercalated discs); skeletal = voluntary + striated; smooth = involuntary + non-striated (hollow organs). The muscular system comprises cardiac, skeletal and smooth muscles (True). White blood cells protect against infection.

5.1.2 Mechanism of action of skeletal muscles

  • Skeletal muscle fibers are large single cells formed by the fusion of many cells. Cytoplasm is mostly myofibrils of two filaments: thick filaments of myosin and thin filaments of actin, organised into contractile units called sarcomeres.
  • Actin & myosin are proteins in every muscle type; myosin is a molecular motor that converts chemical energy from ATP into mechanical energy to pull actin filaments → contraction.
  • Troponin = a complex of three regulatory proteins. In a relaxed muscle, tropomyosin blocks the myosin-binding site on actin (preventing contraction); myosin binding to actin forms cross-bridges → contraction.
  • Mechanism: (1) Muscle activation — a motor nerve sends an action potential to the neuromuscular junction, causing the sarcoplasmic reticulum to release calcium into the muscle cell. (2) Contraction — calcium binds troponin, allowing actin & myosin to bind (cross-bridges) using ATP. (3) Recharging — ATP is re-synthesized. (4) Relaxation — when nerve stimulation stops, calcium is pumped back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum, breaking actin–myosin links (relaxation fails if ATP runs out — rigor mortis).
  • Three types of muscle contraction: Isometric (no length change), Concentric (shortening), Eccentric (lengthening the muscle fibers).

✋ Review trap: the "three types of muscle contraction" are concentric, isometric and eccentric — NOT "centric". Calcium released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum drives contraction.

5.1.3 The axial and appendicular skeletons

  • Humans are born with over 300 bones; many fuse by maturity, leaving on average 206 bones in the adult.
  • Bone = protein collagen + molecule calcium phosphate (strong, lightweight).
  • Four bone-cell types: Osteoblasts (form new bone), Osteocytes (mature bone cells), Osteoclasts (break down bone to shape it), Osteoprogenitor cells (repair fractures).
  • Types of bone by shape: Long (femur, tibia — longer than wide), Short (carpus/wrists — squat/cubed), Flat (cranium/skull, ilium/pelvis, sternum, ribs — flattened), Irregular (vertebrae, sacrum, coccyx, temporal, mandible, palatine, nasal), Sesamoid (small sesame-seed-like, embedded in muscles).
  • Functions of bones: (1) support body & help movement; (2) protect internal organs (ribs protect heart/lungs, skull protects brain); (3) produce blood cells (platelets, RBCs, WBCs in bone marrow); (4) store & release fat; (5) store & release minerals (calcium ~99%, phosphorus, vitamin D).

Axial skeleton = skull, trunk and pelvis of the medial core. Its 80 bones (Table 5.2): | Part | Bones | Function | |---|---|---| | Skull (cranium 8 + facial 14) | 22 | holds/protects the brain | | Ossicles (both ears, 3+3) | 6 | hearing (auditory) | | Hyoid (neck) | 1 | holds the head | | Thoracic cage (ribs) | 24 | protects heart & lungs | | Thorax (sternum) | 1 | binds/abdominal | | Vertebral column | 26 | holds up the body | | Total axial | 80 | |

Skull = cranium bones joined at joints (sutures) + facial bones (mandible/jaw, maxilla/upper jaw, zygomatic/cheekbone, nasal). Rib cage (thoracic cage) = 12 pairs of ribs + costal cartilage + sternum.

Appendicular skeleton = 126 bones — limbs, shoulder (pectoral) and pelvic girdles, and joined appendages (involved in locomotion & manipulation). Table 5.3: shoulder girdles 4, pelvis 2, arms & forearms 6, thighs & legs 8, hands 54, feet & ankles 52 = 126. Total = 80 + 126 = 206 bones.

5.1.4 Joints

  • Joints = where two or more bones meet (with binding tissues), allowing movement.
  • Types (Table): 1. Ball and socket — round head in a cup; movement in all directions (shoulder, hip). 2. Hinge — opens/closes in one plane like a door (elbow, knee). 3. Condyloid — no rotation but allows movement (finger, jaw). 4. Pivot (rotary/trochoid) — one bone swivels in a ring (ulna-radius, neck). 5. Gliding (plane) — limited movement, surfaces slip over each other (wrist). 6. Saddle — back-and-forth & side-to-side, no rotation (thumb base).

✍️ Must-know (review answers): hinge joint = knee; hip joint = ball and socket; bone-strengthening mineral = calcium; two lower-arm bones = radius and ulna. Saddle joints are at the base of the thumb.
