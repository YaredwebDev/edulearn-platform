4.4 Applications of Trigonometric Functions

Angle of elevation: the angle the line of vision must be raised above the horizontal. Angle of depression: the angle it must be lowered below the horizontal.

Example: From a 40 m cliff, angle of depression 60° to an object at base level → tan60°=40/x → x=40/√3 m.

Example: 20 m rope, angle with ground 30° → sin30°=height/20 → height=10 m.

Example: Tree breaks; broken top touches ground 8 m from foot making 30° → tan30°=AB/8 → AB=8/√3, cos30°=8/AC → AC=16/√3; original height = AB+AC = 8√3 m.

✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: In right-triangle applications: identify the opposite/adjacent/hypotenuse relative to the given angle, then use sin, cos, or tan. Angles of elevation/depression are equal when lines are parallel (alternate angles).
