📖 GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS (quick definitions for revision)

Adjective – a word that modifies or gives additional information about a noun.

Adverb of frequency – a word that tells how often an action happens (always, often, sometimes, never).

Adverb of manner – a word that tells how something happens (quickly, slowly, well).

Adverb of time – a word/phrase that tells when something happens (yesterday, now, ago, soon).

Adverb clause of manner – a dependent clause showing how something is done, introduced by as, as if, as though.

Adverb clause of purpose – a clause telling the purpose of the main verb, introduced by so that, in order that, in order to, to, for fear that, lest.

Adverb clause of reason – a clause explaining why, introduced by because, as, since, for, due to, owing to, because of.

Adverb clause of result – a clause showing the result, introduced by so…that, such…that, as a result, consequently, therefore, hence.

Agreement (subject-verb) – the rule that a singular subject takes a singular verb and a plural subject takes a plural verb.

Article – a word used before a noun: a, an (indefinite), the (definite).

Back-formation – reducing a noun to form a verb (editor→edit, television→televise, babysitter→babysit).

Blending – combining parts of two words into one (breakfast+lunch=brunch).

Borrowing – taking words from other languages (French, Latin, Greek).

Bilingual – a person who speaks two languages.

Clipping – a short form of a word keeping its meaning (examination→exam, mathematics→maths).

Clause of purpose – see Adverb clause of purpose.

Clause of result – see Adverb clause of result.

Compound – a word made of two or more words (noun+noun, adjective+noun, verb+noun, or hyphenated).

Comparative degree – the form comparing two things (−er / more).

Complex sentence – one independent clause + at least one dependent clause.

Compound sentence – two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinator (FANBOYS).

Compound-complex sentence – at least two independent clauses + at least one dependent clause.

Conditional sentence – an if-clause + a main clause; types 0, 1, 2, 3.

Conversion – using a word as a different part of speech (advice→advise).

Coordinating conjunction – for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so (FANBOYS).

Definite article – the word "the," referring to a specific noun.

Diphthong – a sound made of a combination of two vowel sounds (e.g., [ai], [ei], [au], [oi]).

Direct object – the thing that receives the action of a transitive verb.

Direct speech – quoting a speaker's exact words in quotation marks.

Discourse markers – linking words (because, therefore, however, moreover).

Executive control – the brain system controlling attention-switching and working memory.

Expository essay – an essay that explains something (definition, cause/effect, comparison, process).

FANBOYS – for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so (coordinating conjunctions).

Fallacy – an error in reasoning that weakens an argument.

Gerund – an -ing verb used as a noun (reading, going, making).

Indefinite article – a/an, referring to a non-specific noun.

Indirect object – the person who benefits from or receives the action.

Indirect/reported speech – retelling someone's words without quotes, with backshift.

Intransitive verb – a verb that takes no object (runs, ached).

Laxative – a medicine that empties the bowels.

Monolingual – a person who speaks only one language.

Multilingual – a person who speaks more than one (often many) language.

Narrative paragraph – a paragraph that tells a story (with characters, theme, setting).

Non-essential (non-defining) clause – extra information set off by commas.

Outline – raw ideas in phrases before writing a final paragraph.

Passive voice – be + past participle; object becomes subject.

Past participle (V3) – the third form of a verb used with have/had/be.

Prefix – a word part added at the beginning that modifies meaning (un-, dis-, re-, im-).

Premise – a view held by arguers, open to different arguments.

Present participle – the -ing form of a verb.

Proboscis – the long sucking mouthpart of an insect (used by bees for nectar).

Quantifier – a word that gives approximate or specific answers to "How much?"/"How many?" (much, many, little, few, some, any).

Relative pronoun – who, whom, whose, which, that (joins a relative clause to its noun).

Simple sentence – one independent clause.

State verb – a verb not normally used in continuous forms (think, believe, know, want, like).

Suffix – a word part added at the end that changes word class (-ness, -ful, -less, -ship).

Superlative degree – the form comparing three or more (−est / most).

Topic sentence – the sentence that carries the main idea of a paragraph.

Transitive verb – a verb that takes an object (is washing, read).

Zero conditional – if + present, present (general truth).

📝 SUMMARISE THE KEY FUNCTIONS

  • Agreeing: Exactly, That's so true, I agree with you 100%, You're absolutely right.
  • Disagreeing (polite): I don't think so, Not really, I'm afraid I disagree, I beg to differ.
  • Advice: You should…, You ought to…, You had better…, Why don't you…?
  • Apologising: I'm sorry for…, Please forgive me for…, It's all my fault.
  • Compliments: You're beautiful/brilliant/awesome; I like your shirt; That looks great on you.
  • Descriptions: first of all, to begin with, then, after that, finally.
  • Conclusion starters: in general, therefore, to sum up, to conclude, finally, in short.
  • Opinion: In my opinion, The way I see it, As far as I'm concerned.
