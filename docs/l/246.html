4.2 Cell Division

◌ Not started
  • Cell doctrine: new cells arise from pre-existing cells by cell division (Rudolf Virchow, 1858) — the basis of continuity of life.
  • Cause of cell division: as a cell grows, its surface-area-to-volume ratio falls, so diffusion across the membrane becomes inadequate; the nucleus's controlling power is minimized. Cell division solves this.

Importance of cell division: - Unicellular organisms (e.g., Amoeba): a means of reproduction (increasing population). - Multicellular organisms: growth, replacement of damaged/dead cells, and repair. - Sexually reproducing organisms: producing gametes (sex cells) in reproductive organs.
