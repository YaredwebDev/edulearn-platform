1.4 Ethical Issues in Biology

◌ Not started

Ethical issues in biology = issues about the rightness or wrongness of using biological discoveries for human health and wellbeing. They deal with concerns in biotechnology, medicine and the environment — whether biotechnological information, products or devices are used rightly or wrongly on human health, lifestyles and livelihoods.

✍️ Must-know: ethical issues in biology relate to unethical, immoral, illegal, unequal, unjust and disrespectful acts toward someone's values/rights; they include the commercialisation/commodification of human organs, assisted reproduction techniques, and manipulation of basic biological organ systems.

Inquiry 1.5 (learn from foresters): discuss the unethical treatment of plants by humans; respect plant physical & biological materials; the unethical uses of seeds/leaves/roots/twigs/barks; "Trees give shade even for an axe-man — everyone is responsible to plant trees."

1.4.1 Ethical treatment of plants and animals during biological studies

1.4.1.1 Ethical treatment of plants Plants are primary producers and feed all living organisms. Trees function properly in decentralised form without any centralised "brain". Severe (life-threatening) treatment leads to total disappearance of plant species. Collecting seeds, berries, roots, leaves, bark or flowers for personal use, medicine, or tooth brushing can harm survival of species and dependent communities.

Unethical treatment of plants (memorise): - Testing seeds or berries with destructive rays - Cutting tree terminals for experiments - Consuming all seeds and fruits without conservation - Unplanned cutting of trees / deforestation - Burning forests and seedlings for farmland - Restructuring tree shapes purely for aesthetic appeal

Ethical care of plants: plant trees (a national responsibility) and conserve jeopardised species "for our common future".

✋ Trap: "Care for plants" is ETHICAL (not unethical). Plants have decentralised function systems (no brain) — an exam true/false trap.

1.4.1.2 Ethical treatment of animals in experiments Researchers use animals for scientific understanding, but the question is whether it is morally acceptable to harm animals. Animals are sentient creatures — they have a nervous system, can feel pain and emotional stress, and have interests and values — so it is unethical to harm animals in experiments or rigorous testing.

The three Rs (Reduction, Refinement, Replacement) — memorise: - Reduction — minimise the number of animals used (better experimental techniques, sharing information). - Refinement — clean up experiments to reduce suffering (less invasive techniques, better medical care, better living conditions, proper handling). - Replacement — substitute animals with alternatives: cell cultures instead of whole animals, biosynthesis, and computer-simulated models.

✍️ Must-know: the three Rs = Reduction, Refinement, Replacement. Replacing experimental animals with biosynthetic tissues/cell cultures/computer models is an ETHICAL treatment of research animals. Animals are sentient (can feel pain).

Inquiry 1.6 (learn from zoologists): conservation of animals as sentient creatures; reducing suffering of research animals; habitat, species and ecosystem balance.
