UNIT 8 · LESSON 7 OF 7 · GRADE 11 AGRICULTURE

🧮 Key facts to memorise

◌ Not started
  • Health = wellbeing; disease = departure from health.
  • Routes of transmission: direct contact, ingestion, mating/uro-genital/placenta, inhalation, vectors.
  • Symptom list for sick animals (appetite/weight, coughing, lameness, isolation, discharges, sunken eyes, raised coat, fever, etc.).
  • Infectious (anthrax, FMD, Newcastle) vs non-infectious (ketosis, rickets, poisoning).
  • Disease table (FMD, CBPP, brucellosis, anthrax, Newcastle, taeniasis) — know cause, host, transmission, symptoms, control.
  • Parasites: internal (tapeworm, roundworm, trypanosome, liver fluke) vs external (tick, fly, louse, mite).
  • Effects: production loss, mortality, economic loss, zoonotic risk (>150 zoonotic diseases).
  • Control: hygiene, management (rotational grazing), quarantine (2 weeks) & isolation, medication/vaccination, spraying & dipping (external), deworming (internal, e.g. piperazine), biosecurity.
  • Indigenous knowledge: plant-based remedies, oral route most common, concoction main preparation, used for anthrax/blackleg/rabies/FMD/bloat/colic/parasites.
  • Review answers: giving drugs to expel internal parasites = Deworming (C); a parasitic disease = Trypanosomiasis (B); major form of herbal preparation = Concoction (D); false about non-infectious = anthrax/FMD/Newcastle are NOT examples of non-infectious (B); diseases communicable to humans = Zoonoses (D); an ectoparasite = Tick (C).

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) define health, disease, symptom, prevention & control; (2) list all routes of pathogen transmission and the general symptoms of sick animals; (3) classify diseases as infectious vs non-infectious and name Ethiopian examples with their cause/host/transmission/symptoms/control; (4) distinguish internal vs external parasites with examples and diseases they cause/transmit; (5) explain the four effects (morbidity, mortality, economic, zoonotic); (6) list the seven prevention/control measures; and (7) explain the role, plants, preparation, administration and treated diseases of indigenous ethno-veterinary medicine? Re-do the review questions and check your answers against the key above.
