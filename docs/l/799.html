5.12 The major sexually transmitted infections (STIs) in Ethiopia

◌ Not started

STIs = communicable diseases transmitted primarily through sexual contact. More than 20 types spread through unprotected intercourse; causative agents = bacteria, viruses, parasites. Types grouped by pathogen:

Bacterial infections: - Bacterial Vaginosis — overgrowth of natural vaginal bacteria; increases risk of post-surgical infection, uterine/fallopian infections & infertility. Symptoms: thin gray/white/green discharge, fishy odor, itching, burning on urination. Prevalence in Ethiopia 2.8–19.4%. - Chlamydia — caused by Chlamydia trachomatis; spreads via vaginal fluid/semen during sex. - Syphilis — life-threatening (Treponema pallidum) affecting brain, nervous system, eyes, heart and other organs in four stages (primary 3–4 weeks, painless chancres; secondary 4–8 weeks, rash on hands/feet; latent, asymptomatic; tertiary 1–10 years, gummatous lesions/cardiovascular/neurological). - Gonorrhea — Neisseria gonorrhoeae; thrives in warm, moist parts; painful urination, abnormal discharge; can be symptomless. - Chancroid — Haemophilus ducreyi; highly contagious painful necrotizing genital ulcer with inguinal lymphadenopathy.

Viral infections: - Genital herpes — two herpes simplex viruses: HSV-1 (mouth; via saliva/sore) & HSV-2 (genital/anal/mouth; via sex). - Genital warts — human papillomavirus (HPV); increases risk of cervical & throat cancer. - Molluscum contagiosum — poxvirus (skin-to-skin); small round bumps on skin. - HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) — attacks immune-system cells; without treatment advances to AIDS. It is a retrovirus, attacking CD4 helper (T) cells. Three stages: (1) Acute (2–4 weeks; flu-like, rapid multiplication); (2) Chronic/asymptomatic/clinical latency (1–7 years); (3) AIDS (10+ years; CD4 < 200 cells/mm³; opportunistic infections; survival ~3 years without treatment). Healthy CD4 = 500–1600 cells/mm³. - Hepatitis B — attacks the liver (acute & chronic); can cause cirrhosis & liver cancer (hepatocellular carcinoma). Virus remains in semen, blood, body fluids. ~296 million living with chronic hep B in 2019 (WHO), 820,000 deaths/year. Prevented by vaccine. Symptoms: jaundice, dark urine, fatigue, nausea, abdominal pain.

Parasites: - Crabs (pubic lice) — attach to pubic hair; spread by close physical/sexual contact & shared towels/bedding. - Trichomoniasis — Trichomonas vaginalis; thin foul-smelling discharge, genital redness/itching, pain on urination/sex. - Scabies — skin disease due to a mite; spread by direct skin-to-skin contact (often during sex → considered an STI).
