4.6 Sex Determination

◌ Not started
  • Sex = biological attributes categorising an individual as male or female.
  • Sex systems vary: some species are hermaphroditic (both sexes in one individual); others are dioecious (separate sexes); haplodiploid = males develop from unfertilised (haploid) eggs, females from fertilised (diploid) eggs.
  • In humans and other mammals, sex is determined by sex chromosomes, X and Y. Humans: 23 pairs = 22 autosomal pairs + 1 sex-chromosome pair.
  • XX = female; XY = male.
  • Male produces sperm half with X, half with Y; female eggs all carry X. Eggs fertilised by X-bearing sperm → female (XX); by Y-bearing sperm → male (XY). So sex of the child is determined by the father's sperm, and the probability of either sex is ~1:1.

✍️ Must-know: male = XY (heterozygous for X), female = XX (homozygous for X). Sex is set at fertilisation.
