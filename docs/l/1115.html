7.4 Grammar: Relative (Adjectival) Clauses

A relative clause gives information about a noun. It begins with a relative pronoun.

PronounUsed forFunctionExample
whopeoplesubjectThe nurse who helped us was kind.
whompeopleobject (formal)The doctor whom we met was friendly.
whosepeople/thingspossessionThe boy whose father is ill stayed home.
whichthings/animalssubject or objectThe service which we did was useful.
thatpeople or thingsrestrictiveThe tasks that we finished took time.
whereplacesplaceThe hospital where we volunteered is in Bahir Dar.
whentimetimeThe day when we served was special.

Defining vs Non-defining clauses

  • Defining (essential) clause — gives essential information needed to identify the noun; NO commas; often uses that.
  • "The volunteers who visited the elderly felt proud." (Only those who visited.)
  • Non-defining (extra) clause — gives extra, non-essential information; set off by commas; cannot use that.
  • "Felege Hiwot Hospital, which is in Bahir Dar, has many patients."
Note: ✋ Trap: Use commas only in non-defining clauses. Don't write "My brother, who lives in Addis Ababa, is a doctor" as a defining clause without commas. whose always shows possession and is followed by a noun.

📝 Writing focus: Descriptive / Expository Essay on Basic Needs

Write a descriptive essay about basic needs (food, water, shelter, clothing, healthcare, education). Structure:

  1. Introduction — define/state the topic and your main idea.
  2. Body — describe each basic need and its importance.
  3. Conclusion — summarise why meeting basic needs matters.
Note: ✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) retell the community-service story (where, who, how the volunteers felt), (2) define all 5+ vocabulary words, (3) use 6+ phrasal verbs (drop/dry/eat), (4) choose who/whom/whose/which/that/where/when correctly, (5) tell defining vs non-defining clauses apart by commas, (6) write a descriptive/expository essay on basic needs?
