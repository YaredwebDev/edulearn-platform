1.4 Graphical Method of Vector Addition

◌ Not started

Vectors are drawn to scale and the resultant is found using a ruler and protractor. Three methods:

1. Triangle (head-to-tail) method — used for two vectors: - Join the head of A to the tail of B. - The resultant R⃗ = A⃗ + B⃗ is drawn from the tail of A to the head of B, forming the third side of the triangle.

2. Parallelogram method — used for two vectors: - Place the tails of the two vectors at a common origin. - Complete the parallelogram. - The diagonal from the common origin is the resultant. - Commutative law: A⃗ + B⃗ = B⃗ + A⃗

3. Polygon method — used for more than two vectors: - Place each successive vector's tail at the previous vector's head (head-to-tail arrangement). - The resultant is drawn from the tail of the first to the head of the last. - Vector addition is commutative and associative.

Special cases of adding two vectors

  • Same direction (parallel): magnitudes add. |R| = |A + B|, direction = common direction. (Resultant is maximum.)
  • Opposite directions (anti-parallel): magnitudes subtract. |R| = |A − B|, direction = the larger vector. (Resultant is minimum.)
  • Perpendicular: use Pythagoras. |R| = √(A² + B²) and θ = tan⁻¹(B/A).

✍️ Must-know: Resultant is maximum when vectors act in the same direction and minimum when they act in opposite directions. For perpendicular vectors: R = √(A²+B²).

Example: Two vectors have magnitudes 6 and 3 units. - Same direction: R = 9 units - Opposite direction: R = 3 units - Perpendicular: R = √(6²+3²) = √45 = 6.7 units
