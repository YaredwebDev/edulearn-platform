3.3 Stress and Strain

◌ Not started

Stress = force per unit area causing deformation: Stress = F/A - SI unit: N/m². - Types: - Tensile stress — forces pull/elongate. - Compressive stress — forces compress. - Bulk (volume) stress — squeezed from all sides. - Shear stress — forces act tangentially.

Strain = the fractional change in length, volume, or geometry due to stress. It is dimensionless. - Tensile/linear strain = ΔL/L₀ - Volumetric strain = ΔV/V₀ - Shearing strain = Δx/L₀

Hooke's law (Robert Hooke, 1678): within the elastic limit, stress ∝ strain, i.e. Stress = k × Strain, where k is the modulus of elasticity. - A large elastic modulus → small strain for a given stress (e.g., steel deforms less than rubber under the same stress). - Three moduli: Young modulus (tensile), bulk modulus (volume), shear modulus (shear).

Example 3.3: Force 9.8 N over area 2×10⁻³ m² → tensile stress = 9.8/2×10⁻³ = 4.9×10³ N/m².

Example 3.4: Weight 98 N on wire length 3 m, diameter 0.4 mm; length increases 2.4 cm. - A = πr² = π(0.2×10⁻³)² = 6.28×10⁻⁷ m² - Stress = 98/6.28×10⁻⁷ = 1.56×10⁸ N/m² - Strain = ΔL/L₀ = 2.4×10⁻²/3 = 0.008
