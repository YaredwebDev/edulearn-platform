4.4 Animal Production Systems

◌ Not started

Systems differ by production processes; classified as extensive, semi-intensive, intensive.

4.4.1 Extensive Production System

Uses small inputs of labour, fertiliser and capital relative to the land area. Common for cattle, sheep, goat farming in low-productivity areas; locally known breeds; feed depends on climate (severe dry-season pasture/fodder shortage). Productivity lower, growth slower, time-to-maturity longer than intensive, but less labour per unit area. Forms: - Pastoralism — an extreme form where herders move animals to pasture from occasional rainfall; a livelihood/way of life for millions in Ethiopia. Problems: feed & water shortage, poor market, disease, number-oriented production. Two types: - Nomads: no permanent home; move continuously with herds in search of water/feed; little long-term planning; mainly arid & semi-arid; based on increasing animal number. - Transhumance: have a permanent home but take flocks/herds away for part of the year; seasonal, recurring, fixed grazing areas & routes; uses distant rainy-season pasture and post-harvest residues. - Agro-pastoral system: livestock important in the farming system; crops grown for subsistence & market; livestock kept for draft, sale, milk/meat/eggs. - Ranching: raising large numbers on fenced land with fixed boundaries; modern alternative to pastoralism; little labour vs land/animals; mainly cattle & sheep for meat, dairy or wool; profit/cash oriented. - Mixed farming: crops + livestock on same farm (cow for milk; goats & sheep for meat/wool; food crops & hay to feed animals; equines for transport; manure improves soil). Natural pasture & crop residues (teff, barley, wheat straws) are major feeds. Subsistence-oriented (for family consumption rather than market).

4.4.2 Semi-intensive System

Between extensive and intensive; high input/output relative to extensive; usually on the periphery of large towns; commonly small-scale producers; animals for family consumption but surplus sold for income; can easily be intensified.

4.4.3 Intensive System

High inputs with high output; modern farming at high stocking density with relatively high inputs/operations; market-oriented (raised to be sold). Feed = grown forage in cut-and-carry (zero grazing), agro-industrial by-products, balanced rations; feed supplemented with proteins, minerals, vitamins. Examples: pig production, broiler production, feedlot operations, commercial dairy. Products: meat, milk, eggs.

Key terms: Cut-and-carry (zero grazing) = fresh grass cut daily and fed to housed animals through the grazing season. Balanced ration = feed supplying the proper amount & proportions of nutrients for a specific purpose (growth, maintenance, lactation, gestation). Feedlot = plot where livestock are kept to be fed intensively to fatten for slaughter (cattle, sheep, goats, camels fattened ~3–4 months).

✋ Trap (extensive vs intensive): - Extensive: low labour/capital, low animal density, largely indigenous animals, small output per land, distant from market, traditional/subsistence. - Intensive: high labour/capital, high density, exotic/crossbred, large output per land, close to market, modern/market-oriented. - Semi-intensive: in between, near towns, small-scale, surplus sold.
