4.2 Mendeleev's Classification of the Elements

  • The earliest modern periodic table was presented simultaneously by Dmitri Mendeleev (Russia) and Lothar Meyer (Germany), arranging elements by increasing atomic weight and observing that elements with similar properties appear at regular intervals.

4.2.1 Mendeleev's Periodic Law

Note: "The physical and chemical properties of elements are the periodic function of their atomic weights."

Features of Mendeleev's table (1871):

  1. Contained only 63 elements (inert gases not yet discovered).
  2. Arranged in 7 horizontal rows (periods) and 8 vertical columns (groups).
  3. Vacant sites were left for undiscovered elements, and their properties were predicted (later verified).

Modifications after discovery of inert gases:

  • Inert gases placed in a new Group 0.
  • Each long period divided into even and odd series.
  • Groups I–VII split into subgroups A and B.
  • Group 0 not split; Group VIII had three sets of three elements.

Period names (rows): period 1 = very short (2); period 2 = first short (8); period 3 = second short (8); period 4 = first long (18); period 5 = second long (18); periods 6 and 7 = very long (32 each).

Group names (columns):

  • Group IA = alkali metals (except H).
  • Group IIA = alkaline earth metals.
  • Group VB = pnictogens.
  • Group VIB = chalcogens.
  • Group VIIB = halogens.
  • Group 0 = noble (inert) gases.

Merits: (1) systematic, easier study of elements; (2) vacant positions guided discovery of new elements.

Demerits: some elements wrongly placed (atomic weights out of order):

  • Ar (40) before K (39); Te (127.6) before I (126.9); Co (58.9) before Ni (58.6).
