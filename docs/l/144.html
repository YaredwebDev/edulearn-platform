4.11 Gene Therapy

◌ Not started
  • Gene therapy = introducing genes into existing cells to modify a person's genes to prevent or cure diseases (e.g., treating a brain tumour caused by a defective/mutated gene).
  • Two types (by which cells are treated): 1. Somatic gene therapy — transfers DNA to somatic cells; effects are NOT passed to the patient's children. 2. Germ-line gene therapy — transfers DNA to sex cells; effects ARE passed to children and subsequent generations.
  • Mechanisms: replace a disease-causing gene with a healthy copy; inactivate a disease-causing gene; or introduce a new/modified gene.
  • Gene therapy products/delivery vectors: plasmid DNA (DNA molecules carrying curative genes), viral vectors (modified viruses as vehicles), bacterial vectors (modified bacteria), human gene editing technology (disrupt/repair mutated genes), and patient-derived cellular gene therapy products (patient's cells modified and returned).
  • Challenges: delivering the gene to the right place & switching it on, avoiding immune responses, not disrupting other genes, and cost.
