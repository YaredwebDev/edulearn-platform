📚 Unit 3 Summary (Key Facts)

  • Inorganic molecules lack both C and H (e.g., water); organic molecules contain both C and H.
  • Water's polarity and hydrogen bonding give it high specific heat, high latent heat of vaporization, surface tension/cohesion, and unusual density.
  • Ions: Na⁺/K⁺ (electrolytes), Ca²⁺ (bones/teeth), PO₄³⁻, Cl⁻ (HCl), Fe (haemoglobin), Cu (Wilson disease), I (thyroid/goiter).
  • Organic macromolecules: carbohydrates (C,H,O), lipids (C,H,O), proteins (C,H,O,N), nucleic acids (C,H,O,N,P).
  • Monomers → polymers by covalent bonding (condensation/polymerization).
  • Carbohydrates: monosaccharides → disaccharides → polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose).
  • Lipids: glycerol + fatty acids; saturated vs unsaturated; phospholipid bilayer.
  • Proteins: amino acids joined by peptide bonds; globular vs fibrous.
  • Nucleic acids: nucleotides (base + pentose sugar + phosphate); DNA vs RNA.
