2.3 Classification of Physical Quantities

◌ Not started
  • Physical quantity: anything that you can measure (e.g., length, temperature, distance, time). Measured values are described in terms of a number and a unit. Each quantity and unit has a symbol.

A. Fundamental vs Derived: - Fundamental (basic) physical quantities: measured directly; cannot be described in terms of other physical quantities. Their units are fundamental units. There are seven fundamental quantities.

Basic quantity Symbol Basic unit Unit symbol
Length l meter m
Mass m kilogram kg
Time t second s
Temperature T Kelvin K
Current I Ampere A
Amount of substance n mole mol
Luminous intensity Iᵥ candela cd
  • Derived physical quantities: depend on one or more fundamental quantities for their measurement. Their units are derived units. Examples: area, volume, density, speed.
Derived quantity Symbol Formula Unit Unit symbol
Speed v distance/time m/s m·s⁻¹
Density ρ mass/volume kg/m³ kg·m⁻³
Acceleration a velocity/time m/s² m·s⁻²
Force F mass × acceleration newton (N) kg·m/s²
Work W force × displacement joule (J) kg·m²/s²
Pressure P force/area pascal (Pa) kg/(m·s²)

B. Scalar vs Vector: - Scalar quantity: has only magnitude (a number with a unit), no direction. Examples: distance, mass, time, temperature, energy. - Vector quantity: has both magnitude and direction. Examples: displacement, acceleration, force, velocity. - A vector is represented by a bold letter or a letter with an arrow (e.g., displacement = S or ⃗S).

✍️ Must-know: Fundamental quantities = measured directly (7 of them); derived quantities depend on fundamentals. Scalar = magnitude only; vector = magnitude + direction.
