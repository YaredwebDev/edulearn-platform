3.3.1 Modal Verbs

Modals are auxiliary verbs that add meaning such as ability, permission, obligation, possibility. They take the base form of the main verb (no "to").

Modal Use Example
can ability; permission; possibility She can drive.
could past ability; polite request; possibility Could I park here?
may permission (formal); possibility You may go. It may rain.
might possibility He might be late.
shall offers/suggestions; future (formal) Shall we leave?
should advice/recommendation You should wear a helmet.
must obligation You must stop at the red light.
will future; prediction The driver will be fined.
would polite request; habit Would you help me?
ought to advice (with "to") You ought to obey the rules.
have to external obligation Students have to wear seat belts.
need to necessity You need to check the brakes.

✋ Traps: (1) Modal + bare verb — "should go," never "should to go"; but ought to and have to / need to DO take "to." (2) must (from the speaker/own view) vs have to (external rule/law). (3) mustn't = not allowed; don't have to / needn't = not necessary.

3.3.2 do / does / don't / doesn't

  • do / don't → I, you, we, they (plural): "Do they obey the rules?" "They don't speed."
  • does / doesn't → he, she, it (singular): "Does she drive?" "He doesn't jump red lights."
  • Used for negatives and questions in the simple present.

3.3.3 Adverbs of Frequency (review)

always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never — say how often something happens; placed before the main verb, after be.
