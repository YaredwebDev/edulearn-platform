6.6 Capacitors and Capacitance

◌ Not started

Capacitor: a device that stores electric charge (and hence electric energy). It consists of two conductors (plates) with equal and opposite charges +q and −q. Capacitance C = charge per unit potential difference: C = q/V. SI unit farad (F) (1 F = 1 C/V). Practical units: μF, nF, pF.

Parallel-plate capacitor: C = ε A / d = (ε₀ κ A)/d, where A = plate area, d = separation, and with a dielectric the capacitance is multiplied by the dielectric constant κ. - Dielectric = an insulator (glass, paraffin, wood, plastic, mica) inserted between the plates. It increases capacitance. - Capacitance factors: larger plate area ⇒ larger C; smaller separation ⇒ larger C; inserting a dielectric ⇒ larger C.

Effects of inserting a dielectric: - (isolated/disconnected capacitor) capacitance increases, potential difference decreases, charge stays the same, electric field decreases (due to polarisation). - (connected to a source) potential difference stays fixed, charge increases (C up ⇒ q = CV up).

Dielectric strength: the maximum electric field a material can withstand before breakdown (air ~3×10⁶ V/m).

Combination of capacitors: - In parallel: equivalent capacitance = sum: C_eq = C1 + C2 + C3 + … (larger than largest). - In series: reciprocal equivalent: 1/C_eq = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + 1/C3 + … (smaller than smallest).

✋ Trap: note the opposite pattern to resistors: capacitors in parallel add (like resistors in series); capacitors in series use reciprocal addition (like resistors in parallel). Charge is the same on series capacitors; voltage is the same across parallel capacitors.

Applications: capacitors store energy; used to smooth power supplies, in flash/photography, electronic timing circuits, memory (via stored charge), etc.

✍️ Must-know formulas: C = q/V; C = κε₀A/d; series 1/C_eq = Σ1/C; parallel C_eq = ΣC. Example 6.19: parallel-plate with mica (κ=6) etc. — find stored charge; Review: charge stored on a parallel-plate capacitor.
