8C Reading Skills: Prevention of Chronic and Infectious Disease

◌ Not started

Key facts: - Successful disease-control efforts in some developing countries have increased life expectancy and changed demographics from predominantly youthful to older/aging populations. In the next ~20 years, chronic diseases are expected to become increasingly important in developing regions and to encompass conditions now attributed to industrialized nations. - Drivers of the trend: changing economics, shifting demographics with lower childhood mortality, and changing lifestyles; migration from rural to urban areas and to previously uninhabited ecosystems may expose people to new infectious agents that underlie chronic disease. - Both newly identified and well-recognized infectious causes of chronic disease (e.g. infections that enter a chronic state, like tuberculosis and malaria) will gain importance; countries with limited research capacity and health services will face growing burdens from both infectious and chronic disease. - Richard Guerrant's example: the long-term consequences of early childhood enteric (gut) and parasitic infections. The chronic impact of repeated malnourishing diarrheal illnesses is greater than that of acute deaths from enteric illness (which claims more than 6,000 children each day). Early diarrheal illnesses affect physical fitness, growth, cognition, and school performance. Diarrhea is a cofactor with malnutrition — it reduces nutritional absorption.

✍️ Reading skill: answer main-idea questions; true/false; dictionary vs contextual meaning (predominantly, attributed, demographic, etiologies, mal-nourishing).
