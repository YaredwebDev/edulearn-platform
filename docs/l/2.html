1.2 The Origin of Domesticated Crops

◌ Not started
  • We cannot know the exact place/time agriculture began; domestication was a deliberate activity that took thousands of years.
  • Religion may have influenced agriculture (some plants/animals kept for religious practices).
  • Plant-selection features considered by hunter-gatherers: ease of harvest, wide availability, ease of transport, high predictability & seasonal distribution, climatic tolerance, good flavour, and suitable grain size.
  • Wild foods consumed: wild rice, grass species, wild oats and legumes (many legumes were toxic — societies detoxified them by trial and error; the poisonous extracts may even have been used as arrow poisons in hunting). Root crops (and in the tropics wild onions, sweet potatoes) were widely eaten. Sources of oil: coconut, oil palm and olive.

✋ Trap: hunter-gatherers did NOT necessarily select for high yield or pest resistance — key selection features were flavour, availability, ease of harvest/transport, predictability and grain size.
