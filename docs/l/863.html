4.2 Cell Division

  • Cell doctrine: new cells arise from pre-existing cells by cell division (Rudolf Virchow, 1858) — the basis of continuity of life.
  • Cause of cell division: as a cell grows, its surface-area-to-volume ratio falls, so diffusion across the membrane becomes inadequate; the nucleus's controlling power is minimized. Cell division solves this.

Importance of cell division:

  • Unicellular organisms (e.g., Amoeba): a means of reproduction (increasing population).
  • Multicellular organisms: growth, replacement of damaged/dead cells, and repair.
  • Sexually reproducing organisms: producing gametes (sex cells) in reproductive organs.

4.2.1 Mitosis

Mitosis = a type of nuclear division where duplicated chromosomes of a single mother cell are distributed between two identical daughter cells, having the same number and kind of chromosomes as the parent nucleus.

  • A diploid (2n) mother cell gives rise to two diploid (2n) identical daughter cells.
  • Term coined by Walther Flemming (1882).
  • Occurs in somatic (body) cells.

Stages of mitosis (Karyokinesis)

  1. Prophase (first phase): each duplicated chromosome (two sister chromatids) pairs up; nuclear membrane breaks down, nucleolus disappears; chromosomes shorten/thicken and become visible; centrosomes move to opposite poles and spindle fibres emerge.
  2. Metaphase (arrangement phase): spindle fully developed; chromosomes line up along the metaphase plate (equatorial plane); sister chromatids attach to spindle fibres from opposite poles.
  3. Anaphase (migrating phase): cohesion proteins (centromere) break down; sister chromatids are pulled apart to opposite poles by the mitotic spindle.
  4. Telophase (reverse of prophase): nuclear membrane reappears around each set of chromosomes (two new nuclei); mitotic spindle breaks down.

Cytokinesis

  • Animal cells: cytoplasm pinches/constricts in the middle → two daughter cells separate.
  • Plant cells: hard cell wall prevents pinching; a new wall (middle lamella) is laid down between the two daughter cells.

Importance of mitosis: growth, repair and replacement of dead/damaged cells; unicellular organisms increase population; after fertilization the zygote (2n) develops into a multicellular organism by repeated mitotic division (cleavage: 2-cell → 4-cell → 8-cell → 16-cell stages).

Key terms:

  • Spindle fibre: long protein fibre from the centrioles; pulls separated chromosomes toward the poles during anaphase.
  • Homologous chromosomes: paired maternal and paternal chromosomes (represent a parent for a unit character); humans have 23 pairs (46 total) in diploid body cells.
  • Sister chromatids: two copies of one chromosome linked at the centromere.
✍️ Must-know: Mitosis = 2 identical diploid daughter cells; used for growth and repair; PMAT (Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase).

4.2.2 Meiosis

Meiosis = a characteristic of sexually reproducing organisms; occurs in reproductive organs (ovaries, testes, anther and ovules of flowering plants).

  • Involves two fissions of the nucleus giving rise to four gametes (sex cells), each with half the number of chromosomes (n) — called reduction division.
  • A diploid (2n) mother cell → four haploid (n) daughter cells.

Why meiosis is important: if human gametes had 46 chromosomes, the zygote would have 92 (not normal). During gametogenesis in ovaries/testes, the 46 chromosomes are reduced to 23; sperm + egg (n + n) → zygote with the normal 46 (2n).

Meiosis I:

  • Prophase I: chromosomes appear shortened/thickened (two chromatids); homologous chromosomes pair up (synapsis); crossing over occurs.
  • Metaphase I: homologous pairs line up on the metaphase plate; centromeres do NOT separate.
  • Anaphase I: homologous chromosomes (each with paired chromatids) separate and move to opposite poles — reduction to haploid (n) occurs.
  • Telophase I: nuclear membrane reforms; cells begin to divide; may be a prolonged interphase but no further DNA duplication.

Meiosis II (similar to mitosis):

  • Prophase II: new spindles form; chromosomes move to the middle.
  • Metaphase II: chromosomes line up; spindle attached to sister chromatids from opposite poles.
  • Anaphase II: centromeres divide; sister chromatids separate and move to opposite ends.
  • Telophase II: nuclear membrane reappears; cytokinesis → four haploid daughter cells.
