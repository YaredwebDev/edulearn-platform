4.4 Electrical Discharge

◌ Not started

Discharging = removing excess charge from a charged body (e.g., by touching it with a conductor). The body becomes neutral. When charges transfer very quickly → electrical discharge (sparks are an example).

Lightning: a very large electrical discharge caused by induction. Negative charge builds at the base of a cloud → induces positive charge on the ground → when charge is large enough, a path forms and the cloud discharges its excess electrons to the ground creating a huge spark; this causes rapid expansion of air → thunder. Air normally is an insulator; it becomes a fair conductor during a strike.

Grounding: providing a pathway (a conductor like a wire or pipe) to drain excess charge harmlessly into the Earth. The Earth is a donor/receiver of charge and always considered neutral. Lightning conductors (rods) are fitted to the tops of buildings to get struck first and conduct the surge safely to the ground.

Safety: If outdoors during lightning, avoid high places/open fields, tall objects, and conductors (water, metal fences). Cars and airplanes (metal shells) divert charges away.

✍️ Must-know: Lightning = huge induction-based discharge between cloud and Earth. Grounding drains excess charge into Earth.
