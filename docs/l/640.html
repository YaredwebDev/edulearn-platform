4.5 Conservation of Mechanical Energy

◌ Not started

Law of conservation of mechanical energy: in the absence of non-conservative (dissipative) forces, total mechanical energy is conserved: - KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f - ½mv_i² + mgh_i + ½kx_i² = ½mv_f² + mgh_f + ½kx_f² (as applicable).

Worked examples: - Example 4.17: 10 kg block released from rest at top of a smooth 10 m-long incline ⇒ speed at bottom from mgh = ½mv² (h = L sin θ). v = √(2gh). - Example 4.18: same incline but rough (μk = 0.2): include the work of friction: mgh − fk d = ½mv² (energy lost to friction). - Example 4.19: 2 kg block at 8 m/s hits a spring, compressing it 20 cm ⇒ KE_i = PE_spring ⇒ ½mv² = ½kx² ⇒ k = mv²/x² = 2×64/0.04 = 3200 N/m. - Example 4.30/ballistic-pendulum is in §4.6.

✍️ Must-know: For a frictionless descent, speed at the bottom depends only on the height lost: v = √(2gh), independent of the path/mass. With friction, subtract the work done by friction from the initial energy.
