6F.1 The writing process / generating ideas (revision)

  • Stages of developing an essay: pre-writing (think of the issue) → brainstorming (plan, generate & list ideas) → mapping/clustering (combine related ideas) → free-writing (first draft as it comes) → editing (check cohesion, spelling, grammar) → writing (final draft).
  • Task: write a five-paragraph essay on the Abay River or the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD): list ideas, main ideas per paragraph, details, first draft.

6F.2 Writing a compare-and-contrast essay

  • An expository essay explains information/facts; a compare-and-contrast essay compares & contrasts two or more things by their similarities and differences.
  • Prompts: whether humans are doing enough to slow down global warming, or whether it's being neglected because of big money/other factors.
  • When editing, check grammar, spelling, punctuation, and coherence (use appropriate connectors/transition words for a smooth flow).

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) explain what global warming is (heat trapped in the atmosphere) and take notes to retell a similar story; (2) pronounce the three nasals (/m,n,ŋ/) and two affricates (/tʃ,dʒ/), state the pronunciation-learning strategies, express preferences with prefer/would prefer/would rather, and run a panel discussion or debate; (3) state the reading's economic effects (nothing is most expensive; damage, lost productivity, climate refugees, coping vs prevention vs rebuilding costs) and answer T/F/"Not Given"/reference items; (4) guess vocabulary in context and use the pay/pick phrasal verbs; (5) form comparatives/superlatives, and use the zero/first/second/third conditionals with unless and inversion; and (6) write a compare-and-contrast essay through the writing-process stages? Try matching the paragraph main ideas and the conditional/unless/inversion and tense exercises.
