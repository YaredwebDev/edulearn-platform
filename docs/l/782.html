10.5 Forest & wildlife degradation: causes & consequences

◌ Not started
  • Forests are vital (firewood, construction, fruits, medicines, timber, food, carbon storage, wildlife habitat). Scientific data: over 50% of tropical forests degraded since the 1960s.
  • Causes of forest degradation: increasing forest fires & diseases, logging (clearing for firewood/timber/extra cropland), and climate change (high temperatures, variable weather); fires used to clear land for agriculture.
  • Effects: loss of trees contributes to climate change, expansion of desertification, soil erosion, reduced food production for humans & livestock.
  • Wildlife degradation = reduction in population size & types of wildlife species. Species classified as endangered (at risk of extinction throughout its range) or threatened (can become endangered soon). Causes: expansion of agriculture, environmental pollution, loss of habitat, loss of mobility & limited vegetation expansion, introduction of non-native species, international trade of fur/bones/horns (ivory)/medicinal plants. Result: total loss of wildlife resources; healthy forest = diverse species; deforested area = sparse vegetation & low diversity.

✍️ Key terms: desertification = vegetation in drylands/arid/semi-arid areas decreases & eventually disappears; arid = <25 mm rain/yr; semi-arid = <50 mm/yr.
