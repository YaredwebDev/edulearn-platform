  • Atoms are electrically neutral because protons = electrons.
  • 1 amu = 1/12 the mass of a carbon-12 atom ≈ 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg.

3.5.2 Atomic Number and Mass Number

  • Atomic number (Z): the number of protons in the nucleus — unique for each element. In a neutral atom, Z = number of protons = number of electrons.
  • Mass number (A): total number of protons + neutrons (total nucleons).
Note: Mass number A = (number of protons) + (number of neutrons)

Nuclear symbol: mass number top-left (superscript), atomic number bottom-left (subscript). e.g., ⁿzX (⁴₂He, ¹²₆C, ⁴⁰₁₉K, ⁶⁵₃₀Zn).

Note: Examples:
Note: - Helium with 2 neutrons: A = 2 + 2 = 4.
Note: - K with mass number 40: p = 19, e = 19, n = 40 − 19 = 21.
Note: - Zn with mass number 65 and 30 protons: n = 65 − 30 = 35.

3.5.3 Atomic Mass and Isotopes

  • Isotopes: atoms of the same element (same protons) with different numbers of neutrons.
  • Same physical/chemical properties (same protons and electrons).
  • Radioisotopes: unstable isotopes that undergo radioactive decay.
  • Hydrogen isotopes: protium (A=1), deuterium (A=2), tritium (A=3).
  • Carbon: carbon-12 and carbon-13 stable; carbon-14 is a radioisotope (used in dating).
  • Atomic mass unit (amu): 1/12 the mass of carbon-12 = 1.660539040×10⁻²⁴ g. Also called the Dalton (Da).
  • Average atomic mass: a weighted average of isotope masses.
Note: Average Atomic Mass = [(% isotope 1)(mass 1) + (% isotope 2)(mass 2) + ...] ÷ 100
Note: Examples:
Note: - Lithium: (7×93 + 6×7)÷100 = 6.51 + 0.42 = 6.93 amu.
Note: - Boron: (10×20 + 11×80)÷100 = 2 + 8.8 = 10.8 amu.
Note: - Neon: (0.9092×19.99 + 0.003×20.99 + 0.0885×21.99) = 20.17 amu.
Note: - Chlorine: (35×75 + 37×25)÷100 = 35.5 amu.

3.5.4 Main Energy Levels

  • Bohr (1913) proposed shells/orbits. The principal (main) energy level is the shell in which an electron is located, denoted by the principal quantum number n (1, 2, 3, 4 ... or K, L, M, N).
  • Energy increases with distance: K < L < M < N (1 < 2 < 3 < 4).

3.5.5 Electronic Configuration on Main Shells

  • Maximum electrons per shell = 2n²: K = 2, L = 8, M = 18, N = 32.
  • Electronic configuration: the systematic arrangement of electrons in the shells of an atom.
  • Fill from the lowest energy shell (K) outward.
  • Valence shell: the outermost shell with electrons.
  • Penultimate shell: the shell just inside the valence shell.
  • Anti-penultimate shell: the shell inside the penultimate shell.
  • Max electrons: valence = 8, penultimate = 18, anti-penultimate = 32.
Note: Examples:
Note: - Calcium (Z=20): 2, 8, 8, 2 (valence shell cannot exceed 8).
Note: - Argon (Z=18): 2, 8, 8.

3.5.6 Valence Electrons

  • Valence electrons: the electrons occupying the outermost shell of an atom.
  • They are furthest from the nucleus, most easily lost, and determine the element's chemical properties and how it reacts.
  • Atoms lose, gain, or share valence electrons during chemical reactions.
Note: Example: Silicon (2, 8, 4) → 4 valence electrons in the M shell. Argon (2, 8, 8) → 8 valence electrons.
