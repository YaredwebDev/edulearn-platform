5.9 Factors Affecting Chemical Equilibrium — Le Chatelier's Principle

◌ Not started

✍️ Must-know definition: Le Chatelier's principle — if a change (stress) is imposed on a system at equilibrium, the position of equilibrium shifts in the direction that tends to reduce (relieve) that change.

5.3.1 Change in concentration

  • Add reactant (A or B) or remove product (C or D) → equilibrium shifts right (more products).
  • Add product (C or D) or remove reactant (A or B) → shifts left (more reactants).
  • Adding/removing reactants/products changes Q but not KC (KC is constant at a given temperature). Adding more I₂ to H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI converts more H₂ to HI.

💡 Experiment 5.1: Fe³⁺(aq, pale) + SCN⁻(aq) ⇌ Fe(SCN)²⁺(aq, deep red). Adding more Fe(NO₃)₃ (more Fe³⁺) deepens the red colour — equilibrium shifts right. Colour intensity correlates with [Fe(SCN)²⁺].

5.3.2 Change in pressure / volume (gas systems only)

Pressure changes affect only systems with gases; they occur by (i) changing a component's concentration, (ii) adding an inert gas, or (iii) changing the vessel volume. - Adding an inert gas has NO effect on equilibrium (it changes total pressure but not the partial pressures/concentrations of reacting species). - Changing volume changes partial pressures: increasing pressure (decreasing volume) shifts equilibrium toward the side with FEWER moles of gas; decreasing pressure (increasing volume) shifts toward MORE moles of gas. Decided by Δn = (c+d) − (a+b) for gases: - Δn < 0 → fewer moles of product gas; increase pressure favours forward (products). (Haber: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, Δn = 2−4 = −2 → high pressure favours NH₃.) - Δn > 0 → more moles of product gas; decrease pressure favours forward. (N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂, Δn = 2−1 = 1 → low pressure favours NO₂.) - Δn = 0 → pressure/volume change has no effect (e.g. H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI).

✋ Trap: pressure effects apply only when the number of gas moles differs between sides AND gases are present. Pure solids/liquids are unaffected; an inert gas at constant volume doesn't shift anything.

5.3.3 Change in temperature

Treat heat as a component. Raising the temperature favours the ENDOTHERMIC direction; lowering it favours the EXOTHERMIC direction. Temperature change also changes the value of KC/KP. - Haber (exothermic, N₂+3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, ΔH = −92 kJ): high temperature favours decomposition (left), reducing NH₃ yield. Methanation CO + 3H₂ ⇌ CH₄ + H₂O (ΔH = −206.2 kJ) has KC falling steeply as T rises: KC = 4.7×10²⁷ (298 K) → 1.38×10⁵ (800 K) → 2.54×10² (1000 K) → 3.92 (1200 K).

💡 Experiment 5.2 (iodine–starch): heating destroys the blue colour / cooling restores it — temperature shifts the (reversible) equilibrium between the coloured and colourless forms.

5.3.4 Effect of a catalyst

  • A catalyst speeds up both forward and reverse reactions by the same factor → it does NOT shift the equilibrium position, does not change KC/KP or the equilibrium composition. It only lets equilibrium be reached faster (important industrially). Example: 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ (ΔH = −197.6 kJ/mol) is very exothermic but very slow; catalysed by Pt, SiO₂, or V₂O₅ at 400–700 °C.

✍️ Must-know summary table (memorise): | Change | Direction system shifts to relieve it | |---|---| | Add reactant / remove product | forward (right) | | Add product / remove reactant | reverse (left) | | Increase pressure (gas, Δn<0) | toward fewer gas moles | | Decrease pressure (gas, Δn>0) | toward more gas moles | | Increase temperature | endothermic direction | | Decrease temperature | exothermic direction | | Add catalyst | no shift (faster only) | | Add inert gas | no shift |
