7.3 Temperature Change and its Effects

◌ Not started
  • Global average temperature increased by about 1.4 °F (0.8 °C) over the past 100 years. The hottest year on record (as of text) was 2016.
  • Greenhouse effect: the natural warming of the Earth when greenhouse gases (carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, water vapor, fluorinated gases) trap heat from the Sun. Naturally important to warm the planet to ~15 °C.
  • Human activity (burning fossil fuels) artificially increases the greenhouse effect → global warming.
  • If greenhouse gases keep being added at the current rate, average temperature could rise 4–12 °F; with big changes (renewables instead of fossil fuels), only 2–5 °F.
  • Higher temperatures cause heat waves (heat cramps, heat stroke, death), and affect oceans, weather, snow/ice, plants/animals.
  • Greenhouse gases: carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, and fluorinated gases.
