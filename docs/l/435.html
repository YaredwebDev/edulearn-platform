4E.1 Parts of speech

  • English has eight parts of speech: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection. The part of speech shows how a word functions in meaning & usage. A single word can function as more than one part of speech in different contexts (e.g. well as adverb He's well-liked vs noun a penny in the well).
  • Also practise spotting extra/unnecessary words in a passage (error-correction editing).

4E.2 Simple future & future-perfect tenses

  • Simple future — tells about something predicted/will happen: I think I will do it again; I will come tomorrow; in 2021 my little child will begin kindergarten.
  • Future perfect — indicates a future event with a definitive end date; an action that will have been completed at some point in the future. Form: will have + past participle. Often with a future time marker: Surafel will have baked a cake; The baker will have baked a cake before you arrive; before that time she will have learned to walk, talk…

4E.3 Adverbial clause of purpose (so that, in order that, lest, so)

  • Tells the purpose of the main-clause verb. Introduced by that, so that, in order that, in order to, so, lest.
  • so that / in order that are normally followed by modal auxiliaries (can, will; may is more formal): I am working hard so that I can pass the test; She worked hard so that she might pass the test.
  • Present tenses can refer to the future after so that / in order that: Send the letter today so that she gets it by Tuesday (= so that she will get it).
  • lest = for fear that: Put on your warm clothes lest you should catch a cold.
  • Examples: I am leaving now so that I will be there by six o'clock; We eat so that we may live.
