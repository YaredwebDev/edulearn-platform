7.3 Guidelines for Site Selection and House Construction

◌ Not started

7.3.1 Guidelines for site selection

Location = where housing is placed relative to soil structure, drainage, road access, proximity to other farm houses, etc. Factors: - Topography and drainage: site should be elevated but fairly level with no abrupt slopes; in a well-drained area; soil porous and slope gentle for efficient drainage. - Feed, water and veterinary services: clean water supply cheaply & in plenty, year-round (for animals, sanitation, workers, fire protection); locate where veterinary services are adequate and feed is cheap. - Accessibility: near good market outlet, power line (electric source), and all-weather roads (easy electricity connection; outages more likely far from a substation). Poor roads hamper marketing fresh produce (milk, meat, fish, egg). - Distance from residences: odours inherent in livestock operations (esp. if manure isn't managed); consider prevailing wind toward non-owned residences; the site should not be near hospitals, schools, residential areas, slaughterhouses or factories. - Health of animals: avoid sites by a national highway or main market road (disease risk); enough space between farm houses to reduce disease spread. - Size: adequate size with future expansion in mind (plan to possibly double the anticipated size); avoid property lines/public-use areas that limit expansion.

7.3.2 Guidelines for construction of an animal house

A good housing system provides adequate space for feeding & exercise, proper ventilation, maximum comfort & protection from environmental stresses, inexpensive to build and economical to operate/maintain, easy to clean & disinfect (minimise mortality), protects from drafts, and has adequate ventilation to prevent dampness. - Orientation: two types. - East–West: avoids direct sunlight; feed & water troughs can be placed under shade all day → increased feed consumption, more manure dropped in shade. - North–South: sun strikes every part of the floor at some time of day → keeps the floor area dry; preferred when paving is too costly. - Floor: extremely important; on a solid, compact foundation (stones, inner side smooth); ideal floor well drained and of cement concrete; facilitates hygienic feeding and removal of wastes; make it non-slippery (grooves/roughened surface). Proper gradient for drainage. - Walls: protect animals from drafts; hard, durable, preferably cement concrete; inside smooth hard finish so no lodgment of dust/moisture; no corners/projections that could injure animals; can use inexpensive local materials (wood, bamboo — wood painted with preservative every 3–5 years); walls may be solid or partially open (windows covered with wire mesh for ventilation, esp. for chicken & pig houses); the width (shorter) side should be solid. - Roof: important to reduce heat/cold stress; preferably galvanised iron sheet (or inexpensive local thatch); high enough to release heat, moisture, pollutants; sloped with an overhang ≥60 cm to protect from sun and rain (direct solar radiation raises heat load → harms production & reproduction). - Ventilation: entry & free circulation of air; smooth air movement supplies fresh air for breathing and removes noxious/toxic gases (CO₂, NH₃); adequate ventilation in all houses avoids dampness; buildings a minimum of ~3 m high to allow sufficient air movement.

✍️ Must-know (construction): floor = cement concrete, drained, non-slippery; walls protect from drafts, smooth inside; roof = galvanised sheet, sloped, overhang ≥60 cm, high enough; ventilation removes CO₂/NH₃ & dampness; East–West avoids sun (shade, + feed intake); North–South keeps floor dry.
