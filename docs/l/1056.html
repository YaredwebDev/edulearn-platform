7.7 Writing: Argumentative Essay

  • A premise = a view held by arguers, open to different arguments (e.g., "Child care at home is the responsibility of the female").
  • Arguing for = supporting the premise; arguing against = opposing it.
  • Steps: be clear on your argument; collect data to convince; explain your point in unemotional language; don't take double positions (choose for or against); check for fallacies (errors in reasoning); summarise at the end.
  • Structure: introduction (general background + thesis), body (detailed explanation with supportive evidence), conclusion (summarise key points).
  • Useful phrases: To list points (In the first place, first of all); to add (what is more, furthermore, moreover, in addition); to introduce conflicting views (It is argued that, opponents of this view say); to express opinion (I believe, In my opinion).

Model (Awramba Community): a self-sufficient community following principles of equality of women, children's rights, caring for the elderly, avoiding bad deeds, and "accepting all humans as brothers and sisters." Income from weaving; profits shared equally regardless of gender/input/capability. Women are equal, not limited to housework; men help with housework — a haven for divorced/widowed single parents.

✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Argumentative essay = clear single position (for/against a premise) + evidence + logical reasoning + conclusion. Avoid fallacies and double positions.

📝 Writing focus

  • Convert direct speech to reported speech (and back).
  • Write all four conditional types.
  • Write an argumentative essay on a premise (e.g., household responsibilities).
  • Practise opinion/agreement/disagreement expressions.
