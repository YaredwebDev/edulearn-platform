4.7 Non-Mendelian Inheritance

◌ Not started

Non-Mendelian inheritance = inheritance patterns that don't follow Mendel's laws (alleles can show different dominance relationships and modes, e.g., co-dominance, incomplete dominance, gene linkage, multiple alleles).

4.7.1 Co-dominance, incomplete dominance and multiple alleles

  • Co-dominance — both alleles are expressed equally in the heterozygote. Example: red (RR) × white (WW) short-horned cattle → roan (RW); F2 = 1 red : 2 roan : 1 white.
  • Incomplete dominance — heterozygotes show an intermediate phenotype (blend). Example: white rose (WW) × red rose (rr) → all pink (Wr); F2 = 1 white : 2 pink : 1 red (1:2:1).
  • Multiple alleles — some genes exist in more than two allelic forms. The ABO blood group is an example: three alleles Iᴬ, Iᴮ, i (A, B, O). Classification is based on the presence/absence of antigens A and B on red blood cells.
  • ABO genotypes/phenotypes (Table 4.4): AA & AO → A; BB & BO → B; OO → O; AB → AB. (Six genotypes.) A and B are co-dominant; O is recessive.
  • Blood transfusion (antigens & antibodies): type A blood has A antigen + anti-B antibodies; type B has B antigen + anti-A antibodies; type AB has both antigens, no anti-A/anti-B (universal recipient); type O has no antigens but both anti-A and anti-B antibodies (universal donor). Transfusing incompatible blood (e.g., B into type A) → antibodies attack the transfused cells.

4.7.2 Rh factor inheritance & medical importance

  • Rh factor = another blood group, inherited independently of ABO. Two alleles: Rh⁺ (dominant, has Rh(D) antigen) and Rh⁻ (no antigen).
  • Rh⁺/Rh⁺ or Rh⁺/Rh⁻ → Rh positive (has D antigen). Rh⁻/Rh⁻ → Rh negative (no antigen).
  • Rh transfusion (Table 4.6): Rh⁺ can donate to Rh⁺ and receive from Rh⁺/Rh⁻; Rh⁻ donates only to Rh⁻ and receives only from Rh⁻.
  • Rh sensitisation (medical importance): an Rh⁻ mother + Rh⁺ father can have an Rh⁺ baby. During pregnancy the mother is exposed to the Rh antigen and builds anti-D antibodies; first pregnancy is usually fine, but in future pregnancies her antibodies can attack an Rh⁺ baby (erythroblastosis fetalis / "Shotelay"). Preventable by giving the anti-D vaccine/immunoglobulin.

✋ Trap: the marriage that is NOT good for the offspring = Rh⁻ woman + Rh⁺ man (risk of Rh sensitisation).

4.7.3 Sex-linked inheritance in humans

  • Males are XY, so any gene on their single X chromosome is expressed whether dominant or recessive (no matching gene on Y to mask it). Therefore men are far more likely to get X-linked disorders than women. The X chromosome is large and carries far more genes than the small Y.
  • Hemophilia (failure of blood to clot) is an X-linked recessive disease carried on the X; a male inherits it from his mother (e.g., carrier mother XᴴXʰ × normal male XY → sons XʰY affected, daughters carriers).
  • Colour blindness is another X-linked trait.

4.7.4 Environmental effects on phenotype

  • The environment can affect phenotype. Phenocopy = an environmentally induced phenotype that mimics a phenotype caused by a gene (often from radiation, chemicals/poisons, temperature shocks).
  • Example: Himalayan rabbits — white coat with black tail/nose/ears at moderate temperature, but develop black coats in cold temperatures, resembling genetically black rabbits.
