2E.1 Past & past-perfect tenses

  • Past simple — a finished past action: I lost my wallet yesterday; Mary broke her leg last week.
  • Past perfect — an action that happened before another action in the past: He had already left the museum when I arrived; Brooke had already moved to Djibouti when the accident happened. (Form: had + past participle.)
  • Past perfect continuous — an action begun in the past and continuing until another past action/event: He had been waiting for two hours when he decided to go back home. (Form: had been + -ing.)
  • Why learn tenses: to show the relationship between actions / locate activities in time, to say what you mean, to gain fluency, to improve all skills, to pass exams.

2E.2 Direct and reported (indirect) speech

  • Direct speech repeats exactly what someone said, put in speech/quotation marks; uses verbs like say, ask, reply, shout. Example: She said, "I'm really hungry."
  • Indirect/reported speech reports in your own words, without quotation marks. Example: He says, "I am going to call you" → He told me (that) he was going to call you.
  • Rules:
  • When reporting in the present simple, tense usually stays, only subject & time adverb change.
  • When reporting the past, move the tense one step back: "I have a new car" → He said he had a new car.
  • Change time expressions (when speaking about the past): tomorrow → the following day, yesterday → the previous day, last week → the previous week.
  • Reporting questions: change the interrogative to an affirmative sentence, back-shift the tense; use if/whether for yes/no questions ("Do they live here?" → You asked me if they lived here); for wh- questions keep the question word ("Where do they live?" → You asked me where they lived). Use the verb ask (often + object pronoun).
  • Reporting orders: report with tell + object + to-infinitive ("Call me back later" → You told me to call you back later; negative → not to do that).
  • Reporting requests: use ask + object + to-infinitive ("Could you call me back later?" → You asked me to call you back later).
  • Note: say is followed by no object; tell/ask take an object pronoun.

2E.3 Relative clauses

  • A clause = part of a sentence with at least a subject & verb. A relative clause describes/defines a noun (introduced by who, whom, whose, which, that, where, when).
  • Defining relative clause — defines/limits the noun; removing it changes the meaning or makes the sentence incomplete: The book that she read was important.
  • Non-defining relative clause — adds extra (unneeded) information; if removed the sentence keeps its meaning; usually set off by commas: Addis Ababa University, which is entirely face-to-face, has offices at Sidist Kilo.

2E.4 The uses of "let" and "make"

  • let = allow/give permission: let + object + bare infinitive (Musa's mother let him use her car; Our teacher let us leave early). Also "make possible" (This card lets you book discount flights). Let's + infinitive = a suggestion (Let's go to the cinema).
  • make = force/compel someone to do something (good or bad): make + object + bare infinitive (His mother made him clean his room; That film made me cry; My brother makes me laugh).
  • In the passive, make takes the infinitive with to: She was made to work on Saturdays.
  • Compare: My boss let me leave early (permission) vs My boss made me stay late (compulsion). (NOT "let me to", "made me to".)
