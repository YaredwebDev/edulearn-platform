5.7 Gametogenesis

◌ Not started

Gametogenesis = formation of gametes (oocytes/sperm) from bipotential primordial germ cells; a haploid daughter (n) is formed from a diploid mother (2n) via meiosis. Called spermatogenesis (male → spermatozoa) and oogenesis (female → ova).

Spermatogenesis (in the seminiferous tubules of the testis, from puberty): the germinal epithelium lines the tubules; cuboidal primordial germ cells (PGCs) + tall somatic Sertoli cells (nurse cells that nourish developing sperm). PGCs divide by mitosis into spermatogonia (diploid, 46 chromosomes); these form primary spermatocytes, which undergo meiosis → two haploid secondary spermatocytes (23 chr.); second meiotic division → four haploid spermatids, transformed by spermiogenesis into mature spermatozoa; maturation in the epididymis; released as semen at puberty.

Oogenesis (in the ovaries): germ cells (oogonium) develop; undergoes meiotic division producing a haploid oocyte and a smaller polar body; gives ~1–2 million cells during embryogenesis; arrests in the follicle.

Menstrual cycle = monthly discharge of blood through the vagina from puberty to menopause (excluding pregnancy), ~28 days average (range 20–45 days). Two significant events: release of one ovum, and preparation of the uterine lining for a fertilized ovum. - Phases/events (Table 5.6): - Menstrual phase (days 1–4): menstruation occurs (uterine lining sheds). - Follicular phase (days 5–13): follicle matures and endometrium develops; FSH stimulates follicle growth. - Ovulation phase (day 14): ovary releases an egg (midway, days 12–14). - Luteal phase (days 15–28): follicle becomes the corpus luteum; endometrium prepared for an egg.
