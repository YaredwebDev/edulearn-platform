2.3 Acceleration

◌ Not started

Acceleration = the rate of change of velocity with time. Occurs when an object speeds up, slows down, or changes direction.

Average acceleration: a⃗ = Δv⃗/Δt = (v − v₀)/(t − t₀) - SI unit: m/s². - Positive acceleration = speeding up; negative acceleration = slowing down (deceleration).

Instantaneous acceleration: acceleration at a specific instant (Δt → 0). - For uniformly accelerated motion, instantaneous = average acceleration.

Key: Acceleration tells how quickly velocity changes; velocity tells how quickly position changes. - Deceleration (slowing) does NOT necessarily mean negative acceleration — it means velocity and acceleration point in opposite directions.

Example 2.5: Car accelerates from rest to 75 km/h in 5 s. 75 km/h = 21 m/s. a = 21/5 = 4.2 m/s².

Example 2.6: Car slows from 15 m/s to 5 m/s in 5 s. a = (5−15)/5 = −2 m/s² (deceleration, to the left).
