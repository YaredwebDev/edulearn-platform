2.8 Hybridisation

◌ Not started

Hybridisation: atomic orbitals on one atom mix to form new equivalent hybrid orbitals of the correct geometry for bonding.

Hybrid Atomic orbital mix % s character Geometry Angle Example
sp one s + one p 50% s, 50% p linear 180° BeCl₂, C₂H₂
sp² one s + two p 33⅓% s, 66⅔% p trigonal planar 120° BCl₃, C₂H₄
sp³ one s + three p 25% s, 75% p tetrahedral 109.5° CH₄, NH₃, H₂O
sp³d + one d — trigonal bipyramidal 90°/120° PCl₅
sp³d² + two d — octahedral 90° SF₆, IF₅
  • sp³ lone-pair compressions: CH₄ 109.5°, NH₃ ~107°, H₂O ~104.5°.
  • sp³d (PCl₅): the axial bonds are longer and weaker than the equatorial bonds (three equatorial at 120° and two axial at 90°).
  • sp³d² (SF₆): octahedral, all six bonds at 90°.
  • IF₅ = sp³d² square pyramid (octahedral with one lone pair).
  • Ethene C₂H₄: each C is sp² with one unhybridised p orbital → the sideways overlap of the two p orbitals makes the π bond.
  • Acetylene C₂H₂: each C is sp, leaving two unhybridised p orbitals → the two C atoms share two π bonds (triple bond).

Steps to choose hybridisation: (1) draw the Lewis structure; (2) count electron groups (bonds + lone pairs) around the central atom; (3) map 2 groups → sp, 3 → sp², 4 → sp³, 5 → sp³d, 6 → sp³d².

✍️ Must-know: sp = 50/50, sp² = 33⅓/66⅔, sp³ = 25/75 (s : p). Geometry and angles follow the hybrid. Ethene = sp² + leftover p (π); acetylene = sp + two leftover p (2 π).
