2.5 Graphical Representation

◌ Not started

Position–time graph (s vs t): - Equation s = v₀t + ½at² is quadratic → graph is a parabola. - The slope of the tangent to the curve at a point gives the instantaneous velocity.

Velocity–time graph (v vs t): - Uniform acceleration → straight line (positive slope if starting from rest passing through origin; if it has initial velocity, the line doesn't pass through origin). - Slope of the v–t graph = acceleration. - Area under the v–t graph = displacement. - Uniform retardation → negative slope (line may touch the time axis if brought to rest). - Non-uniform motion → irregular curve; area under it still gives total displacement.

Acceleration–time graph (a vs t): - Uniform acceleration → straight line parallel to the time axis (slope = 0). - Area under the a–t graph = change in velocity.

Example 2.12: Area under v–t graph = total displacement. s = s₁+s₂+s₃−s₄ (subtract the negative-velocity triangle).

✍️ Must-know: position–time slope = instantaneous velocity (tangent); velocity–time slope = acceleration and area = displacement; acceleration–time area = change in velocity.
