5.8 Types of Diseases

  • Disease: a condition in which the body does not function normally, producing symptoms (headache, fever, pain, weakness).

5.8.1 Infectious Diseases

  • Caused by disease-causing organisms (bacteria, virus, protozoa, fungi); transmitted from person to person (e.g., HIV-AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria).
  • Differentiated by: type of host, mode of transmission, type of causative organism, severity.

Epidemiological terms (note box):

  • Endemic: disease-causing agent exists permanently in a specific place/population (e.g., malaria is endemic in Afar region).
  • Epidemic: diseases that occur suddenly and spread in a specific area/population (e.g., Ebola epidemic in West Africa, 2015).
  • Pandemic: an epidemic that spreads far more widely throughout the whole world (e.g., COVID-19).

Transmission of infectious diseases: direct contact with a patient; drinking contaminated water/eating contaminated food; insect bite; sexual intercourse.

Prevention/control:

  1. Vaccination — major control measure.
  2. Personal hygiene — wash hands after using the toilet and before handling food.
  3. Hygienic food preparation — cover food, clean kitchen surfaces, cook thoroughly.
  4. Boiling/cooking water to kill pathogens.
  5. Proper waste disposal — covered bins, regular collection.
  6. Sewage treatment — water pipes far from toilet drainage.

HIV/AIDS

  • Caused by HIV; HIV infects and enters T-lymphocytes (immune cells that defend the body).
  • HIV replicates in and destroys T-lymphocytes → immune resistance declines → AIDS (result of opportunistic diseases like pneumonia, TB, cancers, weight loss, diarrhea).
  • Transmission: sexual intercourse (fluid transfer); blood transfusion; sharing needles in intravenous drug users; mother to fetus across placenta / during birth; mother to infant in breast milk.
  • Prevention: test blood before transfusion; sterile single-use needles; avoid multiple sexual partners; use condoms; HIV-positive mothers treated with retroviral drugs.

Tuberculosis (TB)

  • Caused by bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis (rarely M. bovis); bacteria live inside human cells, mainly in the lungs.
  • Symptoms: cough, chest pain, shortness of breath, fever, sweating, weight loss.
  • Transmission: airborne droplets (cough/sneeze) inhaled by uninfected people; also undercooked meat and unpasteurized milk (from infected animals).
  • Treatment: isolation during the infectious stage (2–4 weeks); several drugs over 6–9 months; drug-resistant TB needs second-line drugs.
  • Vaccine: BCG vaccine (from animal TB bacteria), protects 70–80%, given in early childhood.
  • Prevention: cover mouth/nose when coughing/sneezing, good ventilation, avoid overcrowding, avoid undercooked meat and unpasteurized milk.

Malaria

  • Caused by protozoa Plasmodium; transmitted by the bite of the female Anopheles mosquito (the vector).
  • Very common in tropical/subtropical regions with high humidity.
  • Life cycle: two hosts (human and mosquito); sexual reproduction in mosquito, asexual reproduction in human. The parasites enter red blood cells and multiply.
  • Prevention/control:
  1. Reduce mosquito population (remove breeding water, biological controls like fish that eat mosquito larvae, insecticides).
  2. Avoid mosquito bites (sleep under a mosquito net, use insect repellent).
  3. Use anti-malarial drugs (Artemisinin combination therapy/ACT, chloroquine); drug resistance is widespread (e.g., chloroquine resistance in Plasmodium falciparum).

COVID-19

  • Coronavirus, family Coronaviridae; causes disease in mammals and birds.
  • Structure: lipid envelope (lipid bilayer), membrane (M), envelope (E), and spike (S) proteins (S needed for interaction with host cells).
  • SARS (2003, China); MERS (2012, Middle East). COVID-19 virus (SARS-CoV-2) first emerged in China (Wuhan) in December 2019; WHO declared it a pandemic in March 2020.
  • Spread: primarily through droplets/particles released when an infected person breathes, talks, laughs, sings, coughs, or sneezes; also by touching contaminated surfaces.
  • Symptoms (2–14 days after exposure): fever, cough, tiredness, shortness of breath, muscle aches, chills, sore throat, headache, chest pain, loss of taste/smell.
  • Diagnosis: laboratory test of a fluid sample from nose or mouth.
  • Prevention: keep at least 6 feet (2 m) distance; avoid crowds and poor ventilation; wash hands with soap for at least 20 seconds or use 60%+ alcohol sanitizer; wear a mask; cover mouth/nose when coughing; avoid touching eyes/nose/mouth; clean/disinfect surfaces.
  • Vaccines: e.g., AstraZeneca AZD1222, BioNTech BNT162.

5.8.2 Non-infectious Diseases

  • Caused by malnutrition, chemical effects, inherited/genetic factors — NOT by disease-causing organisms.
  • Examples: deficiency diseases (Kwashiorkor, Scurvy, night blindness, Marasmus), diseases from cigarette smoke (lung cancer, chronic bronchitis), sickle cell anemia, cancer, allergies, diabetes, podoconiosis.
  • Tobacco use, physical inactivity, harmful use of alcohol, and unhealthy diets increase the risk of dying from non-infectious diseases.
  • Unhealthy diets + lack of activity → raised blood pressure, increased blood glucose, elevated blood lipids, obesity → cardiovascular disease (leading non-infectious cause of premature death).
  • Detection, screening, treatment, and palliative care are key components of the response.
