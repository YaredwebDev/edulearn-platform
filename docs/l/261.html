6.2 Biogeochemical Cycles

◌ Not started

Biogeochemical cycles = the circulation of chemical substances (water, carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus) between organisms and the environment.

6.2.1 The Water (Hydrologic) Cycle

Stores of water: oceans (97.5% of Earth's water — saline), ice caps/glaciers, groundwater, rivers, lakes, atmosphere. Only <1% of fresh water is readily accessible for use.

Processes: 1. Evaporation: water → water vapour (from oceans, lakes, rivers). 2. Transpiration: water loss from plant leaves. 3. Condensation: water vapour cools → clouds. 4. Precipitation: rain, snow, hail falls to the ground. 5. Runoff and infiltration: water flows over land (rivers) and seeps into the ground (groundwater).

6.2.2 The Carbon Cycle

  • Carbon moves between the atmosphere, organisms, oceans, and rocks.
  • Photosynthesis removes CO₂ (fixed into organic matter).
  • Respiration, decomposition, and combustion release CO₂ back to the atmosphere.

  • Overall equation: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O ⇌ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (Photosynthesis →; Respiration ←)

  • CO₂ as a greenhouse gas: traps heat in the atmosphere; increased CO₂ (from burning fossil fuels, deforestation, industry) → enhanced greenhouse effect → global warming/climate change.

6.2.3 The Nitrogen Cycle

Nitrogen (N₂) makes up ~78% of air but is mostly unavailable to organisms in this form — it must be fixed.

  1. Nitrogen fixation: converting N₂ into ammonia (NH₃) / nitrates. - Symbiotic: Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules of legumes (using the enzyme nitrogenase). - Non-symbiotic (free-living): Azotobacter, Klebsiella. - Industrial/atmospheric: Haber process, lightning.
  2. Nitrification: Nitrosomonas oxidizes NH₃ → nitrite (NO₂⁻); Nitrobacter oxidizes nitrite → nitrate (NO₃⁻). Nitrates are the form plants absorb.
  3. Ammonification: decomposers (aerobic/anaerobic bacteria, fungi) break down dead organisms and waste, releasing ammonia/NH₄⁺.
  4. Assimilation: plants take up nitrogen (they prefer NH₄⁺ over NO₃⁻) to build proteins and nucleic acids.
  5. Denitrification: anaerobic bacteria (Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus, Micrococcus) convert nitrates back to N₂ gas, returning it to the atmosphere. - Also produces N₂O (nitrous oxide), a greenhouse gas with a global-warming potential ~300× that of CO₂.

✍️ Must-know (N-cycle microbes): Fixation = Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Klebsiella. Nitrification = Nitrosomonas (→NO₂⁻), Nitrobacter (→NO₃⁻). Denitrification = Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus, Micrococcus.

6.2.4 The Phosphorus Cycle

  • Phosphorus is essential for DNA, RNA, ATP, cell membranes (phospholipids) and bones.
  • Occurs as phosphate ions (PO₄³⁻).
  • Weathering of rocks releases phosphate into soil and water; plants take it up; it moves through food chains; decomposers return it to soil.
  • No significant gaseous phase (unlike C and N cycles).
  • Excess phosphate (and nitrate) run-off into water → eutrophication → algal blooms → O₂ depletion → death of aquatic life.
