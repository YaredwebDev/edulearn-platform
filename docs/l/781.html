10.4 Water pollution: causes & consequences

◌ Not started

Water pollution = presence of unacceptable levels of harmful substances/chemicals in water. - Industrial chemicals (acid/alkaline waste water) — if acidic, adding base neutralises it to ~pH 7 before discharge. Most rivers/lakes have pH 6–8; aquatic organisms need a specific pH range. - Organic wastes (human, animal, agriculture, food processing) can kill aquatic animals like fish. - Fertilisers — phosphorus & nitrogen are inorganic substances; excess reaches water. - Metals enter by erosion; high concentrations are toxic to aquatic organisms. - Toxic substances like chlorine (in disinfectants) — kill harmful bacteria but also useful organisms like fish. - Organic pollutants — gasoline, oil, diesel, pesticide 2-4D — cause health problems to humans & aquatic organisms. - Solids (sand, silt, clay, plant fragments) reduce visibility (problems for fish), harm bottom-dwellers when settling, and make water treatment difficult & expensive. - Microscopic organisms (bacteria, viruses) — via dumping of wastes or flood water from high-animal areas; polluted drinking water causes disease in humans & livestock.
