9B.1 Asking for clarification

  • When you don't understand, first state your lack of understanding, then ask for clarification.
  • Stating lack of understanding: I'm sorry but I'm not sure I understand what you mean by…; Sorry, but I don't quite follow what you're saying about…
  • Asking for clarification: Could you say it in another way? Can you clarify that for me? Could you rephrase that? Would you mind repeating…? (Use could for a slightly more formal tone.)
  • Examples: I'm sorry for asking this but I don't understand why only elderly men participate in conflict resolution.

9B.2 Asking for & responding to apologies

  • Introductory apology phrases: I'm (very/terribly) sorry; Excuse me; Pardon me; I apologize; I'm afraid…
  • Making a full apology: I do apologize for…; I must apologize for…; I'd like to apologize for…; I am so sorry for…; I shouldn't have…; It's all my fault; I'm ashamed of…; Please forgive me for…; Pardon me for this…; Please accept my apologies for…
  • Accepting an apology: That's all right; Never mind; Don't apologize; It doesn't matter; Don't worry about it; Don't mention it; That's OK; I quite understand; You couldn't help it; Forget about it; No harm done.

9B.3 Asking for information

  • Formality depends on the audience: informal with friends, more formal with colleagues, appropriately formal with strangers.
  • Common phrases: Can/Could you tell me…? I'd like to know…; Do you know…? Do you have any idea…? Could anyone tell me…? I wonder if you could tell me…; Would you mind telling me…?
  • Example dialogue asks about when a project begins, who is responsible, and the estimated cost.
