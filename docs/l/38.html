UNIT 6 · LESSON 6 OF 6 · GRADE 11 AGRICULTURE

🧮 Key facts to memorise

◌ Not started
  • Genetics = study of heredity; genes = DNA sections coded for traits; genes in chromosomes.
  • Objectives of breeding: increase yield, improve product quality, produce disease-resistant animals.
  • 3 improvement methods: breed introduction, selection, breeding.
  • Local breeds: small, slow, low yield, but adapted & disease-resistant. Exotic: large, early, high yield, may not adapt.
  • Selection = keep superior parents, cull the rest. Culling = removing poor performers.
  • Artificial selection methods: mass (own performance), progeny (offspring), family (relatives), pedigree (ancestors).
  • In-breeding → in-breeding depression (recessives show). Cross-breeding → heterosis/hybrid vigour (higher than parents).
  • Related breeding: pure lines; cross: new breeds, growth, resistance.
  • AI = deposit semen (other than natural mating); stored at −196 °C in liquid N₂; recto-vaginal method in cattle; exploits superior males; reduces infectious disease spread.
  • ET = move pre-implantation embryos donor→recipient; exploits superior females; ~6 good embryos/cow; no live-animal transport.
  • Oestrus signs: restlessness, vocalisation, red moist vulva, mucous discharge, mounts/stands.
  • Records + identification fundamental; permanent (branding, ear tags, tattooing, ear notching, microchip) vs temporary (tail marking, hair clipping, dyeing).

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) define genetics, gene, DNA, heredity and state breeding objectives; (2) explain breed introduction (local/exotic/cross) with pros & cons; (3) define selection vs culling, and distinguish natural & artificial selection and the four artificial-selection methods; (4) compare in-breeding, line-breeding, out-breeding and cross-breeding (heterosis/hybrid vigour, in-breeding depression) with examples and pros/cons; (5) describe AI (procedure, storage −196 °C, oestrus signs, recto-vaginal method, advantages/disadvantages) and ET (donor/recipient, process, advantages/disadvantages), including which exploits superior males vs females; and (6) explain why records & identification matter, and list permanent vs temporary identification methods? Re-answer the review too (mating proven animals of different breeds = cross-breeding; temporary identification = tail marking; selection by ancestors = pedigree selection; replacing indigenous breeds with exogenous is NOT an objective of breeding; ET exploits superior females).
