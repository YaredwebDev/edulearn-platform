4.2 The Structure and Function of DNA and RNA

◌ Not started

4.2.1 Structure and function of DNA

  • DNA is a ladder-like double helix twisted into a spiral: the sugar and phosphate groups form the two vertical rails, the nitrogenous bases form the rungs.
  • Made of two polynucleotide chains twisting around each other. Nucleotides are the basic building blocks.
  • Each nucleotide = a sugar + phosphate group + a nitrogenous base.
  • Four nitrogenous bases: Adenine (A), Thymine (T), Guanine (G), Cytosine (C).
  • Purines: A and G (double-ring). Pyrimidines: C and T (single-ring).
  • Base pairs: A pairs with T; C pairs with G (joined by hydrogen bonds).
  • DNA sugar = deoxyribose. Sugar+phosphate = the backbone.
  • Each strand has polarity: one end is 5′, the other 3′; the two strands run antiparallel (opposite directions) and each forms a right-handed coil.
  • Watson and Crick discovered the double-helix structure of DNA.

Genes and chromosomes: - Gene = a section of DNA that carries instructions determining an organism's characteristics; the basic structural and functional unit of inheritance. - Genes are located on chromosomes = thread-like structures made of a protein (histone) and a DNA molecule. - Each chromosome holds hundreds to thousands of genes arranged linearly; each gene's unique position = its locus (pl. loci). - Chromosomes exist in pairs in diploid organisms — one from each parent. Human cells have 46 chromosomes = 23 pairs (22 autosomal pairs + 1 sex-chromosome pair).

Function of DNA: store all genetic information needed to grow, develop, reproduce, control the cell and survive; carry and transmit hereditary information from parents to offspring (via DNA replication during cell division).

4.2.2 Structure and function of RNA

  • RNA is single-stranded, contains the sugar ribose, phosphate, and bases A, G, C and Uracil (U) instead of Thymine.
  • Three well-known types (all made on DNA by transcription):
  • mRNA (messenger RNA) — transcribes the genetic code from DNA; carries instructions to the ribosome.
  • tRNA (transfer RNA) — brings amino acids from the cytoplasm to the ribosome; translates the mRNA message into an amino-acid sequence (via its anticodon).
  • rRNA (ribosomal RNA) — forms part of the ribosome; helps translate mRNA into protein.
  • Function of RNA: protein synthesis, and storage of genetic information in some viruses; serves as messenger between DNA and the ribosome.

✍️ Must-know DNA vs RNA: DNA = double helix, sugar deoxyribose, bases A–T / G–C; RNA = single strand, sugar ribose, bases A–U / G–C (U replaces T). DNA stores information; RNA makes proteins.

4.2.3 DNA replication

  • DNA replication = the process by which DNA makes a copy of itself during cell division. It is semiconservative — each original strand acts as a template for a new complementary strand, so each new double helix = one old + one new strand.
  • Replication fork = the Y-shaped structure where the two strands unwound/separate.
  • Leading strand = new DNA synthesized in the same direction as the growing fork. Lagging strand = synthesized in the opposite direction.
  • Key enzymes (Table 4.1): | Enzyme | Function | |---|---| | DNA helicase | binds double-stranded DNA and separates the two strands | | DNA polymerase | adds new nucleotides to the growing strand / polymerises bases in the correct sequence using the template | | RNA primase | synthesises RNA primers complementary to the DNA strand | | DNA ligase | links two DNA fragments by forming a phosphodiester bond | | Topoisomerase | prevents supercoiling ahead of the replication fork |
  • Three stages: (1) helix unwound & unzipped, H-bonds break, strands separate; (2) each strand acts as template; DNA polymerase adds free nucleotides by complementary base pairing (A–T, C–G) in the 5′→3′ direction; (3) the two new strands twist to form two double helices identical to the original.

✍️ Must-know: replication is semiconservative; helicase unwinds, polymerase adds bases 5′→3′, ligase links fragments. Complementary strand example: DNA strand 5′‑CGA TTG CTA‑3′ has partner 3′‑GCT AAC GAT‑5′ (answer B). Lab: DNA can be extracted from a banana (mash → filter → add soap to break membranes → precipitate with cold alcohol).
