2.2 Invertebrates and Vertebrates

◌ Not started

2.2.1 Invertebrate Animals

  • No backbone/vertebral column — the most diverse animal group, found almost everywhere (hot deserts, deep seabeds, dark caves, high mountains).
  • Lack a rigid internal skeleton; many are soft-bodied, but some have an exoskeleton (external skeleton, usually of chitin) protecting soft inner bodies.
  • Cold-blooded — do not regulate their body temperature.
  • Examples: earthworms, insects, spiders, snails, sponges, jellyfish, lobsters, crabs, sea stars, squid.

2.2.2 Vertebrate Animals

  • The most advanced animal group; possess a well-defined internal skeleton with cartilage and a backbone/vertebral column separated into:
  • Axial skeleton — skull, vertebrae, ribs, sternum.
  • Appendicular skeleton — girdles and appendages (limbs).
  • The skull and vertebrae protect the developed brain and nerve cord.
  • More complex/specialised organ systems:
  • Circulatory — a closed circulatory system with a ventral heart (2–4 chambers) and a median dorsal artery.
  • Respiratory — gills or lungs.
  • Nervous — centralised, with a brain and sensory organs (eyes, ears, nostrils).
  • Excretory — paired kidneys.
  • Bilaterally symmetrical.
  • Groups: mammals, birds, fish, reptiles, amphibians. Fish/reptiles/amphibians are cold-blooded; birds & mammals are warm-blooded.
  • All vertebrates are chordates, but not all chordates are vertebrates.

✍️ Must-know: A common characteristic of ALL vertebrates is a well-developed skull (protecting the brain). Compare features in a table: backbone (invertebrates ✗ / vertebrates ✓), exoskeleton (some invertebrates / not internal-vertebrates), body symmetry, circulatory (open in many invertebrates vs closed in vertebrates), nervous system (diffuse vs brain+spinal cord).
