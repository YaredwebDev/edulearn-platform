4.3 The Process of Cell Division

◌ Not started
  • Growth, wound-healing and reproduction happen because of cell division. DNA replicates during cell division.
  • Cell cycle = ordered series of events of cell growth and division producing new daughter cells. Two major phases: interphase and mitotic phase (M phase).

Interphase (preparation) has three stages: - G1 (first gap) — cell is biochemically active; accumulates building blocks of chromosomal DNA & proteins and energy. (Cells not dividing or preparing may enter G0, the resting phase, doing only regulatory/basic functions.) - S (synthesis) — DNA replicates to form identical sister chromatids attached at the centromere; the centrosome duplicates. - G2 (second gap) — replenishes energy and synthesizes proteins for chromosome manipulation; organelles duplicate; final preparations for mitosis.

Mitosis (division of somatic cells → two identical daughter cells). Two main phases: karyokinesis (nuclear division) and cytokinesis (physical separation of cytoplasm). Four steps: 1. Prophase — nuclear envelope breaks into vesicles; chromosomes condense & become visible; centrosomes move to opposite poles; spindle microtubules extend; kinetochores develop at centromeres; Golgi & ER fragment. 2. Metaphase — chromosomes align at the metaphase plate (equatorial plane); sister chromatids held by cohesion proteins. 3. Anaphase — sister chromatids separate at the centromere and are pulled to opposite poles; connections break. 4. Telophase — chromosomes reach poles and decondense; spindles depolymerise; nuclear envelopes reform. Cytokinesis completes division → two genetically identical daughter cells.

✋ Trap: crossing over occurs in Prophase I of meiosis (not mitosis, not prophase II).

Meiosis (division producing sex cells/gametes). Two divisions → four haploid daughter cells; reduces chromosome number by half and produces variation via crossing over and independent assortment. Eight stages (four per meiosis).

Meiosis I (reduction division) — separates homologous chromosomes: 1. Prophase I — chromosomes replicate to sister chromatids; nuclear envelope disintegrates; chromosomes condense; spindle forms. Homologous chromosomes pair up and crossing over occurs (non-sister chromatids exchange genetic material via chiasmata) → increases genetic variation. 2. Metaphase I — homologous pairs align side by side at the equator (random alignment); spindle attaches one chromosome of each pair. 3. Anaphase I — homologous chromosomes pulled to opposite poles (sister chromatids stay together, unlike mitosis/meiosis II). 4. Telophase I — chromosomes reach poles; spindles disappear; nuclear envelope reforms. Cytokinesis → two daughter cells, each with half the parent chromosome set.

Meiosis II — separates sister chromatids (like mitosis): 1. Prophase II — chromosomes condense again into X-shapes; nuclear membrane dissolves; centrioles duplicate; meiotic spindle re-forms. 2. Metaphase II — sister chromatids line up end-to-end in a single line at the equator (not in homologous pairs). 3. Anaphase II — sister chromatids pulled to opposite poles (now individual chromosomes). 4. Telophase II — chromosomes reach poles; membranes form. Cytokinesis → four non-identical haploid daughter cells.

Homologous chromosomes = the pair, one from each gamete/parent. Sister chromatids = the two chromatids of a single replicated chromosome joined at the centromere. Non-sister chromatids = chromatids of homologous chromosomes (these cross over in prophase I).

✍️ Mitosis vs meiosis: mitosis = somatic, one division, 2 identical diploid daughters, no variation. Meiosis = gametes, two divisions, 4 haploid, non-identical daughters (crossing over + independent assortment). Meiosis I is the "reduction division" because it halves chromosome number.
