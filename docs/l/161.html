6.18 Indigenous Conservation Practices in Ethiopia

◌ Not started
  • Indigenous knowledge = a body of knowledge built over generations by a group of local people living in a particular environment (natural phenomena, resource use, seasonal prediction via observation of the sun, moon, stars, animals, winds, temperatures). It has supported calendars, weather prediction, disease treatment, crop production, environmental protection & resource conservation (soil, water, crop seeds, forest, medicinal plants, animals).

The Konso people (southwestern Ethiopia) — internationally recognised, practised for >400 years and registered by UNESCO as a world heritage. Their soil & water conservation practices: making terraces, contour ploughing, crop rotation, mixed cropping, surface mulching, and agro-forestry. - Making terraces — dry-stone terraces on hilly land intercept runoff, reduce erosion, retain soil, collect water & discharge excess; terrace saddles used for agriculture. - Contour ploughing — farming along hills to reduce runoff & prevent erosion. - Crop rotation — planting different crops sequentially on the same plot to improve fertility & reduce pests/weeds. - Mixed cropping — growing two+ species on the same field at the same time (e.g., maize & groundnuts) to maintain soil fertility. - Surface mulching — applying a layer of material (crop residue, branches) to the soil surface to conserve moisture, improve fertility, reduce weeds. - Agro-forestry — trees inside & around farmland (e.g., Moringa stenopetala) for soil conservation + cultural value. - Qero system / Guassa community conservation area (Menz, central highlands, >400 years): communal management that closes the Guassa area to any use for ~3–5 years; opening depends on grass growth/recovery, community needs, crop-harvest success, and drought frequency. The community elects headmen to decide when to open/close for thatch grass & livestock grazing.
