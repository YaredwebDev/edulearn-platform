11.3 Agricultural Water Management

◌ Not started
  • Agriculture is the major sector using water. In developing countries agriculture depends mainly on rainfall. Rainwater can be lost by evaporation, transpiration, runoff, deep percolation. Where rainfall is the only source, plants face water shortage at critical growth stages — especially in semi-arid areas (low, unpredictable rainfall). So reduce water loss by conserving rainfall.
  • Common water-management practices:
  • Rainwater conservation / water harvesting — in arid & semi-arid areas, save rainfall using water-harvesting techniques; e.g. soil pit lined with plastic sheets to store rainwater for when crops face shortage. Rooftop water can be used for irrigation during short droughts.
  • Reducing evaporative loss — evaporation is high when soils are bare or poorly covered; reduce by mulching (organic materials) and leaving some biomass standing at harvest.
  • Soil conservation — practices that conserve soil also improve water conservation: contour farming, contour bunding, bench terraces on steep slopes, storing runoff. Example: bench terrace in Konso reduces flooding & erosion. These slow runoff and let water enter the soil.
  • Drought-tolerant crops for dry areas (adequate yield with low water requirement & low transpiration).
  • Efficient irrigation — minimise losses; adjust water amount, frequency & time to soil properties, crop type & climate.

✍️ Key terms: transpiration = plants absorb liquid water from soil & release water vapour through leaves; deep percolation = downward movement of water beyond root reach.
