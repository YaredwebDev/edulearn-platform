8.3 Internal and External Parasites of Farm Animals

◌ Not started

Parasite = organism that lives in or on another host to get nourishment. Over 1,000 species of parasites affect domestic animals. Parasites cause production loss & high mortality → negative financial impact; may cause or transmit diseases (parasitic diseases).

  • Internal parasites (Endoparasites) — live in the blood or tissues of the animal's body. Examples: tapeworm, roundworm, trypanosome, liver fluke. They interfere with digestion & assimilation, share the animal's food, and cause diarrhoea, anaemia, loss of condition — often death without treatment.
  • External parasites (Ectoparasites) — annoy hosts by biting, embedding or irritating the skin. Examples: ticks, flies, lice, mites. They attack blood/skin/hair; cause anaemia, weight/condition loss, restlessness, skin irritation & destruction. They may cause disease (e.g. mange, scabies) or transmit disease (e.g. red water, trypanosomiasis).
  • Bite of a tsetse fly transmits trypanosomiasis.
  • Mange = infectious skin disease caused by mites → hair loss, scabs, itching.
  • Scabies = contagious skin disease: intense itching, inflammation, red papules.
  • Red water = disease with passage of reddish urine.
