3.7 Factors Affecting Enzyme Action

◌ Not started

Enzymes work best at optimum conditions (temperature, pH); deviations reduce/stop activity. Factors: temperature, pH, inhibitors, activators, radiation, water, enzyme concentration, substrate concentration, end-product concentration.

  • Temperature: enzymes work in an optimum range; too low/high causes loss of activity and denaturation (irreversible). Optimum human-body enzyme temperature ≈ 37 °C.
  • pH: enzymes have an optimum pH; extremes (too acidic or too alkaline) stop the enzyme from binding substrate.
  • Substrate concentration: rate rises with substrate until all active sites are saturated (maximum limit).
  • Enzyme concentration: rate rises with enzyme concentration.
  • Radiation: damages enzyme activity by reducing efficiency and disturbing macromolecules.
  • Water: affects activity beyond the optimum level.
  • End-product (feedback) inhibition: the end product binds to the allosteric site, changes the active-site structure, and prevents the enzyme from working — a cellular control mechanism.

✍️ Must-know: rate of enzymatic reaction increases with increased substrate concentration (until saturation) — True. Boiled potato (denatured catalase) gives no reaction with H₂O₂ — enzymes are denatured by high heat. Extreme pH also stops enzymes.
