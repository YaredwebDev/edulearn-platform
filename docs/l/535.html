2.5 Graphs of Polynomial Functions

◌ Not started

Key properties (from observations): - Graph of a polynomial is smooth (no sharp corners) and continuous (no holes or jumps). - f(x)=|x| is NOT a polynomial (sharp corner at (0,0)); a graph with a hole/jump is not a polynomial.

  • A polynomial of degree n has at most n−1 turning points and intersects the x-axis at most n times.

Quadratic graph (degree 2):

Odd degree (1,3,5,…): ends go in opposite directions. - Leading coefficient positive: up to the right, down to the left. - Leading coefficient negative: down to the right, up to the left.

Even degree (2,4,…): ends go in the same direction. - Leading coefficient positive: both ends up. - Leading coefficient negative: both ends down.

Example: f(x)=x³−1=(x−1)(x²+x+1): x-intercept 1, y-intercept −1, up right/down left (LC>0, odd). f(x)=−x³+1: x-intercept 1, y-intercept 1, down right/up left.

✍️ Must-know: Determine end behaviour from the degree (odd/even) and the sign of the leading coefficient. Find x-intercepts via factorization/rational zero test; use the location theorem to find where zeros sit.
