11.1 Concepts of Natural Resource Use

◌ Not started
  • Many natural resources (especially non-renewable ones) exist in limited or finite quantities (e.g. coal, natural gas, mineral ores). Failure to use them wisely causes rapid depletion, affecting future generations. Proper management ensures sustainability.
  • Carrying capacity = the maximum population level for which a given resource can satisfy food and shelter requirements. Example: if a hectare of grassland feeds 100 sheep, that is its carrying capacity; keeping 150+ is far beyond it. Exceeding carrying capacity leads to starvation, disease & predators that reduce the population. Farming increased the world's carrying capacity by producing more food (intensive crop/livestock production) — but this can damage the capacity of resources to pass to the next generation.
  • Sustainable use = natural-resource use that creates & maintains a condition in which humans use resources to fulfil their social, economic & other requirements — letting us continue to have water, soils, forests, minerals, etc. for current generations without affecting the needs and demands of future generations.
