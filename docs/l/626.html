2.4 Product of Vectors (Scalar / Dot Product)

◌ Not started

Multiplying a vector by a scalar k gives a vector: kA is parallel to A if k>0 and antiparallel if k<0; magnitude scaled by |k|. Example 2.12: S = 35 km 60° N of E ⇒ 1.2S = 42 km same direction; −1.2S = 42 km, 60° S of W.

Scalar (dot) product of two vectors: - A·B = |A||B| cos θ, where θ is the angle between the vectors placed tail-to-tail. - In components: A·B = AxBx + AyBy. - The result is a scalar.

✍️ Must-know (key results): - Dot product of a unit vector with itself = 1 (î·î = ĵ·ĵ = 1). - Dot product of perpendicular unit vectors = 0 (î·ĵ = 0). - A·A = |A|². - Dot product is commutative (A·B = B·A) and distributive (A·(B+C) = A·B + A·C). - Sign: A·B > 0 for θ<90°, = 0 for θ=90°, < 0 for θ>90°.

Uses of the dot product: (a) find the angle between two vectors (cos θ = A·B/|A||B|); (b) find the component of one vector in the direction of another.

Physical applications (worked examples): - Work (Example 2.14): work done W = F·S (dot product of force and displacement). - Power (Example 2.15): power P = F·v (dot product of force and average velocity).

✍️ Must-know formulas: W = F·S = |F||S| cos θ; P = F·v = |F||v| cos θ. In components, compute FxSx+FySy etc.
