1.6 The Quantum Mechanical Model of the Atom

◌ Not started
  • Quantum mechanics describes electrons using two central ideas: the wave behaviour of matter and the uncertainty principle.
  • Heisenberg's uncertainty principle (1927): it is impossible to know precisely both an electron's position and momentum (p = mν) at the same time. Δx · Δp ≥ h/4π. (Δp = uncertainty in momentum; Δx = uncertainty in position.) No practical consequence in the macroscopic world; huge at the atomic scale.
  • Schrödinger (1927): an electron is described by a wave function, ψ; its solutions give allowed energy states. ψ² gives the probability of finding the electron in a region of space.
  • An orbital = the region where there is a high probability of finding an electron. Visualised as an electron cloud whose density varies.

The four quantum numbers: 1. Principal quantum number (n) — main energy level (shell); size & energy; n = 1, 2, 3, 4… analogous to Bohr levels. A shell has n² orbitals. 2. Angular momentum (azimuthal) quantum number (ℓ) — shape of the orbital; values 0 … n−1; letter labels: ℓ=0 → s, ℓ=1 → p, ℓ=2 → d, ℓ=3 → f. Number of orbitals in a subshell = 2ℓ+1. 3. Magnetic quantum number (m_ℓ) — orientation in space; integer values −ℓ … +ℓ (incl. 0). 2ℓ+1 values. 4. Spin quantum number (m_s) — electron spin; +½ or −½. Each orbital holds at most two electrons (opposite spins).

Electron capacity: a subshell holds 2(2ℓ+1) electrons; a shell (n) holds 2n² electrons.

Examples (worked): - "4d": 4 = n, d = ℓ=2. - For n=2: ℓ=0 (2s), m_ℓ=0; ℓ=1 (2p), m_ℓ=−1,0,+1. Shell capacity 2n² = 8. - For n=3: ℓ = 0,1,2; m_ℓ per ℓ as above; orbitals = n² = 9 (3s:1, 3p:3, 3d:5).

Shapes of orbitals: - s orbitals (ℓ=0): spherical; 1s smallest, 2s larger, etc.; all s orbitals are spherical. - p orbitals (ℓ=1): dumbbell (dumb-bell) shaped along three mutually perpendicular axes → pₓ, p_y, p_z (e.g. 2pₓ, 2p_y, 2p_z). - d orbitals (ℓ=2): five, more complex shapes (n≥3). - f orbitals (ℓ=3): seven, most complex (n≥4).

✋ Trap (non-existent orbitals): 1p, 2d, 3f don't exist (ℓ can only go up to n−1). 1s, 2s, 2p, 3p, 3d do exist.
