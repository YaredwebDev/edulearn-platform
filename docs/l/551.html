5.2 Angle Properties of Circles

◌ Not started
  • Arc: a portion of the circumference. Major arc > 180° (3 letters); minor arc < 180° (2 letters).
  • Central angle: formed by two radii with vertex at the center.
  • Inscribed angle: vertex on the circle, formed by two chords.

Theorem 5.6: If an inscribed angle and a central angle intercept the same arc, the inscribed angle is half the central angle (θ=2a).

Example: If central ∠POQ=150°, the inscribed angle PRQ (same arc) = 75°. Conversely, if ∠PRQ=75°, central ∠POQ=150°.

Theorem 5.7: Inscribed angles subtended by the same arc are equal.

Theorem 5.8 (Thales): An angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle (90°).

Example: AB is a diameter, ∠ACB=90° (Thales). In a triangle with another angle 55°, ∠BAC=35°.

Cyclic quadrilateral (Def 5.1): a quadrilateral whose four vertices lie on a circle.

Theorem 5.9: In a cyclic quadrilateral, the sum of either pair of opposite angles is 180°.

Example: A cyclic quadrilateral has angles (4y+2),(y+20),(5y−2),7y. Sum=360° → 17y+20=360 → y=20. So angles: 82°,40°,98°,140°.

✍️ Must-know: Inscribed angle = ½ central angle (same arc). Inscribed angles on same arc are equal. Angle in a semicircle = 90°. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°.
