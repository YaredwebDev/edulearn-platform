3.2 Fiber Crops Production and Management

◌ Not started

Fibre crops produce fibres used in textiles and other industries (cotton and sisal among others). Fibre crops such as cotton are also sources of vegetable oil.

3.2.1 Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum)

  • Produces lint — a soft fluffy fibre growing in a boll (protective case) around the seed; lint is the raw material for the textile industry.
  • Adaptation: warm climate; generally drought-tolerant; does not do well in high-rainfall areas because lint in the boll becomes discoloured (reduces quality). Loam soils suitable.
  • Ethiopian context: the textile industry is rapidly growing (in 2021 >50 garment/cloth manufacturers and 14 textile factories; >35 mills weaving fabric). Demand for cotton is rising; local cotton supply is insufficient, so raw cotton is imported — increasing national cotton production is needed.
  • Land prep & planting: seeds treated with acid to remove seed hairs; row planting ~1 m between rows, 30 cm between plants; depth ~8 cm.
  • Weed/pest: weeds are a major limit → start with a clean seedbed; manual weeding ≥ twice within one month after weeds emerge; chemicals also used.
  • Harvesting: ready when at least half of plants have bolls burst open; 6–7 months to mature. Processing order: harvesting/picking → drying & ginning (separating seeds & debris from fibre) → cleaning (dust/impurities) → spinning (twist fibres into thread) → weaving into fabric.

3.2.2 Sisal (Agave sisalana)

  • Origin: Central America; widely cultivated in the tropics. Produced for its hard fibres used in ropes, sacks, carpets; also paper making and polishing clothes; sisal stem used for fencing, firewood, house construction. Fibre comes from the leaf.
  • Adaptation: drought-resistant; can be grown as a rain-fed crop (rainfall only, no irrigation); altitude from sea level up to ~1800 m.
  • Propagation/planting: vegetative propagation — new plants from cuttings; reproduction via suckers (around the plant) or bulbils (buds on the flower stalk). Suckers/bulbils are grown in a nursery, transplanted when ~40 cm high. Row planting ~2.5 m between rows, 80 cm between plants.
  • Harvesting: first harvest of leaves 2–3 years after transplanting, then annually. Leaves ready when ~1.2 m tall and ~15 cm wide. Processing: scrape leaves (manual or machine) to separate fibre = decortication → wash & dry → mechanically brush/straighten → grade by length & colour → pack for market.

✍️ Must-know (fibre crops): Cotton = soft lint fibre, warm/drought-tolerant, ginning→cleaning→spinning→weaving; Ethiopia imports raw cotton (textile demand > local supply). Sisal = hard fibre (ropes, sacks, carpets) from the leaf; rain-fed, drought-resistant, vegetatively propagated via suckers/bulbils; decortication separates fibre.
