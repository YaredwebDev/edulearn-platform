4.6 The Electric Field

◌ Not started

Electric field = a region where an electric charge experiences a force. Visualized by electric field lines (introduced by Michael Faraday). - Test charge: a small positive charge used to test for an electric field (so small it does not disturb the field).

Properties of electric field lines: - Never intersect/cross. - Perpendicular to the surface of the charge. - Number of field lines ∝ magnitude of charge. - Originate at positive charges, terminate at negative charges. - Lines converge where unlike charges attract; diverge where like charges repel.

Electric field strength = force per unit charge: E = F/q or F = Eq - SI unit: N/C. E is a vector; direction = direction of force on a positive charge.

Electric field due to a point charge: E = k·q/r² (direction radial outward from positive charge, inward toward negative charge).

Example 4.2: q = 2.0 nC at r = 5.0 mm. E = 9×10⁹ × 2×10⁻⁹/(5×10⁻³)² ≈ 7.2×10⁵ N/C, pointing away from the charge.

✍️ Must-know: E = F/q = k·q/r². Field lines point away from positive and toward negative charges.
