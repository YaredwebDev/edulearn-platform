1.8 Electronic Configurations and the Periodic Table of the Elements

◌ Not started

1.8.1 The Modern Periodic Table

  • Elements arranged by increasing atomic number so that similar properties recur → periodic law (modern form): certain physical & chemical properties repeat periodically when elements are arranged by increasing atomic number.
  • 18 groups and 7 periods.
  • The period number = value of n of the outermost (valence) shell. Elements in the same group have similar outer-shell configurations → similar chemical behaviour.
  • Metalloid = element with properties between metals & non-metals (e.g. boron, germanium, silicon).

1.8.2 Classification of the Elements

By the subshell being filled, elements are representative (main-group, s- & p-block), transition (d-block), and inner-transition (f-block) elements. The lanthanides and actinides are placed at the bottom.

1.8.3 Periodic Properties

Two opposing factors govern size & trends: the principal quantum number (n) (size of shell) and the effective nuclear charge (Z_eff) (the nuclear charge the electron actually experiences, reduced by shielding by inner electrons).

(a) Atomic radius - Increases down a group (n increases → larger shell; shielding → valence electrons held less tightly). - Decreases across a period left→right (Z_eff increases → electrons pulled closer). Radius r = d/2 (half the internuclear distance in a bonded diatomic molecule). - Trend is regular for main-group elements but not for transition metals (electrons enter inner shells; once d is filling, radius stays roughly constant because d-electron shielding cancels the Z_eff increase). E.g. V and Zn have almost equal radius.

(b) Ionization energy (IE) - IE = energy required to remove 1 mol of electrons from 1 mol of gaseous atoms/ions. - IE₁ (first IE) = energy to remove the first electron from a gaseous atom: Atom(g) → Ion⁺(g) + e⁻. - IE₂ > IE₁ always (removing from a positive ion is harder). For boron the ionization steps rise steeply after removing core electrons (IE₄ = 25,025 kJ/mol shows the huge jump after the 3 valence electrons are gone). - Trend: increases across a period (smaller atoms hold electrons tighter), decreases down a group. Lowest in a period = Group IA (alkali metals, lose e⁻ easily); highest = noble gases. - Irregularities (exceptions) within a period: a Group IIIA element (ns²np¹) has a lower IE₁ than the preceding Group IIA (ns²) — the np electron is easier to remove than an ns one; a Group VIA (ns²np⁴) element has a lower IE₁ than the preceding Group VA (ns²np³) — the doubly occupied np orbital's electron repulsion makes removal easier.

(c) Electron affinity (EA) - EA = energy change when an electron is added to a gaseous atom: Atom(g) + e⁻ → Ion⁻(g). - EA₁ is usually negative (energy released, e.g. O: EA₁ = −141 kJ/mol). EA₂ is always positive (energy absorbed; e.g. O⁻ + e⁻ → O²⁻, EA₂ = +744 kJ/mol). - Broad trend: more negative across a period left→right. Group VIA & VIIA have the largest negative EAs. Group IIA and VIIIA (18) have small/positive EAs because the added electron must go into a much higher energy level → stable anion does not form.

(d) Electronegativity - Electronegativity = the attraction an atom in a bond has for the bonding (shared) electron pair. Pauling scale: fluorine arbitrarily set = 4.0 (greatest). - Increases across a period (left→right), decreases down a group. Trend parallels ionization energy. F=4.0, Cl=3.0, Br=2.8, I=2.5, At=2.2 (down Group VIIA); Li 1.0→F 4.0 across period 2.

(e) Metallic character - = chemical properties of metals, arising from the ability to lose electrons. - Decreases across a period (left→right; atoms more readily accept electrons to fill a valence shell). - Increases down a group (electrons easier to lose as radius increases).

Advantages of periodic classification (list of six): 1. Based on atomic number (fundamental property). 2. Isotopes averaged in one place (e.g. Cl has Z=17 but atomic mass 35.5). 3. Explains periodicity of properties & links them to electron configuration. 4. Justifies misplaced/misfit elements based on atomic number (e.g. Ar/K anomalous pair). 5. Lanthanides & actinides placed separately at the bottom. 6. Simple, systematic, easy way to remember element properties.

✍️ Must-know trends cheat-sheet (for a period → across, for a group ↓ down): Atomic radius ↓→ ↑↓; Ionization energy ↑→ ↓↓; Electron affinity (more negative) ↑→; Electronegativity ↑→ ↓↓; Metallic character ↓→ ↑↓.
