3F.1 Mechanics: semicolon, apostrophe, hyphen

  • Semicolon (;) — a break stronger than a comma but not as final as a full stop; joins two closely-linked main clauses: The film was a critical success; its lead actors were particularly praised.
  • Apostrophe (') — possession (Malcolm's cat) and omission/contraction (I'm; isn't).
  • Hyphen (-) — links words/parts: in compound words (mother-in-law), to join prefixes (post-Marxist), and at word breaks (eighteenth- and nineteenth-century).

3F.2 Descriptive writing

  • Purpose: give readers a clear picture of people/places/objects/events — make readers see, feel, taste, smell, hear through accurate sensory details. Uses figurative language, adjectives, adverbs, modifiers.
  • Objective description = factual; subjective = the writer's impression/opinion.
  • Steps (process approach): brainstorming → outlining → drafting → redrafting → writing → editing. (Write a description of a classmate for others to guess.)

3F.3 Writing a cause–effect paragraph/essay

  • The writer analyzes causes → consequences/results to show how phenomena are related.
  • Process: choose topic; list causes & effects; use conjunctions/connectors to keep logical flow.
  • Sample connectors in use: because, so, as a result, consequently, due to/owing to, since, because of, for that reason, therefore, hence, thus.
  • Practice essay: "Causes and effects of road accidents" (causes: poor roads, driver inefficiency, pedestrian carelessness, lack of law enforcement; effects: loss of life, injuries, economic loss, mental damage).

3F.4 Summary writing

  • Summarising = giving a concise overview of a text's main points in your own words, always much shorter than the original (length depends on the original & purpose). Do not critique/analyze; give a clear, objective, accurate account without copying text and without missing key points.
  • Process: read carefully → break into sections → identify key points per section → write the summary → check against the original. (Summarise a cause–effect essay to ~⅕ or ¼ of its length.)

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) note causes→effects→solutions of road accidents and report/discuss/speech on them; (2) state the WHO statistics (1.25 m deaths, 50 m injured, Africa 26.6/100k, Ethiopia among highest) and the socio-economic effects; (3) guess vocabulary from context and use affixation (derivational vs inflectional) and "let" phrasal verbs; (4) form adverbial clauses of reason (because/as/since/that) and of result (so that/in order that/so…that/such…that); (5) use the semicolon, apostrophe & hyphen correctly; and (6) write a descriptive paragraph and a cause–effect essay with appropriate connectors, then summarise it? Try the true/false, matching, and "Peace of Mind" vocabulary items, and rewrite sentences shifting between because/as a result/due to/since.
