11.4 Forest and Wildlife Management

◌ Not started
  • Maintaining/improving forest cover: increases carbon storage in aboveground biomass & roots; reduces carbon emission; protects soil from erosion; provides wildlife habitat.
  • Forest-management practices (range of human interventions): planting trees (afforestation) & replanting depleted forests; careful harvest & transport of timber; increasing forest cover by planting where there are no forests; adapting techniques to prevent wild forest fires.
  • Forest management is needed to: provide fuelwood sustainably; make more productive use of forest vegetation; create employment & income for rural communities; contribute to biodiversity conservation; reduce deforestation & desert expansion; minimise vulnerability to climate change; reduce carbon emission.
  • Wildlife management: wildlife destruction usually follows destruction of natural habitats, so protecting wildlife & habitats is integral. Example: allowing domestic animals to graze in Ethiopian national parks violates the wildlife-management objective (protecting grasslands, forests, grazing lands). Walia Ibex and Ethiopian Wolf are among the most endangered endemic wild animals in Ethiopia, mainly due to habitat destruction.
  • Measures: reduce agricultural encroachment into habitats, prevent introduction of invasive species, create buffer zones between settlements & habitats, control population growth.
