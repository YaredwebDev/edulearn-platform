1.5 Vector Resolution

◌ Not started

Vector resolution = the process of breaking a single vector into its components (two or more vectors which sum to the original).

In a rectangular coordinate system, a vector A⃗ is resolved into: - Horizontal component: Aₓ = A cos θ - Vertical component: A_y = A sin θ

where θ is measured counterclockwise from the positive x-axis.

Restoring the magnitude (Pythagoras): |A| = √(Aₓ² + A_y²) Direction: θ = tan⁻¹(A_y/Aₓ)

Example 1.2: Resolve a displacement of 250 km, 30° North of East. - North component: Sₙ = 250 sin 30° = 125 km - East component: S_e = 250 cos 30° = 216.5 km

Example 1.3: Boy walks 3 km East then 2 km North. - |S| = √(3² + 2²) = √13 = 3.6 km - tan θ = 2/3 = 0.666 → θ = 33.69° North of East (or 56.31° East of North)

✍️ Must-know: Ax = A cos θ, Ay = A sin θ. The combined effect of the components equals the original vector.
