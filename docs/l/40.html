7.2 Types of Farm Animal Housing

◌ Not started

Housing type is chosen by the type of animal, climate conditions, local tradition, availability of building materials, and economics. E.g. conventional barns aren't suitable to hot/humid climates; loose housing is more suitable to Ethiopian conditions.

7.2.1 Conventional barns / houses

  • Animals are kept in constructed houses, managed intensively, protected from adverse climate. Barns are completely roofed, with complete walls and windows/ventilators; continual airflow keeps the area comfortable; workers less exposed to weather; hygienic, lower disease prevalence; suitable for heavy-rainfall/temperate climates.
  • Examples: conventional barns for dairy cattle and deep-litter poultry housing. Dairy cows often kept tied in large stalls (can't walk around), fed/watered and milked in the same barn (no separate milking parlour). Tie stalls are being phased out due to animal-welfare concerns.
  • Deep-litter system (poultry/pigs): repeated spreading of straw or sawdust indoors; initial layer for bedding/defecation; new layers added as it becomes soiled.

7.2.2 Loose housing

  • Animals are allowed to move freely over the whole building/pen area. Provides more comfort & free movement for workers and animals → improves productivity & welfare. Animals of similar production line & age kept in groups (group size depends on type/size of farm). Self-feeding is practised; animals walk around free stalls at will. Requires less bedding and less manure removal. Very suitable for tropical climates (most of Ethiopia); cheaper to construct, easier to expand, flexible; all livestock types can be housed this way.

7.2.3 Free range system

  • Animals are left in a large area (natural or cultivated pasture) with watering points & shelters; farm headquarters at the centre. Animals run free all year; an attendant moves with them to protect from predators & thievery. Less intensive than modern farms; less control over environment but more exercise & grazing time. Suited to animals not handled daily (beef cattle, fattening sheep). Enables animals to express natural behaviour.
  • Widely practised in Ethiopia: local chickens (mixed flocks) range outdoors in daylight and are housed in a shed at night; raised with little/no prepared-feed supplementation. For sheep/goats the farmer allows pasture by day and keeps them in a barn at night. Local-range animals have lower productivity and can be attacked by predators.

7.2.4 Modern farms

  • Depending on weather, animals kept in full or half-walled houses in separate pens/enclosures; cut-and-carry or prepared feed is the main feed source. Practised on all common farm animals. Investment cost per animal higher than conventional; productivity is better. Examples: battery cage systems — enriched cages, and hens-aviary (multi-tier) systems for laying hens. Cage systems are the most efficient for egg production, but some worry about the welfare of caged hens.
  • Enriched cages = cages stacked in rows providing limited facilities for nesting, perching and scratching. Hens-aviary systems = multi-tier; tiers are the typical feature giving living space at several levels, letting hens disperse.

Farm Animal Welfare Issues

  • Animal welfare = the well-being of non-human animals. An animal is in good welfare if it is healthy, comfortable, well-nourished, safe, able to express innate behaviour, and free from pain, fear and distress. Good welfare requires disease prevention & veterinary treatment, appropriate shelter, management, nutrition, humane handling and humane slaughter. Protecting welfare is a human responsibility.
  • The five freedoms of animal welfare: 1. Freedom from thirst and hunger. 2. Freedom from discomfort (adequate shelter). 3. Freedom from disease, pain or injury. 4. Freedom from distress and fear. 5. Freedom to exhibit natural behaviours.

✋ Trap (freedoms): "the right to be housed with or apart from other animals" fits freedom to exhibit natural behaviours (companionship is a natural behaviour). Conventional barns keep cows tied; loose housing = free movement (suitable for Ethiopia); modern = enriched cages / aviary systems.
