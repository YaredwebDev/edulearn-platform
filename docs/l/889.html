2.1 Characteristics of Living Things

We cannot define life in a single sentence, but we can list characteristics shared by living systems:

  1. Made of one or more cells — unicellular (one cell, e.g., bacteria) or multicellular (many cells, e.g., plants).
  2. Require energy — all organisms use energy for metabolic activities.
  • Producers/autotrophs: make their own food using sunlight (photosynthesis), e.g., plants.
  • Consumers/heterotrophs: cannot make their own food, consume others.
  1. Respond to stimuli — detect/sense changes (stimuli) in the internal or external environment and make appropriate responses.
  2. Grow — growth is a permanent increase in size and mass due to an increase in cell number, cell size, or both.
  3. Reproduce — process that makes more of the same kind of organism (sexually or asexually).
  4. Excrete — removal of metabolic wastes (e.g., carbon dioxide) produced by chemical reactions.
  5. Display ordered complexity — living things are both complex and highly ordered (atoms → molecules → ...).
  6. Maintain homeostasis — regulation of internal conditions to maintain stability (e.g., stable body temperature).
  7. Possess adaptations that evolve over time — organisms evolve adaptations to their environments.
