9.3 Word Formation: Borrowing

◌ Not started

Borrowing = combining the beginning of one word and the end of another, OR taking words wholly from other languages. - Blend-type borrowing: breakfast + lunch = brunch. - French borrowings: chancellor, country, court, crime, defendant, evidence, government, jail, judge. - Latin borrowings: agile, abdomen, anatomy, discus, excavate, expensive, fictitious, gradual, habitual. - Greek borrowings: atmosphere, climax, comedy, data, history, parasite, pneumonia, skeleton. - Amharic words borrowed from English in use today: television, jacket, gas, mango, computer, phone, etc.

✍️ Must-know: English has borrowed words from many languages (French, Latin, Greek). Know the examples given. The six word-formation processes to remember: borrowing, affixation, clipping, blending, compounding, derivation.
