2.1 Cereal Crops Production and Management

◌ Not started

2.1.1 Teff (Eragrostis abyssinica)

  • Native to Ethiopia (staple); used as livestock feed in South Africa. Very nutritious; contains no gluten (gluten causes allergy in some people; maize & sorghum also lack gluten). Teff straw is valuable animal feed. Storable long-term; less affected by pests/diseases than other cereals → low-risk, reliable, high-value crop. Ranks first in cultivated area in Ethiopia.
  • Production: grows in wide environments incl. low-fertility or waterlogged soils; altitude sea level to ~2800 m (main producing areas 1500–2800 m); best on loam and clay soils (high nutrients & water-storage).
  • Land preparation: needs very smooth seedbed (tiny grains); soil cultivated 4–5× and levelled; animals driven over the surface to smooth it.
  • Planting: usually by broadcasting; row planting (20 cm rows) now common. After planting, branches dragged or animals driven over to cover seed. Shallow furrows every 3–6 m drain excess moisture. Main planting July/August; harvest Nov/Dec (highland) or earlier in mid/lowland.
  • Fertilisation: ~60 kg N + 26 kg P per ha (adjust to soil fertility).
  • Weed/disease: competes poorly with weeds at seedling stage → early (manual) weeding ~1 month after planting; herbicide 2,4-D for broad-leaf weeds; fungal diseases if excess water.
  • Harvesting: when stems and panicle turn yellow. National average yield ≈ 14.6 quintals (1.46 t)/ha. Rich in carbohydrates, protein, fats and minerals (iron, calcium, magnesium).

2.1.2 Maize (Zea mays)

  • Origin: Central America. High yield/unit area; seeds covered by husk (protects from rain/birds); does not shatter (no seed loss on late harvest). Used as staple food, animal feed (green or dry), residues as firewood, and for starch & oil in industry.
  • Production: altitude ~500–2400 m; yields/unit area highest of Ethiopian cereals; ranks second in area. Better on loam soils without excess moisture. Grown as mono crop or mixed with legumes (haricot bean) or cereals (teff).
  • Land prep: plow 2–3×.
  • Planting: row planting 25 cm between seeds, 75 cm between rows; depth 5–7 cm; usually planted at start of rains (March–April).
  • Fertilisation: ~41 kg N + 20 kg P per ha.
  • Weed/pest/disease: manual weeding 2–3× or herbicides — pre-emergence (e.g. Atrazine, before weeds emerge) and post-emergence (e.g. 2,4-D). Insects: maize stalk borer, aphids, army worm, weevil. Fungal (rust, gray leaf spot, blight) and viral (strike virus) diseases — control by crop rotation (not mono) and adjusting planting time. Earthing up ~1 month after planting reduces lodging (plants falling over in wind).
  • Harvesting: maturity 3–5 months (early vs late varieties); harvest when seeds develop a black layer at the bottom tip; grain moisture ~25% at harvest → dry to ≤13% for safe storage. National average yield ≈ 32.5 quintals (3.25 t)/ha — the highest.

2.1.3 Wheat (Triticum aestivum)

  • Origin: Asia (China, Pakistan, India). Globally the most important cereal, then rice, then maize. Makes bread, macaroni, spaghetti.
  • Production: needs cool weather; altitude ~1800–3000 m in Ethiopia; well-drained loamy fertile soils (black, red, brown); rotated with lentils, pea, teff, potato.
  • Land prep: 2–3 cultivations.
  • Planting: broadcast or rows (25 cm spacing), depth ~5 cm; usually June–July.
  • Fertilisation: ~45 kg N + 25 kg P per ha.
  • Weed/pest/disease: manual weeding from ~1 month after planting; 2,4-D for broad-leaf weeds; fungal rust and smut in warm humid areas → crop rotation, resistant varieties, adjusting planting time.
  • Harvesting: when dry golden-yellow with no green parts. National average ≈ 24.5 quintals (2.45 t)/ha. Source of carbohydrates, proteins, fats, minerals, fibre. Wheat contains gluten.

2.1.4 Barley (Hordeum vulgare)

  • Origin: Middle East. Used as human food; residue for livestock; used for malting (controlled germination producing enzymes that turn cereal starch to sugars, then fermented to alcohol). Growth requirements similar to wheat.
  • Production: altitude ~2200–3000 m; well-drained loam.
  • Land prep: 2–3 cultivations (like wheat).
  • Planting: broadcast or rows (25 cm), depth ~5 cm.
  • Fertilisation: ~60 kg N + 26 kg P per ha for food barley; for malting barley nitrogen must be reduced (high N lowers malt quality).
  • Weed/pest/disease: most weeds/diseases of wheat also affect barley; similar control (resistant varieties, rotation, changing planting time). Insects: army worm, grasshopper, barley shoot fly.
  • Harvesting: matures 3–4 months; indicator = drying & yellowing. National average ≈ 19 quintals (1.9 t)/ha. Source of carbs, protein, fat, fibre.

2.1.5 Sorghum (Sorghum bicolor)

  • Origin: Africa; staple in drier Africa. Stalk used for animal feed and construction (fences, houses).
  • Production: altitude ~500–1500 m; reasonable yields on soils too poor for other crops; well-drained fertile clay soils; well-adapted to dry areas; can stay dormant during dry periods and resume growth when moisture returns — called 'the camel crop'. Rotated with haricot bean, soybean, sesame, cotton.
  • Land prep: 2–3 cultivations.
  • Planting: broadcast or rows 0.5–1 m between rows, ~40 cm between plants; depth ~2.5 cm; usually March–May. Earthing up ~1 month after planting resists lodging.
  • Fertilisation: ~32 kg N + 10 kg P per ha.
  • Weed/pest/disease: striga (parasitic weed) is the major weed causing massive losses → hand weeding before seed set, plus crop rotation and resistant varieties. Also insects, birds and fungal smut. Control = integrated (rotation + resistant varieties + chemicals).
  • Harvesting: matures ~4 months; harvest when grains start drying (moisture ~30%). National average ≈ 23 quintals (2.3 t)/ha.

✋ Trap (altitudes & adaptation — frequent exam question): wheat & barley = highland (~1800–3000 / 2200–3000 m); teff & maize = mid-altitude (maize 500–2400); sorghum = lowland & moisture-deficit (500–1500 m). Maize has the highest yield/ha; teff has the largest area. Teff/maize/sorghum lack gluten; wheat contains gluten.
