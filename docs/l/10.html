2.2 Teff (Eragrostis abyssinica)

◌ Not started
  • Native to Ethiopia (staple); used as livestock feed in South Africa. Very nutritious; contains no gluten (gluten causes allergy in some people; maize & sorghum also lack gluten). Teff straw is valuable animal feed. Storable long-term; less affected by pests/diseases than other cereals → low-risk, reliable, high-value crop. Ranks first in cultivated area in Ethiopia.
  • Production: grows in wide environments incl. low-fertility or waterlogged soils; altitude sea level to ~2800 m (main producing areas 1500–2800 m); best on loam and clay soils (high nutrients & water-storage).
  • Land preparation: needs very smooth seedbed (tiny grains); soil cultivated 4–5× and levelled; animals driven over the surface to smooth it.
  • Planting: usually by broadcasting; row planting (20 cm rows) now common. After planting, branches dragged or animals driven over to cover seed. Shallow furrows every 3–6 m drain excess moisture. Main planting July/August; harvest Nov/Dec (highland) or earlier in mid/lowland.
  • Fertilisation: ~60 kg N + 26 kg P per ha (adjust to soil fertility).
  • Weed/disease: competes poorly with weeds at seedling stage → early (manual) weeding ~1 month after planting; herbicide 2,4-D for broad-leaf weeds; fungal diseases if excess water.
  • Harvesting: when stems and panicle turn yellow. National average yield ≈ 14.6 quintals (1.46 t)/ha. Rich in carbohydrates, protein, fats and minerals (iron, calcium, magnesium).
