2F.1 Summary writing

  • A summary = a brief statement/account of the main points of a text.
  • Rules: written as one paragraph; begin with an introductory sentence stating the text's title & main point; write in your own words; include only the original text's ideas (no opinions/interpretations/comments); don't copy word-for-word; identify main points first.

2F.2 Writing a narrative essay

  • A narrative essay tells a story, in chronological order.
  • Steps: think of the topic → plan (what to include; words/phrases to inspire) → sequence ideas logically → write first draft → check spelling, punctuation & grammar → write final draft → share & include comments.
  • Model: The Striving Jungle of Africa (a vivid, admiring first-person description of Merkato, Addis Ababa, using engaging, descriptive, evaluative language, punctuation, and a tour-guide character). Write a similar story about a place you visited.

2F.3 Punctuation & mechanics (this unit)

  • Comma (,) — separates elements/clauses; before coordinating connectors joining two main clauses.
  • Apostrophe (') — contractions (I didn't, I'd) and possession (writer's).
  • Colon (:) — before a list/series (…four subjects: mathematics, physics, chemistry, economics) and to introduce/explain.
  • Semicolon (;) — joins two closely related independent clauses, often with a connector/conjunctive adverb (I had to complete the assignment by Friday; otherwise I would have failed the course).

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) take notes & summarise a listening text and explain the Gregorian calendar & ancient Ethiopian civilisations; (2) pronounce the eight diphthongs, interview using open/specific/motivational/unconventional questions, and express preferences with prefer/would rather/rather than; (3) define civilization and list its criteria, plus answer in your own words; (4) use vocabulary strategies and telephone phrasal verbs; (5) form the past/past-perfect/past-perfect-continuous, convert direct↔reported speech (incl. questions, orders, requests, time changes), classify defining/non-defining relative clauses, and use let/make correctly; and (6) write a summary and a chronological narrative essay with correct commas, apostrophes, colons and semicolons?
