7.3 Equation of a Line

◌ Not started

7.3.1 Gradient (slope)

Definition (Def 7.2): The gradient/slope m of a line through P(x₁,y₁), Q(x₂,y₂), x₁≠x₂: m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁) = (change in y)/(change in x)

  • Positive slope: line rises left→right. Negative: falls. Zero: horizontal. Vertical line: no slope (undefined).

Examples: - P(2,1),Q(4,7): m=(7−1)/(4−2)=3. - P(7,3),Q(−4,3): m=0 (horizontal). P(2,3),Q(2,−7): undefined (vertical).

7.3.2 Slope in terms of angle of inclination

The inclination is the anticlockwise angle θ from the positive x-axis to the line (0°≤θ<180°). m = tanθ

  • Acute inclination → positive slope; obtuse → negative slope (m=tan(180°−α)=−tanα).

Example: inclination 45° → m=tan45°=1. Inclination 120° → supplement 60° → m=−tan60°=−√3.

✍️ Must-know: slope = tan(inclination). Horizontal line has m=0; vertical line has no slope.

7.3.3 Different forms of the equation of a line

  • Point-slope form: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)
  • Slope-intercept form: y = mx + b (b = y-intercept)
  • Two-point form: y − y₁ = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁) · (x−x₁)
  • General form: Ax + By + C = 0
  • Intercept form: x/p + y/q = 1 (p=x-int, q=y-int)

Properties of m: m>0 line rises; m<0 line falls; m=0 horizontal; vertical line has no slope.

Examples: - m=3, b=−1 → y=3x−1. - m=4 through P(−5,0): y=4(x+5)=4x+20. - m=−2 through P(4,3): y−3=−2(x−4) → y=−2x+11. - Through (1,4),(2,6): m=2 → y−4=2(x−1) → y=2x+2.

✍️ Must-know: Use point-slope form with a given point & slope; slope-intercept for quick graphing; two-point form for two points. All can be written as Ax+By+C=0.
