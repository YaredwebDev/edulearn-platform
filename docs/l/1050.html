7.1.1 Listening: Ethiopia — The Land of Origins

Key facts from the listening text:

  • Ethiopia is landlocked (has no coastline) — but it has not always been landlocked.

  • Its neighbours (six): Eritrea, Djibouti, Somalia, Kenya, South Sudan, and Sudan.

  • Highest part: Ras Dashen; lowest part: the Danakil/Dallol Depression.

  • Africa's two biggest geological structures in Ethiopia: the Great Rift Valley and the Ethiopian Highlands / Rift.

  • Ethiopia is the most populous landlocked country in Africa (and Africa's second-most populous overall).

  • Two famous international organisations based in Ethiopia: the African Union (AU) and the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa (UNECA).

  • Lucy (Dinknesh) was discovered in Ethiopia (Hadar, Afar). Ardi is older than Lucy.

  • Called "Land of Origins" for Lucy/human origins; "Thirteen Months of Sunshine" for its climate.

7.1.2 Reading Part I: Ethiopia Through Writers' Eyes

Series of paragraphs from "Selamta" magazine by various writers/geographers:

  • Yves-Marie Stranger: Ethiopia's borders have not always been well defined — at times it was thought to cover all of sub-Saharan Africa, or only the area between Egypt's desert and the confluence of the Blue Nile and Atbara rivers. The Greek Bible has the spawn of Shem, Kush and Ham go to Egypt and Aethiopia; a legend has Axum's capital founded by Ityopis, son of Kush.

  • Strabo's Geographica (circa 7 BC): dwellers of the waterless coast went inland every five days to drink; lived in caves or cabins made of whale bones/spines and olive branches; the Chelonophagi (Turtle-eaters) lived under turtle shells; they cast their dead into the sea as food for fish.

  • Hasan Ben Ahmed El Haimi (Yemeni Embassy to Gondar, 1648): described a King's stronghold — a stately stone-and-lime edifice in Gondar (Abyssinia), the finest building there (other dwellings were "nests of grass").

  • Aida Edemariam (The Guardian, 2007): returned to Ethiopia for only the second time in 13 years; re-acquainted with Gondar and Lalibela.

  • Selamta (May–June 2017): leaving Lalibela at dawn, driving through mountain escarpments; Mekedim says "let's walk from here" — and the vista of jagged cliffs and valleys unfolds.

✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Comprehension answers — Aethiopia is the ancient name for Ethiopia/the region; Yves-Marie doubts the borders because they have not always been well defined; Strabo's dwellers lived in caves/cabins (or under turtle shells); they threw their dead in the sea for the fish; El Haimi referred to Gondar; Aida first came 13 years ago; Mekedim advised walking to see the vista.

7.1.3 Reading Part II: Ethiopia's Heritages Registered by UNESCO

  • Harar Jugol: fortified historic town in eastern Ethiopia, 525 km from Addis Ababa, on a plateau with deep gorges delimited by deserts and savannah. The walls (built 13th–16th centuries) made it "the fourth holy city" of Islam. Five historic gates divided the city into five vicinities. Numbers 82 mosques (3 dating from the 10th century) and 102 shrines.

  • The Konso Cultural Landscape (Terraces): extensive dry stone terraces that retain soil from erosion, collect water, discharge excess, and create terraced fields; hills contoured with dry stone walls up to 5 m high. The walled towns (paletas) sit on high plains/summits for defence, circled by 1–6 rounds of walls; the inner cultural spaces (moras) are central to Konso life.

✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Harar Jugol = fortified holy city (Islam's 4th holy city), 82 mosques, 102 shrines, 525 km from Addis. Konso = dry-stone terraced landscape, paletas (walled towns) and moras (central spaces), walls up to 5 m. Heritage = individual, group, and national identity.
