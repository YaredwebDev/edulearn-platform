2.2 Average and Instantaneous Velocity

◌ Not started

Average velocity (vector) = displacement ÷ time interval: v⃗_av = Δs⃗/Δt = (s − s₀)/(t − t₀) - SI unit: m/s (also km/h, mi/h, cm/s).

Average speed (scalar) = total distance ÷ total time: v_av = total distance / total time = s_tot/t_tot

Instantaneous velocity = velocity at a specific instant (Δt → 0). The magnitude of instantaneous velocity is the instantaneous speed (the speedometer reading).

✍️ Must-know: Average speed equals the magnitude of average velocity only when motion is in a straight line in one direction; otherwise they differ. Never average two speeds by (v₁+v₂)/2.

Example 2.4: A car travels 100 km at 50 km/h, then 100 km at 100 km/h. - t₁ = 100/50 = 2 h, t₂ = 100/100 = 1 h; total t = 3 h - v_av = 200 km / 3 h = 66.7 km/h (not 75 km/h!)
