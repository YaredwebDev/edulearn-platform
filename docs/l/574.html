2.1 Revision on Natural Numbers and Integers

◌ Not started
  • Natural numbers ℕ = {1, 2, 3, …}
  • Integers ℤ = {…, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, …}

Every natural number is an integer, but not every integer is a natural number (e.g., −7 and 0 are integers but not natural).

2.1.1 Euclid's Division Lemma

Theorem: Given a non-negative integer a and a positive integer b, there exist unique non-negative integers q (quotient) and r (remainder) such that:

a = b × q + r, with 0 ≤ r < b

  • a = dividend, b = divisor, q = quotient, r = remainder.
  • If r = 0, then a is divisible by b.

Examples: - 38 ÷ 4 → 38 = 4×9 + 2 (q=9, r=2). - 5 ÷ 14 → 5 = 14×0 + 5 (q=0, r=5). - 12 ÷ 3 → 12 = 3×4 + 0 (q=4, r=0). - 2574 ÷ 8 → 2574 = 8×321 + 6 (q=321, r=6).

✍️ Must-know: a = bq + r with 0 ≤ r < b. The remainder is always less than the divisor. If r = 0, a is divisible by b.

2.1.2 Prime and Composite Numbers

  • Prime number: exactly two distinct factors (1 and itself). E.g., 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 53.
  • Composite number: more than two factors. E.g., 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 18.
  • 1 is neither prime nor composite.
  • 2 is the only even prime.
  • Factors of a number are always ≤ the number.

Example: 18 = 1×18 = 2×9 = 3×6, so 18 is composite. 53 = 1×53 only, so 53 is prime.

2.1.3 Divisibility Test

A number is divisible by: - 2 if its unit (last) digit is divisible by 2 (even). - 3 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 3. - 4 if the number formed by its last two digits is divisible by 4. - 5 if its unit digit is 0 or 5. - 6 if it is divisible by both 2 and 3. - 8 if the number formed by its last three digits is divisible by 8. - 9 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 9. - 10 if its unit digit is 0.

Example: 2,334: divisible by 2 (unit digit 4 even), by 3 (sum 12), by 6 (÷2 and ÷3); NOT divisible by 4 (34 not ÷4), 5, 8, 9 (sum 12), or 10.

Prime Factorization & Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

  • Prime factorization: expressing a composite number as a product of primes.
  • Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic: every composite number can be factored into primes in a unique way (apart from order).

Examples: - 6 = 2×3 - 30 = 2×3×5 - 72 = 2³×3² - 456 = 2³×3×19 (456÷2=228, ÷2=114, ÷2=57, ÷3=19)

2.1.4 GCF and LCM

  • Common factor: a factor of each of several numbers.
  • GCF / HCF: the greatest common factor. Use prime factorization and take common primes with the least power.
  • LCM: the least common multiple. Take each prime with its highest power.
  • GCF × LCM = a × b for two numbers a, b.

Examples: - GCF(36, 56): 36 = 2²·3², 56 = 2³·7 → common prime 2 with least power 2² → GCF = 4. - GCF(12, 18, 36): 12=2²·3, 18=2·3², 36=2²·3² → GCF = 2·3 = 6. - LCM(6, 10, 16): 6=2·3, 10=2·5, 16=2⁴ → LCM = 2⁴·3·5 = 240.

✍️ Must-know: GCF = common primes with smallest powers; LCM = all primes with biggest powers; and GCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × b.
