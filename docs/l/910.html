4.3 Types of Asexual Reproduction

4.3.1 Fission

  • The organism divides into two (binary) or more (multiple) equal parts.
  • Binary fission: common among bacteria, algae, protozoa. The unicellular parent divides by mitosis into two approximately equal parts; the bacterial nucleus is replicated first so each daughter gets one copy. Rapid — some bacteria divide every 20–30 minutes, forming a bacterial colony.
  • Multiple fission: the nucleus divides repeatedly; each daughter nucleus breaks away with a small portion of cytoplasm, producing many daughter cells (common among some parasitic protozoa, e.g., malarial parasites).

4.3.2 Fragmentation

  • Breakdown of a parent organism into parts that develop into whole organisms.
  • Observed in fungi, plants, animals, algae.
  • Example: Spirogyra (filamentous green algae) fragments into many filaments, each growing into a mature filament. Multicellular animals (worms) can break into parts, each regenerating into a complete individual.

4.3.3 Budding

  • The organism divides into two unequal parts; common in fungal species and invertebrates (hydra).
  • A bulge (bud) forms on the side of the cell; the nucleus divides mitotically; one daughter nucleus migrates into the bud; the bud detaches from the mother cell.
  • Example: yeast buds on the surface of the cell or hypha.

Sporulation

  • Most fungi reproduce asexually by forming spores. Spores disperse by air currents and grow new hyphae → mycelium. A swelling at the tip (sporangium — spore sac) forms. Examples: Penicillium and Mucor moulds.

4.3.4 Vegetative Propagation

  • A method of asexual reproduction in plants where structures with lateral meristems (roots, stems, buds, leaves) give rise to new self-supporting individuals.

Natural vegetative propagation:

  • Stolons (runners): grow along the soil surface; roots, flowers, leaves produced at nodes (e.g., cultivated strawberry).
  • Rhizomes: horizontal underground stems; buds at nodes produce shoots (e.g., couch grass).
  • Corms: rounded, fleshy, similar to rhizomes; store food (e.g., gladiolus).
  • Tubers: modified stems that store starch (e.g., potato, Solanum); swollen ends of stolons with buds.
  • Bulb: modification of a stem with enlarged fleshy leaves (underground storage unit, e.g., onion).

Artificial vegetative propagation:

  • Grafting: combines favourable stem and root characteristics; the stem to be grafted = scion, the root = stock.
  • Cutting: a portion of stem with nodes/internodes placed in moist soil (e.g., hibiscus); some leaves root in water (crotons).
  • Layering (runner): a stem attached to the plant is bent and covered with soil.
  • Also marcotting and micropropagation.

4.3.5 Parthenogenesis

  • "Virgin birth" — a form of asexual reproduction in which an unfertilized egg develops into an adult organism.
  • Example: honeybees (Apis mellifera) — unfertilized eggs develop into males (drones). Whip tail lizard (Aspidoscelis uniparens) produces only females via parthenogenesis (each is a clone of its mother).
✍️ Must-know: Fission = divides into 2+ equal parts; Budding = divides into 2 unequal parts; Fragmentation = breaks into parts; Parthenogenesis = unfertilized egg develops; Vegetative propagation = plant parts give rise to new plants.
