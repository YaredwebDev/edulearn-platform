15.6 Malnutrition and Its Causes in Ethiopia

◌ Not started

Malnutrition = a condition when the diet doesn't contain the right amount of nutrients. Two main types: under-nutrition (lack of healthy food) and over-nutrition (excessive unhealthy intake). Forms: - Obesity / overweight — excess nutrient intake relative to requirements. Clinically overweight when body weight >20% above the recommended for height. Risk of chronic illness (diabetes, heart disease, cancers). - Stunting — chronic/recurrent under-nutrition (poor diet, repeated infection, inadequate psychosocial stimulation); height-for-age below average for age. - Wasting — indicator of acute/short-term under-nutrition; child very thin for height & sex (often from lack of food, infection, diarrhoea). Severe wasting → child mortality. Defined as weight-for-height <-2 SD (WHO standard). - Being underweight — indicator of both acute & chronic under-nutrition; low weight-for-age; body mass 15% below normal range. Weight-for-age <-2 SD (WHO). - Micronutrient deficiency — deficiency of vitamins/minerals. Main ones of global concern: iron deficiency (anaemia), vitamin A deficiency (night blindness), iodine deficiency (goitre), zinc (poor growth), calcium (weak bone), folic acid (neural tube defect).

Body Mass Index (BMI)

  • BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²). High BMI indicates high body fatness. BMI screens weight categories that may lead to health problems (does not diagnose body fatness/health). For adults simple BMI works (most over 18 completed physical development); children/adolescents still growing → consider age & sex.
  • BMI ranges: 18.5–25 normal; 25–30 overweight; >30 obese; <18.5 thin/underweight.

Magnitude of malnutrition in Ethiopia

Malnutrition is among the major public-health problems, affecting mainly children, pregnant & lactating women. Child malnutrition trend (under 5):

Indicator 2000 2005 2011 2016 2019 Status
Stunting 57.7% 50.8% 44.4% 38.4% 36.8% Very high
Wasting 12.2% 12.2% 9.7% 9.9% 7.0% Medium
Underweight 41% 33% 29% 23.3% 21% High
Overweight 1.8% 4.0% 1.7% 2.8% 2.3% –

(EDHS) — note the overall declining trend in stunting & underweight from 2000 to 2019.

Causes of malnutrition (three levels)

  1. Immediate causes: lack of access to sufficient quality/quantity of food; poor health status & frequent infectious diseases.
  2. Underlying causes: household food insecurity; sub-optimal feeding & caring practices and health services; WASH (Water Access, Sanitation & Hygiene) practices.
  3. Basic causes: political, financial, social, cultural & environmental conditions; infrastructure.

Impacts of malnutrition

Multifaceted, at individual/community/region-country level, short & long term. Short-term: weakness & altered immunity → more vulnerable to infections & death (one-third of child mortality attributed to malnutrition worldwide). Long-term: impaired mental development, poor educational performance, decreased adult productivity; low birth weight tracks into adulthood raising risk of diabetes & cardiovascular disease; malnourished people fail to recover & perform work → poor economic status for individuals, societies & country.

Mechanisms for solving malnutrition

  • Educating communities about the dangers of malnutrition.
  • Promoting improved & nutrition-sensitive agricultural practices.
  • Prioritising infants, pregnant & lactating women for nutrition-based interventions.
  • Adopting dietary changes — eating a variety of nutrient-rich foods.
  • Promoting diversified, nutrient-dense, safe foods.
  • Promoting animal-source foods, fruits, vegetables, bio-fortified & fortified foods, iodized salt.
  • Supporting families to manage factors affecting children's nutritional intake.
  • Timely treating underlying medical conditions causing malnutrition.

✍️ Memorise energy shares recommended by the text summary: carbohydrates ~55%, fats ~30%, proteins ~15%. BMI ranges and the four food-security pillars are frequent exam items.
