2.4 Molecular Geometry: VSEPR Theory

◌ Not started

VSEPR (Valence-Shell Electron-Pair Repulsion): electron pairs (bonding and lone) around a central atom repel and arrange themselves as far apart as possible. Lone pairs repel more strongly than bonding pairs.

✍️ Must-know repulsion order: lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bonding pair > bonding pair–bonding pair. So a lone pair compresses the bond angles below the ideal value.

Ideal shapes & angles (as in the textbook VSEPR table): - AB₂ (2 bp, no lp) → linear, 180° (e.g. BeCl₂, CO₂). - AB₃ (3 bp) → trigonal planar, 120° (BF₃). - AB₂E (2 bp + 1 lp) → bent, <120° (SO₂). - AB₄ (4 bp) → tetrahedral, 109.5° (CH₄). - AB₃E (3 bp + 1 lp) → trigonal pyramidal, ~107° (NH₃). - AB₂E₂ (2 bp + 2 lp) → bent, ~104.5° (H₂O). - AB₅ (5 bp) → trigonal bipyramidal, 90°/120° (PCl₅). - AB₄E (4 bp + 1 lp) → seesaw, lone pair sits equatorial (SF₄). - AB₆ (6 bp) → octahedral, 90° (SF₆); AB₅E → square pyramidal (IF₅, 90°).

VSEPR guidelines to apply: draw the Lewis structure; count electron groups around the central atom; treat double/triple bonds as one group; place lone pairs as far apart; a single unshared pair causes a small angle compression.

✍️ Must-know numerical angles: NH₃ ≈ 107°, H₂O ≈ 104.5°, SO₂ slightly under 120°, ideal sp³ = 109.5°.

Molecular polarity from shape: the same dipole-moment ideas above are applied after the geometry is known (NH₃ polar, CCl₄ non-polar, etc.).
