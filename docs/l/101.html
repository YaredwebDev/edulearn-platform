16.2 Dietary Diversification Strategies

◌ Not started
  • Dietary diversity = the number of different food items/food groups consumed over a given time. Low dietary diversity is a particular problem in Ethiopia, where the diet is often based on starchy staples (teff, maize, sorghum, enset, wheat) and lacks animal-source foods (meat, fish, eggs, dairy) and fruits & vegetables.
  • Strategies for food & dietary diversification at community/household level:
  • Promotion of mixed cropping & integrated farming systems.
  • Introduction of new crops of high nutritional importance (e.g. soybean).
  • Encouraging use of underexploited traditional foods & home gardens.
  • Fostering urban farming of high-nutrition crops (deep-coloured fruits, green leafy vegetables).
  • Encouraging small animals (goats, sheep, poultry) and fish farming.
  • Advancing improved preservation & storage of fruits/vegetables (reduce wastage, post-harvest loss, seasonality effects).
  • Strengthening small-scale agro-processing & food industries.
  • Encouraging income generation at household level.
  • Nutrition education: complementary feeding (food in addition to breast milk for children 6–23 months), food quality & safety, hygiene & sanitation.
