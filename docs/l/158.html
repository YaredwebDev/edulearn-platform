6.7 Conservation of Natural Resources in Ethiopia

◌ Not started
  • Conservation = the care, protection, and wise use of natural resources so they can be used by future generations; also the preservation, management or restoration of natural environments/ecological communities. Aims to manage resources for current public benefit and sustainable social & economic use.
  • Growing population, rapid industrialization and urbanization have increased demand and caused depletion, so conservation is crucial.

Wildlife in Ethiopia: huge potential of national/global importance. Wildlife = living things in the natural environment not yet domesticated (here, mainly animals). Importance: maintain natural ecological processes, store genetic material for the future, secure wildlife tourism, contribute to the national economy, and manufacture goods. Examples: Lion, Stressemann's Bushcrow, Walia Ibex, Ethiopian Mountain Chameleon. - Causes of wildlife loss/decline: natural disasters (earthquakes, floods, droughts, tornadoes, wildfire) and human activities (agricultural expansion, development, deforestation, poaching, illegal wildlife trade, habitat change, climate change, overexploitation, pollution). - Conservation mechanisms: wildlife conservation = protecting wildlife & their habitats, based on conservation areas. Ethiopia has established 24 national parks, 2 wild sanctuaries, 6 wildlife reserves, 5 community conservation areas, and 10+ controlled hunting areas. (Sample parks & years: Abijata-Shala Lakes 1963, Awash 1958, Bale Mountains 1962, Gambella 1966, Mago 1984, Omo 1959, Simien Mountains 1959, Yangudi Rassa 1969, etc. Sanctuaries: Babile Elephant 1962, Senkele Swayne's Hartebeest 1964.)

Plants in Ethiopia: diverse with rich endemic elements — a centre for crop genetic diversity. Examples: Mekmeko (Rumex abyssinicus), African juniper (Juniperus procera), Cabbage tree (Moringa stenopetala), Kosso (Hagenia abyssinica). - Causes of plant loss/decline: same natural disasters + deforestation, climate change, invasive species, overexploitation, pollution, land degradation. - Conservation mechanisms: - Indigenous practices (long-standing traditions/ways of life): taboos/forbidden practices, domestication, reserves, selective harvesting, sacred groves (church & mosque compounds, graveyards, monasteries protect indigenous plants), traditional farming & agroforestry systems preserving genetic diversity. - Modern practices: botanical gardens, controlling invasive species, recovery/restoration/preservation of genes in a bank, and preservation of seeds in the Ethiopian national gene bank. - In situ conservation (on-site, in natural habitats/areas) vs ex situ conservation (off-site, e.g., gene banks, zoos, botanical gardens).

Soil and water in Ethiopia: wide climate/topography/parent material produces varied soils and surface/groundwater. Water resources support hydropower (e.g., the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam/GERD on the Abay/Nile River), irrigation, and fishing. Problems: soil erosion and decline in water quantity/quality (causes: deforestation, poor land husbandry, industrial/home wastes). - Conservation mechanisms — mechanical: bunds, terraces, diversion ditches, check dams, micro-basins, hillside terraces. - Conservation mechanisms — biological: enclosing degraded land, tree-seedling production, planting on farmland (agroforestry), afforestation, tree plantations around homesteads. The Green Legacy initiative (launched June 2019) aims to build a green, climate-resilient Ethiopia.
