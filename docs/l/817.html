3.5 Carbohydrates

◌ Not started
  • Made of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen; ratio of H:O is 2:1.
  • Functions: energy source, energy storage, cell-membrane components, structural components.
  • Three groups: monosaccharides, disaccharides, polysaccharides.

a. Monosaccharides (one sugar unit) - General formula (CH₂O)ₙ (or CₙH₂ₙOₙ); C:H:O = 1:2:1. - By number of carbons: trioses (3C), pentoses (5C), hexoses (6C). - Hexose formula: C₆H₁₂O₆. - Glucose, galactose, and fructose are isomers (same formula, different structure). - Roles: energy source in respiration (C–H bonds release energy to make ATP from ADP); building blocks for starch, glycogen, cellulose; ribose/deoxyribose used in RNA/DNA/ATP.

b. Disaccharides (two monosaccharides) - Maltose = glucose + glucose (product of starch breakdown by amylase) - Lactose = glucose + galactose (milk sugar) - Sucrose = glucose + fructose (transport sugar in plants, table sugar) - General formula C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁.

c. Polysaccharides (many glucose units) — storage & structural - Starch: plant's stored sugar (in roots, seeds); broken down to glucose. - Glycogen: animal storage form; highly branched; stored in liver and muscle; animal analogue of starch. - Cellulose: plant cell walls; provides structural support; every other glucose monomer is densely packed → rigidity/strength; dietary fibre (humans can't digest; herbivores digest with help of cellulase from rumen bacteria).

Food tests: - Benedict's test (reducing sugars): blue copper(II) sulphate → brick-red copper(I) oxide precipitate when reducing sugar present (all monosaccharides, maltose). Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar. - Iodine test (starch/glycogen): iodine turns starch blue, dextrin reddish-purple.

Functions of carbohydrates: primary energy source; storage (starch/glycogen); cell-membrane components for intercellular communication; structural components (cell walls, chitin in fungi/exoskeleton of insects).
