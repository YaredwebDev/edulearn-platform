7D.1 Context clues — types

Context clues are hints within a sentence/paragraph/passage used to understand new words. The main categories: - Definition — the term is defined/explained (clues: that is, commas, dashes, parentheses): His emaciation, that is, his skeleton-like appearance, was frightening. - Synonym — a word with the same meaning nearby: The math test was arduous; …the difficult exam… - Antonym — a word of opposite meaning nearby: the easy grammar test…but the arduous math test… - Example — illustrations of the word: amphibious creatures such as frogs and turtles. - General sense of the sentence — infer from overall meaning. - Practise: mitigate, indigenous, lucid, vociferous, impudent, incessant, implausible, surfeit, emaciated, complexity; and in the Diborah passage: precocious, flabbergasted, dexterity, gloated, agape.

7D.2 Contextual phrasal verbs

  • pull ahead (move in front), point out (indicate), point up (highlight/emphasize), pull out (of) (withdraw from), pull back (retreat/withdraw), pull in (earn money; also a train arriving), pull off (remove; succeed in doing), pull away (move ahead of competition), print out (produce a printed copy).
