2.1 Position and Displacement

◌ Not started

Position: the location of an object relative to a reference point (origin/frame of reference). It can be negative or positive. Units: cm, m, km. - A frame of reference is an arbitrary set of axes from which position and motion are described.

Distance (scalar): the total length of the path travelled between initial and final positions.

Displacement (vector): the difference between the initial and final position vectors — the shortest straight-line distance from start to finish, with a direction. Δs⃗ = s − s₀

Key fact: The magnitude of displacement can be less than or equal to the distance, but never greater. Distance is the path length; displacement is the straight-line change in position.

Example 2.1: A person walks 70 m East, then 30 m West. - Displacement = 70 − 30 = 40 m East - Total distance = 70 + 30 = 100 m

Example: O→A (60 km), A→B back (25 km): distance = 85 km, displacement = 35 km. Returning to O: distance = 120 km, displacement = 0.
