6.4 Surface Area and Volume of Composed Solids

◌ Not started

A composed solid is made of two or more solids. Volume = sum of the parts' volumes. Surface area = sum of exposed surfaces (be careful not to count hidden shared faces).

Example (flask = frustum of cone + cylinder): frustum radii 4 & 8, slant height l=√(4²+16²)=4√17; cylinder radius 8, height 14. - TSA = frustum LSA (48√17π) + frustum lower base (64π) + cylinder LSA (2π·8·14=112π) = 16π(3√17+11). - Volume = frustum volume (⅓π·16·(64+16+32)=1792π/3) + cylinder volume (π·64·14=896π) = 4480π/3.

Example (cone + cylinder): cone radius 4 height 3 (l=5), cylinder radius 4 height 7. - TSA = cone LSA(20π) + cylinder LSA(56π) + cylinder lower base(16π) = 92π. - Volume = cylinder (π·16·7=112π) + cone (⅓π·16·3=16π) = 128π.

Example (drilled): cylinder radius 10, height 12, drilled a triangular prism (3-4-5 right triangle). TSA=2πrh+ph+2πr²−2(½ab)=240π+144+200π−12=(440π+132)≈1513.6. Volume=1200π−½·3·4·12=1200π−72≈3696.

✍️ Must-know: Break into known solids, compute each, sum. For TSA count only exposed faces; for drilled/removed parts subtract.
