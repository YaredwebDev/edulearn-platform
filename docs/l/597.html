6.4 Properties of Regular Polygons: Pentagon, Hexagon, Octagon, Decagon

◌ Not started

Lines of symmetry

  • A line of symmetry is a line through which the two halves of a figure match exactly (mirror line).
  • An n-sided regular polygon has n lines of symmetry.
  • The lines of symmetry meet at the center of the polygon.
  • If n is odd, lines connect vertex to side; if n is even, lines connect vertex to vertex and side to side.

Example: A regular heptagon (7 sides) has 7 lines of symmetry.

Inscribed and circumscribed polygons

  • Circumcircle: the circle passing through all vertices (polygon is inscribed in it).
  • Incircle: the circle inside the polygon touching each side at its midpoint.
  • Apothem (a): the perpendicular segment from the center to the midpoint of a side.
  • A circle can always be inscribed in / circumscribed about any regular polygon.

Central angle

  • The central angle is the angle at the center formed by two radii to adjacent vertices.
  • Each central angle = 360° / n.
  • The central angles divide the polygon into n congruent isosceles triangles.

Examples: n = 5 → 72°; n = 8 → 45°; if central angle = 12°, then n = 360/12 = 30 sides.

Perimeter, area, and apothem of a regular polygon inscribed in a circle of radius r

Theorem 6.1: For an n-sided regular polygon inscribed in a circle of radius r: - s = 2r sin(180°/n) (side length) - a = r cos(180°/n) (apothem) - P = 2nr sin(180°/n) (perimeter) - A = ½ aP (area)

Example — regular hexagon (n=6): central angle = 60°, so triangle AOB is equilateral: s = r, P = 6r. Apothem a = r cos 30° = (√3/2)r. Area = 6 × (√3/4)r² = (3√3/2)r².

Example 2: Square inscribed in radius 5 cm: s = 2(5) sin 45° = 5√2 cm.

Example 2b: Apothem of regular pentagon, radius 6 cm: a = 6 cos 36° ≈ 6×0.809 = 4.854 cm.

Example 2c: Area of regular nonagon, radius 10 cm: A = 9(100) cos 20° sin 20° ≈ 289.24 cm².

✍️ Must-know: s = 2r sin(180°/n), a = r cos(180°/n), P = 2nr sin(180°/n), A = ½ aP. The central angle = 360°/n.
