6.3 Farm Animal Reproductive Technologies

◌ Not started

Reproduction techniques make breeding safe and efficient; they disseminate genes of interest and increase reproductive capacity. Examples: Artificial Insemination (AI) and Embryo Transfer (ET).

6.3.1 Artificial Insemination (AI)

  • AI = collecting sperm from a male and depositing it into a female's reproductive tract (vagina, cervix or uterus) by a method other than natural mating. The most important reproduction technology for genetic improvement in domestic animals.
  • Procedure: semen is collected hygienically from superior males; semen (spermatozoa) is handled, diluted and stored in a freezer at −196 °C in liquid nitrogen (in a liquid-nitrogen tank) until needed. Effective AI needs accurate herd supervision & heat (oestrus) detection.
  • Signs of oestrus/heat in females: loss of appetite; constant vocalisations; restlessness; rubbing against herd-mates; vulva lips redder & more moist; swelling around the vulva; thin mucous discharge from the vulva; stops when mounted; mounts other animals.
  • AI technique: the safe/best method in cattle is the recto-vaginal method. Inseminate in a sheltered area (avoid wind, dust, rain); instruments sterile; cow in heat controlled in a crush; the inseminator (apron, gumboots, gloves) thaws the semen straw in tepid water (~1 min), loads a sterilised AI gum covered with a plastic sheath, and guides the cervix through the rectal wall.
  • Advantages of AI: wider variety of superior males used; many more females can be bred to one superior male; reduces spread of infectious diseases (contagious abortion, vibriosis); no need to keep a male on the farm (semen from AI centres); accurate breeding & parturition records; old/heavy/injured males can be used.
  • Disadvantages/limitations: needs well-trained operators; specialist equipment/facilities; more time than natural service; improper cleaning/unhygienic conditions lower fertility; if the bull isn't properly tested, genital diseases can spread.

6.3.2 Embryo Transfer (ET)

  • ET = an artificial method whereby newly formed embryos (before implantation) are removed from a donor female and transferred into the reproductive tract of a recipient of the same species. Donors are highly productive/superior; recipients are inferior in productivity. Powerful tool for genetic improvement; disseminates desirable genes from superior females (horses, cattle, sheep, goats, pigs).
  • ET process in cattle: donor cows get hormone treatment to produce more than one ovulation at a time → artificially inseminated with superior bulls → ~7 days later a veterinarian recovers embryos with a catheter + recovery fluid through a filter → embryos evaluated under a microscope (average ~6 good embryos per cow) → transferred into a surrogate/recipient to carry to term, or frozen for later use.
  • Advantages of ET: quickly multiplies offspring of top females; easier/faster genetic exchange between countries; no transport of live animals; reduces disease-transmission risk; enhances storage & expansion of rare genetic stock.
  • Disadvantages of ET: high cost; skilled technicians needed; may be time-consuming.

✍️ Must-know (AI vs ET): AI exploits superior males (semen at −196 °C in liquid N₂; most important reproductive technology). ET exploits superior females (embryos from donor to recipient/surrogate). AI reduces infectious-disease spread & needs no male on farm; ET enables no-live-animal transport and faster cross-country gene exchange.
