3.1.2 Inorganic Ions

◌ Not started

Inorganic ions are charged entities (atoms with unshared outer-shell electrons). When discussed in the human body they are often called minerals.

Classification by daily requirement: - Macronutrients: required in larger amounts — Sodium, Magnesium, Potassium, Chloride, Phosphorus, Sulfur. - Micronutrients: required in trace amounts — Iron, Copper, Iodine, Manganese.

Key inorganic ions

a. Hydrogen ions (H⁺) - Maintain balance and equilibrium; found in cytoplasm, intercellular fluid, blood, CSF. - Concentration expressed as pH (scale 1–14); the higher the pH, the lower the H⁺ concentration (inverse relationship).

b. Sodium ions (Na⁺) - Most important extracellular cation; higher concentration in extracellular fluid than in cells. - Requirement: 3–6 g/day (as common salt); excess is removed in urine; lower salt intake needed for high blood pressure.

c. Potassium ions (K⁺) - Major intracellular cation (higher inside cells). - Requirement: 3–4 g/day; sources: bananas, oranges, potatoes, chicken, liver. - An electrolyte (carries electric charge when dissolved); necessary for normal cell, nerve, and muscle function.

d. Calcium ions (Ca²⁺) - Most abundant inorganic ion; mainly in cytoplasm, bones, teeth. - Requirement: ~800 mg/day; sources: milk and milk products, leafy vegetables, egg yolk, fish, beans. - Calcium = 1–1.5 kg of body weight; 99% in bones and teeth, 1% in cells/extracellular fluid.

e. Phosphate ions (PO₄³⁻) - Negatively charged; ~1 kg in the body, mostly with calcium in bones and teeth. - Requirement: ~800 mg/day (like calcium); sources: milk, cereals, meat, eggs.

f. Chloride ions (Cl⁻) - Negatively charged, in extracellular fluids; works with sodium. - Requirement: 5–10 g/day. - Maintains osmotic pressure, fluid balance, acid–base balance; makes HCl for gastric juice (digests protein, kills pathogens); activates salivary amylase.

g. Iron ions (Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺) - ~70% in haemoglobin in red blood cells; sources: beans, red meat, liver, spinach, pumpkin seeds, fish. - Needed for O₂ and CO₂ transport (haemoglobin, myoglobin), cytochromes (electron transport chain), peroxidase enzyme. - Deficiency diseases: iron-deficiency anemia (too little iron), hemosiderosis (excess iron), hemochromatosis (abnormal iron deposits in liver, spleen, skin, pancreas).

h. Copper ions (Cu²⁺) - Sources: shellfish, seeds/nuts, organ meats, wheat, chocolate. - Needed for synthesis of haemoglobin, collagen, elastin, and normal nervous-system development. - Wilson disease: abnormal copper metabolism → copper deposits in liver (cirrhosis) and brain (damage); kidney deposits → renal failure.

i. Iodine ions (I⁻) - Sources: iodized salt, dairy, seafood, plants grown in iodine-rich soil. - Vital for thyroid health; used to make thyroid hormones. - Deficiency → hypothyroidism, underactive thyroid → goiter (enlarged thyroid). Hyperthyroidism = overactive thyroid. - Requirement: 150 µg/day for adolescents and adults.

✍️ Must-know diseases: Goiter = iodine deficiency; Anemia = iron deficiency; Wilson disease = copper metabolism.
