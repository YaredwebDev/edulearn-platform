4C Reading Skills: Indigenous Forest Conservation

◌ Not started

Key facts (FAO/FILAC report, Forest Governance by Indigenous and Tribal Peoples): - Indigenous & tribal peoples play vital roles in climate action & fighting poverty; deforestation is significantly lower in indigenous/tribal territories where collective land rights are formally recognised. - Amazon Basin: indigenous/tribal territories lost 0.17% of forest carbon/year (2003–2016) vs 0.53%/year outside indigenous territories & protected areas (0.36% more). - Improving tenure security of these territories is an efficient & cost-effective way to reduce carbon emissions. - Indigenous forestry practices: assisted forest regeneration, selective harvesting, reforesting & assisted growth of trees within existing forests. - These peoples have great culture/knowledge/natural resources but lowest incomes & poorest access to services; were among the worst hit by COVID-19. - The FAO proposal is built on six pillars: recognition of collective territorial rights; compensation for environmental services; community forest management; revitalization of ancestral knowledge; strengthening of grassroots organisations & territorial-governance mechanisms. - The Global Assessment Report notes biodiversity decline is lower where indigenous people own land, yet indigenous knowledge is often absent from global conservation approaches. - The report calls for a new relationship with indigenous peoples and allocating resources to revitalise their "intangible wealth of cultures and ancestral knowledge" (significance heightened after the Odisha/Similipal forest fires).

✍️ Reading skill: answer true/false & multiple choice from the text; identify pronoun references (their/these/it/these people); group-discuss and write a short report on local forests (carbon, land ownership, conservation mechanisms).
