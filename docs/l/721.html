1.2 Vector Representations

◌ Not started

Vectors are represented algebraically or geometrically. - Algebraically: by a bold letter (A) or with an arrow over the letter (A⃗). The magnitude is written |S| or S ≡ |S|. - Geometrically: by an arrow (an arrow-tipped line segment). - The length of the arrow represents the magnitude (drawn to scale). - The arrow head represents the direction. - The initial point of the arrow is the tail; the final point is the head.

Steps to draw a vector graphically: 1. Decide on a scale and write it down (e.g., 1 cm = 2 N). 2. Determine the arrow length using the scale. 3. Draw the arrow with a filled-in arrow head. 4. Write the magnitude.

Types of vectors: 1. Zero (null) vector: zero magnitude, no direction. 2. Unit vector: magnitude equal to one. 3. Equal vectors: same magnitude and same direction. 4. Negative of a vector: same magnitude, opposite direction.

Example 1.1: Draw 16 km East using a scale. If 1 cm = 4 km, then 16 km = 4 cm, pointing East.
