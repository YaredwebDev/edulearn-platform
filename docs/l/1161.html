5.4 Theorems on Angles and Arcs Determined by Lines Intersecting Inside, on and Outside a Circle

Theorem 5.10 (Chords intersecting inside): The measure of the angle formed between two intersecting chords equals ½ the sum of the intercepted arcs.

Two secants meeting outside a circle

Example: m(arcPQ)=68°, m(arcRS)=128°; chords PR and QS intersect at T → m∠RTS=½(68°+128°)=98°.

Theorem 5.11 (Secants & tangents intersecting outside): The angle formed by two secants, two tangents, or a secant and a tangent meeting outside the circle equals ½ the positive difference of the intercepted arcs.

  • Two secants: x=½[m(arcCE)−m(arcBD)].
  • Two tangents: x=½[m(arcBDC)−m(arcBC)].

Example: m(arcBD)=43°, m(arcCE)=107° → m∠CAE=½(107°−43°)=32°.

Additional facts:

  • Product of segments of intersecting chords: AP·PB = CP·PD.
  • Tangent line meets the circle at one point and is ⊥ to the radius to that point.
  • Tangents from an external point are equal in length.
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Chords inside → angle = ½(sum of arcs). Secants/tangents outside → angle = ½(difference of arcs). Tangent ⊥ radius at point of contact.
