3.4 The Young Modulus

◌ Not started

Young modulus (Y) = ratio of tensile (or compressive) stress to longitudinal strain: Y = (F⊥/A) / (ΔL/L₀) - Unit: N/m² or Pascal (Pa) — same as stress, since strain is dimensionless. - For a wire of radius r hanging with mass m: A = πr², F = mg, so Y = (mg × L₀)/(πr²ΔL) - Measures the resistance of a solid to a change in its length. - Relevant only for solids.

Young modulus values (N/m²): Tungsten 35×10¹⁰; Steel 20×10¹⁰; Copper 11×10¹⁰; Brass 9.1×10¹⁰; Aluminium 7.0×10¹⁰; Glass 6.5–7.8×10¹⁰. - Steel has the largest value among common metals → steel is the most elastic and is preferred in heavy-duty machines and structural designs. - Wood, bone, concrete, glass have small Young moduli.

Example 3.5: Steel piano wire L₀=1.6 m, d=0.20 cm, stretches 0.25 cm, Y=2×10¹¹ N/m². A = π(0.0010)² = 3.14×10⁻⁶ m². F = Y(ΔL/L₀)A = 2×10¹¹(0.0025/1.6)(3.14×10⁻⁶) = 980 N.

Example 3.6: Sphere m=30 kg on steel wire L=15 m, A=9×10⁻⁶ m², Y=200×10⁹ N/m². - Stress = mg/A = (30×9.8)/9×10⁻⁶ = 3.27×10⁷ N/m² - ΔL = (1/Y)(F/A)L₀ = 3.27×10⁷/200×10⁹ × 15 = 2.45×10⁻³ m = 2.45 mm

✍️ Must-know: Y = (F/A)/(ΔL/L₀). Steel has the highest Young modulus among common metals (most elastic).
