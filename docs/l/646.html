5.3 Heat Capacity and Specific Heat Capacity

◌ Not started
  • Heat capacity (C) = amount of heat required to raise the temperature of an object by 1 °C (or 1 K). C = Q/ΔT. SI unit J/K (1 J/K = 1 J/°C).
  • Specific heat capacity (c) = amount of energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kilogram of a substance by 1 °C (or 1 K). c = Q/(m ΔT), so Q = mcΔT. SI unit J/(kg·°C) or J/(kg·K).

✍️ Must-know formula: Q = m c ΔT (heat gained/lost = mass × specific heat × temperature change).

Table values (illustrative): water has a notably high specific heat (~4186 J/kg·°C); metals (aluminium ~900, iron, copper, gold ~129, lead, silver) have lower values; brass, glass, carbon/graphite also listed. Mercury and alcohol are given for liquids; steam for a gas.

✋ Trap: "higher specific heat" means it takes more energy to raise its temperature → it warms up and cools down more slowly. Water's high c explains moderate coastal climates (oceans store huge heat) and why land heats faster than water, and why a city (concrete) stays warmer at night than surrounding countryside (soil) — concrete has higher specific heat than soil.

Worked examples: - Example 5.1: raise 600 g of lead from 20°C to 100°C ⇒ Q = mcΔT. - Review: heat to raise 100 g gold 20→100°C (c_gold = 129 J/kg·°C); 0.80 kg aluminium pan heating 0.20 L water 25→85°C; % of heat used to heat the pan; temperature rise of a 50 kg steel mass falling 50 m with 50% of KE → heat.
