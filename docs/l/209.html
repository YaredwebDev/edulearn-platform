5.6 Malnutrition

◌ Not started
  • Malnutrition: the insufficient, excessive, or imbalanced consumption of nutrients, which leads to health problems.
  • Causes: poverty, famine (drought/flood), soil erosion, wars, too little land for too many people, ignorance of proper nutritional requirements.
  • Dual burden of malnutrition: undernutrition + overweight/obesity + diet-related non-communicable diseases.

Under nutrition (most common form — lack of protein and dietary energy): - Wasting (low weight-for-height): recent and severe weight loss; higher risk of death in children if untreated. - Stunting (low height-for-age): chronic/recurrent undernutrition; prevents reaching physical and cognitive potential. - Underweight (low weight-for-age). - Micronutrient deficiencies: lack of essential vitamins and minerals.

Over-nutrition / obesity: - When diet contains too much fat → coronary heart disease; fatty substances build up in arteries → blood clots → heart attack. - Obesity: excess food stored as fat. Caused by imbalance between energy stored and energy released (high-energy foods without equal physical activity, or decreased physical activity). - Health risks: heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, arthritis. - Controlled by: eating less high-energy food, more exercise, balanced diet with lower energy intake.

Body Mass Index (BMI): - BMI = body mass (kg) / height² (m²). - Status: below 18.5 = underweight; 18.5–24.9 = normal; 25.0–29.9 = overweight; 30.0 and above = obese. - Being 20% above the recommended weight also indicates obesity. - Developed by Adolphe Quetelet (Belgian). Note: muscular athletes may show high BMI but not be obese.

Example: BMI of 80 kg, 1.85 m tall: BMI = 80/(1.85)² = 80/3.4225 ≈ 23.4 → normal weight (not obese).

✍️ Must-know: BMI = weight(kg)/height²(m²). <18.5 underweight; 18.5–24.9 normal; 25–29.9 overweight; ≥30 obese. Wasting = low weight-for-height; stunting = low height-for-age; underweight = low weight-for-age.
