9F.1 Cohesive devices & thesis statement in expository texts

  • Expository text = nonfiction/informational text organised by the author's purpose/content, not by a story (news articles, textbooks, manuals). Text structures: cause & effect, concept & definition, sequential, proposition & support. The thesis statement states the topic and lists the key aspects to be discussed; it usually appears in the introduction (toward its end).
  • Cohesive devices connect ideas. Types for expository writing:
  • Addition: and, besides, furthermore, moreover, on top of that
  • Contrast: but, however, nevertheless, on the other hand, on the contrary, instead of
  • Comparison: likewise, similarly, correspondingly, in the same way
  • Emphasis: in fact, indeed, certainly, actually, as a matter of fact
  • Concession: though, although, despite/in spite of the fact
  • Examples: for example, for instance, in other words, that is
  • Reason: because, as, since, accordingly
  • Result: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, as a result of
  • Conclusion: in sum, in brief, in short, finally, in conclusion
  • Sequence: first, second, then, next, thereafter
  • Practice text: "Student's life" — write a thesis statement and identify cohesive devices (leisure time should contribute to a student's physical, mental and spiritual well-being; the activities: exercise & sport, artistic pursuits, relaxation).
  • Write an expository text on "The role of indigenous conflict resolution mechanisms": brainstorm → draft → redraft → write → edit; an introduction with the thesis toward the end; three body paragraphs linked by cohesive devices; one conclusion.

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) explain the concept & role of indigenous conflict resolution (from the listening), summarise a lecture, and predict/identify main ideas; (2) ask for clarification, apologise & accept apologies, ask for/give information with the right formality, and give an oral presentation; (3) state the reading's main ideas (PDRMs & their many names; Ethiopian history under Fetha Negast, Gadaa/Shimgillina; marginalisation in the 1950s–60s codification; current trend to synchronise PDRMs) and write a summary; (4) use synonyms/antonyms/homophones, idioms, and put phrasal verbs; (5) distinguish nominative vs accusative pronoun case and use past continuous vs past perfect continuous; and (6) write an expository essay with a clear thesis & cohesive devices? Try the PDRM reading questions, the pronoun-case & past-continuous exercises, and the expository text task.
