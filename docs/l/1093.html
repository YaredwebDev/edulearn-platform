3.3 Grammar

3.3.1 Modal Verbs

Modals are auxiliary verbs that add meaning such as ability, permission, obligation, possibility. They take the base form of the main verb (no "to").

ModalUseExample
canability; permission; possibilityShe can drive.
couldpast ability; polite request; possibilityCould I park here?
maypermission (formal); possibilityYou may go. It may rain.
mightpossibilityHe might be late.
shalloffers/suggestions; future (formal)Shall we leave?
shouldadvice/recommendationYou should wear a helmet.
mustobligationYou must stop at the red light.
willfuture; predictionThe driver will be fined.
wouldpolite request; habitWould you help me?
ought toadvice (with "to")You ought to obey the rules.
have toexternal obligationStudents have to wear seat belts.
need tonecessityYou need to check the brakes.
Note: ✋ Traps: (1) Modal + bare verb — "should go," never "should to go"; but ought to and have to / need to DO take "to." (2) must (from the speaker/own view) vs have to (external rule/law). (3) mustn't = not allowed; don't have to / needn't = not necessary.

3.3.2 do / does / don't / doesn't

  • do / don't → I, you, we, they (plural): "Do they obey the rules?" "They don't speed."
  • does / doesn't → he, she, it (singular): "Does she drive?" "He doesn't jump red lights."
  • Used for negatives and questions in the simple present.

3.3.3 Adverbs of Frequency (review)

always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never — say how often something happens; placed before the main verb, after be.

📝 Writing focus: Informal Letters

A personal/informal letter has this structure:

  1. Sender's address (top right) and date (below it).
  2. Salutation: "Dear ...," / "Hi ...,"
  3. Opening: ask how the reader is.
  4. Body: the main content (e.g. describe a trip, an accident, an experience).
  5. Closing: "Best wishes," "Your friend," "Yours," + signature.
  6. Friendly, conversational tone.
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: The textbook provides two model informal letters. Study their layout (address → date → greeting → body paragraphs → sign-off) and the friendly tone used.
