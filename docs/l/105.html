UNIT 16 · LESSON 6 OF 6 · GRADE 11 AGRICULTURE

🧮 Key facts to memorise

◌ Not started
  • Diversified food production → better dietary diversity & nutrition; eat ≥4 food groups/meal.
  • Integrated farming = crops + animals + fish synergy ("no waste").
  • Ethiopia's diet is staple-heavy (teff, maize, sorghum, enset, wheat), low in animal-source foods & fruits/veg → low dietary diversity.
  • Diversification strategies: mixed cropping, integrated systems, new crops (soybean), traditional foods/home gardens, urban farming, small animals & fish, better storage, agro-processing, income generation, nutrition education (complementary feeding 6–23 months).
  • Nutrient enrichment/modification = raise bioavailability; goal reduce micronutrient deficiencies.
  • Four techniques: fortification, germination, fermentation, roasting.
  • Fortification: staples & condiments; common fortified: cereals, milk products, fats/oils, tea/beverages, infant formula; cost-effective.
  • Germination: soak 8–16 h, sprout 12–24 h; vitamin B ~doubles, vitamin C ~10×; increases digestibility at no cost.
  • Fermentation: micro-organisms (yeast/bacteria); improves digestibility & mineral availability; Ethiopian examples milk curd, bread, Injera.
  • Roasting: dry heat seals juices, flavours fleshy food.
  • Indigenous knowledge: Chuko (barley flour + butter, Oromia); enset/Kocho (south); wild plants rich in minerals (bamboo shoot, stinging nettle, mushrooms).
  • Review answers: NOT a dietary diversification strategy = Mono-cropping (C); low-cost nutrient-enrichment technique = Germination (B); introducing/producing variety of food from agricultural activities = Food diversification (D); fortified foods = provide essential vitamins & minerals (A); all fermented in Ethiopia EXCEPT = Kita (C).
