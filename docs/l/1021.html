3.1 Reading: Punctuality (the core "story"/text)

The passage has four paragraphs and argues that punctuality is a vital life skill. Key points:

Paragraph 1 — Definition & importance:

  • Punctuality = doing something exactly at an appointed/scheduled time.
  • It is a quality of a duty-minded and disciplined person; reflects good image of work & people.
  • A life skill that contributes to success in every aspect of life.

Paragraph 2 — Punctuality shows respect:

  • Being on time for a meeting/event/class/party shows due respect to the people coordinating and participating.
  • In Ethiopia, many people arrive ten or more minutes late — an "internal clock lagging behind"; they apologise under the guise of the old platitude 'yehabesha ketero' (the socially-accepted Habesha norm of procrastination).
  • Late arrival is wrongly interpreted as a sign of importance, greatness, or business.
  • Scheduling and managing time is a quality of modern managers/executives; punctuality should be the culture of every citizen regardless of position or wealth.

Paragraph 3 — Punctuality builds reputation:

  • Punctual people are assumed to be duty-minded, responsible, and courteous; seen as diligent, civilized, disciplined.
  • A level-headed executive assigns responsibility to the committed/punctual person.
  • Executives must be role models; the proverb: "Procrastination is a thief of time."

Paragraph 4 — Punctuality conveys respect & recognition:

  • Promptness shows you recognise the importance of the meeting/work/assignment and respect the people.
  • Tardiness signals less attention to the work and its objectives.
  • An official would give added responsibilities to those who finish on time, not those who "throw something together at the last minute."
  • Progress, prosperity and civilisation are strongly connected with conscious time management and utilisation. "The more we develop the habit of punctuality, the better we achieve the goal we set earlier."
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Core argument = punctuality is (1) a life skill, (2) shows respect, (3) builds a responsible/diligent reputation, (4) is essential for progress. Note the Ethiopian cultural angle ('yehabesha ketero') and the proverb "Procrastination is a thief of time."
