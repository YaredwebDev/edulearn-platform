6.1 Electromagnetic (EM) Waves

◌ Not started
  • A wave transfers energy from one place to another without transferring matter.
  • Mechanical waves use matter to transfer energy and cannot travel through empty space (e.g., water waves, sound waves).
  • EM waves can travel through empty space or through matter. They are produced by charged particles (e.g., electrons) that vibrate back and forth.
  • An EM wave consists of oscillating electric and magnetic fields vibrating at right angles to the wave motion → EM waves are transverse waves.
  • An EM wave has a frequency (number of vibrations per second) and a wavelength (distance between crests/troughs).

Radiant energy from the Sun: energy carried by EM waves is called radiant energy. ~92% of the radiant energy reaching Earth is carried by infrared and visible light; a small amount is ultraviolet (causes sunburn).

✍️ Must-know: EM waves are transverse, made of vibrating electric and magnetic fields, and need no medium to travel.
