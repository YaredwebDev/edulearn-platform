3.2 Properties and Functions of Enzymes

◌ Not started

3.2.1 General properties of enzymes

Enzymes accelerate reaction rates; they neither affect the nature of the products nor are changed by the reaction.

A. Physical properties (Figure 3.2): denaturation, solubility, colloids, biocatalysts, precipitation, molecular weight, enzyme activity. - Denaturation — breaking intra- and inter-molecular non-covalent bonds that distort the enzyme's shape and active site. Enzymes are denatured by high heat (above ~40 °C), extreme pH (too low/high), heavy metals, high salt, solvents, other reagents. Once denatured they cannot be renatured (they lose activity permanently). Enzymes are thermolabile. - Solubility — enzymes dissolve in water, salt (NaCl), dilute glycerol and alcohol (which can cause denaturation). - Colloidal nature — enzymes are large/high-molecular-weight, so they show little/no dialysis across a semipermeable membrane. - Biocatalyst property — very small amounts of enzyme convert large quantities of substrate and remain unchanged. - Precipitation — separation of enzymes for analysis using aqueous or ethanol solvents. - Molecular weight — enzymes are large protein biomolecules holding polypeptide chains. - Enzyme activity — depends on temperature, pH, enzyme concentration and substrate concentration; enzymes show highest activity at optimum temperature and pH.

B. Chemical properties (Figure 3.3): sensitivity, regulation, specificity, catalysis, reversibility. - Heat & pH sensitivity — enzymatic reactions activate at optimum heat and pH. - Regulation — controlling enzyme activity by activators and inhibitors. - Catalysis — enzymes have high catalytic efficiency, transforming ~100–10,000 substrates per second; catalyzed reactions proceed 10³–10⁸ times faster than non-catalyzed. - Reversibility — enzymes catalyze both anabolic (synthesis) and catabolic (decomposition) directions. - Specificity — how restrictive the enzyme is in its choice of substrate; a completely specific enzyme has only one substrate. Types of specificity: bond specificity (specific for a bond), group specificity (specific for a group), substrate specificity (acts only on a particular substrate), optical specificity (acts on a specific optical configuration/isomer), co-factor specificity (specific to the substrate and cofactors).

✍️ Must-know: enzymes are protein, thermo-labile, colloidal, but NOT inorganic catalysts. Enzyme activity depends on temperature, pH, enzyme & substrate concentration. Molecules that increase activity = activators; that decrease activity = inhibitors.

3.2.2 The function of enzymes

  • Enzymes speed up reactions, essential for respiration, digesting food, liver, muscle and nerve function.
  • Turnover number = number of substrates converted by one enzyme molecule per second at saturated (fully occupied) active sites.
  • Enzymes are markers of disease states (myocardial infarction, jaundice, pancreatitis, cancer, neurodegenerative disorders).
  • Each enzyme has an active site with a unique shape.
  • Everyday examples: sucrase breaks sucrose; lactase breaks lactose (milk sugar); carbohydrase breaks carbohydrates into sugars; lipase breaks fats into fatty acids; protease breaks protein into amino acids.

Table 3.1 — enzymes in the body & functions (memorise): | Enzyme | Function | |---|---| | Lipases | split fats (blood, gastric/pancreatic/intestinal juices, adipose tissue); digestion | | Amylase | in saliva; changes starches into sugars | | Maltase | in foods (potatoes, pasta, beer) & saliva; breaks maltose into glucose | | Trypsin | in small intestine; breaks proteins into amino acids | | Lactase | small intestine; breaks lactose into glucose and galactose | | Helicase | unwinds DNA | | DNA polymerase | forms new copies of DNA (nucleic acid molecules) | | Acetylcholinesterase | breaks down the neurotransmitter acetylcholine in nerves & muscles |

✍️ Must-know: enzymes lower activation energy → faster reactions. Lab activity: H₂O₂ on a potato slice bubbles because the enzyme catalase decomposes hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen.
