2.6 Relative Velocity in One Dimension

◌ Not started

Relative velocity = the velocity of an object with respect to another object/observer (frame of reference).

If v_A and v_B are velocities of A and B with respect to the ground: - Relative velocity of A w.r.t. B: v_AB = v_A − v_B - Relative velocity of B w.r.t. A: v_BA = v_B − v_A

  • Same direction: magnitude of relative velocity = difference of the two speeds.
  • Opposite directions: magnitude of relative velocity = sum of the two speeds.

Example 2.14: Two cars at 100 km/h. - Opposite directions: v_AB = 100 − (−100) = 200 km/h; v_BA = −200 km/h. - Same direction: v_AB = 100 − 100 = 0 (at rest relative to each other).

✍️ Must-know: Objects moving in the same direction → relative velocity is the difference; opposite directions → it is the sum.
