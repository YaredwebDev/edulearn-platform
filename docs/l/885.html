1.5 The Light Microscope

  • Most microscopes have several powerful lenses allowing viewing at more than 100× actual size.
  • Light rays are focused onto the specimen on a slide; light transmitted through the specimen is focused by eye (ocular) and objective lenses.
  • Total magnification = eyepiece magnification × objective magnification. (e.g., ×10 eyepiece × ×40 objective = ×400 total.)

1.5.1 Parts and Functions of the Light Microscope

PartFunction
Eyepiece (ocular)At the top; you look through it; standard magnification 10× (optional 5×–30×).
Eyepiece tubeHolds the eyepiece above the objective lens; flexible in binoculars.
Objective lensesMajor lenses that further magnify the specimen; typically 4×–100×.
ArmSupport/holder connecting all components so the microscope can be used properly.
Body tubeConnects the eyepiece to the objective lenses.
Nose pieceRotating mount that holds many objective lenses; lets you change magnification.
Adjustment knobsFocus the microscope: fine (minimal movement, sharpens image) and coarse (brings specimen into general focus quickly).
StageWhere the specimen is placed for viewing.
Stage clipHolds the slide in place.
ApertureHole in the stage through which transmitted light reaches the stage.
Microscopic illuminatorThe light source at the base (replaces a mirror), about 100 V.
CondensersLenses that collect and focus light from the illuminator into the specimen (important for clear sharp images at high magnification, 400× and above).
Diaphragm (iris)Under the stage; controls the amount of light reaching the specimen.
BaseThe very bottom; supports the microscope.

1.5.2 Handling and Using a Light Microscope

  • Grip the microscope by the arm with one hand and support the base with the other; hold upright.
  • Make sure the slide is clean and dry; place the most promising region in the middle of the stage hole.
  • Always focus on low power (4×) first; use coarse knob first, then fine knob.
  • Use immersion oil only with the 100× objective (one drop only).
  • Always focus by moving the lens and specimen further apart, never closer.
  • Never touch lens surfaces with fingers.
  • Clean with lens paper and alcohol when done; place the 4× objective over the stage, lower the stage, turn off light, wrap cord, and return to cabinet with oculars toward the back.
