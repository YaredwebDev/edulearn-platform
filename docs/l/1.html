1.1 Definition of Common Terms Used in Crop Production

◌ Not started
  • Agriculture = the deliberate cultivation of crops and rearing of animals.
  • Shifting cultivation = a form of agriculture in which an area of ground is cleared of vegetation, cultivated for a few years, and then abandoned for a new area.
  • Settled agriculture = the practice of using the same plot of land continuously over a long period to grow crops or rear livestock (uses yield-enhancing practices: manure, fertiliser, soil bunds for erosion control, optimised water use). It is more advanced than shifting cultivation.
  • Subsistence farming = the practice of growing crops and raising livestock sufficient only for one's own use, without any surplus for trade (uses simple techniques within household capacity; any excess may be stored or sold for household income).

✍️ Must-know distinctions: shifting = temporary clearing then abandoning; settled = continuous same land with improvements; subsistence = only enough for the household (no surplus for trade).

Brainstorming note: Before agriculture, societies survived by hunting animals and gathering plants. Even today some hunter-gatherers exist where climate/land does not favour crop culture (deserts, Arctic, pockets of tropical rain forest). People first domesticated animals mainly for food, later bred them for non-food materials. Crop + livestock production became the major means of survival.
