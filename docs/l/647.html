5.4 Thermal Expansion

◌ Not started

Thermal expansion = the change in size (shape, area, volume) of matter in response to a change in temperature. Solids expand in linear, area, and volume ways; liquids and gases only in volume (they have no definite shape).

Linear expansion: ΔL = α L ΔT. (α = linear coefficient of expansion, unit °C⁻¹ or K⁻¹; L = original length.)

Area (surface) expansion: ΔA = β A ΔT, with β = 2α.

Volume expansion: ΔV = γ V ΔT, with γ = 3α (and γ = 3β/2 …; in the book γ relates to α). Real coefficient of volume expansion of a liquid in a container: γ_real = γ_apparent + γ_container.

✍️ Must-know relations: β = 2α and γ = 3α for a solid. ΔL = αLΔT; ΔA = βAΔT; ΔV = γVΔT.

✋ Peculiarity of water: between 0 °C and 4 °C water's volume decreases as temperature rises; its density is maximum at 4 °C. (This anomaly explains ice floating etc.)

Applications of thermal expansion (in solids, liquids, thermometers): - Riveting two metal sheets: as the hot rivet cools it contracts and tightens the joint. - Bimetallic strip (two different metals joined — they bend on heating because they expand differently); used in thermostats/thermometers. - Removing a stopper from a glass bottle: heat the neck slightly so it expands and the stopper comes out. - Liquid thermometers rely on the thermal expansion of mercury or alcohol. - Boiling of liquids; medicine bottles left with some space at the top so contents don't spill after expansion; railroad tracks buckle if no expansion gaps are left; ball bearings fitted by thermal expansion/contraction. - Bridges built in segments with expansion gaps (a steel bridge built at 20°C with a 4 cm gap between 20 m segments — find the max temperature before buckling).

Worked examples: Example 5.2 (bridge expansion gap); Example 5.3 (brass plate cooled to pass into a cylinder — area expansion); Example 5.4 (alcohol overflow from a heated aluminium reservoir — real volume expansion, and water-vs-glass expansion in a flask); Example 5.5 (real & apparent expansion of mercury in a brass container).
