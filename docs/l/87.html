14.1 Introduction to Mechanized Farming

◌ Not started
  • Mechanization = the use of machinery to replace human or animal labour. In agriculture, it uses tools & machines for land development, crop production, harvesting, storage, animal rearing and farm-gate processing. It replaces simple farm tools / crude implements (which slow farming down).
  • Advantages of farm mechanization: saves time; reduces difficulty in farming; saves labour; reduces farm health hazards; encourages large-scale farming; increases on-farm output & income; encourages cooperation among farmers (they buy machinery together).
  • Disadvantages / limitations: machinery can be expensive to purchase or hire; fossil-fuel machinery causes environmental pollution; tyre traffic of heavy machines causes soil compaction & poor root growth; may hamper farmers' well-established social capital (e.g. Debo or Guza); reduces manual farm work available for workers; requires continuous energy supplies (fuel & electricity).
  • Levels of mechanization — categorised as low, fair or high. Three main power sources: human, animal, mechanical. 1. Low level: manual power used exceeds 33%. 2. Fair level: animal power used 34–66%. 3. High level: mechanical power used 67–100%.
