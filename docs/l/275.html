2.1 Introduction to the Octet Rule

◌ Not started

Why do atoms bond? When two atoms react they usually do so by achieving a noble-gas electron configuration — i.e. by filling or emptying their valence shell so they end up with an octet of valence electrons (2 for the small atoms H and He).

  • The octet rule: atoms tend to gain, lose, or share electrons until they are surrounded by eight valence electrons.
  • The group number in the periodic table tells the number of valence electrons (Group IA = 1 … Group VIIA = 7; helium has 2).
  • If an atom already has 8 valence electrons (a noble gas) it is very stable and generally does not bond.

Three ways atoms reach an octet (the three types of bond formation you will compare): 1. Ionic bonding — electron transfer (metal to non-metal). 2. Covalent bonding — electron sharing (between non-metals). 3. Metallic bonding — a "sea" of delocalised electrons (between metals).

✍️ Must-know: The octet rule and the "octet of 8, duet of 2 (H, He)" rule explain bonding. Group number = number of valence electrons.
