5.2 Magnetic Field

◌ Not started

Magnetic field = the region around a magnetic material or moving electric charge within which the force of magnetism acts. Represented by magnetic field lines.

Properties of magnetic field lines: - Never intersect each other. - Form closed loops. - Outside a magnet: from North to South. Inside a magnet: from South to North. - Closeness/density of lines ∝ field strength (lines closer together where the field is strong, e.g., near the poles). - Opposite poles attract → field lines converge; like poles repel → lines diverge.

Similarities between electric & magnetic fields: both produced by two "poles"/charges; like repel / unlike attract; field direction is that of force on a positive charge / on the North pole.

Differences: + and − charges can exist separately, but North and South poles always come together (no magnetic monopole observed); electric field lines have start/end points while magnetic field lines are continuous closed loops.

✍️ Must-know: Magnetic field lines form closed loops, point N→S outside and S→N inside, never intersect, and are denser where the field is strong.
