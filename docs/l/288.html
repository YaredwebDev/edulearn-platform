3.2 Kinetic Theory and Properties of Matter

◌ Not started

Kinetic theory of matter — every substance is made of many tiny particles (ions, atoms, molecules) in continuous random motion; motion increases with temperature.

Six assumptions (core): 1. All matter is made of constantly moving particles. 2. Particles have both kinetic and potential energy; solids have the least, gases the most. 3. The difference between states = energy content and particle motion; phase changes occur when particle energy changes. 4. Temperature measures the average kinetic energy of particles. 5. There are spaces between particles; empty space grows from solid → liquid → gas. 6. Attractive forces between particles (intermolecular forces) get stronger as particles come closer.

Properties of gases: no fixed shape/volume; easily compressed (large spaces); low density; exert pressure in all directions (wall collisions); flow and diffuse easily.

Properties of liquids: definite volume, no definite shape; higher density than gases; slightly compressible; are fluids (flow more slowly than gases).

Properties of solids: definite shape & volume; highest density (particles very close); extremely hard to compress; do not flow.

✍️ Must-know ranking (from Exercise 3.2): increasing intermolecular force & density: gas < liquid < solid; increasing compressibility: solid < liquid < gas; increasing kinetic energy: solid < liquid < gas.
