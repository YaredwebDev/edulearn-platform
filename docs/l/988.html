3.1 Historical Development of the Atomic Theories of Matter

  • The concept of the atom dates to early Indian and Greek philosophers (~400 B.C.). The word atom comes from the Greek 'a-tomos' meaning "uncuttable" / "non-divisible."
  • A scientific theory: a well-tested, broad explanation of a natural phenomenon (NOT just an educated guess).

The Indivisible Atom (Greek philosophers):

  • Empedocles (mid-5th century BC): all materials made of four elements — earth, air, water, fire.
  • Plato: adopted Empedocles' theory, coined the term "element."
  • Aristotle: adopted the four elements; differentiated them by hot/cold, wet/dry. The Greek concept lasted 2,000+ years.
  • Leucippus (c. 440 BC): originated the atom concept.
  • Democritus (460–370 BC): taught atomism — indivisible, indestructible atoms are the basic components of all matter.

Five major points of the Greek atomic idea:

  1. All matter is composed of atoms (too small to be seen, cannot be further split).
  2. There is a void — empty space between atoms.
  3. Atoms are completely solid.
  4. Atoms are homogeneous, with no internal structure.
  5. Atoms differ in size, shape, and weight.
  • The Greek atomic model = the "solid sphere."
  • Aristotle and others opposed atomism, so it was buried until the 16th–17th centuries.
  • Lavoisier (18th century) proved that in burning there is no net gain or loss of weight, establishing modern chemistry.

Drawback of Greek philosophers: they relied on reason and logic, not experiment and observation ("all thought, no action").
