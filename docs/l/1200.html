5.1 Revision on Right-Angled Triangles

Definition: A right-angled triangle has one angle equal to 90°.

Properties:

  • The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse (always the longest side).
  • The other two sides adjacent to the right angle are the base and the perpendicular side.
  • The sum of the other two interior angles is 90° (each acute).
  • Pythagoras theorem: b² + p² = h² (b = base, p = perpendicular, h = hypotenuse).
Right-angled triangle

Examples:

  • Missing side: x² + 4² = 6² → x² = 20 → x = 2√5.
  • Height of an equilateral triangle of side 6: draw altitude → right triangle with hypotenuse 6 and base 3 → 3² + x² = 6² → x² = 27 → x = 3√3.

Conversion of Pythagoras: if b² + p² = h², then the angle between b and p is 90°.

Check which are right-angled:

  • 5, 6, 2: 5² + 2² = 29 ≠ 6² = 36 → NOT right-angled.
  • 3, 4, 5: 3² + 4² = 25 = 5² → right-angled.
  • √5, 2, 3: (√5)² + 2² = 9 = 3² → right-angled.
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Pythagoras: b² + p² = h². The hypotenuse is always the longest side, opposite the 90° angle.
