10.2 Major Types of Natural Resources

◌ Not started

Classification helps identify & execute sustainable management to improve quality of life now and for future generations. Forests, soils and water are closely associated with agriculture and are the focus of this unit.

10.2.1 Forests

  • A forest contains plants/trees standing very close together; tall trees' leaves form a continuous canopy, which reduces the amount/quality of light reaching the ground and influences other climatic variables — determining the plants, animals and micro-organisms that live there.
  • Importance of trees in the environment: firewood, fruits, medicines, timber, food; construction materials; shade & wind protection; aesthetic & spiritual value; habitat for wild animals; source of non-timber products (honey); improve soil fertility through roots & leaf litter (adds organic matter); protect soil from erosion & prevent flooding; absorb CO₂ & water, produce O₂ by photosynthesis, keeping carbon in biomass and reducing release to the atmosphere.

10.2.2 Soils

  • Soil = a mixture of mineral & organic particles of varying sizes, formed by weathering of rocks at the surface. Weathering breaks rocks into smaller particles (soil parent materials); air/water enter cracks forming chemicals; bacteria & plants colonise rock fragments, gradually transforming into soil.
  • Importance: typical mineral soils ≈ 5% organic material, 45% minerals, 25% water, 25% air. The top layer supports plant growth (food & forage crops), providing water, oxygen & nutrients. Soil is a habitat for soil organisms (earthworms, bacteria, fungi). Soil is a major storehouse of carbon — storing carbon reduces CO₂ release and helps minimise climate change. Soil properties (nutrient content, organic matter, reaction/acidity-alkalinity, top-soil depth, salinity, organism diversity) determine crop productivity. Only if activities improve soil properties can high yields be obtained.

10.2.3 Water

  • Water covers 70% of the earth's crust; ~97% is in oceans & seas; of the rest ~2% fresh water and ~1% frozen (ice caps & glaciers).
  • Surface water = any aboveground body of water (lakes, rivers, streams, ponds), used for agriculture, industry, fish/wildlife, electricity generation, recreation, domestic purposes.
  • Ethiopia — "the water tower of Africa": eight river basins + one lake basin (Rift Valley Lakes). The country has large groundwater resources and huge potential for small/medium/large-scale irrigation (→ agricultural productivity & food security) and hydropower.
  • River basins (source → flow): Abbay (Sekela, W. Gojam → West, Sudan); Awash (Ginchi → North-east, border lakes); Baro-Akobo (Illubabor → West, Sudan); Genale-Dawa (Bale → East, Somali); Mereb (Zalanbessa → West, Eritrean); Omo-Gibe (Ambo → South, Rudolph Lake); Tekeze (Lasta/Gidan → West, "Chew bahir"); Wabishebele (Bale mountains → East, Somali); Rift Valley Lakes (Arsi mountain → South).

✍️ Key terms: ice caps = mass of ice covering a large area; glaciers = huge mass of ice slowly moving over land; river basin = portion of land drained by a river and its tributaries.
