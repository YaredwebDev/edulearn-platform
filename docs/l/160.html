6.5 Impact of Human Activities on the Environment

◌ Not started
  • Humans depend on the environment but also exploit it through destructive practices affecting the whole ecosystem — present & future generations.
  • Major activities affecting the environment: burning coal & natural gas, using refrigerants/coolants, applying pesticides, waste incineration/disposal, using volatile chemicals, agricultural activities, industrial & sewage discharges, construction & transport, urban development, mining.
  • These cause: environmental pollution, climate change, global warming, acid rain, ozone-layer depletion, loss of biodiversity, toxic bioaccumulation, and resource depletion.

6.5.1 Environmental pollution

Pollution = contamination of components of the environment that adversely affects normal environmental processes — adding substances/energy faster than they can be dispersed, diluted, decomposed, recycled or stored harmlessly. Causes: natural events (forest fires, volcanoes) but mainly human activities; rapid population growth & industrialization aggravate it. Types: air, water, land, noise, light.

Air pollution = contamination of the air by harmful gases, dust, smoke. Causes: pollutants added faster than the environment can dissipate them — carbon monoxide, nitrogen dioxide, ozone, lead, sulfur dioxide, smog, particles from transport, industry, burning coal/gas, refrigerants, volatile chemicals. Effects: the leading environmental health risk; depletion of the ozone layer; respiratory & cardiovascular disorders; changes soil pH; causes acid rain; infects plants → harmful food.

Water pollution = contamination of water bodies interfering with beneficial use (excess toxins). Causes: factories, industrial/agricultural wastes, sewage, oil transport, chemical fertilizers & insecticides/herbicides, pathogenic microorganisms, water hyacinth (Emboch). Effects: depletion of fish populations, destruction of biodiversity, and waterborne diseases leading to death. Eutrophication (accelerated algal growth from phosphorus/nitrates/detergents) is a water-pollution result.

6.5.2 Climate change

  • Climate = the usual weather of a place. Climate change = a change in the usual weather patterns (rain, temperature) over a month/season.
  • Causes: natural (internal fluctuations of energy/water/carbon; solar variations; volcanic eruptions) and human — especially combustion of fossil fuels, raising levels of greenhouse gases: carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane, nitrous oxide, fluorinated gases.
  • Effects: greenhouse effect → global warming (heat trapped like a greenhouse/blanket).

6.5.3 Global warming

  • Global warming = an average rapid rise in the earth's temperature. Major causes: greenhouse gases from human activities (burning fossil fuels, industry).
  • Effects: flooding, melting ice caps, rising oceans, loss of species, health problems; also drought, rising sea levels, extinctions, heavy rainfall/flooding, heat stress, poverty & displacement.

✍️ Must-know: CO₂ both supports photosynthesis & traps heat (warming), but human-produced CO₂ leads to higher global warming. Greenhouse gases = CO₂ (and methane, nitrous oxide, fluorinated gases) — NOT chlorine/nitrogen/oxygen.

6.5.4 Ozone-layer depletion

  • Ozone (O₃) = three oxygen atoms; toxic as a gas but vital in the stratosphere (the atmosphere has four layers: troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere). The ozone layer absorbs harmful UV rays from the sun (which cause skin cancer), preventing them reaching earth.
  • Cause of ozone depletion: release of CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) used in refrigerators/fire extinguishers, HCFCs, and halons. CFCs cause reactions breaking down ozone; chlorine reacts with ozone → oxygen (O₂) + chlorine monoxide (ClO), creating a catalytic cycle that keeps destroying ozone (ozone hole). Reactions: Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂; ClO + O → Cl + O₂.
  • Effects: exposure to UV → skin cancer & cataracts, weakened immune system, skin ageing, respiratory disease; affects terrestrial/aquatic animals & plants; increases ground-level ozone (smog).
  • Chief air pollutant depleting ozone = nitrogen oxides & fluorocarbons (CFCs).

6.5.5 Acid rain

  • Acid rain = rain more acidic (lower pH) than natural rain.
  • Causes: acid-forming pollutants (from burning fossil fuels, power plants, industry, internal-combustion exhaust) — sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides. SO₂ & NO₂ react with water → sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) and nitric acid (HNO₃):
  • 2SO₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 2H₂SO₄
  • 4NO₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 4HNO₃
  • CO₂ + water → carbonic acid (makes rain slightly acidic).
  • Effects: damages plants (strips the waxy leaf coating → leaching of calcium, magnesium, potassium → drying), prevents seed germination, degrades soil nutrients, dissolves aluminum (stops plant growth), affects soil microorganisms & aquatic animals (fish, amphibians), reduces drinking-water quality, causes acute/chronic health problems via dissolved metals, corrodes pipes & leaches heavy metals (iron, lead, copper), and damages stone/metal buildings & monuments.

6.5.6 Loss of biodiversity

  • Biodiversity = the variety of life (genes, species, individuals within a species, biological communities from ecosystem to global biosphere). Loss of biodiversity = decrease in the number of genes, species and communities.
  • Causes: (1) habitat loss & degradation/fragmentation (the most important — reduces food & living space); (2) overexploitation (harvesting beyond replacement capacity); (3) pollution; (4) climate change; (5) invasive species (non-native species that outcompete natives).
  • Effects: threatens the earth's ability to provide food, water, fertile soils & pest/disease protection; lowers ecosystem productivity & quality of services; affects economies & societies; threatens global food security and development of new medicines.

6.5.7 Toxic bioaccumulation

  • Bioaccumulation = increase in the concentration/accumulation of chemicals in living organisms' body tissues. Persistent bioaccumulative toxic substances (PBTs) don't degrade easily; they accumulate in fatty tissues and increase in concentration along the food chain (biomagnification).
  • Sources: synthetic chemicals with halogen atoms (fluorine, chlorine, bromine), DDT, and metals (lead, mercury).
  • Process: plants absorb small amounts → primary consumers eat plants (low concentration) → toxin can't be excreted → passes to secondary & higher consumers → concentration becomes highest in organisms at the top of the food chain.
  • Effects: mutagenic DNA damage, cancer, neurological/reproductive/developmental toxicity, immune damage. Lead → impaired brain development in fetuses/infants/children. Mercury → damages nervous & reproductive systems. DDT makes bird-of-prey eggshells thin so eggs break during incubation.
  • Concentration of DDT increases at each trophic level.

6.5.8 Resource depletion

  • Resource depletion = when consumption of natural resources becomes faster than they can recover → they become scarce.
  • Major causes: (1) overpopulation; (2) poor farming practices (poor irrigation/soil management, heavy machinery, excessive pesticides/fungicides/herbicides killing soil microorganisms); (3) overconsumption (industrial-scale mineral & oil exploration); (4) industrial & technological development (toxins/chemical by-products + rising demand).
