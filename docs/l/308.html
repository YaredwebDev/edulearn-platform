6.9 Aldehydes and Ketones

◌ Not started

6.3.1 Structure & naming

  • Aldehyde: carbonyl (C=O) bonded to a hydrogen (+ a carbon, or two H in methanal). General R–CHO. Simplest = methanal (formaldehyde), HCHO.
  • Ketone: carbonyl bonded to two carbons. General R–CO–R′. Simplest = propanone (acetone), CH₃COCH₃.
  • IUPAC naming: aldehydes — replace "e" of the alkane with "–al" (methanal, ethanal, propanal, butanal); the carbonyl is always C-1, so no number is needed. Ketones — replace "e" with "–one" and number the longest chain containing C=O from the end nearer the carbonyl (propanone, butanone, pentan-2-one, pentan-3-one).
  • Example named compounds: 2-ethylpentanal; 3-methylbutanal; 3-methylbutanal vs 4-methylpentanal-type (number toward C=O end).

6.3.2 Physical properties

  • The carbonyl bond is polar (δ− O, δ+ C). Aldehydes/ketones are polar and interact by dipole–dipole forces → higher boiling points than non-polar compounds of comparable mass, but lower than alcohols/carboxylic acids (which H-bond).
  • E.g. butanal bp 76 °C and butanone bp 80 °C (both MM 72) vs diethyl ether 34 °C (MM 74) and pentane 36 °C (MM 72).
  • Low-molecular-weight aldehydes/ketones are soluble in water (carbonyl H-bonds with water). Table: methanal & ethanal & propanone miscible (∞); propanal 16; butanal 7; butanone 26; solubility falls as chain grows.
  • Formalin (aqueous formaldehyde, methanal) preserves biological specimens.

6.3.3 Preparation & reactions (survey; Project 6.2)

  • Preparation: oxidation of primary alcohols (mild oxidant) → aldehydes; oxidation of secondary alcohols → ketones; ozonolysis of alkenes.
  • Reactions: (i) addition (Grignard reagents; alcohol addition → hemiacetals and acetals); (ii) oxidation (aldehydes are easily oxidised to carboxylic acids; ketones resist oxidation) and (iii) reduction (catalytic/metal-hydride reduction back to alcohols).
  • Chemical tests distinguishing aldehydes from ketones rely on the aldehyde's easy oxidation (aldehydes reduce mild oxidants, ketones do not).

✍️ Must-know: only aldehydes are readily oxidised to carboxylic acids (ketones are not) — the basis of tests that distinguish them (e.g. Tollens'/Fehling-style in practice). Carbonyl polarity: δ+ on C, δ− on O.
