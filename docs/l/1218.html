8.3 Vector Operations

8.3.1 Addition of vectors

Triangle law: AB→ + BC→ = AC→. The sum (resultant) is the third side of the triangle formed by placing the initial of the second at the terminal of the first.

Triangle law of vector addition

Parallelogram law: For two co-initial vectors, construct a parallelogram; the diagonal from the common initial point is the sum.

Parallelogram law

Example: FH→ + HG→ = FG→. EF→ + FG→ = EG→ and GH→ + HE→ = GE→ (parallel and opposite).

Subtraction of vectors: a − b = a + (−b). Draw the first vector, then from its terminal draw the opposite of the second; the resultant joins initial of first to terminal of the second.

Addition/subtraction of column vectors:

  • (p, q) + (r, s) = (p+r, q+s).
  • (p, q) − (r, s) = (p−r, q−s).

Example: ā = (−4, −3), b̄ = (5, 0):

  • ā + b̄ = (−4+5, −3+0) = (1, −3).
  • ā − b̄ = (−4−5, −3−0) = (−9, −3).

8.3.2 Multiplication of a vector by a scalar

Definition: k·AB→ is the vector whose magnitude is k times that of AB→; direction is the same if k > 0, opposite if k < 0.

  • If |k| > 1 it enlarges; if 0 < |k| < 1 it shortens.
  • k(x, y) = (kx, ky).
  • Two vectors are parallel if one is a scalar multiple of the other: b̄ = k·ā.

Example: ā = (2, 4): −ā = (−2, −4); 2ā = (4, 8); (1/2)ā = (1, 2). All are parallel.
