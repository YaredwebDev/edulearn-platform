9.1 Reading: Equality, Equity and Justice

◌ Not started

(the core "story"/text of the unit, adapted from Kumar, S. 2017)

The passage distinguishes three related but different concepts:

  • Equality aims to give everyone the same things so all can enjoy full, healthy lives. It works only if everyone starts from the same place and needs the same things.
  • Equity involves understanding and giving people what they need to enjoy healthy lives — treating people according to their different needs.
  • Justice is the collective responsibility of a free and just society to protect civil and human rights for every individual regardless of gender, race, ethnicity, origin, sexual orientation, class, physical or mental ability, and age. It can be seen as a scheme of law in which every person receives their due.

Examples used in the passage: - School funding (equality vs equity): Equality = every student gets the same resources. Equity = students who lag (often low-income students, students of colour) get more resources to catch up and close the achievement gap. - Runners on a track: Starting everyone at the same line (equality) is unfair because inner lanes are shorter; staggering the starting positions (equity) offsets the disadvantage. Tailored treatment is a surer path to fairness.

Key quotes/ideas: - John Rawls — justice is of central importance in political practice and theory. - Confucius — "In a country well governed, poverty is something to be ashamed of; in a country badly governed, wealth is something to be ashamed of." - Mahatma Gandhi — "Peace will not come out of a clash of arms but out of justice lived and done by unarmed nations in the face of odds." - In a materialistic world, ethics, laws and order are cared about less; people measure others by richness. - The court system is the best way to practise fairness and justice found so far — a trial by jury gives the defendant and accuser each a chance to present their case; the jury is meant to be fair and to check whether the law is justly applied. - We must advocate for equality, equity and justice, educate people about their rights, and treat others fairly.

✍️ Must-know: Questions test (a) the difference between equality and equity, (b) the definition of justice, (c) the examples (school funding, runners), (d) the quoted thinkers, and (e) vocabulary in context. This unit's passage is rich in ideas — be ready for true/false and comprehension questions on the examples.
