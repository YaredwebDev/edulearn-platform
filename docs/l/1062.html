8.5 Grammar

8.5.1 Quantifiers (much, many, little, few, some, any, a lot of)

  • How many? (countable): many, few, a few, several, a lot of, a number of.
  • How much? (uncountable): much, little, a little, a lot of, a great deal of.
  • much/many — usually in negatives & questions: "We haven't got much petrol."
  • little/few (negative sense, "not enough"): "There was little food." a little/a few (positive, "some"): "a few countries," "a little money."
  • some/any — see Unit 4.
Note: ✋ Trap: "We had a lot of rain" (not "much/many" in affirmative). "Have you been to many countries?" "It costs much money" (usually question/negative: "Does it cost much?").

8.5.2 Clauses of Reason

Explain why something happens. Introduced by conjunctions: because, as, since, for, due to, owing to, on account of, or the phrase because of.

  • "Daniel joined the club because / as / since he wanted to improve his intonation."
  • "Because of the severe weather, the flight was delayed." (because of + noun phrase)
  • ✋ because + clause; because of + noun: "I couldn't see because it was dark." / "Because of the darkness, I couldn't see."

8.5.3 Clauses of Manner

Show how something is done/happened. Introduced by: as, as if, as though.

  • "The woman acts as a chairperson."
  • "That pupil behaves as if he were a teacher." (unreal comparison → subjunctive "were").
  • "The old man acts as though he were a Ugandan."

8.5.4 The Present Perfect Tense (review)

Form: Subject + has/have + past participle (V3).

Uses (from the unit):

  1. Actions completed in the immediate/recent past: "I have already written my homework."
  2. Past actions with no given time (indefinite): "Someone has stolen my laptop."
  3. Actions focusing on the present effect rather than the past action: "He has ignored his work and frequently played games."
  4. Actions begun in the past and continuing to the present: "We have never given our hands to our enemies since the dawn of history."
  5. An action done once or many times before: "I have never seen an elephant."
  6. Experiences/changes over a period of time: "Our university has brought about a significant change over the last ten years."

Affirmative/Negative/Interrogative: "We have learned…" / "We have not learned…" / "Have we learned…?"

✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Present perfect = have/has + V3; focus on present effect, indefinite past, experience, or continuing action. Signal words: already, yet, just, since, for, never, ever, recently.
