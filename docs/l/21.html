4.2 Importance of Animal Production

◌ Not started

Animal production / husbandry = keeping farm animals for human use. Roles: consumable food, work/draught power, by-products (clothing, feed, fertiliser, medicine), and economic/cultural/social roles.

  • Food & nutrition: >34% of global food-protein supply comes from farm animals; animal products are rich in fats, minerals, vitamins. Animals convert high-fibre feeds into edible human food.
  • Livelihood & economy: animal production provides livelihood for 65% of Ethiopia's population; since 2018 livestock contributes 45–48% of agricultural GDP and 16–19% of total export earnings. Income, capital accumulation, savings.
  • Work: traction & transport — bullocks and camels draw ploughs, harrows, ridges; animals used in crop husbandry (seeding) and crop processing (threshing); camels, donkeys, horses transport people & goods.
  • Raw materials: skins & wool (cottage industry, household products); bones & hooves → adhesives; fats → candles & soap; internal-organ extracts → pharmaceutical hormones/drugs.
  • Livestock feed: slaughter residues → bone meal, fish meal, blood meal, meat meal (protein/amino-acid sources for monogastric feeding).
  • Clothing: skin, wool, fur → clothing, footwear, bags, drums; poultry feathers → mattresses & pillows.
  • Socio-cultural roles: cattle ownership is an index of social wealth/status; livestock kept for savings/investment, security/insurance, stability, and social functions (rituals, funerals, ritual slaughter, bride wealth).

✍️ Must-know numbers: >34% global food protein from farm animals; livestock livelihood of 65% of Ethiopians; 45–48% of agricultural GDP; 16–19% of export earnings. Farm animals supply food, work, raw materials, feed, clothing and socio-cultural value.
