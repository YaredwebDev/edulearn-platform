13.1 Definitions of Climate Change and Variability

◌ Not started
  • Climate change = a long-term (decades) change in temperatures & weather patterns. Extreme weather events (unusual floods, long droughts, wildfires, unusual snowfalls, intense hurricanes, warming of the earth) become more common. Climate change causes fundamental changes in ecosystems.
  • Climate variability = short-term (days, weeks, months) variability in climate. It does not cause fundamental ecosystem changes — organisms can adapt (e.g. some years below-average, others average/above-average rainfall).
  • Effects of climate change (examples): decrease in crop & animal productivity; species threatened or extinct; changes in water availability; changes in storm patterns/frequencies; increased flooding of coastal areas; changes in rainfall amount & pattern; increased drought frequency; drying of surface-water resources; increased soil erosion; increase in desertification.
  • Cause: production & release of greenhouse gases that trap heat → global warming. Main greenhouse gases: carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O). Accumulation → climate change; also ozone-layer depletion exposing earth to harmful radiation (can cause skin cancer).
  • Sources: human activities (agriculture, forest clearing/burning, fossil-fuel burning) and natural processes; climate change is mainly caused by human activities.

✍️ Key terms: greenhouse gases = gases that absorb heat radiation from the earth's surface and reradiate it back, trapping heat (causing global warming); fossil fuels = fuels from fossilised plants/animals (coal, oil, natural gas); carbon sinks = processes/activities/mechanisms that remove greenhouse gases from the atmosphere.
