2.3 Structure and Function of Plant Parts

A typical angiosperm has two major external systems:

  • Shoot system (above ground): stem, branches, leaves, buds, flowers, fruits.
  • Root system (below ground): primary/tap root, lateral/branch roots, root hairs, root cap.
  • Roots vs underground stems: roots do not bear leaves or buds.

External structure of a leaf

  • Petiole (leaf stalk), lamina/blade (broadest flat part), midrib (contains the vein/transport vessels), margin, base, tip/apex.
  • Large, thin lamina → large surface area for light collection and short diffusion distance for gas exchange through stomata.

Types of roots

  • Tap root: one large primary vertical root with few lateral roots; penetrates deep for anchorage and deep water; feature of dicots.
  • Fibrous root: thin, moderately branching roots of similar size from the stem base; efficient at absorbing surface water; holds soil (prevents erosion); feature of monocots.

2.3.1 Internal structure of a leaf

  • Outer layer (epidermis): single layer of tightly packed cells.
  • Upper epidermis covered with a waxy cuticle → transmits light but restricts water loss.
  • Lower epidermis contains bean-shaped guard cells forming stomata (regulate O₂ release, CO₂ intake, water loss); stomata are more abundant on the lower epidermis.
  • Middle layer (mesophyll):
  • Palisade layer: columnar cells just below the upper epidermis, packed with the largest number of chloroplasts → does most of the photosynthesis.
  • Spongy layer: irregular, loosely arranged cells with fewer chloroplasts and numerous air spaces → allow diffusion of O₂, CO₂, water vapour.

2.3.2 Internal structure of a stem (dicot)

From outside in: Epidermis (cuticle, compact cells) → Hypodermis (collenchyma, thickened corners → mechanical strength) → Cortex (thin-walled cells for food storage) → Endodermis (innermost cortex layer, starch sheath) → Vascular bundles (xylem + phloem + cambium, arranged in a ring around the pith) → Pith (central, stores food, helps internal water translocation).

Monocot vs dicot stem: dicot has few (4–8) vascular bundles in one or two rings with a well-developed pith; monocot has scattered vascular bundles.

2.3.3 Internal structure of a root (dicot)

From outside in: Piliferous layer (outermost, single-celled root hairs, no cuticle) → Cortex (multi-layered, stores food and water) → Endodermis (barrel-shaped, controls water movement into xylem; Casparian strip) → Pericycle (single layer, site of origin of lateral roots) → Vascular bundles (xylem + phloem + cambium) → Pith.

✍️ Must-know: Root hair = single cell; best suited for absorption (large surface area). Endodermis may be called the starch sheath. Palisade layer has the most chloroplasts.
