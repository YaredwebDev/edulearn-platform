2.1 Field crops, cereals and pulses (overview)

◌ Not started
  • Crop production is influenced by factors a farmer can control (crop & site selection, land amount, management practices, type/amount of inputs) and factors cannot be controlled (unexpected end of good weather, locust invasion). Site/crop selection needs knowledge of soil, water and growth requirements.
  • Management practices: appropriate planting time, planting distance, pest-control strategy, when to harvest, post-harvest management. Inputs: fertiliser, irrigation, lime (to correct acidity).
  • Field crops = crops grown on a wide scale mainly for human consumption; they are annuals (complete a life cycle in a single season). Two major groups: cereals and pulses (legumes).

Cereals = grass-characteristic crops grown mainly for their edible seeds; monocots (single cotyledon, single leaf at emergence); contain carbohydrates, proteins, fats, fibres; grow in a wide range of environments (lowland to highland, many soil types/climates). Ethiopia's five major cereals: teff, maize, wheat, barley, sorghum.

Pulses = seeds of legume crops grown for edible seeds (consumed as protein-rich dry seeds or immature pods as vegetables); also animal feed. Pulses are dicots (two cotyledons) and many fix nitrogen via root nodules, improving soil fertility for following crops; hence used in rotation and mixed with cereals. In Ethiopia pulses are mostly grown without mineral fertiliser (only organic manure at times), with minimal soil cultivation and poor weed control → low yields. Major pulses in Ethiopia: faba bean, field pea, haricot bean.

✍️ Must-know distinction: Cereals = monocots (edible seeds, no nitrogen fixation). Pulses/legumes = dicots, nitrogen-fixers (via root nodules). Mixing cereals with legumes in cropping improves nitrogen for the cereal → higher overall productivity. Cereal farming is much heavier on soil cultivation than pulse farming.
