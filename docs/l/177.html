2.6 The Five-Kingdom System of Classification

◌ Not started
  • The two-kingdom system was found inadequate (many organisms didn't fit).
  • Whittaker (1969) proposed the five-kingdom classification based on: cell structure, body organization, mode of nutrition, reproduction, and phylogenetic relationships.
  • The five kingdoms: Animalia, Plantae, Fungi, Monera, Protista.

✍️ Must-know: Whittaker's five kingdoms = Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia.

Viruses note box: Viruses are NOT included in any kingdom — not considered living because they lack key characteristics (don't grow, develop, or respire on their own). They are made of nucleic acids (DNA or RNA) surrounded by a protein coat; they replicate only inside a living host cell.

2.6.1 Kingdom Monera

  • Includes eubacteria (true bacteria) and archaebacteria (archaea).
  • Eubacteria: strong cell walls; various shapes; heterotrophic or autotrophic (photosynthetic or chemosynthetic); live in most habitats; some cause disease (strep throat, pneumonia), most are harmless/helpful.
  • Archaebacteria: very different cell walls; make their own food; live in extreme environments (swamps, deep-ocean hot-water vents, evaporating ponds), often with no oxygen.

Important characteristics of Monera: - Simple prokaryotic unicellular organisms. - Lack a well-defined nucleus or any membrane-bound organelles. - Most have a rigid cell wall. - Various modes of nutrition (autotrophs or heterotrophs). - Known as decomposers and mineralizers in the biosphere.

2.6.2 Kingdom Protista

  • The most diverse kingdom; no such thing as a typical protist.
  • Single-celled or multicellular; microscopic or very large; some autotrophs, some heterotrophs.
  • All protists are eukaryotes — this is their one common feature.
  • Protozoans: animal-like, one cell.
  • Algae: plant-like, photosynthetic autotrophs (no roots/stems/leaves); unicellular algae are the basis of aquatic food chains and produce much of Earth's oxygen.
  • Some protists resemble fungi but have different cell walls.

2.6.3 Kingdom Fungi

  • Eukaryotic organisms including yeasts, moulds, and mushrooms.
  • Bodies made of thread-like structures called hyphae.
  • Cell wall made of chitin and polysaccharides.
  • Heterotrophic — absorb nutrients from the environment (extracellular digestion: secrete enzymes, digest, then absorb).
  • Saprophytes (decomposers): absorb soluble organic matter from dead substrates (fallen logs, animal corpses, wastes).
  • Can live as parasites (pathogenic — Candida albicans causes thrush, Tinea pedis causes athlete's foot) or mutualists (e.g., fungi inside termites digest wood).
  • Can be symbionts: lichens (with algae) and mycorrhiza (with plant roots).
  • Reproduce asexually by vegetative means (fragmentation, fission, budding), by spores, and sexually; spores produced in fruiting bodies.
  • Yeast (Saccharomyces) is used to make injera rise and produce alcohol (Tej, Tella, Beer).

2.6.4 Kingdom Plantae

  • All eukaryotic, multicellular, autotrophic organisms.
  • Make their own food by photosynthesis; have chloroplasts and chlorophyll; produce oxygen.
  • Cells have a rigid cell wall made of cellulose.
  • Nearly all live on land; sexual reproduction predominates; reproduce asexually by vegetative propagation or sexually.
  • Over 250 000 species (flowering plants, mosses, ferns, coniferous plants).

Plant key terms: Angiosperms = seed plants that make flowers/fruits (monocots, dicots); Gymnosperms = seed plants that do not make flowers/fruits (conifers); Bryophytes = mosses, liverworts, hornworts (no vascular tissue, seedless); Vascular plants = have xylem and phloem; Monocots = one seed leaf, parallel veins; Dicots = two seed leaves, branching leaf veins.

2.6.5 Kingdom Animalia

  • All multicellular, heterotrophic, eukaryotic organisms.
  • Heterotrophs that ingest food and digest it within their bodies (unlike fungi that absorb).
  • Lack cell walls; held together by structural proteins, most abundant being collagen (found only in animals).
  • Have specialized muscle cells and nerve cells (organized into muscle and nervous tissue) — central to what it means to be an animal.
  • Most reproduce sexually.
  • Classified into invertebrates (no backbone) and vertebrates (with a backbone).
  • Invertebrates: sponges, flatworms, roundworms, annelida, Mollusca, Arthropoda (Insects, Arachnids, Crustacea, Myriapods).
  • Vertebrates: fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals.

✍️ Must-know: Plants = cell walls of cellulose, autotrophic. Fungi = cell walls of chitin, heterotrophic (absorb). Animals = no cell walls (collagen), heterotrophic (ingest), have muscle + nerve cells.
