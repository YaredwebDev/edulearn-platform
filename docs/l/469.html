10C Reading Skills: The Impact of New Technologies on the Labour Market

◌ Not started

Key facts (balanced/expository text): 1. Fear view: new technologies (automation, AI, industrial robots) are often seen as a danger to existing and future jobs — a perception they will make work redundant and cause massive job destruction. 2. Counter view: automation, like earlier technological waves, increases demand for labour in other sectors, creates new jobs, raises wages & living standards. Historically, technological advances increased productivity and created more jobs than they destroyed. 3. Transition disruptions: progress is sometimes accompanied (esp. during transitions) by labour-market disruptions; technology brings deep structural economic change, creating new jobs/sectors while destroying/modifying others — with major consequences for certain groups, especially low-skilled workers. 4. Growing concerns: in a context of high/rising inequality & polarization in the labour market, people worry about the future workforce. Past experience shows both rising incomes and stable labour share because new technologies generated new tasks that offset the job losses induced by automation. 5. The future of work depends on how AI, robots & automation allocate tasks between labour and capital, and on government preparation/measures to support the population during the transition so the technologies are inclusive and beneficial to all.

✍️ Reading skill: main idea of intro; frequent cohesive connector (contrastive — "however" links paragraphs); writer's view (positive/neutral); text type (expository/argumentative); true/false; opposing views in two sentences; word guessing (prospects, sustained, disruptions, polarization, induced, allocation, inclusive); pronoun reference (they, others, it); write a one-paragraph summary.
