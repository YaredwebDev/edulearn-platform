3.4 Vertical Motion and Free Fall

◌ Not started

Free fall: when things move vertically (up or down) under the force of gravity alone, the resulting motion is free fall. The acceleration is constant, called the acceleration due to gravity, g. Near Earth's surface g ≈ 9.8 m/s² (acting downward). Air resistance is neglected.

Equations of freely falling bodies (take down as +y or up as +y consistently; g is the acceleration magnitude): - v = u + gt - s = ut + ½gt² - v² = u² + 2gs

✋ Trap: for an object thrown upward, at the top of its path its velocity is momentarily zero but its acceleration is still g downward (never zero). Free-fall acceleration is the same for all objects regardless of mass (in vacuum).

Terminal velocity: the point at which the velocity of a falling object no longer increases (air drag balances weight) → net force zero → zero acceleration. (Only reached when air resistance matters.)

Reaction time experiment: measure reaction time by catching a dropped ruler. Reaction distance = Reaction time × Speed. (Reaction time = time taken for a person to react to a sudden event.)

Galileo's view: (Activity compares Aristotle's, Galileo's and Newton's views of motion; Galileo argued objects fall with the same acceleration regardless of mass, against Aristotle's idea that heavier falls faster.)

✍️ Must-know formulas for vertical motion: treat as constant acceleration a = g (≈9.8 m/s²). Upward throw: use a = −g with up positive; top reached when v = 0; rise height h = u²/2g; time to top = u/g.

Typical review problems (drop a ball from a building, throw a ball vertically up then measure time to ground, rebound problem using impulse/contact time, throwing a stone into a well). The worked Activity/Example problems cover: a ball thrown up at 20 m/s from 25 m high building — (a) max height rise, (b) total time to ground; a stone dropped into a well at 10 m/s down, time to reach water 60 m below.
