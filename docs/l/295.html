4.2 The Rate of a Reaction

◌ Not started

Rate measures how fast the concentration of a reactant or product changes with time.

For a reaction Reactants → Products, reactants are consumed (concentration ↓) and products formed (concentration ↑). So we monitor the decrease in [reactant] or the increase in [product].

  • Rate = Δc/Δt = (change in concentration)/(change in time).
  • For A → B: rate = −Δ[A]/Δt = Δ[B]/Δt. The minus sign is needed because Δ[A] is negative (A disappears); rate must be positive.

Handling coefficients (from the general reaction aA + bB → cC + dD):

✍️ Must-know rate expression: rate = −(1/a)·Δ[A]/Δt = −(1/b)·Δ[B]/Δt = +(1/c)·Δ[C]/Δt = +(1/d)·Δ[D]/Δt

Worked Example 4.1: rate expressions: - (a) I⁻ + OCl⁻ → Cl⁻ + OI⁻ (all coefficients 1): rate = −Δ[I⁻]/Δt = −Δ[OCl⁻]/Δt = Δ[Cl⁻]/Δt = Δ[OI⁻]/Δt. - (b) 3O₂ → 2O₃: rate = −(1/3)Δ[O₂]/Δt = +(1/2)Δ[O₃]/Δt. - (c) 4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O: rate = −(1/4)Δ[NH₃]/Δt = −(1/5)Δ[O₂]/Δt = +(1/4)Δ[NO]/Δt = +(1/6)Δ[H₂O]/Δt.

Worked Example 4.1(2): 2NO + 2H₂ → N₂ + 2H₂O; if −Δ[NO]/Δt = 5.0×10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹s⁻¹, then rate of N₂ formation = (1/2)×(rate NO disappearance) = 2.5×10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹s⁻¹.

✋ Trap: a reactant with coefficient 2 is consumed twice as fast as one with coefficient 1; products form at rates proportional to their coefficients (NO₂ forms 4× faster than O₂ in 2N₂O₅ → 4NO₂ + O₂).

Determination of rate of reaction. Rate = change in an observable property over time (colour, temperature, pressure, mass loss, precipitate). Example: thermal decomposition 2N₂O₅(g) → 4NO₂(g) + O₂(g) (colourless → brown NO₂). Monitor pressure rise (2 → 5 moles of gas) or the brown colour. From Table 4.1: - 300–400 s: rate of formation of O₂ = (0.0049 − 0.0040)/(400−300) = 9×10⁻⁶ mol L⁻¹s⁻¹. - Rate of formation of NO₂ (300–400 s) = 3.7×10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹s⁻¹ = 4× the O₂ rate (matches the 4:1 coefficient ratio). - Rate of decomposition of N₂O₅ (300–400 s) = 1.9×10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹s⁻¹. - Rate is fast at the start and decreases as the reaction proceeds (reactant concentration falls). - Average rate is over a time interval; instantaneous rate is the slope of a tangent to the concentration–time curve at a specific time; the initial rate is the instantaneous rate at t = 0 (tangent at the start). Rate must always be reported with the time.

✍️ Must-know: rate decreases with time because reactant concentration decreases. Graph slope (hypotenuse) = average rate; steeper slope = faster rate. Draw a tangent for instantaneous/initial rate.

Worked Example 4.2 (using Table 4.1): if [N₂O₅] = 1.20×10⁻² mol L⁻¹ at 300 s, find [NO₂], [O₂] at 300 s. N₂O₅ reacted = 2.00×10⁻² − 1.20×10⁻² = 0.8×10⁻² mol L⁻¹. Since each N₂O₅ gives 2 NO₂ and ½ O₂: [NO₂] = 2×0.8×10⁻² = 1.6×10⁻² M; [O₂] = ½×0.8×10⁻² = 0.4×10⁻² M (per the balanced equation).

Worked Example 4.3 (instantaneous rate): H₂ + 2ICl → I₂ + 2HCl; given [H₂] and [ICl] vs time (0–8 s). Plot [H₂] vs time, draw a tangent at t = 2 s; instantaneous rate = −(slope of tangent).
