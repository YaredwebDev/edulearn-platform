6.2 Alcohols — definition & classification

◌ Not started
  • Alcohols are derivatives of hydrocarbons in which one or more H atoms are replaced by a hydroxyl group (–OH). The –OH group is directly attached to a saturated (aliphatic) carbon.
  • Only ethanol is present in all alcoholic beverages. Methanol (industrial), 2-propanol etc. are different.

Classified by number of –OH groups: - Monohydric (one –OH): ethanol, 1-propanol, 1-butanol. - Dihydric (two –OH, glycols/diols): ethane-1,2-diol (ethylene glycol), 1,3-propanediol. - Trihydric (three –OH): propane-1,2,3-triol (glycerol/glycerine). - Polyhydric (≥3 –OH): e.g. glycerol, longer sugar alcohols.

Classified by carbon bearing –OH (for monohydric alcohols): - Primary (1°): the –OH carbon is attached to one alkyl group (R–CH₂–OH). E.g. ethanol, 1-propanol, 1-butanol, 2-methyl-1-butanol. - Secondary (2°): –OH carbon attached to two alkyl groups (R₂CH–OH). E.g. 2-propanol, 2-butanol, 3-pentanol. - Tertiary (3°): –OH carbon attached to three alkyl groups (R₃C–OH). E.g. 2-methyl-2-propanol, 2-methyl-2-butanol.

✍️ Must-know: count alkyl groups on the carbon holding –OH to classify 1°/2°/3° alcohol. The only alcohol in drinks is ethanol; antifreeze = ethane-1,2-diol; glycerol = propane-1,2,3-triol.
