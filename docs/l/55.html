9.5 Dairy Cattle Housing Management

◌ Not started
  • Two common dairy houses: loose housing & conventional barn (see Unit 7). Housing type depends on climate, tradition, building supplies. Loose housing with a milking parlour is ideal for tropical regions like Ethiopia.
  • Housing must be appropriate size (stand/lie easily); reduces respiratory infections & injuries; proper ventilation (fresh, pure, dust-free air); good drainage; floor of rough concrete finish with ~1.5% slope toward the drain, not slippery.

  • Sanitation: key to maximising milk quantity & quality; prevents contamination from milking procedures, equipment, milk-contact surfaces, handlers, storage/packaging. Sanitation routines = regular cleaning of floors, bedding, walls, feeding surfaces; modern sanitation equipment + properly diluted chemicals prevent mastitis, ringworm, mange, foot rot and enteric diseases (E. coli, Salmonella). Flies/insects disturb animals and spread bovine babesiosis and theileriosis.
  • Manure management: manure is a valuable nutrient source for crops & improves soil health. A large cow produces 20–30 kg faeces + 10–14 kg urine per day plus shed effluent. Manure contains nitrogen, phosphorus, carbon — mismanaged, it causes smell and groundwater/water-course pollution.
  • Most common method: storage in a pit and spreading. Pit should hold ~2–3 days' manure; wide channels for urine & faeces removal, a concrete-lined channel from floor to pit. Manure applied as natural fertiliser, composted, or used for biogas production.
