2.2 Taxonomy of Living Things

2.2.1 Principles of Classification

  • Classification: the process of grouping things based on their similarities.
  • Taxonomy: the science of naming, identifying, and classifying organisms.
  • Taxonomists: scientists who study taxonomy.
  • Biologists classify organisms by degrees of apparent similarities and differences — external and internal structures, where the organism lives, and genetic makeup to reveal evolutionary relationships.
  • The greater the physical similarity, the closer the biological relationship is assumed.

Key terms: Identification = identifying organisms using characteristic features; Nomenclature = the aspect of taxonomy dealing specifically with naming organisms.

2.2.2 Taxonomic Hierarchies

  • Aristotle (384–322 BC), Greek philosopher, developed the first widely accepted classification system — grouped plants into trees, shrubs, herbs; animals into those with/without red blood.
  • Carolus Linnaeus (1707–1778), Swedish botanist, "father of taxonomy," introduced a taxonomic hierarchy in the 1700s.
  • Taxonomic hierarchy: arranging organisms into successive levels in decreasing or increasing order.
  • Ranks from highest to lowest: Kingdom → Phylum (Division) → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species.
  • Each rank is a taxon (plural: taxa).
  • Linnaeus developed a two-kingdom system (Plantae and Animalia), which did not distinguish eukaryotes/prokaryotes, unicellular/multicellular, or photosynthetic/non-photosynthetic organisms.
  • Three domains (rank above kingdom): Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya.
  • Bacteria: prokaryotic cells (no nucleus), small; cell wall contains peptidoglycans; use chlorophyll for photosynthesis; divide by binary fission.
  • Archaea: prokaryotic; cell wall does NOT contain peptidoglycans; often live in extreme environments.
  • Eukarya: cells with a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; includes protists, fungi, plants, animals; DNA as linear chromosomes with histone proteins; reproduce asexually and sexually.
✍️ Must-know: Taxonomic hierarchy: Kingdom → Phylum/Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species. (Plant taxonomists use "division" instead of phylum.) Three domains: Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya.
