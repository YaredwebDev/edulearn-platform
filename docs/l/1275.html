7.7 Thermal Expansion of Materials

  • Thermal expansion: the increase in size of a substance when heated (particles move faster and farther apart). Most materials expand when heated and contract when cooled.
  • In general: gases expand more than liquids, and liquids expand more than solids.
  • Different materials expand at different rates.
  • Linear expansion: the increase in any one dimension (length) of a solid.
  • Coefficient of linear expansion (α): the increase in length of a 1 m rod of a given substance when its temperature increases by 1 K. Unit: 1/°C (or °C⁻¹).

Formula:

  • ΔL = α·L₀·ΔT (7.11), where:
  • ΔL = change in length, L₀ = original length, ΔT = change in temperature, α = coefficient of linear expansion.

Coefficients of linear expansion (α, in °C⁻¹): Aluminum 2.6×10⁻⁵, Brass 1.9×10⁻⁵, Concrete 1.2×10⁻⁵, Copper 1.7×10⁻⁵, Glass (common) 8.5×10⁻⁶, Glass (Pyrex) 3.3×10⁻⁶, Gold 1.4×10⁻⁵, Iron/steel 1.2×10⁻⁵, Lead 2.9×10⁻⁵, Nickel 1.3×10⁻⁵, Quartz (fused) 0.5×10⁻⁶, Silver 1.9×10⁻⁵.

Note: Example 7.5: Gold rod L₀ = 1.5×10⁻¹ m, heated from 27 °C to 49 °C. ΔL = (1.4×10⁻⁵)(1.5×10⁻¹)(22) = 4.62×10⁻⁵ m.
Note: Example 7.6: Brass rod L₀ = 50 cm, heated 25 °C → 70 °C. ΔL = (1.9×10⁻⁵)(0.5)(45) = 0.043 cm → final length = 50.043 cm.

Applications/notes:

  • Run hot water over a tight metal lid to loosen it (metal expands more than glass).
  • Bimetallic strip: two thin strips of metals with different coefficients welded together. When heated, it bends (toward the metal with the smaller α); used in thermostats and switches.
  • A hole in a solid expands when the solid is heated (expands with the same percent as the material).
  • For construction, choose metals that don't vary much with temperature (e.g., fused quartz has very small α).
