4.6 Male Reproductive Structures

◌ Not started

Organs: paired testes, epididymis, vas deferens, accessory glands (seminal vesicles, prostate gland, bulbourethral glands), and penis. - Testes: sites of sperm production; composed of seminiferous tubules (where sperm develop); between the tubules are cells producing testosterone. Housed in the scrotum (outside the abdominal cavity, providing a slightly lower temperature needed for viable sperm). - Epididymis: where sperm maturation occurs. - Vas deferens: the ejaculatory duct that joins the urethra (carries both sperm and urine through the penis). - Accessory glands: seminal vesicles (secrete fructose-rich fluid), prostate gland (produces alkaline secretions that raise the pH of the female reproductive tract), bulbourethral glands (secrete lubricating mucus that clears the urethra of residual urine). These secretions provide food, lubricate, and counteract vaginal acidity. - Semen: a mix of sperm, proteins, nutrients, ions, signalling molecules. Sperm constitute less than 5% of semen volume.

Sperm structure (relation to function): - Head: packed with genetic material, covered by an enzyme that helps penetrate the egg. - Mid-section: contains many mitochondria supplying energy for flagellar movement. - Flagellum: used to swim toward the egg.

✍️ Must-know: Sperm produced in the seminiferous tubules of the testes; mature in the epididymis; travel via vas deferens and urethra. Seminal vesicles = fructose; prostate = alkaline; bulbourethral = lubricating mucus. Testosterone = male hormone.
