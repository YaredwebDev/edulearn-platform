6.1 Common Characteristics of Waves

◌ Not started
  • Oscillation (periodic motion): a to-and-fro motion about a fixed point (equilibrium position) that repeats itself. Examples: a swing, a pendulum, a mass on a spring.
  • Wave: a disturbance that moves from its source accompanied by the transfer of energy. A wave is comprised of a series/train of pulses traveling in a medium.

Common characteristics of all waves: - Rest position: the undisturbed position of particles when they are not vibrating. - Displacement: the distance a point in the medium has moved from its rest position. - Trough: the lowest point below the rest position. - Crest: the highest point above the rest position. - Period (T): the time for one complete cycle (or for one complete wave to pass a given point). Unit: second (s). - Frequency (f): the number of complete waves passing a point per unit time. Unit: Hertz (Hz) = cycle/s. - f = 1/T and T = 1/f (6.1) — period is the reciprocal of frequency. - Amplitude (A): the maximum displacement from the equilibrium position. Unit: meter (m). - Wave speed (v): the distance a wave travels in one second. Unit: m/s. - Wavelength (λ): the distance between identical points on adjacent waves (e.g., between two adjacent crests or troughs). Unit: meter. - Wave speed = frequency × wavelength: v = λ × f (6.2)

Example: A wave of frequency 2 Hz means 2 waves pass per second, so each takes 0.5 s → period = 0.5 s.
