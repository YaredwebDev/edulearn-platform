4.9 Genetic Disorders

◌ Not started
  • Genetic disorder = a disease caused by a change/mutation in an individual's DNA. Mutation = a change in DNA sequence from replication errors or environmental factors. Examples named: diabetes, cancer, Down syndrome, Turner syndrome, hemophilia, cystic fibrosis, albinism.
  • Three main categories:

4.9.1 Single-gene (monogenic) disorders

Caused by defects/mutations in one particular gene; three inheritance patterns: - Autosomal dominant — expressed in the heterozygous condition; on the autosomes, inherited the same regardless of sex. Example: Huntington's disease (progressive neurodegenerative, appears later in life). - Autosomal recessive — expressed in homozygous condition; occurs when both parents carry the gene and the child gets a defective gene from each (parents are unaffected carriers). Examples: cystic fibrosis, albinism. - X-linked — on sex chromosomes; females need two recessive copies (XʰXʰ) so X-linked recessive disorders are less common in females. Example: hemophilia (blood-clotting disorder).

4.9.2 Chromosomal disorders

Result from changes in chromosome number or structure (usually errors in cell division). Types: - Down syndrome / trisomy 21 — three copies of chromosome 21 (2n+1). - Aneuploidy — wrong number of chromosomes (2n−1, 2n+1, etc.). - Deletion — part of a chromosome missing. - Inversion — a segment flips around and reinserts. - Translocation — a segment moves from one location to another.

4.9.3 Multifactorial disorders

Caused by changes in multiple genes interacting with environmental/lifestyle factors (smoking, alcohol, poor diet, little sleep, pollution). Examples: diabetes, cancer.

✍️ Must-know examples: Huntington = autosomal dominant; cystic fibrosis & albinism = autosomal recessive; hemophilia = X-linked recessive; Down syndrome = trisomy 21 (chromosomal); diabetes & cancer = multifactorial.
