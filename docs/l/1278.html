📖 GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS (quick definitions for revision)

Absolute zero – the lowest possible temperature (0 K = −273.15 °C), where a substance has no thermal energy.

Acceleration – the rate of change of velocity (m/s²).

Accuracy – closeness of a measurement to the true value.

Acoustics – the branch of physics that studies sound and its transmission, production, and effects.

Amplitude (A) – the maximum displacement from the equilibrium position.

Antinode – a position of maximum displacement in a standing wave.

Astrophysics – uses physics to study astronomical objects and phenomena.

Average speed – total distance divided by total time.

Average velocity – total displacement divided by total time (a vector).

Bimetallic strip – two thin strips of metals with different coefficients of linear expansion welded together (bends when heated).

Classical physics – physics developed from the Renaissance to the end of the 19th century.

Compound machine – a machine made of a combination of simple machines.

Compression – a region where particles are pushed together (in a longitudinal wave).

Contact force – a force that involves physical contact between objects.

Coefficient of linear expansion (α) – the increase in length of a 1 m rod of a substance when its temperature rises by 1 K.

Crest – the highest point above the rest position of a wave.

Deceleration – the negative of acceleration (slowing down).

Decibel (dB) – the unit of loudness of sound.

Density – mass per unit volume (kg/m³).

Derived physical quantity – a quantity that depends on one or more fundamental quantities (e.g., speed, density, force).

Derived unit – a unit that depends on fundamental units (e.g., N, J, Pa).

Diffraction – the spreading out/change in direction of a wave passing through an opening or around a barrier.

Direction changer – a machine that changes the direction of force.

Displacement – the change in an object's position (a vector).

Distance – the total path length traveled by a body (a scalar).

Dynamics – the branch of mechanics dealing with forces that cause motion.

Echo – the reflection of sound from hard surfaces.

Effort (F) – the force exerted on a machine.

Efficiency (η) – the ratio of work output to work input (= M.A/V.R).

Electromagnetic wave – a wave that does not need a medium (light, radio, X-rays).

Electromagnetism – the branch of physics studying electric and magnetic fields.

Energy – the capacity to do work (Joule).

Force – a push or a pull that changes the state of motion or shape of a body (vector, newton).

Force multiplier – a machine where the load is greater than the effort.

Frame of reference – a set of coordinates used to determine the positions of objects.

Frequency (f) – the number of complete waves passing a point per unit time (Hz).

Friction – the resistance to motion between surfaces in contact.

Fulcrum – the pivot point of a lever.

Fundamental (basic) physical quantity – a quantity measured directly (length, mass, time, temperature, current, amount of substance, luminous intensity).

Fundamental unit – the unit of a fundamental quantity (m, kg, s, K, A, mol, cd).

Gravitational potential energy – the energy of an object due to its height/position (E_p = mgh).

Greenhouse effect – the warming of the Earth when greenhouse gases trap heat from the Sun.

Hooke's law – restoring force is proportional to displacement (F = −kx).

Inertia – the tendency of an object to resist change in its velocity; mass is a measure of inertia.

Instantaneous speed/velocity – the speed/velocity at a particular instant.

Interference – the superposition of two or more waves (constructive or destructive).

Kinetic energy – the energy of an object due to its motion (E_k = ½mv²).

Kinetic friction – friction when one surface slides/rolls over another.

Lever – a rigid bar free to turn about a fulcrum.

Load (L) – the force exerted by a machine.

Longitudinal wave – a wave where the propagation is parallel to particle vibration (sound).

Loudness – the perception of the energy of sound; depends on amplitude (dB).

Machine – a device designed to do mechanical work easily.

Mechanical advantage (M.A) – the ratio of load to effort.

Mechanical energy – the sum of kinetic and potential energies.

Mechanics – the branch of physics dealing with motion and force.

Motion – a continuous change in position of an object relative to a reference frame.

Newton's first law – a body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by an unbalanced force (law of inertia).

Newton's second law – acceleration is proportional to net force and inversely proportional to mass (F = ma).

Newton's third law – every action has an equal and opposite reaction (F₁₂ = −F₂₁).

Node – a position of zero displacement in a standing wave.

Non-contact force – a force that does not involve physical contact (magnetic, gravitational).

Nuclear physics – the branch of physics studying atomic nuclei.

Optics – the branch of physics studying the behavior and properties of light.

Oscillation (periodic motion) – a to-and-fro motion about an equilibrium position.

Period (T) – the time for one complete cycle/wave.

Physics – the branch of natural science that describes the basic mechanisms of the universe.

Physicist – a person who studies physics.

Pitch – the highness/lowness of a sound; depends on frequency.

Power – the rate of doing work or transferring energy (P = W/t, watt).

Precision – the closeness of repeated measurements to each other.

Pressure – force per unit area (pascal).

Pulse – a single traveling disturbance.

Rarefaction – a region where particles move apart (in a longitudinal wave).

Reference frame – same as frame of reference.

Refraction (sound) – the bending of a wave due to a change in its speed.

Scalar quantity – a quantity with only magnitude (distance, mass, time, energy).

Scale – a set of numbers used to measure or compare the level of something.

Scientific notation – a number written as d × 10ⁿ (1 ≤ d < 10).

Significant figures – the meaningful digits in a measurement.

Simple machine – a device that makes doing work easier using a single force.

Spring constant (k) – the stiffness of a spring (N/m).

Static friction – friction when a body tends to slide but does not move.

Superposition principle – resultant displacement is the sum of individual wave displacements.

Temperature – the degree of hotness/coldness; measure of average kinetic energy.

Thermal expansion – the increase in size of a substance when heated.

Thermocouple – a thermometer using voltage (temperature difference between two junctions).

Thermometer – a device for measuring temperature.

Timbre (quality) – the property distinguishing tones of the same pitch/intensity from different sources.

Transverse wave – a wave where propagation is perpendicular to particle vibration.

Trough – the lowest point below the rest position of a wave.

Uniform motion – motion in a straight line with constant velocity/speed.

Vector quantity – a quantity with both magnitude and direction (displacement, velocity, force).

Velocity – the rate of change of displacement (a vector).

Velocity ratio (V.R) – the ratio of distance moved by effort to distance moved by load.

Watt (W) – the SI unit of power (1 W = 1 J/s).

Wavelength (λ) – the distance between identical points on adjacent waves.

Weight – the gravitational force on a body (W = mg).

Work – the product of force and displacement in the direction of the force (Joule).
