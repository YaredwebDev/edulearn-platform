6B.1 Pronunciation: consonants, nasal & affricative sounds

  • Consonants: sounds like /ð/ (the, father, them, that) vs /θ/ (think, birthday, south, math). Place & manner of articulation determine the sound.
  • Nasal sounds — air is completely blocked in the mouth and passes through the nose. The three English nasals:
  • /m/ — lips closed (bilabial): mad, clam.
  • /n/ — tongue tip pressed against the alveolar ridge (alveolar): no, man.
  • /ŋ/ — back of the tongue against the soft palate (velar): going, fun, song, sing.
  • Affricative sounds — two oral affricates, both post-alveolar:
  • /tʃ/ — chick, match.
  • /dʒ/ — jam, badge.
  • Strategies for learning pronunciation: listen to yourself; slow down; picture the sound; pronounce it out physically; watch yourself in a mirror; copy experts/native speakers; practise alone; find a language partner for feedback; pay attention to intonation; sing songs.

6B.2 Likes & dislikes / preferences

  • Use prefer / would prefer / would rather (review from Unit 2).
  • Meaning: prefer is used to talk generally about likes/dislikes/what we want (He prefers reading books); would prefer and would rather are more specific to a choice (I would rather go home now).
  • Form: prefer/would prefer can be followed by the gerund (-ing) or infinitive (to + verb); would rather is followed by the base form (verb without "to"): I prefer living in a city; I would prefer to be told the truth; Would you rather stay at a hotel?

6B.3 Panel discussion

  • A panel discussion involves a moderator (guides discussion, elicits questions), panelists (share knowledge, informative & entertaining), and an audience (listens & asks). Hold one to solve global-warming problems; prepare notes; then make a public speech on the results.

6B.4 Debate

  • Debate the proposition "It is difficult to reduce global warming." Use arguing expressions: My objection is…; I agree/disagree with the idea that…; I didn't agree with…; In my view…; In my opinion…
