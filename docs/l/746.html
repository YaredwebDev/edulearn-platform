4.5 Coulomb's Law of Electrostatics

◌ Not started

Charles Coulomb (1785): the electrostatic force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

F = k·|q₁|·|q₂| / r² - k = 1/(4πε₀) ≈ 9×10⁹ N·m²/C² (electrostatic constant). - ε₀ = 8.85×10⁻¹² C²/N·m² (permittivity of free space). - Force is directed along the line joining the charges: attractive if charges have unlike signs, repulsive if like signs.

Example 4.1: q₁ = 5.0 µC, q₂ = −12.0 µC, r = 30 cm = 0.3 m. F = 9×10⁹ × (5×10⁻⁶)(12×10⁻⁶)/(0.3)² = 6.0 N, attractive.

✍️ Must-know: F = k·q₁q₂/r², k = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C². Like charges repel, unlike attract.
