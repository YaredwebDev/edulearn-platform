4.11 Electrical Safety

◌ Not started
  • The fluids inside the body are good conductors; dry skin has much higher resistance (insulates like the plastic around a wire).
  • Damage from electric shock depends on: magnitude of current, duration, and the part of the body it passes through.
  • 5 mA or less → mild sensation, little damage.

  • 10 mA → hand muscles contract (can't let go).

  • ~100 mA for a few seconds → can be fatal (paralyses respiratory muscles).
  • ~1 A → serious/fatal burns.
  • Fuse: the cheapest protection device; a low-melting-point metal wire inserted in the circuit. During a short circuit or overload, excess current melts the fuse, breaking the circuit. Protects machines/equipment.
  • Ground/case wire (third wire): if a "phase" (live) wire touches the metal case, the current goes to ground through the third wire, blowing the fuse/tripping a breaker before harming the person.
  • Color coding in Ethiopia: phase = red/yellow/blue; ground = greenish-yellowish; neutral = black.
  • Ground-fault interrupters (GFIs / RCDs): shut off current (in <1 ms) when small currents (~5 mA or more) leak to ground; used in kitchens, bathrooms, etc.

Safety tips: never use frayed/damaged cords; unplug appliances before working on them; avoid water near plugged-in appliances; never touch power lines with anything; respect warning signs.

✍️ Must-know: Never handle switches with wet hands; never throw water on an electric fire (impure water conducts electricity).
