12.5 Biodiversity Conservation and its Status in Ethiopia

◌ Not started
  • Biodiversity conservation = the planned management of biodiversity in an ecosystem to prevent overexploitation, pollution & destruction, and to ensure biodiversity is maintained.
  • Major objectives: maintain a sustainable use of species & the ecosystem; maintain life-supporting systems; maintain essential ecological processes.
  • Why conservation is needed: human activities (habitat destruction, invasive species, harvesting) have caused huge losses. Species diversity lets researchers develop improved varieties; diverse plants serve food production & medicines; biodiversity gives regulatory & cultural services. In Ethiopia, ~85% of the population lives in rural areas and depends on natural resources → conservation is vital for livelihoods.
  • Biodiversity conservation in Ethiopia:
  • Diverse ecosystems at different altitudes with plant genetic resources: field crops; horticultural crops (fruits & vegetables); wild plants; medicinal plants; forests; rangelands & forage plants.
  • Rich animal & wildlife genetic resources. Farm-animal genetic resources include indigenous cattle & other domestic breeds. ~29 mammal types and 18 bird species are endemic to Ethiopia.
  • Threats in Ethiopia: deforestation, habitat destruction, poor regeneration; expansion of invasive species; clearing forest for agriculture; forest fires; illegal tree harvest (threatens medicinal plants). Overgrazing/over-browsing, invasive species, and conversion of grazing land to cultivated land threaten forage resources. Efforts: clearing invasive species, selecting & multiplying locally adapted forage, enclosing degraded rangelands, banning open grazing.
  • Wild animals threatened by expansion of agriculture & grazing near habitats, deforestation, overgrazing & overharvesting (fish), unbalanced water use & siltation. Endangered wild animals: Walia Ibex (Capra walie), Mountain Nyala (Tragelaphus buxtoni), Ethiopian Wolf (Canis simensis).
  • Conservation efforts: involving local communities; closing forests to reduce interference; protected areas — national & regional parks, wildlife sanctuaries, wildlife reserves, botanical gardens & herbariums, controlled hunting centres.

✍️ Memorise three Ethiopian endemic/endangered mammals to name in a short answer: Walia Ibex, Mountain Nyala, Ethiopian Wolf; and the three major Ethiopian invasive species: Parthenium weed, Prosopis juliflora, Water hyacinth.
