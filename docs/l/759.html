5.4 Magnetic Field of a Current-Carrying Conductor

◌ Not started

A moving charge (or current) produces a magnetic field. A long straight wire carrying current I produces a magnetic field in the form of closed concentric circles around the wire, lying in a plane perpendicular to the wire.

Magnitude: B = μ₀I / (2πd) - μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ T·m/A (permeability of free space). - d = perpendicular distance from the wire. - B ∝ current, B ∝ 1/d.

Units: SI unit of B = tesla (T); 1 gauss (G) = 10⁻⁴ T. Strong permanent magnets ~2 T; superconducting electromagnets ~10 T or more; Earth's field ~5×10⁻⁵ T (0.5 G).

Direction — right-hand rule: wrap the right hand's fingers around the wire with the thumb pointing in the direction of the current; the curled fingers give the direction of the magnetic field. - Reversing the current reverses the field direction.

Example 5.1: I = 3 A, d = 2 cm. B = (4π×10⁻⁷ × 3)/(2π × 0.02) = 3×10⁻⁵ T.

✍️ Must-know: B = μ₀I/2πd. B ∝ I and ∝ 1/d. Right-hand rule gives field direction (circles around the wire).
