15.3 Food Groups and Their Sources

◌ Not started

A food group = a collection of foods sharing similar nutritional properties or biological classification, grouped by the nutrient they contain in abundance. Typically six groups. Most people (esp. the poor) eat only one/two staple foods, so diversification is important. A healthy diet can be achieved by consuming food from at least four food groups.

Food group Functions Examples
Vegetables mostly vitamins, minerals, water; fibre for digestion & to prevent constipation tomato, cabbage
Fruits mostly carbohydrates, vitamins, minerals, water oranges, apples
Legumes & nuts mainly protein — growth, repair, body building; also fat & carbohydrates soybean, other legumes, nuts
Animal foods protein, fats, vitamins, minerals; growth & strong bones meat, egg, milk
Fats & oils extra energy (fat), essential fatty acids, fat-soluble vitamins oil seeds (soybean, sesame, linseed, groundnut), cooking oil, margarine
Staples energy (proteins, vitamins, minerals depending on processing) cereal grains (sorghum, maize, barley, oats, wheat, teff, rice); starchy roots (sweet potato, cassava, false banana/enset); starchy fruits (banana)
