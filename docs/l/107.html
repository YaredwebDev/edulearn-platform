1.2 Biology and Technology

◌ Not started
  • Biology = the study of life: structures, functions, growth, origins, evolution and distribution of living organisms.
  • Technology = the application of scientific knowledge, skills, methods and processes for the production of devices and tools for scientific investigations (also "the sum of techniques, skills and processes used in the production of goods/services").
  • The blend of biology and technology forms biotechnology — a technology that utilises biological systems to develop useful products. It is the integration of natural and engineering sciences to apply organisms, cells, parts and molecular analogues for products/services. Example: people use yeasts to make bread and beer.

✍️ Must-know: biology + technology = biotechnology. Biotechnology uses biological systems to develop products (exam MCQ "Biotechnology is a technology that utilises ___ → biological systems"). Designing tech from nature = imitation; "the system of manufacturing biological elements" = synthetic biology.

1.2.1 The benefits of biology to technology

Biology supplies materials that let us imitate, design, adapt and develop technologies and solve complex human problems; the diversity of nature stimulates new technologies. 1. Medicinal plants — plant biochemical substances used to develop medicines; scientists imitate nature to make artificial versions. 2. Gene engineering & biosynthetic materials — imitating genes enables gene engineering to cure genetic conditions and the making of biosynthetic materials to repair damaged body tissues. 3. Building design — termite-mound architecture → buildings with constant temperature/humidity chimneys. 4. Bullet train design — the nose of bullet trains imitates the beak of the kingfisher as it dives into water.

💡 Inquiry 1.2 (match technology → natural source): hexagonal structures ← honeycombs/bees; drone design ← insects/birds; preserving live vaccines ← natural cryo/sugar-protection mechanisms; repellent sprays ← natural plant defences; cutting saws ← animal teeth.

1.2.2 Uses of technology in biology

The uses of technology in biology = how technological tools solve human problems and support biological investigation (the practical application of biotechnological instruments/services).

Fields of biological study that use technology: 1. Biochemical studies — investigate carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids. 2. Biomedical studies — detailed chemical information on medicinal plants. 3. Biophysical studies — use physical devices to gather biological information at all scales (molecular, organismic, population). 4. Environmental studies — multidisciplinary study of human–environment interactions. 5. Bioinformatics — computer technology to collect, store, analyse and disseminate biological data (DNA & amino-acid sequences). 6. Biogeographical information — distribution of species across geographic ecosystems through geological periods.

Key technological devices (memorise what each does): - Digital thermometer — measures body temperature. - Pregnancy urine test — detects the hormone Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (HCG), produced in the placenta ~6–10 days after fertilisation, in urine/blood. One line = control (negative); two lines = pregnancy (positive). - Diabetic (blood sugar) test — measures blood glucose from a finger-tip blood sample. - HIV test — detects HIV infection. - Microscope — magnifies objects. - CITS (Computer Information Technology Scanning) — investigates diseases/cancerous areas of the body. - CT scan (Computed Tomography) — sophisticated X-ray technology taking many X-ray pictures to detect/screen diseases. - PET scan (Positron Emission Tomography) — imaging that checks for cancer/disease; uses a dye with radioactive tracers (swallowed, inhaled, or injected); visualises changes in metabolic processes & physiological activity (blood flow, chemical composition, absorption). - GPS (Global/Geographical Position System) — collects biogeographical data on landscape mapping, plants, animals, human movements. - Handheld body-fat calculator — measures body fat.

✍️ Must-know device–use matching: body temperature → digital thermometer; pregnant? → urine HCG test; blood sugar → diabetic test; HIV → HIV test; disease/cancer imaging → CITS/CT/PET; magnifying → microscope; geographic/geographic data → GPS; body fat → handheld calculator.

Lab activity 1.3: measure body temperatures of 10 students, graph highest & lowest; visit a health centre to discuss pregnancy, diabetic & HIV tests.
