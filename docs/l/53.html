9.3 Feeding Management of Dairy Cattle

◌ Not started
  • Feeding management matters because (1) nutrition maintains health & productivity, and (2) feed alone is 60–70% of the cost of milk production. Balance the diet and avoid overfeeding.
  • Feed types: roughages (grazing, hay, silage, roots; e.g. Timothy, Clover, Alfalfa) and concentrates (supplementary, not staple — high-energy feeds + protein supplements).
  • Concentrate percentage should be higher in the first 4 months of lactation (high production); reduce concentrates for low producers / late lactation to cut costs.
  • General rule: two-thirds of daily dry matter from roughages, remaining third from concentrates.
  • Dry matter (DM) intake: an efficient milking cow needs DM ≈ at least 3% of body weight daily (e.g. 400 kg → 12 kg/day). Higher-producing cows need >4% (e.g. 450 kg high producer >25 L/day → 18 kg/day). Maximising DM intake feeds rumen microbes → more nutrients for milk, growth, reproduction, body condition.
  • Indicators of enough DM: milk yield/composition on target, feed left in troughs, no cows "waiting to be fed", body condition on target.
  • Indicators of not enough: low/variable yield (esp. milk-fat %), cows shout & wait for feed, rush to fresh forage/troughs, eat all feed, low body condition.
  • Water: abundant cool, clean, pure water is vital. To produce 1 L milk a cow needs ~5 L water (varies with stress/weather/disease/etc.). Place water near feed sources and in milking-parlour return alleys; troughs: minimum 10 cm length per cow at ~90 cm height.
