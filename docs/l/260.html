6.1 Food Chain, Food Web, and Ecological Pyramids

◌ Not started

6.1.1 Ecological Terms

  • Ecology: the scientific study of the interactions between organisms and their environment.
  • Population: all the organisms of the same species living in a particular area.
  • Community: all the populations of different species living and interacting in an area.
  • Ecosystem: a community of organisms together with their non-living (abiotic) environment.
  • Habitat: the place where an organism lives.
  • Biotic factors: living components (producers, consumers, decomposers).
  • Abiotic factors: non-living components (temperature, light, water, soil, air).

6.1.2 Trophic Levels and Food Chains

  • Producers (autotrophs): make their own food by photosynthesis (green plants, algae). = Trophic level 1.
  • Primary consumers (herbivores): eat producers. = Trophic level 2.
  • Secondary consumers (carnivores): eat primary consumers. = Trophic level 3.
  • Tertiary consumers (top carnivores): eat secondary consumers. = Trophic level 4.
  • Decomposers/saprophytes (bacteria, fungi): break down dead organic matter, recycling nutrients.

Food chain: a linear sequence of organisms in which each organism is eaten by the next.

Example: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk.

Food web: an interconnected network of many food chains; more realistic than a food chain (which is unidirectional; a food web is multi-directional/interwoven).

6.1.3 Energy Transfer (10% Rule)

  • Only about 10% of the energy in one trophic level is transferred to the next.
  • About 90% is lost as heat, work, and undigested/egested material (faeces).
  • This limits food chains to usually 3–4 trophic levels (too little energy remains for a 5th level).

6.1.4 Ecological Pyramids

Pyramid of energy: always upright (energy always decreases up the levels); never inverted. Pyramid of numbers: can be inverted (e.g., one tree → many birds → many fleas). Pyramid of biomass: can be inverted (e.g., in aquatic ecosystems: small biomass of phytoplankton supports a larger biomass of zooplankton/tilapia: Chlorella → Daphnia → Tilapia).

Pyramid type Shape Can it be inverted?
Energy Always upright No
Numbers Usually upright Yes (tree → birds → fleas)
Biomass Usually upright Yes (aquatic: Chlorella → Daphnia → Tilapia)

Biomass: the total mass of living organisms at a trophic level at a given time (dry weight often used).

✍️ Must-know: Only ~10% energy passes to the next level (90% lost as heat/work/egestion). Pyramid of energy is always upright.
