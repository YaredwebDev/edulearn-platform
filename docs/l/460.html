8F Writing Skills

◌ Not started

8F.1 Mechanics and punctuation

  • Mechanics = conventions governing the technical aspects of writing (spelling, punctuation, capitalization, abbreviations). Punctuation = the symbols that help readers process sentences (apostrophes, colons, commas, dashes, exclamation points, hyphens, periods, question marks, quotation marks, semicolons, slashes).
  • Practise correcting an unpunctuated/uncapitalised paragraph (capitalise sentence starts & "I", apostrophes for contractions/possessives, commas, etc.).

8F.2 Cohesive devices

  • Cohesive devices = words that connect words, phrases, sentences, and paragraphs to make a smooth, coherent flow of ideas (and, but, however, also, moreover, in conclusion…).
  • Categories & examples: Addition (also, moreover, in addition, furthermore, and, too, besides); Comparison (similarly, likewise, in the same way); Contrast (but, however, whereas, while, nevertheless, on the other hand, in contrast, yet, although, despite, instead); Enumeration (first(ly), second(ly), next, finally, then); Concession (of course, granted that, although it is true that, I admit that); Exemplification (for example, for instance, such as, to illustrate, specifically); Inference (then, otherwise, in that case, that implies); Summary/Conclusion (in short, in conclusion, to sum up, therefore, to summarize, in a nutshell); Time & Sequence (since, before, after, meanwhile, at first, later, next, subsequently, as soon as); Result (therefore, hence, thus, consequently, as a result, accordingly, because, since, results in, leads to).
  • Practise filling blanks with and/but/or/so/because and other cohesive devices; identify sentences that break the logical flow (cohesion) in a paragraph about reading as a hobby.

8F.3 Writing a report

  • Interview a classmate about weekend plans, take notes, and write a report of the interview, punctuating correctly and using cohesive devices.

8F.4 Argumentative essay

  • An argumentative essay uses evidence and facts to support the claim it's making; its purpose is to persuade the reader. It requires investigating a topic, collecting/generating/evaluating evidence, and establishing a position.
  • Model: replacing public libraries with iPads/tablets — identify the issue, the thesis/proposition, cohesive connectors, and compare intro & conclusion.
  • Write a five-paragraph argumentative essay on: "HIV/AIDS is not a problem now." Plan arguments, list main ideas per paragraph, and write the essay.

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) summarise the HIV/AIDS listening lecture from notes and interview/report community awareness; (2) debate, interview (with expressions), and run a panel discussion; (3) state the reading's main ideas (life-expectancy rise & demographic shift → chronic disease burden; rural–urban migration & new infectious agents; diarrheal illness/Guerrant example & the 6,000-children figure) and answer questions; (4) use the pull/put/punch phrasal verbs and give dictionary vs contextual meanings; (5) build adverbial clauses of manner & comparison, form the passive in all tenses, and use quantifiers (few/a few, little/a little, count/uncountables); and (6) punctuate correctly, use cohesive devices, write an interview report, and write a five-paragraph argumentative essay? Try the passive, quantifier, cohesive-device, and argumentative-essay exercises.
