5.4 Feed Formulation Practices

◌ Not started
  • Feed formulation = quantifying the amounts of feed ingredients to combine into a single uniform mixture meeting an animal's nutrient requirements. Requires knowledge of cost & availability of ingredients, nutrient content of feed, and nutrient requirements of the animal.

Types of rations

  • Ration = amount of feed given to an animal in a 24-hour period. Balanced ration = all nutrients in the right proportion & amount.
  • Maintenance ration: minimum feed to maintain an animal (depends on body mass/type); body mass stays constant; ~half of an animal's feed is for maintenance.
  • Production ration: additional feed (beyond maintenance) for producing milk, eggs, meat.

Characteristics of a good ration

Should satisfy total dry-matter requirement (based on weight); provide highly digestible nutrients incl. minerals & vitamins; be palatable; be fairly bulky (satisfy hunger, expel undigested material); be digestible (improved by grinding/crushing); be fresh & free of weeds/dust.

Balancing methods

Methods: Trial-and-error, Pearson Square, substitution formulation, computer-assisted. Pearson Square is the most well-known method of balancing animal diets. It calculates the portion of two feeds needed to meet a protein (CP) or energy requirement — one feed must be above and the other below the desired level.

Pearson Square steps (for CP): 1. Draw a square; put the desired %CP in the centre. 2. Place feed 1 (name + %CP) upper-left; feed 2 lower-left. 3. Subtract desired %CP from each feed across the diagonal and write results on the right (disregard sign): top-left − middle = bottom-right; bottom-left − middle = top-right. 4. Add the two right-hand numbers (= total parts); divide each by the total and multiply by the required weight to get the amount of each feed.

Example 1 (textbook): maize CP 9.5%, soybean meal (SBM) CP 42%, desired 16%. Differences: SBM 42−16=26; maize 16−9.5=6.5; total 32.5 parts. For 100 kg: maize = (26/32.5)×100 = 80 kg, SBM = (6.5/32.5)×100 = 20 kg. Exercise 5.1: broilers need 23% CP starter; maize 10.5%, SBM 37.4%. Apply the same method to 2000 kg.

✋ Trap: in Pearson Square, put the larger difference for the lower feed on the opposite (diagonal) corner and vice-versa — i.e. the difference from feed 1 becomes the portion of feed 2 and vice versa. Always disregard signs and use absolute values.
