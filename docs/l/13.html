2.9 🧮 Key facts to memorise

◌ Not started
  • Cereals = monocots, edible seeds, no N-fixation; Pulses = dicots, nitrogen fixers.
  • 5 cereals: teff, maize, wheat, barley, sorghum. 3 pulses: faba bean, field pea, haricot bean.
  • Altitudes: wheat & barley highland (1800–3000, 2200–3000); teff (1500–2800) & maize (500–2400) mid; sorghum lowland (500–1500) + dry.
  • Highest yield/ha = maize (32.5 q); largest area = teff (14.6 q avg); wheat 24.5 q; sorghum 23 q; barley 19 q; faba bean 19 q; field pea & haricot bean 14 q.
  • Gluten-free: teff, maize, sorghum. Gluten present: wheat (also barley).
  • Planting times: teff Jul/Aug; maize Mar–Apr; wheat Jun–Jul; sorghum Mar–May.
  • Fertiliser N:P per ha — teff 60:26, maize 41:20, wheat 45:25, barley 60:26 (food) & lower for malting, sorghum 32:10.
  • Herbicides: pre-emergence Atrazine; post-emergence 2,4-D (broad-leaf). Integrated pest management preferred.
  • Maize: no shattering, husk-covered seeds, black layer at maturity, store at ≤13% moisture.
  • Sorghum = 'camel crop' (dormant in drought); striga is its major parasitic weed.
  • Haricot bean = export crop; weevils in storage → dry to <12%.
  • Benefits of mixing cereals + legumes: legumes fix N → better cereal nutrition/yield.

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) define field crops and distinguish cereals from pulses (monocot vs dicot, nitrogen fixation) with examples; (2) for each of teff, maize, wheat, barley, sorghum state the origin, altitude/adaptation, land prep, planting, fertilisation, key pests/diseases/weeds, harvest indicator and national average yield; (3) for faba bean, field pea and haricot bean do the same (origin, altitude, spacing, weeding, harvest, yield, special features); (4) explain gluten-free vs gluten cereals and the special features (smooth seedbed for teff, black layer for maize, striga for sorghum, malting N for barley); and (5) explain integrated weed/pest management and the benefits of mixing cereals with legumes? Re-answer the review too (highest-yield cereal = maize; integrated weed management is most effective; wheat contains gluten; cereals are monocots; pulses are dicots & N-fixers; fertiliser rate set by soil fertility level; sorghum is lowland/water-deficit adapted).
