2.2 Chemistry as an Experimental Science

2.2.1 Scientific Method

Steps in the scientific method:

  1. Observation and asking a question.
  2. Data collection and forming a hypothesis (a tentative explanation).
  3. Testing the hypothesis through experiments.
  4. Analysis of results and drawing a conclusion.
  • A hypothesis that is confirmed by repeated experiments may be promoted to a theory (a well-tested explanation).

2.2.2 Laboratory Safety Rules and Symbols

21 key laboratory safety rules (selected must-know):

  1. Wear safety goggles at all times.
  2. Wear a laboratory coat and tie back long hair.
  3. Never work alone in the laboratory.
  4. Know the location of safety equipment (fire extinguisher, eyewash, first aid).
  5. Never taste chemicals.
  6. Never pipette liquids by mouth — use a pipette filler.
  7. Smell chemicals by wafting, never directly.
  8. Never point a test tube at yourself or others.
  9. Always pour acid into water, never water into acid (dilution rule).
  10. Do not heat flammable liquids with an open flame.
  11. Use tongs or heat-proof gloves for hot apparatus.
  12. Keep the work area clean and free of clutter.
  13. Dispose of chemicals in proper containers; never pour into the sink unless told.
  14. Read labels twice before using a chemical.
  15. Report all accidents immediately.
  16. Never run or play in the lab.
  17. Do not eat, drink, or chew gum in the lab.
  18. Wash hands after experiments.
  19. Handle glassware carefully; dispose of broken glass in a designated bin.
  20. Keep flammable materials away from flames.
  21. Follow the teacher's instructions exactly.

2.2.3 Common Laboratory Equipment

  • Beaker, Erlenmeyer (conical) flask, round-bottom flask — containers/heating.
  • Test tube, test-tube holder/rack — small-scale reactions.
  • Bunsen burner — heating source.
  • Measuring cylinder, pipette, burette, volumetric flask — measuring liquids/volume.
  • Balance (top-loading/electronic) — measuring mass.
  • Thermometer — temperature.
  • Funnel, filter paper — filtration.
  • Evaporating dish, crucible, tongs — heating/evaporating.
  • Glass rod, spatula — stirring/transferring.
  • Tripod, wire gauze, clamp stand — support.

2.2.4 Writing a Laboratory Report

Prelab preparation: read the experiment, write a prelab, know the purpose.

Lab report sections:

  1. Title, date, name.
  2. Objective/Purpose.
  3. Materials and apparatus.
  4. Procedure (written before or as you work).
  5. Observations and data (recorded as you go).
  6. Calculations (with correct sig figs and units).
  7. Results/Conclusion.
  8. Discussion/Errors.

Graph rules:

  • Give the graph a title.
  • Label both axes with quantity and unit.
  • Choose a scale so the graph fills the page (not cramped).
  • Plot points clearly; draw the best-fit line or curve.
✍️ Must-know: title, labeled axes (with units), and axes filling the page.
