5.3 Production of Some Important Nonmetals

5.3.1 General properties of nonmetals

Physical: solids/liquids/gases, non-lustrous, non-malleable, non-ductile, low density, low melting/boiling points, soft, non-sonorous, poor conductors.

Chemical: react with O₂ to form oxides; don't displace H from acids; form acidic or neutral oxides; combine with H to form stable hydrides; don't react with water; electronegative (gain electrons to form negative ions); oxidizing agents.

5.3.2 Production of N, P, O, S, Cl

A. Nitrogen (N)

  • ~80% by volume of atmosphere, as N₂ (diatomic, triple bond → very inert).

  • Compounds: NaNO₃ (Chile saltpeter), KNO₃ (saltpeter); in DNA & proteins.

  • Production: fractional distillation of liquid air (air compressed, cooled, condensed; N₂ distills off first).

  • Laboratory: NH₄Cl + NaNO₂ → NaCl + N₂ + 2H₂O.

  • Physical: colourless, odourless, tasteless; inert due to strong N≡N triple bond.

  • Chemical: at high temp reacts with reactive metals to form nitrides: 6Li + N₂ → 2Li₃N; 3Ca + N₂ → Ca₃N₂; 3Mg + N₂ → Mg₃N₂. Combines with O₂: N₂ + O₂ → 2NO; N₂ + 2O₂ → 2NO₂. NO₂ (reddish brown) dimerizes to N₂O₄ (colourless). Also N₂O (laughing gas), N₂O₃, N₂O₅.

  • Haber process: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, using finely divided Fe catalyst, 200–300 atm, 400–600°C. Ammonia used for fertilizers and HNO₃.

  • Uses: food packaging (inert), semiconductors, liquid N₂ as refrigerant (preserve semen/blood); major use = ammonia production.

B. Phosphorus (P)

  • Exists only in combined state: rock phosphate Ca₃(PO₄)₂, fluoroapatite Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆F₂. In teeth, bones, DNA.

  • Allotropes = different forms of same element, same physical state, different physical properties (due to different atom arrangement). Two common allotropes: white & red phosphorus.

  • White phosphorus: very poisonous, white waxy, P₄ molecules, unstable; melts 44.1°C; spontaneously ignites in air → stored under water.

  • Red phosphorus: denser (2.16 g/cm³), much less reactive; P₄ linked into polymer; doesn't need storage under water; soluble in CS₂.

  • Production: white P from 2Ca₃(PO₄)₂ + 6SiO₂ + 10C → 6CaSiO₃ + P₄ + 10CO (electric furnace). Red P by heating white P in sunlight.

  • Chemical:

  • P₄ + 3O₂ → P₄O₆; P₄ + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀

  • P₄O₆ + 6H₂O → 4H₃PO₃ (phosphorous acid); P₄O₁₀ + 6H₂O → 4H₃PO₄ (phosphoric acid)

  • P₄ + 6Cl₂ → 4PCl₃; P₄ + 10Cl₂ → 4PCl₅

  • Uses: red P for matches; white P for phosphoric acid & phosphorus compounds; fireworks, smoke bombs, rat poison, tracer bullets; fertilizers.

C. Oxygen (O)

  • Most abundant element on earth; ~46.6% by weight of crust; ~20% by volume of air.

  • Production: fractional distillation of liquid air.

  • Allotropes: O₂ (dioxygen) and O₃ (ozone, triatomic). Ozone produced in stratosphere by UV: 3O₂ →UV 2O₃ (absorbs harmful UV).

  • Physical: colourless, odourless, tasteless; liquid at −182.96°C (slightly bluish); density 1.429 g/L (denser than air 1.29 g/L).

  • Chemical: relatively reactive; combines with most elements to form oxides.

  • Metals → basic oxides: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO; 2Ca + O₂ → 2CaO.

  • Non-metals → acidic oxides: S₈ + 8O₂ → 8SO₂; P₄ + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀.

  • Supports combustion: C + O₂ → CO₂; CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O.

D. Sulphur (S)

  • Found native & in compounds: galena PbS, pyrites FeS₂ (fool's gold), cinnabar HgS, sphalerite ZnS, gypsum CaSO₄·2H₂O, barite BaSO₄, H₂S in natural gas & crude oil.

  • Extraction: Frasch process — superheated water (~170°C) + compressed air forced down concentric pipes; froth of molten sulphur, air & water brought to surface.

  • Nearly half the S needed comes as waste product of other industries (H₂S from natural gas, SO₂ from roasting sulphide ores) — reduces pollution & acid rain.

  • Allotropes: rhombic (α, most stable, S₈ molecules, yellow octahedral, m.p. 385.8 K) and monoclinic (β, stable only above 95.3°C).

  • Physical: tasteless, odourless, brittle, pale yellow, poor conductor, insoluble in water.

  • Chemical: relatively unreactive at room temp; reacts when heated.

  • With metals → sulphides: 8Fe + S₈ → 8FeS.

  • Burns in O₂: S₈ + 8O₂ → 8SO₂.

  • Contact process for H₂SO₄:

    1. S₈ + 8O₂ → 8SO₂
    1. 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃ (catalyst, high temp)
    1. SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇ (oleum)
    1. H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄ (98% commercial).

E. Chlorine (Cl)

  • Group VIIA (halogen); most abundant halogen. Found only in compounds (chlorides of Na, K, Ca, Mg). Chief source: NaCl (seawater or rock salt).

  • Production: electrolysis of concentrated NaCl solution (brine):

  • Anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻

  • Cathode: 2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻

  • Cell: 2NaCl + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂

  • NaOH & Cl₂ kept apart (they react to form NaOCl: NaOH + Cl₂ → NaOCl + HCl).

  • Physical: greenish-yellow gas at room temp; melts −102°C, boils −34°C; fairly soluble in water; extremely poisonous.

  • Chemical: highly reactive; powerful oxidizing agent; reacts with almost all elements except noble gases, C, N.

  • 2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃; 2Al + 3Cl₂ → 2AlCl₃.

  • H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl (with charcoal catalyst).

  • Displaces less reactive halogens: Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂; Cl₂ + 2KI → 2KCl + I₂.

  • With water (disproportionation — Cl both oxidized & reduced): Cl₂ + H₂O → HCl + HOCl.

  • Uses: bleaching agent & disinfectant (kills germs), removes colours from wood pulp, paper, cotton.
