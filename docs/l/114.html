2.3 Reproduction in Animals

◌ Not started

Reproduction = the process by which living organisms duplicate themselves, ensuring continuity of their species.

2.3.1 Asexual reproduction

  • Involves a single individual; no fusion of gametes from two parents.
  • More common among invertebrates than vertebrates.
  • Budding and fragmentation — most common, especially in aquatic animals.
  • Parthenogenesis — unfertilised eggs develop into new offspring (some insects and vertebrates; e.g. honeybees/aphids).

✍️ Must-know: development of a female gamete (egg) into a new organism WITHOUT fertilization = parthenogenesis (review MCQ). Common features of budding/fragmentation/parthenogenesis: single parent, no gamete fusion, genetically identical offspring (mitosis-based).

2.3.2 Sexual reproduction

  • Involves two parents and fusion of gametes; offspring get genetic material from both parents.
  • Parents are diploid (2n); gametes are haploid (n):
  • Males produce sperm (n) in the testes; sperm stored in the epididymis until ejaculation.
  • Females produce an ovum/egg (n) that matures in the ovary.
  • Fusion of sperm + egg = fertilisation → zygote (2n).
  • Two types of fertilisation:
  • External — eggs released into an aqueous environment (frogs). Needs large numbers of gametes to increase the chance of fertilisation in water.
  • Internal — eggs fertilised inside the female (released from ovary into uterine/oviduct tubes). More advantageous for land animals (protects gametes/embryo from drying out; higher chance of fertilisation; protects developing young).

Embryonic development stages (memorise order): 1. Cleavage — series of mitotic divisions of the zygote → an eight-celled structure → a hollow ball. 2. Blastula/blastula — hollow ball of cells. 3. Gastrulation → gastrula with germ layers. 4. Organogenesis — germ layers develop into tissues, organs and organ systems → embryo → adult.

✍️ Must-know: germ layers are formed by the gastrula (gastrulation). Zygote→cleavage→blastula→gastrula→organogenesis. In mammals the blastula becomes a blastocyst which implants in the uterus wall.

2.3.3 Reproduction in insects — metamorphosis

  • Insects = largest class of phylum Arthropoda (the largest animal phylum); segmented bodies, jointed legs, external skeleton (exoskeleton).
  • Eggs are usually fertilised internally; some insects undergo parthenogenesis.
  • Metamorphosis = a series of major body-structure changes as the insect develops (controlled by chemical substances/hormones — ecdysone).
  • Complete metamorphosis — FOUR stages: egg → larva → pupa → adult. (e.g. honeybees, butterflies, beetles.) The pupa is the resting/inactive stage.
  • Incomplete metamorphosis — THREE stages: egg → nymph → adult. (e.g. grasshoppers, lice.)

✍️ Must-know: complete metamorphosis has 4 stages (egg–larva–pupa–adult); incomplete has 3 (egg–nymph–adult). Metamorphosis is regulated by the hormone ecdysone. 2.3.4 Reproduction in frogs

  • Frogs are amphibians; Ethiopian examples: Ptychadena species (sub-Saharan incl. Ethiopia), Ptychadena harenna, Leptopelis ragazzi (Bale Mountains, Shoa forests).
  • Frogs do not produce amniotic eggs; eggs are covered with a jelly-like substance and laid in water to avoid drying out.
  • Mostly external fertilization (female releases eggs into water, male releases sperm over them); a few species have internal fertilization. Frogs lay large numbers of eggs to overcome the low chance of fertilisation/survival in water.
  • Metamorphosis → eggs hatch into a tadpole (larval stage, very different from adult) which develops into the adult frog.

2.3.5 Reproduction in crocodiles

  • Large semi-aquatic reptiles; internal fertilization. Mating usually under water (season often July–August).
  • Lay eggs (10–100 depending on species) buried in sand or deposited in mound vegetation.
  • Unlike frogs, crocodiles lay hard, leathery eggs that protect the young (reptilian/amniotic egg adaptation).

2.3.6 Reproduction in birds

  • Internal fertilization; most birds monogamous (one male + one female for the breeding season); some polygamous (several partners). Polygyny = a male mating with multiple females.
  • Male birds have no external genital organs; females have a single ovary.
  • The ovum is produced in the ovary and travels down the oviduct where fertilization occurs. Oviduct parts & functions (Table 2.1 — memorise): | Part | Function | |---|---| | Infundibulum | funnel-shaped upper part; searches out & engulfs the yolk into the oviduct (site of fertilization) | | Magnum | longest part; secretes albumen (egg white) | | Isthmus | short part; forms the shell membranes (glands produce sulfur-containing amino acids) | | Uterus | longest time; forms the eggshell over the membranes | | Vagina | final section; separated from uterus by a sphincter; during oviposition muscles relax and the egg is laid through the cloaca |
  • Both sexes have a cloaca; during mating the male transfers sperm to the female cloaca.
  • The developing egg has a fluid-filled amnion (thin membrane forming a closed sac around the embryo).
  • Egg parts: yolk, chalaza, albumen, membranes, air sac, shell.
  • Incubation/brooding = keeping eggs warm with body heat so embryos develop; females develop a brood patch (skin with densely packed blood vessels) to transfer heat; birds rotate eggs for even warmth.
  • Hatching: the chick uses an egg tooth (hard tip of its bill) to break the shell, then loses it. Chicks often call to parents before hatching.
  • Parental care: building nests (often hidden / camouflaged eggs); care length varies by species (some end at hatching, some extended).

✍️ Must-know: site of fertilization in the chicken = infundibulum. Shell is formed in the uterus; albumen in the magnum; shell membranes in the isthmus. Hens lay eggs without a rooster because the (unfertilised) ovum still forms an egg.

2.3.7 Reproduction in rats (mammals)

  • Rats: black rat (Rattus rattus — warmer climates), brown rat (Rattus norvegicus — temperate), and the Giant mole rat / giant root rat (Tachyoryctes macrocephalus), which is endemic to Ethiopia (high-altitude Afro-alpine shrub/grasslands such as the Bale Mountains).
  • Male rat reproductive system: testes, scrotum, seminiferous tubules, epididymis, vas deferens, penis with bacula.
  • Female rat reproductive system: two ovaries, oviducts, uterine horns, vagina with vulva.
  • Internal fertilization; zygotes develop in the mother during gestation (pregnancy).
  • Gestation periods: brown rat 22–24 days; black rat ~22 days; giant mole rat 37–49 days.
  • Blastula/blastocyst travels down the oviducts, implants in the uterine horns, differentiates into embryonic + extra-embryonic tissue. The umbilical cord connects blood vessels to the mother; the placenta transports oxygen from mother to embryo and removes waste; the amniotic sac protects the embryo.
  • Rats give birth to hairless, deaf, sealed-eyelid offspring; normally 7–12 pups per litter (fewer for giant mole rats). Young weaned ~after 45 days. Sexual maturity: brown/black rats 3–4 months; giant mole rats 4–6 months.
  • Parental care: females build nests and nurse pups (even other females' pups); male rats do not participate in parental care.

Progression from frogs → rats (advancement in reproduction): external fertilization + many jelly eggs in water (frog) → internal fertilization + leathery shelled eggs on land (crocodile/bird) → internal fertilization + embryo develops inside the mother (mammal/rat). Advancements: internal fertilization protects gametes/embryo; shelled/amniotic eggs allow land laying; in mammals the placenta & mammary milk support offspring.
