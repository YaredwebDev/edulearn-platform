2.2 Graphical Method of Addition of Vectors in 2-D

◌ Not started

A resultant vector is the single vector that produces the same effect as the vectors being added.

✍️ Must-know (three geometric laws): - Triangle law: vectors are joined head to tail to form a triangle; the resultant closes the triangle. - Parallelogram law: vectors are placed tail to tail; a parallelogram is drawn with the vectors as adjacent sides; the diagonal through the tails is the resultant. - Polygon law (for >2 vectors): vectors are joined head to tail in any order; the vector from the tail of the first to the head of the last is the resultant. Graphical results are approximate (they depend on scale drawing).

  • Vector addition is done by translation — moving a vector does not change its magnitude or direction.

✋ Trap/common error: if C = A + B, the magnitude C is NOT generally equal to |A| + |B|. Only when A and B are parallel and same direction does |C| = |A|+|B|.

Subtraction of vectors: A − B = A + (−B). Add the negative of B to A.

✅ Review: adding a vector to its own negative (A + (−A)) gives the zero/null vector. The minimum number of vectors of equal magnitude required to give a zero resultant is three (three equal vectors 120° apart); but for two unequal vectors their sum can only be zero if they are equal-and-opposite.
