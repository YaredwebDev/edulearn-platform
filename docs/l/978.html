1.1 Definition and Scope of Chemistry

1.1.1 Definition of Chemistry

Chemistry is the science that deals with the properties, composition, and structure of substances (elements and compounds), the transformations they undergo, and the energy that is released or absorbed during these processes.

Key terms:

  • Substance: a particular kind of matter with uniform properties (e.g., gold, silver, water, soap, table salt).
  • Matter: a physical substance that occupies space and possesses rest mass (e.g., book, pencil, television).
  • Property: the attribute, quality, or characteristic by which a substance is distinguished from others.
  • Composition: the nature of something's ingredients/constituents; how a whole or mixture is made up (e.g., table salt = sodium + chlorine).
  • Structure: the arrangement and relationships between the parts of something complex (e.g., a building's parts arranged in order).
  • Transformation: a marked change in form, nature, or appearance, accompanied by energy changes.

1.1.2 Scope of Chemistry

The study of modern chemistry has five main disciplines:

BranchWhat it studies
Physical chemistryMacroscopic and atomic properties; rates of reactions, energy transfers, molecular structure.
Organic chemistrySubstances containing carbon (over 20 million carbon-based chemicals).
Inorganic chemistrySubstances not primarily based on carbon (rocks, minerals; energy & IT materials).
Analytical chemistryThe composition of matter — separating, identifying, and quantifying components.
BiochemistryChemical processes occurring in living things (cellular processes, disease states).

The scope of chemistry extends to agriculture, medicine, food production, and building construction, explaining the natural world, preparing people for careers, and producing informed citizens. Chemistry can also produce dangerous substances (e.g., chlorofluorocarbons, oxides of nitrogen, carbon, and sulphur) that harm life and the environment.

✍️ Must-know: The five branches of chemistry are physical, organic, inorganic, analytical, and biochemistry. Chemistry = central science.
