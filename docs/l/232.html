2.7 Photosynthesis

◌ Not started
  • The leaves of green plants are the largest "food factories" on Earth.
  • Photosynthesis = the process by which chlorophyll-bearing organisms trap solar energy and convert it into chemical energy (food).
  • Plants are producers/autotrophs; animals are consumers/heterotrophs.

2.7.1 The photosynthetic apparatus (Chloroplast)

Two distinct parts: - Granum: stacks of flattened sacs called thylakoids; contains chlorophyll, enzymes, co-factors → site of the light reaction. - Stroma: gel-like colourless matrix; site of sugar (carbohydrate) synthesis via carbon fixation → the dark reaction.

2.7.2 Light-absorbing system

  • Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll a and b and accessory pigments that absorb light in the visible range (400–700 nm).
  • Maximum absorption at 400–500 nm (blue) and 600–700 nm (red).
  • Light of 500–600 nm (green) is reflected, not absorbed → this is why leaves look green.

2.7.3 Mechanism of photosynthesis

1. Light reaction (light-dependent stage) — occurs in the granum: - Photolysis: splits water (H₂O → H⁺ + O₂); O₂ is released to the atmosphere through stomata. - Excites electrons → they pass down the electron transport system (ETS) generating high-energy ATP. - The ATP and H⁺ are inputs for the dark reaction.

2. Dark reaction (light-independent stage) — occurs in the stroma: - Can occur without light as long as H⁺ and ATP are supplied. - Carbon fixation: H⁺ combines with CO₂ → glucose (carbohydrate) is the immediate result.

Overall equation:

Chlorophyll
6CO₂ + 6H₂O ———————————> C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
Sunlight

✍️ Must-know: The source of oxygen released in photosynthesis is water (from photolysis), not CO₂.

2.7.4 Testing a leaf for starch

  • Iodine turns starch blue-black.
  • Two problems before testing: leaf cuticle is impermeable to iodine, and chlorophyll interferes with the colour.
  • Procedure: (1) boil leaf in water (kills cells, stops reactions); (2) boil in alcohol (removes chlorophyll), turning off the Bunsen burner first (alcohol is flammable); (3) dip in cold water; (4) add iodine → blue-black confirms starch.
