5.1 Symmetrical Properties of Circles

Definition: A circle is the locus (set) of points in a plane equidistant from a fixed point (the center). The constant distance is the radius r.

  • Area: A = πr². Perimeter (circumference): P = 2πr.

Line of symmetry: a line about which the figure folds so one half coincides with the other. Any diameter of a circle is a line of symmetry; a circle has infinitely many lines of symmetry.

Example: A circle inscribed in a square has 4 lines of symmetry (those of the square).

Theorem 5.1: The line segment joining the center of a circle to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.

Chord with perpendicular from center

Theorem 5.2: The perpendicular drawn from the center of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.

Theorem 5.3: Equal chords of a circle are equidistant from the center.

Theorem 5.4: If the angles subtended by chords at the center are equal, the chords are equal.

Theorem 5.5: Equal chords subtend equal angles at the center.

Example: Chord of radius-5 cm circle is 8 cm long. Let M be midpoint: OM=√(OB²−MB²)=√(25−16)=√9=3 cm from center.

✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Center-to-midpoint-of-chord ⊥ chord; perpendicular from center bisects chord. Use the Pythagorean theorem (radius, half-chord, distance from center) for chord problems.
