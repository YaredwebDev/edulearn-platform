1.3 Making of Physics Knowledge

◌ Not started

This section asks: What is the process of gaining knowledge in physics? and how scientific knowledge is constructed.

Experimental (experiential) knowledge — knowledge gained through experience: - I. Sensory perception — the dominant source of experiential knowledge; we gather knowledge by seeing, touching, hearing, etc. - II. Introspection — "a sixth sense" that looks into the most intimate parts of our minds; lets us inspect our own feelings and how our thoughts operate (e.g., describing a pain to a doctor — the doctor cannot directly experience it and relies on your introspective description). - III. Memory — like a recording device that captures events roughly in the order they occur. - IV. Testimony — knowledge from others; written testimonies are usually more reliable than oral ones (though much depends on the integrity of the author, publisher, and fact-gathering methods).

Non-experiential knowledge — knowledge gained by reasoning/method rather than direct experience: - The Scientific method = an ordered series of steps to acquire knowledge based on experimental evidence. - Example in physics (boiling point vs altitude): you decide to heat water at different altitudes and record the boiling temperature, then analyse the results (the experiment shows the boiling point changes with altitude).

✋ Trap: "gathering knowledge by seeing, touching, hearing" is sensory perception. Written testimony is generally more reliable than oral testimony, but reliability still depends on the author's/publisher's integrity and methods.
