3.5 Static Equilibrium

◌ Not started

Static equilibrium occurs when an object/system remains at rest and does not tilt or rotate — all opposing forces (and torques) are balanced.

Two conditions must be satisfied: 1. First condition of equilibrium (translational): the net force must be zero. ΣF⃗_net = F₁ + F₂ + ... + F_n = 0 - In 2D: ΣF_x = 0 and ΣF_y = 0. - True for both static equilibrium (v = 0) and dynamic equilibrium (constant velocity).

2. Second condition of equilibrium (rotational): the net torque must be zero. Στ⃗ = τ⃗_net = 0 - Clockwise torque = counterclockwise torque.

Torque = the twisting force causing rotation: τ = F·r·sin θ (unit: N·m) - τ = magnitude of torque (N·m), F = force (N), r = distance from pivot to point of force application (m), θ = angle between force and lever arm. - If r is perpendicular to F: τ = F·r.

Examples of static equilibrium: a book on a table (gravity up = normal force down), a balanced seesaw.

Seesaw example: Girl 30 kg at 2.5 m; boy 25 kg must sit at x where m_A g(2.5) = m_B g(x): x = (m_A/m_B)(2.5) = (30/25)(2.5) = 3.0 m

Problem-solving procedure: 1. Draw a free-body diagram (all forces and their points of application). 2. Choose a coordinate system; resolve forces: F_x = F cos θ, F_y = F sin θ (θ anticlockwise from +x-axis). 3. Write equilibrium equations: ΣF_x = 0, ΣF_y = 0. 4. For torque, choose any axis (to reduce unknowns, choose axis through a force so its lever arm is zero). Give each torque a sign (CCW positive, CW negative). 5. Solve the equations (three equations → max three unknowns).

Example 3.7: Horizontal cord force = 30 N. F cos 30° = 30 N → F = 30/0.86 = 34.9 N. F sin 30° = F_W → F_W = 0.5 × 34.9 = 17.5 N.

Example 3.8: Child A 30 kg at 2.5 m balances child B 25 kg: x = 3.0 m (as above).

✍️ Must-know: Static equilibrium needs net force = 0 AND net torque = 0. τ = F·r·sin θ. ΣF_x=0, ΣF_y=0, Στ=0.
