6.2 Pyramids, Cones and Spheres

◌ Not started

Pyramid (Def 6.1): a solid with a polygonal base and an apex (vertex) not in the base plane; each base point is joined to the apex. - Altitude/height: perpendicular from apex to the base plane. - Right pyramid: foot of altitude at the center of the base. - Regular pyramid: base is a regular polygon and lateral edges are equal.

Cone (Def 6.2): a pyramid whose base is a circular region. Right circular cone: foot of altitude at center of base. - Slant height l: the segment from the apex to a point on the base circle; l=√(h²+r²).

Net (Def 6.3): a flat 2D pattern that folds into the 3D solid.

6.2.1 Surface Area of Pyramids and Cones

Regular pyramid (base perimeter p, slant height l, base area BA): - LSA = ½·p·l (½ × perimeter × slant height) - TSA = BA + ½pl

Example: regular square pyramid, base edge 4, slant height 5 → p=16, LSA=½·16·5=40, BA=16, TSA=56 cm². Example: base edge 6, lateral edge 7 → slant height from half-base: l²=7²−3²=40 → l=2√10; LSA=½·24·2√10=24√10; BA=36; TSA=36+24√10.

Right circular cone (radius r, slant height l): - LSA = πrl (also = ½pl with p=2πr) - TSA = πrl + πr² = πr(l+r) - l = √(h²+r²)

Example: base diameter 8 (r=4), height 3 → l=√(9+16)=5 → LSA=20π, BA=16π, TSA=36π cm².

✍️ Must-know: Pyramid LSA = ½pl; cone LSA = πrl. Always find the slant height via l=√(h²+r²) for cones.

6.2.2 Horizontal cross-sections of pyramids and cones

Horizontal cross-section (Def 6.4): the intersection of the pyramid/cone with a plane parallel to the base plane.

Theorem 6.1: Every horizontal cross-section of a triangular pyramid is a triangle similar to the base. Theorem 6.2/6.3: The ratio of the area of a cross-section to the area of the base is k²/h², where h is the altitude and k is the distance from the vertex to the cross-section plane.

Example: base area 270, h=6, cross-section 4 from vertex → A_cs/270=4²/6²=16/36 → A_cs=270·(4/9)=120 cm². Example: square pyramid h=10, base side 5 → base area 25; cross-section 3 from vertex → A_cs/25=9/100 → A_cs=9/4 cm².

✍️ Must-know: Area ratio = (k/h)². When given distance from the base, k = h − (distance from base).

6.2.3 Volume of Pyramids and Cones

Cavalieri's Principle: two solids of equal height with equal cross-sectional areas at every level have equal volumes.

Theorem 6.7: Volume of a pyramid V = ⅓(BA)·h (base area × height ÷ 3). Theorem 6.8: Volume of a cone V = ⅓πr²h.

Example: square pyramid base edge 4, h=7 → V=⅓·16·7=112/3 cm³. Example: cone r=5, h=6 → V=⅓·π·25·6=50π cm³.

6.2.4 Surface Area and Volume of Sphere

Sphere (Def 6.5): a solid bounded by a closed surface every point of which is equidistant from a center. - Radius: segment from center to surface. Diameter: through the center to the surface. - Great circle: a cross-section through the center (radius = sphere's radius). Small circle: any other cross-section. - Hemisphere: one of the two halves divided by a great circle.

  • Surface area: SA = 4πr²
  • Volume: V = 4/3πr³

✍️ Must-know: Sphere SA=4πr², V=4/3πr³. Pyramid/cone volume = ⅓(base area × height).
