1C Reading Skills: Environmental Hazards

◌ Not started

Reading strategies taught: previewing, brainstorming, idea/concept-mapping for prediction; then read for gist, specific information, and implied info. Passage (key content): Human activities pollute the water, air and soil. Although the Industrial Revolution succeeded technologically, it introduced huge quantities of air pollutants. Anthropogenic (human-caused) air pollution accounts for ~9 million deaths per year worldwide. Pollution is linked to climate change, causing food-safety issues, ice/glacier melting, animal extinction and plant damage. - Short-term effects of air pollution: COPD, cough, shortness of breath, wheezing, asthma, respiratory disease, hospitalisation (morbidity). - Long-term effects: chronic asthma, pulmonary insufficiency, cardiovascular disease & mortality; (per a Swedish cohort study) diabetes; malign effects in early life (respiratory, cardiovascular, mental, prenatal) → infant mortality or chronic adult disease. - Air pollution mainly affects large urban areas (road emissions); danger of industrial accidents (toxic fog). Developing countries suffer more due to overpopulation, uncontrolled urbanisation & industrialisation; ~3 billion people use wood/solid fuel indoors (women at highest risk from indoor air pollution).

✍️ Reading skill to practise: identify the purpose of the text (to raise awareness / persuade), recognise the main idea per paragraph and supporting details, and answer true/false, reference ("it/this/those/their" refer to…), synonym (e.g. "adverse/unfavourable", "unprecedented/unparalleled", "susceptible/at risk", "disparity/inequality") and comprehension questions. Organise your summary as introduction–body–conclusion.
