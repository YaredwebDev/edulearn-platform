5.2 The Reproductive System

◌ Not started

Function: produce & deposit sperms (male) and egg cells (female). Internal fertilization by sexual intercourse: the penis is inserted into the vagina and sperm pass through the cervix into the uterus and fallopian tubes to fertilize the ovum.

5.2.1 Human reproductive systems

Male reproductive system (external genitalia: penis, testes, scrotum; internal: prostate, vas deferens, urethra): - Penis — the copulatory organ with urinary & sexual functions (erection & ejaculation). Erection = blood flows into the corpus cavernosa & corpus spongiosum; arteries dilate, veins compress → blood "trapped." - Urethra — tube from bladder to the meatus; carries urine and (in males) expels semen during ejaculation. - Scrotum — sack of thick skin protecting the testes and controlling temperature for sperm creation. - Testes — oval pair in the scrotum producing testosterone and sperm (spermatogenesis).

Male structures & functions (Table 5.4): | Structure | Function | |---|---| | testes | produce sperm cells; produce testosterone | | seminiferous tubules | produce immature sperm cells | | epididymis | matures & stores sperm in coiled tubules | | vas deferens | carries sperm from epididymis to the urethra junction | | seminal vesicle | secretes fructose (energy for sperm) into semen | | prostate gland | secretes alkaline buffer (protects sperm from acidic vagina) | | Cowper's gland | secretes mucus-rich fluid protecting sperm from acids in the urethra | | urethra | carries semen during ejaculation; carries urine out | | penis | deposits sperm into the vagina; contains the urethra |

Female reproductive system (ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus, vagina, accessory glands, external genitalia) functions: produce eggs, secrete sex hormones (estrogen), provide a site for fertilization, gestate a fetus, and give birth. - External: labia majora (large lips; enclose/protect; hair & glands at puberty), labia minora (small lips; surround openings to vagina & urethra), Bartholin's glands (near vaginal opening; secrete mucus), clitoris (small sensitive protrusion comparable to the penis; covered by the prepuce; can become erect). - Internal: vagina (canal joining cervix to outside; the birth canal), uterus/womb (hollow pear-shaped organ housing the fetus; parts = cervix [lower, opens into vagina] + corpus [main body]), ovaries (small oval glands each side producing eggs & hormones), fallopian tubes (carry ova from ovary to uterus; site of fertilization).

Female structures & functions (Table 5.5): | Structure | Function | |---|---| | ovaries | produce estrogen & progesterone; site of ova development & ovulation | | fallopian tubes (oviducts) | carry the ovum to the uterus; usually site of fertilization | | fimbria | sweep the ovum into the oviduct after ovulation | | uterus (womb) | pear-shaped organ where embryo/fetus develops; involved in menstruation | | cervix | separates vagina from uterus; holds fetus in place; dilates at birth | | vagina | extends cervix→outside; passage for sperm & menstrual flow; birth canal |

✍️ Must-know (review): NOT part of male system = uterus; NOT part of female system = sperm duct; male sex cells = sperm, made in the testes; female sex cell = egg.

5.2.2 Gametogenesis

Gametogenesis = formation of gametes (oocytes/sperm) from bipotential primordial germ cells; a haploid daughter (n) is formed from a diploid mother (2n) via meiosis. Called spermatogenesis (male → spermatozoa) and oogenesis (female → ova).

Spermatogenesis (in the seminiferous tubules of the testis, from puberty): the germinal epithelium lines the tubules; cuboidal primordial germ cells (PGCs) + tall somatic Sertoli cells (nurse cells that nourish developing sperm). PGCs divide by mitosis into spermatogonia (diploid, 46 chromosomes); these form primary spermatocytes, which undergo meiosis → two haploid secondary spermatocytes (23 chr.); second meiotic division → four haploid spermatids, transformed by spermiogenesis into mature spermatozoa; maturation in the epididymis; released as semen at puberty.

Oogenesis (in the ovaries): germ cells (oogonium) develop; undergoes meiotic division producing a haploid oocyte and a smaller polar body; gives ~1–2 million cells during embryogenesis; arrests in the follicle.

Menstrual cycle = monthly discharge of blood through the vagina from puberty to menopause (excluding pregnancy), ~28 days average (range 20–45 days). Two significant events: release of one ovum, and preparation of the uterine lining for a fertilized ovum. - Phases/events (Table 5.6): - Menstrual phase (days 1–4): menstruation occurs (uterine lining sheds). - Follicular phase (days 5–13): follicle matures and endometrium develops; FSH stimulates follicle growth. - Ovulation phase (day 14): ovary releases an egg (midway, days 12–14). - Luteal phase (days 15–28): follicle becomes the corpus luteum; endometrium prepared for an egg.

5.2.3 Positive & negative feedback controlling the menstrual cycle

  • A feedback mechanism returns the body to its normal internal state (homeostasis).
  • Positive feedback (days 12–14): estrogen stimulates the hypothalamus to release GnRH, which makes the anterior pituitary secrete LH and FSH → ovulation.
  • LH — regulates testes (men: stimulate testosterone/sperm) and ovaries (women: weeks 1–2 stimulate follicles to make estradiol; around day 14 an LH surge causes ovulation; weeks 3–4 remnants form the corpus luteum producing progesterone).
  • FSH — stimulates ovaries to grow follicles & eggs. Women are born with ~1–2 million eggs; ~400,000 reach puberty; ~400 ovulate across the reproductive years.
  • Negative feedback leads to menstruation: if no fertilization, the corpus luteum dies, progesterone drops, uterine lining breaks down → period. Positive feedback leads to ovulation.

5.2.4 Fertilization and pregnancy

  • Fertilization = union of egg & sperm in the ampulla of the fallopian tube to produce a zygote (23 + 23 chromosomes). The zygote divides as it moves to the uterus; ~6 days after fertilization the embryo attaches to the uterine lining (implantation), completed within ~9–10 days.
  • Pregnancy (gestation) = fetus developing in the womb; lasts about 40 weeks (280 days) from the last menstrual period. More than one fetus = multiple pregnancy (twins/triplets).
  • Signs & symptoms of pregnancy: missed periods, tender breasts, morning sickness (nausea/vomiting), hunger, frequent urination; confirmed by a pregnancy test.

5.2.5 Mechanism of action of contraceptives

Contraception = intentional prevention of pregnancy (hormonal drugs, devices, practices, chemicals, surgery). Reasons: plan family size, reduce pregnancy-related risk, reduce teenage pregnancy, have healthy babies, prevent STIs like HIV/AIDS, and balance population growth with economic growth. - Barrier contraceptives — a physical barrier between sperm and egg. Examples: male condom, female condom, diaphragm, cervical cap. Condoms are the only contraceptive that also prevents STIs. - Diaphragm — small soft silicon dome placed in the vagina, blocking sperm from the uterus. - Non-barrier contraceptives — chemical barriers/spermicides, oral pills, IUDs, hormonal implants, injections, post-pill, contraceptive ring. - Spermicides — sperm-killing substances (foams, creams, gels, films, suppositories), often with barrier devices. - Oral contraceptive pills (COC) — combined estrogen + progesterone; prevent ovulation, thicken cervical mucus, thin uterine lining. Progesterone-only pill (POP) thickens cervical mucus & can stop ovulation; must be taken daily. - Intrauterine device (IUD) — small T-shaped (copper or hormonal); prevents fertilization/implantation; thickens mucus & thins lining. - Contraceptive implant — small plastic rod under the upper-arm skin, lasts ~3 years releasing progesterone. - Contraceptive injection (Depo-Provera = medroxyprogesterone acetate) — synthetic progesterone; prevents ovulation; ~6% may still get pregnant. - Post pill (emergency contraceptive pill, ECP) — levonorgestrel (a progestin), used within 72 hours after unprotected sex. - Contraceptive (vaginal) ring — releases continuous estrogen + progesterone. - Sterilization — permanent: women = tubal ligation; men = vasectomy (cuts/blocks each vas deferens).

Table 5.6 — contraceptive summary (key points): oral contraceptives inhibit ovulation (regulate cycle; not for women over 35 who smoke); injectables (Depo) ~1% failure, inhibit ovulation; IUD prevents implantation, continuous protection; spermicides chemically kill sperm; diaphragm mechanically blocks the cervix; condom mechanically prevents sperm entering the vagina + STI protection; sterilization (tubal ligation 0.04%, vasectomy 0.15%) most reliable but permanent.

✍️ Must-know (review): male condoms are best for STI protection; diaphragm is another barrier method besides condom; IUD types = copper & hormonal (not titanium); vaginal ring left ~3 weeks; natural family planning = basal temperature, cervical-mucus tracking, calendar tracking.

5.2.6 Causes of infertility in humans

Infertility = inability to reproduce naturally. Female infertility = unable to conceive after unprotected sex. Male infertility = low sperm production, abnormal sperm function, or blockages preventing delivery of healthy sperm.

Female causes: ovulatory problems (sparse/absent periods), thyroid problems (over/under-active prevent ovulation), pelvic surgery complications (fallopian-tube damage/scarring, cut ovaries), cervical-mucus problems (hard mucus blocks sperm), fibroids (non-cancerous growths), endometriosis (growths blocking ovaries), pelvic inflammatory disease, sterilization, and medicine/drug side effects (e.g., marijuana, cocaine, cannabis, tobacco; blood-pressure meds, anti-depressants, anti-psychotics; alcohol misuse).

Male causes: low/abnormal/dysfunctional sperm, blockages, chronic illness, injuries, inherited disorders, hormonal imbalance, varicocele (dilated veins around the testicle), hypogonadism, urogenital infection, undescended testicles. - Symptoms of male infertility: immotile sperm, testicular discomfort/swelling/lump, groin/testicle surgery, low sperm count (fewer than 15 million/mL or <39 million per ejaculate). - Risk factors: smoking, alcohol, drugs, overweight, toxin exposure, past/present STDs, overheating/trauma to the testicles.

5.2.7 The major sexually transmitted infections (STIs) in Ethiopia

STIs = communicable diseases transmitted primarily through sexual contact. More than 20 types spread through unprotected intercourse; causative agents = bacteria, viruses, parasites. Types grouped by pathogen:

Bacterial infections: - Bacterial Vaginosis — overgrowth of natural vaginal bacteria; increases risk of post-surgical infection, uterine/fallopian infections & infertility. Symptoms: thin gray/white/green discharge, fishy odor, itching, burning on urination. Prevalence in Ethiopia 2.8–19.4%. - Chlamydia — caused by Chlamydia trachomatis; spreads via vaginal fluid/semen during sex. - Syphilis — life-threatening (Treponema pallidum) affecting brain, nervous system, eyes, heart and other organs in four stages (primary 3–4 weeks, painless chancres; secondary 4–8 weeks, rash on hands/feet; latent, asymptomatic; tertiary 1–10 years, gummatous lesions/cardiovascular/neurological). - Gonorrhea — Neisseria gonorrhoeae; thrives in warm, moist parts; painful urination, abnormal discharge; can be symptomless. - Chancroid — Haemophilus ducreyi; highly contagious painful necrotizing genital ulcer with inguinal lymphadenopathy.

Viral infections: - Genital herpes — two herpes simplex viruses: HSV-1 (mouth; via saliva/sore) & HSV-2 (genital/anal/mouth; via sex). - Genital warts — human papillomavirus (HPV); increases risk of cervical & throat cancer. - Molluscum contagiosum — poxvirus (skin-to-skin); small round bumps on skin. - HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) — attacks immune-system cells; without treatment advances to AIDS. It is a retrovirus, attacking CD4 helper (T) cells. Three stages: (1) Acute (2–4 weeks; flu-like, rapid multiplication); (2) Chronic/asymptomatic/clinical latency (1–7 years); (3) AIDS (10+ years; CD4 < 200 cells/mm³; opportunistic infections; survival ~3 years without treatment). Healthy CD4 = 500–1600 cells/mm³. - Hepatitis B — attacks the liver (acute & chronic); can cause cirrhosis & liver cancer (hepatocellular carcinoma). Virus remains in semen, blood, body fluids. ~296 million living with chronic hep B in 2019 (WHO), 820,000 deaths/year. Prevented by vaccine. Symptoms: jaundice, dark urine, fatigue, nausea, abdominal pain.

Parasites: - Crabs (pubic lice) — attach to pubic hair; spread by close physical/sexual contact & shared towels/bedding. - Trichomoniasis — Trichomonas vaginalis; thin foul-smelling discharge, genital redness/itching, pain on urination/sex. - Scabies — skin disease due to a mite; spread by direct skin-to-skin contact (often during sex → considered an STI).

5.2.8 Epidemiology of STIs in Ethiopia

  • Epidemiology = study of the transmission, causation, outbreak, surveillance, monitoring and control of a disease.
  • ~1.4 million people are infected with STIs every day globally. In Ethiopia the highest reported rates are among 15–24-year-olds.
  • Risks of STI epidemic: unprotected sex, multiple sexual partners, history of STIs, forced sexual activity, alcohol/recreational-drug misuse, injecting drugs (needle sharing), and being young (half of new STIs are in people aged 15–24).

✍️ Must-know (review): gonorrhea & syphilis are bacterial STIs; genital herpes → painful blisters/burning; untreated syphilis affects the brain & heart; chancroid, scabies & molluscum contagiosum are other STIs; condom use, fewer partners and delaying sex all lower STI risk.
