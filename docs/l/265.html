1.1 Introduction — Historical Development of the Atomic Nature of Substances

◌ Not started

Key historical idea: Are the views of different Greek philosophers on matter continuous or made of tiny particles?

  • Most ancient philosophers (Plato, Aristotle) believed matter is continuous (infinitely divisible).
  • Democritus (460–370 BC) disagreed: if you divide matter, you eventually get tiny, indestructible particles called atomos ("atoms", = indivisible). His idea was based on philosophical speculation, not experiment, so it was not widely accepted.
  • Acceptance came only in 1808, when John Dalton published an atomic theory based on the laws of chemical combination.

✍️ Must-know: Democritus' atoms were an idea; Dalton made atoms a working scientific theory grounded in experiments/laws.
