UNIT 3 · LESSON 13 OF 13 · GRADE 11 BIOLOGY

🧮 Key facts/formulas to memorise

◌ Not started
  • Activation energy = minimum energy to convert reactants → products; enzymes lower it
  • Enzyme reaction scheme: S ⇌ ES ⇌ ES* ⇌ EP ⇌ E + P
  • Michaelis–Menten: V = Vmax[S]/(Km + [S]); Km = [S] at half Vmax
  • Enzyme catalysis: ~100–10,000 substrates/s; 10³–10⁸× faster than uncatalysed
  • Apoenzyme + cofactor (prosthetic group or coenzyme) = holoenzyme
  • Six enzyme classes: oxidoreductases, transferases, hydrolases, lyases, isomerases, ligases
  • Optimum human enzyme temperature ≈ 37 °C; denaturation ~>40 °C, extreme pH, heavy metals
  • Malting steps (order): steeping → germinating → kilning
  • Protein levels: primary (sequence) → secondary (α-helix, β-sheet) → tertiary (3-D single chain) → quaternary (multiple subunits, e.g. hemoglobin)

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) define enzyme & activation energy; (2) list physical & chemical properties incl. all the specificity types; (3) name body enzymes (amylase, maltase, lactase, trypsin, lipase, helicase, DNA polymerase, acetylcholinesterase) and their functions; (4) describe the four protein structures; (5) distinguish lock-and-key vs induced fit and define ES/transition state; (6) explain allosteric regulation, phosphorylation, and competitive/non-competitive/uncompetitive inhibition; (7) classify enzymes structurally (apoenzyme/cofactor/holoenzyme) and functionally (6 classes); (8) state the factors affecting enzyme action; (9) use the Michaelis–Menten relation; (10) give industrial uses of enzymes (papain→meat, rennin→cheese, etc.); and (11) explain malting and the production of Tella? Re-answer the review too (boiled potato → no reaction; "–ase" = enzyme; optimum pH; transferase transfers methyl groups; malting order = steeping→germinating→kilning; enzymes are NOT inorganic catalysts; first enzyme = diastase).
