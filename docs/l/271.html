1.7 Electronic Configurations and Orbital Diagrams

◌ Not started

Three governing principles: 1. Aufbau (building-up) Principle: electrons fill the lowest-energy orbitals first. Filling order: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p, 6s, 4f, … 2. Hund's Rule: electrons fill degenerate (equal-energy) orbitals singly (parallel spins) before pairing. E.g. in the three 2p orbitals, each gets one electron before any gets a second. 3. Pauli's Exclusion Principle: no two electrons can have the same four quantum numbers (they must differ in at least one).

Notation: - Subshell (sublevel) notation: number (n) + letter (s,p,d,f) + superscript electron count, e.g. H (Z=1) = 1s¹. - Orbital diagram: boxes/circles for orbitals; ↑ = m_s=+½, ↓ = m_s=−½. - Examples: He 1s²; Li 1s²2s¹; N 1s²2s²2p³; Ne 1s²2s²2p⁶; etc. - Core vs valence electrons: electrons in the outermost principal level = valence electrons (important for bonding); inner = core electrons. - Condensed notation uses a noble-gas core in brackets: Li = [He]2s¹; Mg = [Ne]3s²; Cl = [Ne]3s²3p⁵; Ca = [Ar]4s²; Sc = [Ar]4s²3d¹.

Aufbau exceptions (special stability): | Element (Z) | Expected | Observed | |---|---|---| | Cr (24) | [Ar]4s²3d⁴ | [Ar]4s¹3d⁵ (half-filled 3d stable) | | Cu (29) | [Ar]4s²3d⁹ | [Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰ (filled 3d stable) |

✍️ Must-know: 4s fills before 3d. More exceptions occur among heavier transition elements where subshell energies are closer. Know first-row transition metals Sc→Zn electron configurations (Sc [Ar]4s²3d¹ … Zn [Ar]4s²3d¹⁰).
