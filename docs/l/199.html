4.9 Fertilization and Pregnancy

◌ Not started

Mating/copulation: sexual arousal → male erection (blood into erectile tissue); female vagina produces mucus to lubricate; male inserts the penis and ejaculates semen into the top of the vagina.

Fertilization: - Sperm swim through the cervix and uterus into the oviduct; one sperm bumps into the egg, enters its cytoplasm, and its nucleus fuses with the female nucleus = fertilization. - The released egg survives about 24 hours; sperm can fertilize an egg for about 2–3 days; so there is only a short fertile period of about 4 days each month when fertilization might occur.

Twins (multiple births): - Identical (monozygotic) twins: from one zygote; strikingly alike, always the same sex. - Non-identical (dizygotic/fraternal) twins: from two zygotes; resemble each other like any siblings. - Triplets, quadruplets, quintuplets may include a pair of identical twins.

Pregnancy and development: - The zygote divides (2 → 4 → ... ) into a solid ball of cells (early embryo), travels down the oviduct to the uterus, and sinks into the uterine lining = implantation. - After 8 weeks, when all organs are formed, the embryo is called a fetus. - The embryo is enclosed in a fluid-filled sac = amnion (water sac); the fluid = amniotic fluid (protects from damage). - The placenta attaches to the uterine lining and connects to the embryo by the umbilical cord; oxygen and nutrients (glucose, amino acids) pass across the placenta to the embryo; carbon dioxide passes back. Blood of mother and embryo do not mix.

Neural tube defects (NTDs) note box: birth defects of the brain, spine, or spinal cord; occur when the neural tube does not close properly in the first month of pregnancy. Women should take folic acid rich foods before and during pregnancy (at least 3 months before).
