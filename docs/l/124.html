3.4 Enzyme–Substrate Models

◌ Not started

Two models describe how the active site and substrate fit/complement each other: 1. Lock and Key model — the active site is rigid; a substrate of the correct shape fits exactly (like a key into a lock) via non-covalent interactions. Once the reaction passes to the transition state and forms products, the active site cannot accommodate changes. 2. Induced fit model — the active site is flexible; on substrate binding the enzyme changes shape to mould around the substrate, forming the enzyme–substrate complex and stabilising the transition state. Binding can enhance or suppress enzyme activity. This is generally the more accurate model.

Enzymatic transition state: describes reaction rates; notation E = enzyme, S = substrate, ES = enzyme–substrate complex, ES* = activated enzyme–substrate complex (transition), EP = enzyme–product.

✍️ Must-know: the two specificity/enzyme–substrate models = Lock and Key and Induced fit (exam MCQ answer = "A and B").
