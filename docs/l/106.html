1.1 Learning from Nature

◌ Not started

Nature = the physical, natural and material world of life that exists without human intervention (landscapes, water & forest ecosystems, weather, organisms, geology, celestial bodies, inanimate objects). Organisms have structures well-adapted to survive in their environment.

Core idea (biomimicry / imitation): scientists and engineers learn from nature by imitating the physical structures, shapes, materials and functional mechanisms of natural things. The better we understand biological materials, the better we can: - sketch biological structures, - design functional mechanisms, and - build more efficient technologies at macro (large) and nano (very small) scales.

✍️ Must-know: designing technologies from nature is done through imitation. The successful design of technologies "from robotics to material sciences" is by imitating nature.

Unit-level learning outcomes: adapt technologies from nature; illustrate the impact of biology & technology on society and the natural world; indicate the benefits of biology to technology and vice versa; recognise ethical issues in biology.

Examples of technology that imitates nature (memorise several): 1. Swallow nests inspired house-building design. 2. Weaving was developed by studying the formation of spider webs. 3. Buildings imitate the mound architecture of termites (which have chimneys with constant temperature & humidity) — famous example: the East Gate Center building in Harare. 4. The first heavy aircraft & wings/flight techniques imitated the wings of birds and bats. 5. The photograph camera was engineered by imitating the human eye. 6. The movement of bio-robots was designed from the movement of a kangaroo. 7. Injection needles were copied from the mosquito's proboscis. 8. Sensors of electrical devices imitate biological neurons. 9. A cutting saw imitates the sharp teeth of animals. 10. Synthetic bulletproof vests imitate the spider's silk (spinning silk). 11. Ceramics imitate crack-resistant shellfish exoskeletons. 12. A robotic arm was imitated from an elephant trunk.

✍️ Must-know pairings (likely exam items): bulletproof vests ← spider silk; camera ← human eye; building/ventilation ← termite mound (East Gate Center, Harare); bullet-train nose ← kingfisher beak; needle ← mosquito proboscis; saw ← animal teeth; ceramics ← shellfish exoskeleton; robotic arm ← elephant trunk.

Lab activity 1.1: build a model human hand with jointed finger structures and compare how closely it matches a real hand (what is missing / what other tech could imitate nature).
