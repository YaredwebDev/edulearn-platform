4.4 The Concept of Work

◌ Not started
  • In physics, work means the use of force to move an object. Work is done only when a force causes displacement in the direction of the force.
  • Holding a heavy chair or carrying a bucket horizontally at constant velocity does no work (force not in the direction of displacement).
  • Work = force × displacement (in the direction of force): W = F∥ × S (4.6), where F∥ is the force parallel to the displacement and S is the displacement.
  • Work is a scalar quantity. SI unit: Newton-meter (N·m) = Joule (J).
  • 1 Joule = work done when 1 newton moves an object 1 meter in the direction of the force.
  • Work is a transfer of energy from one object to another through the force.
  • Conditions for work to be zero: force = 0, or displacement = 0, or force perpendicular to displacement.

Example 4.4: F = 60 N, S = 12 m → W = 720 N·m = 720 J. Example 4.5: W = 600 J, S = 3 m → F = W/S = 200 N.
