4.4 Grammar

4.4.1 Future intentions: "be going to" vs "will"

  • be + going to + infinitive (am/is/are going to speak/write/do):
  • a plan or intention decided earlier: "I am going to take the entrance exam to join a university."
  • a prediction from present evidence: "The sky is dark; it is going to rain."
  • will + infinitive:
  • a decision made at the moment of speaking: "OK, I will call him."
  • promises, offers, predictions without present evidence: "I will help you." "It will be difficult."
  • ✋ Difference: "be going to" = pre-planned/intention or evidence-based prediction; "will" = spontaneous decision/promise/opinion prediction.

4.4.2 Present Continuous Tense

Used for:

  1. Action happening now: "We are discussing present continuous now."
  2. Action happening in the present period (not necessarily this second): "Nowadays, heavy rain is falling all over the country."
  3. An action that is boring/frustrating due to repetition: "I don't understand why the woman is changing her behaviour."

4.4.3 Indefinite pronouns: some & any

  • some — affirmative statements; sometimes questions expecting a "yes" / offering: "I have some books." "Would you like some tea?"
  • any — negative sentences and most questions: "I don't have any money." "Do you have any questions?"
  • any can also be used in affirmative sentences to mean "no matter which": "Take any book you like."
Note: ✋ Trap: "Some of my bananas are not ripe" (affirmative, some). "Haven't you bought any?" (question). "He seldom gets any sleep" (negative word "seldom" → any).

4.4.4 Clauses of Result

Indicate the result of an action. Introduced by: so…that, such…that, so, as a result, consequently, therefore, hence, thus, for this/that reason, owing to, due to.

  • so + adjective/adverb + that: "Ethiopia is so rich in natural resources that investors want to invest."
  • such + (adjective) noun + that: "Ethiopia is such a rich country that investors want to invest."
  • so + many/much/little/few + noun + that: "We have so many problems that everyone is vigilant." "He gave so little attention that the road stayed bad."
  • ✋ Rule: "so" is followed by an adjective or adverb, or by many/much/little/few + noun. "such" is followed by adjective + a/an + singular countable noun or adjective + plural/uncountable noun.
  • Exception (inversion): "So careful a girl was she that…"; "He had never seen such outstanding a student…"
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: the so/such rule — the #1 trap. so + (adj/adv) OR so+many/much/little/few+noun; such + (adj) noun. "so careful a girl" and "such a careful girl" are both correct but different order.
