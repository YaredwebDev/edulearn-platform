7F.1 Descriptive essays (process approach)

  • Purpose: to paint a picture in the reader's mind by appealing to the five senses (sight, hearing, touch, smell, taste).
  • Use vivid sensory details; distinguish objective (concrete, factual) from subjective (personal/emotional) description. Purely objective writing is hard; reduce subjectivity by adding objective factors (size, colour, shape, distance) before personal opinions/feelings.
  • Sections of a descriptive essay:
  • Introduction: attract the reader's attention with an interesting fact/phrase/quote without confusing with too much info.
  • Thesis statement: define the essay's scope (narrow the points to clarify purpose) and create suspense toward the body.
  • Body paragraphs: usually three (add more if needed); one central idea per paragraph — fusing ideas confuses the reader.
  • Concluding paragraph: restate the thesis, summarise the main points, and add an intriguing closing statement.
  • Language tip: descriptive writing "shows, not tells"; it uses figurative language & sensory details; an essay's style is expressive and may include opinions, comparisons, personal/sensory perceptions. Model essay: the drive from Florida to upstate New York (tiger lilies, "smelling Grandma's apple pie").
  • Process approach: brainstorming → outlining → drafting → re-writing → editing.
  • Tasks: write a five-paragraph descriptive essay about an event/place/picture; a narrative essay about your heroes; write business letters; use wish for aspirations.

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) explain what patriotism is and distinguish traditional vs modern views, and summarise the listening text; (2) ask for/give permission, give & receive praise (three S's), give a public speech on Adwa, and debate whether patriotism is declining; (3) retell the reading: why Adwa was a symbol of national unity (only African country to resist colonisation before 1914; Menelik's outflanking in March 1896; sealed unification), with true/false, reference & summary answers; (4) use definition/synonym/antonym/example/general-sense clues and the pull/point/print phrasal verbs; (5) classify adverbs, form adverbial clauses of time & place, order adjectives, use modals, and build WH-/Yes-No/tag questions (with imperatives, negative adverbs/subjects, somebody→they, and one); and (6) write descriptive & narrative essays and business letters through the process approach? Try the Adwa questions, the adjective-ordering and tag-question exercises, and the context-clue passages.
