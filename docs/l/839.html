1.1 Major Branches of Biology

Biology is the study of living organisms and life processes. It is divided into three major branches:

Major branchWhat it studiesExamples of sub-disciplines
Zoology (Animal biology)The study of animalsHerpetology (reptiles), Ichthyology (fish), Mammalogy (mammals), Ornithology (birds), Entomology (insects)
BotanyThe study of plantsPlant structure, properties, biochemical processes, classification, plant diseases, plant–environment interactions
MicrobiologyThe study of microscopic organisms (microbes) that cannot be seen with the unaided eyeBacteriology (bacteria), Mycology (fungi); includes bacteria, archaea, protists, viruses, microscopic algae and fungi

Botany facts to remember:

  • Plants provide food, shelter, clothing, medicine, ornament, and tools.
  • Through photosynthesis, plants convert solar energy into chemical energy of food and release oxygen as a by-product — both essential for all life.
Note: 📌 Ethiopian examples in this unit: Capra walie (Waliya ibex) is an endemic wild animal of Ethiopia; Ruta chalepensis L. (Tena Adam) is a traditional herbal medicine of Ethiopia.

Fields of biology based on the structure studied (Table 1.1)

FieldWhat it studies
MorphologyExternal form and structure (e.g., shape, leaf texture)
AnatomyInternal bodily structure revealed by dissection (e.g., stomach, liver, heart)
HistologyDetails of tissue structure (e.g., parenchyma, connective tissue)
CytologyThe study of cells (e.g., plant cell, nerve cell)
Cell biologyStructure, function, and components of the cell (e.g., mitochondrion, ribosome, nucleus)
Molecular biologyStructure and function of informational molecules (e.g., DNA, RNA)

Fields of biology concerned with life processes (Table 1.2)

FieldWhat it studies
PhysiologyNormal functions of living organisms and their parts (e.g., photosynthesis, digestion)
EmbryologyDevelopment of the embryo from zygote to form and shape (blastula, gastrula)
EcologyInteraction of organisms with their environment (food chain, biomass, biosphere)
TaxonomyIdentification, nomenclature, and classification of organisms (e.g., human = Homo sapiens)
PaleontologyOrigin, growth, and structure of organisms of the past (fossils)
EvolutionChange in species characteristics over generations by natural selection (e.g., Darwin's finches)
GeneticsHeredity and variation (gene concept, Mendel's laws)
ExobiologyOrigin, evolution, distribution, and future of life in the Universe (life on Moon/Mars)

Relationship between biology and other sciences (Table 1.3)

Biology is linked to chemistry, physics, and other sciences. Examples:

  • Chemistry — structure of lipids and proteins in cell membranes; formation of oxyhaemoglobin; acid–base equilibrium (homeostasis); osmosis/diffusion.
  • Physics — cohesion and adhesion → surface tension and capillary action (transport of water in plants).
✍️ Must-know: Anatomy = internal structure (dissection); Morphology = external form; Histology = tissues; Cytology = cells.
