1.1 Reading: National and Global Population Growth

(by Mesfin Aberra — the core "story"/text of the unit)

The passage has four paragraphs. Understand the main idea of each and be ready to answer questions about it.

Paragraph 1 — Environmental degradation:

  • Rapid population growth → environmental degradation → poverty and famine.
  • Rich and poor countries degrade the environment differently: the rich (industrialisation, over-consumption), the poor (dependence on natural resources).
  • Two forces are blamed: anthropogenic (human-caused) factors and lack of material resources / illiteracy.

Paragraph 2 — Ethiopia's rural picture:

  • About 85% of Ethiopia's population lives in rural areas, concentrated in the central and northeastern highlands.
  • Causes of land degradation: deforestation, over-farming, soil erosion.
  • The 2003 famine affected more than 13 million people; about 6 million face permanent food insecurity.
  • The Green Legacy campaign is mentioned as a response to reforestation.

Paragraph 3 — Poor women's health:

  • Poor women suffer most — about 1 in 14 women die during pregnancy or childbirth; about 114 infants die per 1,000 live births (Save the Children, 2004).
  • Recommended solutions: girls' education and women's empowerment; raising the ratio of female officials.

Paragraph 4 — Global population:

  • World population grew from 1 billion to a 7-fold increase in about 200 years.
  • Reached 7 billion in 2011; roughly 9.4 billion now.
  • Growth is driven by more children surviving to reproductive age, urbanisation, and migration.
  • A growing population needs a growing economy — and must comply with environmental management: conservation, preservation, rehabilitation, sustainability.
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Reading-comprehension questions here test (a) main idea of each paragraph, (b) cause–effect links (growth→degradation→poverty/famine), (c) specific figures (85% rural, 13 million famine victims, 1 in 14 women, 114 infants, 7 billion in 2011), and (d) vocabulary in context. Learn the figures — they are frequently quoted in the questions.
