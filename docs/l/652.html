6.1 Coulomb's Law

◌ Not started

Properties of electric charge: - Two types of charge: positive and negative; like charges repel, unlike charges attract. - Charge is conserved: in any charging process the electrons gained by one object equal the electrons lost by the other (charge can't be created or destroyed). - Charge is quantized: q = n·e, where e = elementary charge ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. Any charge is an integral multiple of e.

✍️ Must-know: charge is quantized (q = ne, e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C) and conserved. To make +1 C requires ≈ 6.25×10¹⁸ protons; −1 C likewise electrons.

Coulomb's law: the electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the separation: - F = k q1 q2 / r², where k ≈ 8.99×10⁹ N·m²/C² (electrostatic constant). k = 1/(4πε₀). - Force is repulsive for like charges, attractive for unlike charges. The force is a vector along the line joining the charges.

✋ Trap / comparison: Coulomb's law resembles Newton's law of gravitation (both are inverse-square). But gravitational force between an electron and a proton is enormously weaker than the electrostatic force between them.

Force due to multiple charges: use the superposition principle — the net force on a charge is the vector sum of the individual forces from each other charge (F_net = F₁ + F₂ + …).

Worked examples: Example 6.2 (two identical charged water drops — force and number of excess electrons); Example 6.3 (three charges on the x-axis — net force on a charge).

Definition of a coulomb: the SI unit of charge. A coulomb is the amount of charge passing a point when a current of 1 A flows for 1 s (q = It), and ≈ 6.25×10¹⁸ elementary charges.

✍️ Electrostatic induction / attraction: a charged balloon can attract a neutral stream of water because it polarises/induces charge (attraction between a charged and a neutral body).
