4.1 Radian Measure of Angle

An angle is a measure of rotation of a ray about its vertex. The original ray is the initial side, the final position is the terminal side, and the rotation point is the vertex.

  • Anticlockwise rotation → positive angle. Clockwise → negative angle.

Standard position: vertex at origin, initial side along the positive x-axis.

Degree measure: one degree (1°) = 1/360 of a complete revolution. 1°=60′ (minutes), 1′=60″ (seconds).

Radian measure: an angle subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius has measure 1 radian. For an arc of length l in a circle of radius r subtending angle θ: θ = l/r or l = rθ.

Conversion: π rad = 180°.

  • Radians = (π/180)×degrees.
  • Degrees = (180/π)×radians.

Examples:

  • 120° = (π/180)·120 = 2π/3 rad.
  • (4/3)π = (180/π)·(4π/3) = 240°.
  • 6 rad = (180/π)·6 ≈ 343.6° (using π≈22/7).
  • Circle radius r, central angle 30° intercepts arc 11 cm: θ=π/6, r=l/θ=11/(π/6)=21 cm.
  • Minute hand 1.5 long, in 40 min turns (2/3)(360°)=(4π/3) rad → tip moves l=rθ=1.5·(4π/3)=2π units.
