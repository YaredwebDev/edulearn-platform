📖 GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS (quick definitions for revision)

Accuracy – closeness of a measurement to the true/accepted value.

Alkali metals – Group IA elements (except H), highly reactive metals (e.g., Na, K).

Alkaline earth metals – Group IIA elements (e.g., Mg, Ca).

Anion – a negatively charged ion (more electrons than protons), e.g., Cl⁻, O²⁻.

Anode – the positive electrode.

Anode rays (canal rays) – streams of positive ions passing through a perforated cathode.

Antibiotics – medicines that control infection and cure diseases.

Anti-penultimate shell – the shell inside the penultimate shell (max 32 electrons).

Atom – the smallest particle of matter that takes part in a chemical reaction; from Greek "atomos" (uncuttable).

Atomic mass unit (amu/Dalton) – 1/12 the mass of a carbon-12 atom (≈1.66×10⁻²⁴ g).

Atomic number (Z) – the number of protons in an atom's nucleus (unique for each element).

Atomic radius – one-half the distance between nuclei of adjacent atoms.

Average atomic mass – weighted average of the masses of an element's isotopes.

Bohr model – model with electrons in concentric stationary orbits/shells (K, L, M, N).

Cathode – the negative electrode.

Cathode rays – streams of electrons from the cathode of a discharge tube.

Cation – a positively charged ion (more protons than electrons), e.g., Na⁺, Ca²⁺.

Chemical bond – the force that holds atoms together to form molecules/compounds.

Chemical law – a law of nature relevant to chemistry.

Chemistry – the study of the properties, composition, structure, transformations, and energy of substances.

Compound – a substance made of two or more elements combined in fixed proportions.

Coordinate (dative) bond – a covalent bond in which one atom supplies both shared electrons.

Covalent bond – a bond formed by sharing electron pairs between atoms.

Dalton's Atomic Theory – theory that elements are made of atoms that cannot be created or destroyed (1803/1808).

Density – mass per unit volume; d = m/V.

Deuterium – hydrogen isotope with 1 proton + 1 neutron (A=2).

Disinfectant – chemical that kills microbes on surfaces.

Double bond – two pairs of electrons shared between two atoms (O=O).

Electron – negatively charged subatomic particle (−1, mass ≈ 1/2000 proton).

Electron affinity – energy change when an electron is added to a neutral gaseous atom.

Electronegativity – the tendency of an atom in a molecule to attract shared bonding electrons toward itself.

Electronic configuration – the systematic arrangement of electrons in the shells of an atom.

Energy level (shell) – region around the nucleus where electrons reside, with a definite energy (K, L, M, N).

Group – a vertical column in the periodic table (18 groups; same valence electrons).

Halogens – Group VIIA elements (F, Cl, Br, I), the most reactive nonmetals.

Hypothesis – a tentative explanation that can be tested by experiment.

Ion – an atom or molecule with a net positive or negative charge.

Ionic bond – electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions (metal + nonmetal).

Ionization – the process of a neutral atom losing or gaining valence electrons.

Ionization energy – energy required to remove an electron from a gaseous atom to form a cation.

Isobars – atoms of different elements with the same mass number (e.g., Ar-40, Ca-40).

Isotopes – atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.

Law of Conservation of Mass – mass is neither created nor destroyed in chemical reactions (Lavoisier).

Law of Definite Proportions – a compound always contains fixed proportions (by mass) of its elements (Proust).

Law of Multiple Proportions – masses of one element reacting with a fixed mass of another are in a ratio of small whole numbers (Dalton).

Lewis (electron-dot) formula – representation of an atom's symbol with dots for valence electrons.

Mass number (A) – total number of protons + neutrons in the nucleus.

Matter – a physical substance that occupies space and possesses mass.

Mendeleev's Periodic Law – properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic weight.

Metallic bond – attraction between positive ion cores and delocalized valence electrons (electron sea).

Mole – amount of substance containing as many entities as atoms in 0.012 kg of carbon-12.

Modern Periodic Law – properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic number (Moseley).

Molecule – the smallest particle of a substance with independent existence representing its properties.

Noble (inert) gases – Group VIIIA elements, very stable (full valence shell).

Nonpolar covalent bond – bond with electrons shared equally (ΔEN < 0.5).

Nucleus – tiny, dense, positively charged center of the atom (protons + neutrons).

Octet rule – atoms tend to form compounds to achieve 8 valence electrons (noble-gas configuration).

Period – a horizontal row in the periodic table (7 periods).

Penultimate shell – the shell just inside the valence shell (max 18 electrons).

Periodic law – statement that properties recur periodically when elements are arranged by atomic number/weight.

Plum pudding model – Thomson's model: electrons embedded in a sphere of positive charge.

Polar covalent bond – bond with electrons shared unevenly (ΔEN 0.5–2.0).

Precision – closeness of repeated measurements to each other.

Principal quantum number (n) – number designating a main energy level (1,2,3... or K,L,M,N).

Protium – hydrogen isotope with 1 proton, 0 neutrons (A=1).

Proton – positively charged subatomic particle (+1, 1.0073 amu), in the nucleus.

Radioisotope – an unstable isotope that undergoes radioactive decay.

Representative (main group) elements – Groups IA–VIIA with incompletely filled s/p subshells.

Scientific law – a statement, based on repeated experiments, that describes/predicts phenomena.

Scientific theory – a well-tested, broad explanation of a natural phenomenon.

Single bond – one pair of electrons shared between two atoms.

SI units – the International System of base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).

Substance – a particular kind of matter with uniform properties.

Triad – Dobereiner's group of three elements where the middle has the mean atomic weight.

Triple bond – three pairs of electrons shared between two atoms (N≡N).

Tritium – hydrogen isotope with 1 proton + 2 neutrons (A=3).

Uncertainty – the doubt about the result of every measurement.

Valence electrons – the electrons in the outermost (valence) shell; determine chemical properties.

Valence shell – the outermost shell containing electrons (max 8).

Wafting – gently fanning fumes toward the nose to smell a chemical safely.
