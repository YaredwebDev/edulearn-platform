3.2 Uniformly Accelerated Motion — the Five Equations of Motion

◌ Not started

For constant acceleration along a straight line (u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time, s = displacement):

  1. v = u + at
  2. s = ut + ½at²
  3. v² = u² + 2as
  4. s = ½(u+v)t
  5. s = vt − ½at²

✍️ Must-know (the five kinematic equations) — this is exam-critical. Identify the knowns first: choose the equation containing the unknown that links the three known quantities. Worked examples in the book apply these to (a) stopping distance of a vehicle and (b) an Ethiopian Airlines aeroplane landing (accelerating from touchdown, braking to stop) — solve by listing knowns, choosing the equation, substituting, solving.

General problem-solving steps from textbook: (1) draw/sketch the situation; (2) list the knowns; (3) identify the unknown; (4) select the appropriate equation; (5) substitute & solve; (6) interpret units/sign.
