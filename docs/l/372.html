5.5.1 Simple Present — Active & Passive Voice

  • Active: subject does the action. "The president makes an inspiring speech."
  • Passive: object becomes subject; subject + be (am/is/are) + past participle (V3). "An inspiring speech is made (by the president)."
  • Uses: when the writer/speaker is more interested in the object than the doer; used to describe processes, analyse ideas/events.
  • ✋ Teachers often prefer active voice in academic writing (clearer, stronger); but process description needs passive.

Passive with modal verbs (can/may/might/should/have to/had to/ought to/used to/need to): - Structure: object + modal + be + V3. - "You may take this medicine." → "This medicine may be taken." - "We should finish this chapter." → "This chapter should be finished." - "We must eat a balanced diet." → "A balanced diet must be eaten."

✍️ Must-know: Passive = be + V3. For modals: modal + be + V3. When converting, move the object to subject position, keep the tense, and add "by + doer" only if needed. Used heavily in describing processes (e.g., coffee making: "the beans are washed, roasted, pounded…").

5.5.2 Subject-Verb Agreement (very important for matric)

  1. Subjects and verbs agree in number. Singular subject → singular verb; plural → plural: "One of the employees is…" "Two of the employees are…"
  2. "one of / either of / neither of" + plural countable noun + singular verb: "One of the pressing problems is sustaining unity." But a relative pronoun agrees with its antecedent: "One of the problems which ring in the mind… are…" (antecedent "problems" → plural).
  3. together with / along with / as well as / in addition / including / with → verb agrees with the first subject: "The Prime Minister, together with his officers, has visited…"
  4. either…or / neither…nor → verb agrees with the nearest subject: "Either the children or their father has arrived." "Neither their father nor the children have arrived."
  5. Words before prepositions determine the verb: "The titles in this book are in question form."
  6. Non-count nouns take singular; but with phrasal quantifiers (a piece of, an item of, a slice of, a bar of) the verb agrees with them: "The slices of bread have been…" "The items of information are dangerous."

✋ Trap examples: "The director, together with some teachers, is visiting…" (first subject). "One of the guests who have arrived late is Lemma." "A ream of paper and a tube of ink cost…" (two subjects → plural).

5.5.3 "do" vs "make"

  • make (create/produce/cause): make an occasion, impression, effort, comment, offer, change, attempt, gesture, noise, a decision, an arrangement, a mistake, money, coffee.
  • do (perform an activity/action): do your best, business, the cleaning, a course, homework, damage, a favour, duty, gardening, exercise, the shopping, a job.
  • ✋ Trap: "make an effort" but "do your best"; "make a comment" but "do homework"; "make a change" but "do damage." Memorise the collocations.
