5E.1 Adverb clauses of reason (revision, in full)

  • Gives the reason/cause for the main clause; introduced by since, as, because, now that, as long as. Position can vary; a comma is used when the clause comes first.
  • Examples: Since we live near the river, we often go swimming; I watered the roses because they were dry; Now that the final exams are approaching, every student is revising; As long as you follow the procedure, you will make it happen.

5E.2 Verbs followed by gerunds, infinitives, or both

  • Gerund = -ing form used as a noun. Infinitive = to + base form.
  • Verbs followed by a gerund (-ing): admit, anticipate, avoid, can't help, can't see, complete, consider, deny, discuss, dislike, enjoy, feel like, finish, imagine, keep, mind, recommend, suggest, quit, risk. Examples: He enjoys having a bath in the evening; I don't recommend taking the bus; She avoided telling him; I have finished cooking.
  • Verbs followed by an infinitive (to + verb): afford, agree, aim, ask, bother, care, decide, expect, fail, help, hesitate, hope, learn, offer, prepare, promise, refuse, seem, volunteer, want, would like. Examples: She agreed to bring the food; I promise to help you; He decided to study Biology; She asked to come with us.
  • Verbs followed by both (with meaning changes): forget, remember, regret, try, stop, go on, quit, need, love, prefer, propose, neglect.
  • Meaning differences (key):
  • forgot to meet (didn't meet) vs forgot meeting (forgot the memory).
  • remembered to visit (didn't forget the duty) vs remembered visiting (recalled the memory).
  • regret to promise vs regret promising.
  • stopped to call (stopped in order to call) vs stopped calling (no longer called).
  • went on to learn (next action) vs went on learning (continued).
  • tried to open (attempted) vs tried opening (experimented with method).
  • quit to work here (left elsewhere to work here) vs quit working here (stopped working).

5E.3 Participial phrases and their functions

  • A participle = a verbal adjective modifying a noun/pronoun. Types:
  • Present participle = base + -ing (active): the rising sun; the winning athlete; a laughing man.
  • Past participle = usually -ed/-en (passive/completed): the broken news; the printed document; the cut flowers; the washed car.
  • A participial phrase = a participle + its modifiers/objects; it acts as an adjective placed near the noun it modifies (before or after), or at the start of a sentence to modify the subject.
  • The man standing by the gate is the new manager (present, after noun).
  • Feeling depressed, we went to bed early (present, at start, modifies "we").
  • With a broken heart, she said goodbye (past).
  • Stunned by the blow, Mike gathered his senses (past, at start).
  • Perfect participle = having + past participle (shows the action finished before the main verb): Having finished the report, she took a nap; Having signed the document, Merga felt relief.
