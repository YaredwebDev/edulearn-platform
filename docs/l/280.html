2.6 Metallic Bonding

◌ Not started

The metallic bond is described by the electron-sea model: metal atoms release their valence electrons into a shared "sea" of delocalised (mobile) electrons, and the resulting cations are held together by attraction to that electron sea.

  • Conduction electrons are free to move → metals conduct electricity and heat well.
  • The mobile electron sea can slide → metals are malleable and ductile (can be hammered and drawn without shattering, because the structure does not rely on directed bonds).

✍️ Must-know melting-point evidence for metallic-bond strength: Li melts at 180 °C, Be at 1287 °C (higher charge + more valence electrons → stronger metallic bond). Down group I: Na 89 °C vs K 63 °C (weaker as atom gets bigger). Gallium is a low-melting metal (mp 29.8 °C) that boils very high (bp 2403 °C).
