3.1 Oil Crops Production and Management

◌ Not started

3.1.1 Sesame (Sesamum indicum)

  • Origin: Africa. Mainly produced for its oil — used in cooking food, in other foods (e.g. margarine), and in paints and cosmetics (soaps). Oil-extraction by-products feed animals.
  • Adaptation: semi-arid lowland areas up to ~1200 m altitude; needs fairly high temperatures (25–27 °C) in the growing season; sensitive to excess water, tolerant of drought but sensitive to extreme shortage; high rainfall during flowering drops flowers → lower yield. Suited to sandy loam soils that don't hold excess moisture.
  • Land prep & planting: sesame is sensitive to weeds → clean fields/seedbed, free of big clods (small seeds). Broadcast or row planting: 50 cm between rows, 10 cm between plants.
  • Weed management: very sensitive to weed competition at early growth → weed from emergence to ~4–5 weeks.
  • Harvesting: up to 5 months to mature; harvest when about one third of leaves, stems and pods turn yellow; delayed harvest → pods break, seed loss. National average ≈ 7.4 quintals (0.74 t)/ha.

3.1.2 Noug (Guizotia abyssinica)

  • Origin: Ethiopia (the country is the largest producer). Produced mainly for oil; accounts for ~half of all food-oil production in the country → important source of good-quality food oil. Also used for paints and soaps; residual cake is animal feed.
  • Adaptation: mid-altitude ~1800–2000 m; does not need high rainfall (can survive on residual soil moisture after rains); tolerates excess soil moisture; performs well on clay-dominated soils that accumulate water.
  • Land prep & planting: well-prepared seedbed for germination; usually broadcast, or rows 25 cm apart drilling seeds ~2 cm deep; usually planted June or July.
  • Weed management: at least one weeding at early stage; later becomes competitive and can dominate weeds. Can be attacked by parasitic weeds — dodder and orobanche → uproot & burn before they seed (else huge spread and yield loss).
  • Harvesting: when leaves change green → brown/yellow; late harvest loses seeds by shattering; too-early harvest gives deformed seeds (reduced quality). National average ≈ 8 quintals (0.8 t)/ha. Mostly for local consumption.

✋ Trap (oil crops): Sesame = lowland semi-arid (up to ~1200 m, 25–27 °C). Noug = mid-altitude (1800–2000 m), clay/excess-moisture tolerant, ~half of Ethiopian food-oil. Both: oil (food, paints, soaps) + residue for animal feed. Noug's parasitic weeds = dodder & orobanche.
