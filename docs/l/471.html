10E.1 Conditionals (revision)

  • Type 1 — two kinds:
  • Zero/Present conditional (general truth, what you normally do): If + simple present → simple present: If I go to a friend's house, I usually take a gift; When I have a day off, I often go to the beach.
  • Probable/first conditional (probable future): If + present → will + verb: If you come tomorrow, you'll get me in my office.
  • Type 2 (imaginary/unreal present): If + simple past → would + verb: If I owned a car, I would drive to work (but I don't own one).
  • Type 3 (regret/hypothesis about the past, impossible): If + past perfect → would have + past participle: If you had worked hard, you would have passed your exam; If she had found his number, she might have called.
  • Common mistakes to correct: no "will" in the if-clause (if I do this, not if I will do); tense agreement; unless already negative (Unless she is careful, not unless she is not careful); inversion Had I arrived on time, I would not have missed it.
  • Situations practice (using 1st/2nd/3rd conditionals).

10E.2 Adverbial clauses of condition

  • Adverbial clauses of condition describe the conditions necessary for a specific action/event to happen; introduced by if, whether (or not), unless, in the event of, provided (that) (and inversion forms with had).
  • If it rains tonight, I'm not going to work tomorrow.
  • Meseret can't attend unless her parents allow it.
  • He's always doing crazy stunts whether or not they're considered safe.
  • In the event of a hurricane, you must stay inside.
  • Had I come here earlier, I could have helped you.
  • They'll approve your request provided you pay the appropriate amount.

10E.3 Simple present vs present progressive (review)

  • Simple present = habits, routines, general truths, scheduled facts: Every Monday, Hagitu drives her kids to football; I usually work as a secretary; The business cards are normally printed in New York.
  • Present progressive = actions happening now, temporary situations, future plans: I am studying French this summer; The child is sleeping; It is raining; I'm going to a movie tonight.
  • Note adverbs like always/usually/normally (simple) vs currently/at the moment (progressive).

10E.4 Types of adjectives

  • Adjectives = one of the eight parts of speech. The eight types: 1. Proper adjective — derived from a proper noun: the English language; the Indian Ocean; the Victorian attitude. 2. Descriptive/qualitative/attributive adjective — shows kind/quality: a brave person; a beautiful child; a careful mother (some come from nouns: a law college, a flower garden). 3. Quantitative adjective — shows how much (quantity), followed by a singular material/abstract noun: any apple, much bread, little milk. 4. Numeral adjective — shows number/serial order (two main classes — definite number vs order). 5. Demonstrative adjective — points out which person/thing: this boy, that person, those men. 6. Distributive adjective — refers to each one of a number; only four: each, every, either, neither. 7. Interrogative adjective — asks a question: whose pen is this? which flower do you like best? 8. Possessive adjective — my, our, your, his, her, its, their before a noun.
