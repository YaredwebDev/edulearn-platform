3.10 Malting in Ethiopian Tradition

◌ Not started

Malting (sprouting) = the process of steeping, germinating and drying grain to convert it into malt — limited controlled germination that mobilises amylases, proteases and other enzymes to hydrolyse/modify grain components.

Steps of modern malting (in order): 1. Steeping — cleaning grain kernels and bringing them to life with water & oxygen (immersed in water/air for a specified time, ~24–48 h). Activates existing enzymes and stimulates production; activates starch- and protein-hydrolysing enzymes. Done when barley reaches sufficient moisture. 2. Germinating — growth & modification; rootlets emerge and a shoot (acrospire) grows. Modification = breakdown of protein/carbohydrates opening the seed's starch reserves, over ~4–6 days → green malt. Controlled temperature/moisture/airflow/water spray. 3. Kilning (heating) — drying the green malt to stop further germination (prevent the plant using all the starch the brewer needs); develops flavour & colour, achieves enzymatic activity and friability. Gives the malt colours/flavours for ales and lagers.

✍️ Must-know (MCQ): the three steps of malting = steeping, germinating and kilning (option C).

Why is malting done? To modify barley grain structure and allow synthesis/activation of enzymes (β-glucanase, α-amylase, β-amylase, protease) to make malt for brewing, distilling or food production. The main purpose is to produce α-amylase and β-amylase that convert grain starches into sugars (monosaccharides, disaccharides, maltose, maltodextrins); yeast then uses these sugars in alcoholic fermentation to produce alcohol.

Traditional malting for local drinks: sprouting barley grains to make enzymes (α-amylase, β-amylase) for fermenting Tella. Steps: soak (steep) → germinate (sprout) → kiln (heat). Grains used: barley (most preferred), maize, millet, sorghum. Barley is best because of its high starch-to-protein ratio and adhering husk. Ethiopian local drinks: Tella, Teji, Booka, Cheka, Keribo, Korefe, Shameta, Borde (for holidays, weddings, celebrations).

✍️ Must-know: malting (Tella production) uses sprouted barley to produce α- and β-amylase which convert starch into fermentable sugars; yeast ferments these to ethanol. Barley is the preferred grain.
