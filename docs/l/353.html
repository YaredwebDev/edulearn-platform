2.6 Punctuation (comma, semicolon, colon)

◌ Not started

Comma (,) — main uses: 1. Separate items in a list: "We bought tea, coffee, sugar and bread." 2. After an introductory phrase/clause: "After the meeting, we left." 3. Before a coordinator joining independent clauses: "It rained, so we stayed in." 4. Around a non-essential clause/appositive: "My father, a teacher, lives here." 5. Between adjectives: "a long, tiring journey." 6. After discourse markers: "However, I agree." 7. Around a name in direct address: "Tigist, please come."

Semicolon (;) — joins two related independent clauses (no conjunction): "She works hard; she rarely rests." Also before conjunctive adverbs: "He was late; however, he apologised."

Colon (:) — introduces a list, an explanation, a quote, or time (chapter:verse / clock time): "Bring three things: a pen, a book and a notebook." "2:30 p.m."

✍️ Must-know: The famous comma example — "Let us eat, mother." vs "Let us eat mother." The comma saves the meaning! Semicolon = a stronger pause than a comma but weaker than a full stop. Colon = introduces.
