📖 GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS (quick definitions for revision)

Adjective – a word that modifies or gives additional information about a noun.

Adverb of frequency – a word that tells how often an action happens (always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never).

Antonym – a word with the opposite meaning (fast/slow, safe/dangerous).

Article – a word used before a noun: a, an (indefinite), the (definite).

Bane – a source of harm or ruin (opposite of boon).

Boon – a benefit; a blessing (opposite of bane).

Collocation – words that naturally go together (heavy traffic, strong argument, rich culture).

Conditional sentence – an if-clause + a main clause; types zero, 1, 2, 3.

Connectors of addition – words that add another point (and, also, besides, moreover, furthermore, in addition).

Connectors of sequence – words that order ideas or time (first, next, then, finally, later).

Defining (essential) clause – a relative clause that gives essential information; takes NO commas.

Descriptive essay – an essay that describes a person, place, community or thing with rich detail.

Discourse markers – linking words (because, however, therefore, moreover).

Equality – giving everyone the same things so all can enjoy full, healthy lives.

Equity – understanding and giving people what they need to enjoy healthy lives (tailored to need).

Expository essay – an essay that explains something (definition, cause/effect, comparison, process).

First conditional – If + present, will + V1 (probable future).

Gerund – an -ing verb used as a noun (reading, gardening, helping).

Justice – the collective responsibility of a free and just society to protect rights for every individual; every person receives their due.

Modal verb – an auxiliary adding meaning such as ability, permission, obligation (can, may, must, should, etc.).

Non-defining (non-essential) clause – a relative clause giving extra information; set off by commas.

Past continuous – was/were + -ing (action in progress at a past time).

Past participle (V3) – the third form of a verb used with have/has/been.

Phrasal verb – a verb + particle whose meaning often differs from the verb alone (give up, cut down).

Polite agreement / disagreement – phrases to agree or disagree courteously (I'm afraid I disagree...).

Prefix – a word part added at the beginning that modifies meaning (un-, dis-, anti-, ante-, en-, ex-).

Present continuous – am/is/are + -ing (action now; definite future plan).

Present perfect – have/has + V3 (unfinished action; result; experience; since/for/just/already/yet).

Present perfect continuous – have/has + been + -ing (action started in past and continuing to now).

Second conditional – If + past (were), would/could/might + V1 (unlikely/unreal present).

Simple past – V2 / did + V1 (completed past actions).

Simple present – V1 / V1-s (habits, general truths, schedules).

State verb – a verb not usually used in continuous forms (think, believe, know, want, like).

Suffix – a word part added at the end that changes word class (-ate, -ise/-ize, -ment, -tion).

Synonym – a word with the same or similar meaning (big/large, end/finish).

Tag question – a short question added to confirm information (Birds are free, aren't they?).

Third conditional – If + had + V3, would/could/might + have + V3 (impossible past).

Topic sentence – the sentence that carries the main idea of a paragraph.

Wh-questions – questions beginning with who, what, where, why, when, which, how, whose.

Word formation – creating new words using prefixes and suffixes.

Yes/no questions – questions that can be answered "yes" or "no" (with do/does/did/have/modals).

Zero conditional – If + present, present (general truths/facts).

📝 GRAMMAR CHEAT-SHEET (one-line reminders)

  • Tag questions: positive → negative tag, and vice-versa; "I am → aren't I?"
  • Simple present: V1-s for he/she/it; negatives/questions with do/does.
  • Present continuous: am/is/are + -ing.
  • Simple past: V2; did + V1 for negative/question.
  • Past continuous: was/were + -ing.
  • Present perfect: have/has + V3; since (point) / for (period).
  • Present perfect continuous: have/has + been + -ing.
  • Modals of obligation: must (own view) / have to (external) / mustn't (prohibited) / don't have to & need not (not necessary).
  • Zero conditional: if + present, present (facts).
  • 1st conditional: if + present, will + V1 (probable).
  • 2nd conditional: if + past (were), would + V1 (unlikely).
  • 3rd conditional: if + had + V3, would have + V3 (impossible past).
  • Relative pronouns: who (people), which (things), that (either), whose (possession), where (place), when (time).
  • Defining clause: no commas; non-defining clause: commas.
  • since = point in time; for = period of time.
