3C Reading Skills: Socio-Economic Impacts of Road Traffic Accidents

◌ Not started

Key facts from the WHO-based passage (memorise for comprehension & vocabulary): - Road traffic accident is both a public health and development issue. - Every year >1.25 million die and ~50 million are injured/disabled on the world's roads. - Injured people occupy 30–70% of orthopedic beds in developing-country hospitals. - Road injuries estimated to be the ninth leading cause of death globally, predicted seventh by 2030. - Low- & middle-income countries hold 54% of registered vehicles but suffer ~90% of road deaths (an asymmetrical relationship); crashes cost them ~5% of GDP. - Africa has the highest regional fatality rate: 26.6 per 100,000 (global ~17.5); Africa owns only 2% of vehicles but ~16% of world deaths. - Ethiopia: among the highest death rates; 2013 fatality ~4,984.3 per 100,000 vehicles/year (vs 574 across sub-Saharan Africa); deaths comparable to those from malaria; casualties per crash ~30× the US figure. - Effects: households pushed into poverty (medical care costs + loss of income earners); national economy hurt (consumes resources, damages property, kills/disables the productive age group). - The passage argues for timely, accurate, relevant data collection/analysis to examine trends & find solutions.

✍️ Reading skill: read for purpose & central theme, recognise organisation (emphatic/development), use skimming for gist & scanning for details, complete main-idea/supporting-detail tables, and answer direct & inferential questions.
