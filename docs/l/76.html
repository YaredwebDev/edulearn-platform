12.4 Threats to Biodiversity

◌ Not started
  1. Land use change — converting forests, grasslands & woodlands to cultivated land destroys natural habitats; done to expand cropland; loses many plant & animal species.
  2. Unsustainable use of resources — overexploitation: overgrazing (exposes soils to erosion → loss of species & ecosystem services), uncontrolled fishing, overuse of water resources, overharvesting of tree species. Examples: overharvesting of timber threatened Hagenia abyssinica; over-pumping from lakes/wetlands caused Lake Haramaya to disappear for years (only returning via restoration).
  3. Invasive species — compete against local species for resources & habitats → loss of biodiversity & ecosystem services. Ethiopian examples: - Parthenium weed (Parthenium hysterophorus) — invades forage & croplands → yield losses. - Prosopis juliflora — invaded rangelands in the Awash Valley (Afar & Somali regions) → loss of forage species. - Water hyacinth ("Emboch") — major threat to the Awash river basin & Lake Tana: dense canopy limits boat traffic & recreation, shades native aquatic plants, harms aquatic animals, reduces oxygen & fish production, and reduces lake water volume (can threaten the lakes' existence).
  4. Climate change — increased max & min temperatures in Ethiopia; changed rainfall distribution; shortened crop-growing season; decrease in crop varieties needing long seasons. (Discussed in Unit 13.)
  5. Environmental pollution — improper management of solid & liquid wastes containing toxic substances that enter water/forests/soils (habitats) → harm to organisms, reduction in species/populations, even total species loss. Irrigation with runoff containing dissolved pesticides/fertilisers affects soil & aquatic organisms; eroded soils with high N & P pollute water → harm fish, possible extinction.
  6. Population growth — rising population raises demand for food & services; unchecked growth → expansion of agricultural lands, overexploitation of resources, use of marginal/unsuitable lands.
