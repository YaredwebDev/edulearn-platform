7.4 Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

Theorem 7.1 (Parallel): Two non-vertical lines are parallel iff they have the same slope (m₁=m₂).

Theorem 7.2 (Perpendicular): Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular iff m₁·m₂ = −1 (their slopes are negative reciprocals).

Parallel and perpendicular lines

Examples:

  • Line through P(6,4),R(7,11) has m=7; line through A(0,0),B(2,14) has m=7 → parallel.
  • Line through A(6,0),B(0,12) has m=−2; line through P(8,10),Q(4,8) has m=1/2 → −2·(1/2)=−1 → perpendicular.
  • Line through (5,4) parallel to y=2x+1 → y−4=2(x−5) → y=2x−6. Perpendicular → m=−1/2 → y=−½x+13/2.
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Parallel ⟺ equal slopes. Perpendicular ⟺ slopes multiply to −1 (negative reciprocals).
