9.4 Grammar

9.4.1 Articles: Definite and Indefinite

  • Definite article "the": refers to a specific noun/group: "the freckles on my face," "the frog in the pond," "the breakfast on my plate."
  • Indefinite articles "a/an": refer to a non-specific noun: "a Bajaj from the garage" (any Bajaj), "an event in history."
  • Choice depends on the SOUND at the start of the next word:
  • an before a vowel sound: an elephant, an Ethiopian woman, an hour, an heir, an honorarium, an honest person.
  • a before a consonant sound: a cow, a tree, a uniform, a union, a European, a one-eyed person (these begin with a consonant/y/w sound despite the letter).
  • "the" used before singular/plural/countable/uncountable nouns, and in fixed expressions: the teacher(s), the milk, the Sahara Desert, the Awash River, the earth, the old (old men/women), the guitar, the piano, the first/second/last/largest/smallest.
  • "a" means one; "an" also means one (before vowel sounds).
Note: ✋ Trap: an hour (silent h → vowel sound), an honest man, an heir, but a uniform, a European, a one-eyed person (vowel letter but consonant sound). "the old" (the + adjective = group of people).

9.4.2 State Verbs (thoughts & opinions)

State verbs are not normally used in continuous forms. Wrong: "I am thinking that…" → "I think that…". Wrong: "Are you believing…?" → "Do you believe…?" Wrong: "The teacher is hoping…" → "The teacher hopes…".

  • Verbs of thoughts: think, understand, know, realize, recognize.
  • Verbs of opinion: believe, hope, feel, see.
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: State verbs (think, believe, know, understand, want, like, realize, recognize, hope, feel) use the simple present, not continuous. This is a classic correction exercise.

9.4.3 Simple present, simple past, simple future (revision)

  • Simple present: habitual actions, general truths, future plans/schedules; singular subject → verb + -s: "She washes her hair twice a week." Negative/question with do/does: "Does she wash…? / She does not wash…" "The janitors clean the office every morning. Do they clean…? They do not clean…"
  • Simple past: completed past actions; used to for past habits: "When I was a child, I used to play with mud." Regular –ed; irregular forms (took, became, could, went). Negative/question with did: "Did you help…? / I did not help…"
  • Simple future: will + V1: "I will wash my clothes tomorrow morning." Negative/question with will: "Will we go…? / We will not go…" Other modals: may, can.
Note: ✋ Trap corrections (from the unit): "Did your teacher gave…?" → "Did your teacher give…?"; "His sister does not helps…" → "does not help"; "The Minister will came…" → "will come"; "Water boils at 100°C but freezes at 0°C"; "Before I got married, I bought a house"; "Where are you born?" → "Where were you born?"; "I used to smoke" (not "use to smoked").

9.4.4 Conditional Sentences to express wishes

  • Wishes use if + subject + were… : "If I were a bird, I would fly." "If I were you, I would take the offer." "What would you do if you were my sister?"
  • Wish-type conditionals express imaginary/unreal conditions (Type 2) or unreal past (Type 3): "If I had had time, I would have enjoyed with you." "If she had replied, I would have written again."
  • Practice: "I would buy a house if I won the lottery" (unreal present). "Michael Jackson would be happy if he was/were a white American."
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: A wish uses "if + were" (all persons) for unreal present; use Type 3 (had+V3 / would have+V3) for unreal past. "If I were the teacher, I would teach effectively."
