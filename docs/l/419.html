Two listening texts: 1. The Gregorian Calendar — the most widely used calendar; the international standard for representation of dates & times; a solar calendar based on a 365-day common year in 12 months of irregular lengths; 11 months have 30 or 31 days, February has 28 (common year). A leap year occurs ~every 4 years when one extra/intercalary day is added on 29 February → 366 days. The year divides into 7-day weeks numbered 1–52 or 53; Monday is the international standard start of week (though the US & Canada start on Sunday). The Gregorian was created to correct the slight inaccuracy of the Julian calendar's solar measurements; adopted ~1580/1582. 2. Ancient Civilizations in Ethiopia — Aksum (home of the towering ~30-m stelae), Gondar (17th-century palaces, gardens, grand public baths), the Zagwe dynasty (rose at a commercial crossroads between Egypt, Sudan's goldfields and the Red Sea). Ethiopia has remarkable world-heritage monuments (contradicts the "no monuments" idea). Vocabulary: perched, uninitiated, testimony, pilgrimage.
