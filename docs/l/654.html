6.3 Electric Potential

◌ Not started

Electric potential energy: the work required to bring a charge to a position. Electric potential V = electric potential energy per unit charge: V = U/q. - Potential due to a point charge: V = k q / r (scalar; can be positive or negative depending on charge sign). - Potential difference (voltage) ΔV = V_B − V_A = W/q (work per unit charge to move between points). Unit volt (V); 1 V = 1 J/C. - Potential difference in a uniform field: ΔV = E d (V = Ed for parallel plates separated by d), and E = V/d.

✍️ Must-know: V = kq/r (point charge); potential difference ΔV = W/q; in a uniform field V = Ed. Potential of a system = algebraic (scalar) sum of individual potentials.

Equipotential lines/surfaces: lines/surfaces connecting points of equal potential. Characteristics: 1. Equipotential surfaces never intersect. 2. Uniform-field equipotentials are parallel lines; a point charge's are concentric spheres. 3. They are crowded near the charge where E is stronger. 4. No work is required to move a charge along an equipotential line (no potential change). 5. Equipotentials are always perpendicular to electric field lines.

✋ Trap: potential is a scalar — add potentials algebraically (signs matter); electric field is a vector — add fields with directions. If potential is constant in a region, the electric field there is zero.

Worked examples: Example 6.7 (potential of 1 μC at 1 mm); Example 6.8 (potential at a point from two charges); Example 6.9 (change in potential energy moving a charge toward a charged sphere).
