9E.1 Nominative vs accusative (pronoun) case

  • Case = the grammatical relationship of nouns/pronouns to other words. English has three cases: nominative (subject), genitive/possessive, and accusative (object).
  • Nominative/subjective case marks the subject of a verb: I, you, he, she, it, we, they, who. She became President in 1991; Jim and Huck travelled by boat.
  • Accusative/objective case marks the direct/indirect object of a verb or the object of a preposition: me, you, him, her, it, us, them, whom. They love apple pies (apple pie = direct object); Mary wrote him a letter (him = indirect object); The children went to school (school = object of to).
  • Choose the correct pronoun: It was he who did the work (nominative after be); Tom is several inches taller than I/he (after than, subject of understood verb) vs informal than me; You and he are invited; One of us; object of preposition with him, etc.
  • Relative pronouns: who (subject) vs whom (object): my grandfather, who is from St. Martinsville…; colonists, who were proud… (subject → who).

9E.2 Past continuous & past perfect continuous

  • Past continuous (past progressive) = was/were + present participle (-ing). Used for: a continuing action at a point in the past; two actions in progress at the same time; a longer background action interrupted by another (I was making dinner when she arrived); an action at a precise past time (At 6 o'clock I was eating dinner); past habitual action (She was talking constantly in class).
  • Not every verb suits the continuous (stative verbs); e.g. At noon he arrived (not was arriving).
  • Past perfect continuous = had been + present participle (-ing). Shows an action that began in the past, continued, and ended at a defined later point in the past. Often used with when, for, since, before: Martha had been walking three miles a day before she broke her leg; He had been throwing rocks at her window for five minutes before she came out.
  • Learn past simple + past continuous together; and past perfect + past perfect continuous together.
