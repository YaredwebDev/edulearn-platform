3.2 Density and Specific Gravity

◌ Not started

Density = mass per unit volume: ρ = m/V - SI unit: kg/m³; also g/cm³. 1 kg/m³ = 10⁻³ g/cm³. - Density is a characteristic property of a pure substance; it determines whether an object sinks or floats. Rock (denser than water) sinks; wood floats; oil floats on water. - Temperature and pressure affect density.

Specific gravity (relative density) = ratio of the density of a substance to the density of a standard substance (usually water at 4 °C): SG = ρ_substance / ρ_water - Water at 4 °C is used because it has its highest density at 4 °C (not 0 °C or 100 °C). - SG is a dimensionless (unitless) quantity.

Uses of specific gravity: geologists/mineralogists determine mineral content of rocks; comparing purity of gems; urinalysis.

Some densities (kg/m³): water (4°C) 1×10³; mercury 13.6×10³; aluminium 2.7×10³; iron 7.86×10³; copper 8.92×10³; gold 19.3×10³.

Example 3.1: A mineral of mass 54 g and volume 20 cm³. - ρ = 54×10⁻³ / 20×10⁻⁶ = 2.9×10³ kg/m³ = 2.7×10³ kg/m³ (rounded) - SG = 2.7×10³/1×10³ = 2.7 → it is aluminium.

Example 3.2: Solid iron ball, radius 18 cm, ρ = 7860 kg/m³. V = 4/3 πr³ = 0.024 m³. m = 7860 × 0.024 = 188.64 kg.

✍️ Must-know: ρ = m/V. SG = ρ_substance/ρ_water (water at 4 °C). SG is unitless. 1 kg/m³ = 10⁻³ g/cm³.
