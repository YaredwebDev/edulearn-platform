8.5 Prevention and Control of Common Farm Animal Diseases

◌ Not started

8.5.1 Prevention & Control measures

Prevention = measures applied to prevent the occurrence of a disease. Control = measures applied to prevent transmission after the disease has occurred. Practices: 1. Proper hygiene/sanitation — washing feed & water troughs; separating sick from healthy animals; burning or burying dead animals. 2. Good management — well-nourished animals resist disease/parasites; keep house ventilated & dry; in pasture use rotational grazing; avoid overcrowding. 3. Quarantine & isolation — buy only healthy-looking animals from reputable farms; quarantine new arrivals 2 weeks; isolate any sick animals. 4. Medication — timely vaccination; quarantine diseased animals; use drugs (powder, liquid, solid). 5. Spraying or dipping — spray/dip animals with chemical solutions to control external parasites (flies, lice, mites, ticks); dipping in water with wettable powders controls ticks/mites/lice; do it regularly. 6. Deworming — giving drugs to expel internal parasites; e.g. piperazine controls roundworm; also drugs/antibiotics/drenches. 7. Biosecurity — measures to prevent the introduction/spread of harmful organisms to animals & plants: controlling animal mixing, restricting unnecessary personnel entry, using protective clothing.

8.5.2 Indigenous Knowledge in Livestock Disease Treatment

  • Plant-based remedies are the most important, sometimes the only, method of managing livestock diseases in Ethiopia; inexpensive alternative to science-based treatment.
  • Farmers mostly acquire ethno-veterinary knowledge from their elders. Practices include mechanical, physical, pharmacological, surgical, ritual and managerial methods.
  • Medicinal plants mostly collected from the wild; parts used: leaf, root, rhizome, bulb, bark, seed, stem, whole parts — alone or combined.
  • Preparation methods: concoction (mixing — the major form), squeezing, crushing, pounding. Farmers are aware of toxicity and provide antioxidants; dose determined using household utensils or the hand.
  • Administered mainly by the oral route (most common).
  • Diseases treated: anthrax, black leg, ectoparasites, endoparasites, rabies, FMD, bloat, colic.
  • Beyond plants, some animal fluids are used: e.g. Somali pastoralists in Ethiopia immunise animals against rinderpest using a solution of urine, milk and faeces from an animal with mild rinderpest.
