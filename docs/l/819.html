3.7 Proteins

◌ Not started
  • Elements: C, H, O, N (sometimes P and S).
  • General amino-acid formula: RCH(NH₂)COOH — central carbon bonded to an amine group (-NH₂), a carboxyl group (-COOH), a hydrogen atom, and an R group (side chain).
  • More than 50% of the dry mass of most cells is protein.
  • 20 different amino acids occur in proteins, differing only in their R group.
  • Peptide bond: covalent bond between amino acids formed by condensation (loss of water: one loses -OH, the other loses -H). Many amino acids → polypeptide.
  • Hydrolysis (adding water) breaks peptide bonds during digestion.
  • Sites of protein synthesis: ribosomes.
  • By shape:
  • Globular proteins: curl into a ball; soluble; roles in metabolic reactions (enzymes, haemoglobin, antibodies).
  • Fibrous proteins: long strands; insoluble; structural (keratin → hair/nails/skin; collagen → skin, tendons, cartilage, bones).

Functions of proteins: structural (collagen, elastin, keratin); enzymes (biological catalysts lowering activation energy — DNA polymerase, lysozyme, nitrogenase, lipase); hormones (GH, FSH); respiratory pigments (myoglobin, haemoglobin); transport (serum albumin, channel/carrier proteins); motor (actin, myosin); storage (ferritin, ovalbumin, casein); toxins (diphtheria toxin).

Food tests: - Biuret test: protein in alkaline solution reacts with copper ions → mauve/purple colour. - Xanthoproteic test: concentrated nitric acid + NaOH → identifies aromatic amino acids (phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan).
