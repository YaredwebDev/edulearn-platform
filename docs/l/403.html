9.5 Writing: Letters

◌ Not started

9.5.1 Informal (personal) Letter

  • Written to a family member, close friend, or acquaintance; casual, personal language.
  • Layout: sender's address (top left or right), date below it, salutation ("Dear ___,"), then introduction, body, conclusion, and a complimentary close (Best wishes, Love, Lots of love), then your name.
  • Example (book): Kuma Baysa writing to a friend about a summer trip to Gununo (oxen market, football training).

9.5.2 Formal (business) Letter

  • Formal communication between two or more parties; informational, persuasive, motivational, or promotional.
  • Three key elements: (1) identify & write to your audience (avoid technical words they won't understand); (2) present your objective clearly and concisely (get to the point, no long-winded vagueness); (3) remain professional (polite, state the problem and relevant info, avoid threats and slander).
  • Six elements of a formal letter: 1. Heading — writer's name, address, date. 2. Salutation — greeting ("Dear …:") with title used. 3. Inside address — receiver's name, address. 4. Body — simple, direct, relevant details. 5. Closing — "Sincerely," / "Sincerely yours,". 6. Signature — signed in hand with a pen.
  • Two formats: Full block (all elements against the left margin); Modified block (like full block but date line, closing, and signature on the right).

✍️ Must-know: Formal letter = audience + clear objective + professional tone; 6 elements (heading, salutation, inside address, body, closing, signature); full-block vs modified-block formats. Informal letter = casual, to friends/family, with address, date, salutation, body, close.
