9C Reading Skills: Strategies of Indigenous Conflict Resolution

◌ Not started

Key facts (adapted from Journal of Indigenous Social Development): 1. Popular dispute resolution mechanisms (PDRMs) — societies worldwide use indigenous systems to prevent/resolve conflicts, often based on community customs, family relationships, or institutional practices alongside formal state processes. They are known by many names: unofficial law, traditional institutions of conflict resolution, traditional justice systems, non-state laws, customary dispute resolution, restorative justice, alternative dispute resolution. The text uses PDRMs because popular is associated with the broad masses involved. 2. PDRMs in Ethiopia have a long history — practised for centuries, encouraged even under the Fetha Negast. Elders (Shimagelle) or ad-hoc appointees resolved conflicts at local level. Still practised: the Gadaa among the Oromo, the Shimgillina among the Amhara, etc. Even after criminal-court procedures, some use PDRMs for reconciliation and to control acts of revenge. Many rural/village communities refer complaints to indigenous agents rather than police. 3. Marginalisation since the 1950s–60s — when the imperial regime carried out extensive codification and modernizing/unifying of the laws; a similar trend followed colonialism/slave trade in many African countries. 4. Result: PDRMs are not recognized by law or properly organised. Yet where poverty & illiteracy prevail and judicial processes are inaccessible/costly, traditional resolution is the best method. During codification, PDRMs on family relations & contract interpretation were kept only where they did not contradict the Codes, under supervision of formal courts. 5. Current positive trend: officials & researchers now accept the international/regional tendency to synchronize and apply PDRMs in the justice system (hosting forums/conferences/workshops, establishing research centres, sponsoring scholars). Most people favour this consensus — a great leap forward.

✍️ Reading skill: find words from context (funding/sponsoring, harmonize/synchronize, peacemaking/conciliatory, takes priority/prevails, modernizing/codification, presenting/referring, amicably); pronoun reference; text type (informative) & writer's position (advocator); summary/paragraph-table.
