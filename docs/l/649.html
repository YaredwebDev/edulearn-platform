5.6 Calorimetry

◌ Not started

Calorimetry = the measurement of the quantity of heat exchanged between substances. Law of calorimetry: for an isolated system, heat lost by the hot body = heat gained by the cold body.

To measure specific heat you must know: the mass, the heat energy supplied, the starting temperature, and the final temperature.

Methods to measure specific heat capacity: 1. Heat-exchange / mixture method (calorimeter): a hot object is dropped into cold water in a calorimeter; at equilibrium, heat lost by hot object = heat gained by water (+ calorimeter/stirrer). 2. Electrical method: an electrical heater of known power P for time t supplies Q = Pt; Q = mcΔT ⇒ solve for c. (Assume 100% efficiency, ignore heat to thermometer and surroundings.)

Methods to measure specific latent heat: electrical method (Q = Pt = mL) or method of mixtures (e.g. adding ice/steam to water and using Q = mL + mcΔT).

✍️ Must-know (calorimetry balancing): heat gained by cold object(s) = heat lost by hot object(s). Write each as mcΔT, and for phase changes mL. Set sum of gains = sum of losses and solve for the unknown c or L.

Worked examples: - Example 5.7: 0.05 kg metal bar heated to 200°C immersed in 0.40 kg water at 20°C, equilibrium 22.4°C ⇒ find specific heat of metal (heat exchange method). - Example 5.8: 100 W heater, 5 min, warms 0.50 kg block 20°C→86.6°C ⇒ identify the metal (aluminium). - Example 5.9: copper calorimeter + liquid heated electrically — compute the liquid's specific heat (identify as water). - Example 5.10: bolt dropped into water+copper calorimeter — find specific heat of the bolt. - Example 5.11: mixture method to find the specific latent heat of fusion of ice. - Steam latent-heat experiment: m L + mcΔT balancing.

Practical uses of specific heat: cooking utensils made of low-specific-heat (copper, aluminium) with polished bottoms heat up fast; high-specific-heat materials (wood) are insulators; utensil handles made of high-specific-heat insulating material; the ocean is a huge heat reservoir because of water's high specific heat, stabilising climate.

Practical uses of latent heat: steaming food, cooling drinks with ice, using salt to melt ice on roads, steam-electric energy, and the water→cloud→rain cycle (evaporation/condensation).
