1.3 Early Experiments to Characterize the Atom

◌ Not started

1.3.1 Discovery of the Electron — Cathode Rays (Thomson, 1897)

  • Apparatus: two electrodes sealed in an evacuated glass tube. The negative electrode = cathode, positive = anode.
  • Passing high voltage makes the tube glow greenish; the glow is caused by cathode rays originating from the cathode.
  • Cathode rays travel from cathode to anode; some pass through a hole to form a beam.
  • The beam bends away from the negative plate and toward the positive plate → it consists of negatively charged particles (electrons).
  • Cathode rays also bend in a magnetic field.
  • Properties are independent of the cathode material → electrons are constituents of all matter.
  • Thomson measured the electron's mass-to-charge ratio, mₑ/e = −5.686 × 10⁻¹² kg C⁻¹, but could not get mass or charge separately.
  • Robert Millikan (1909) measured the charge on the electron with the oil-drop experiment: e = −1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. Using Thomson's ratio, electron mass = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg.

✋ Trap: X-rays (not cathode rays) are used to see broken bones.

1.3.2 Radioactivity and the Discovery of the Nucleus

  • Radioactivity (radioactive decay): the spontaneous emission of particles and/or radiation from unstable nuclei (U, Ra). It disproved the idea atoms are inert/indestructible.
  • Three types of rays emitted:
  • Alpha (α) particles: positive, mass ≈ 4× a hydrogen atom, charge twice the electron's magnitude → identical to helium nuclei (²He⁴, i.e. He²⁺). Bent toward the negative plate.
  • Beta (β) particles: electrons coming from inside the nucleus; deflected toward the positive plate.
  • Gamma (γ) rays: high-energy electromagnetic rays, no charge, unaffected by electric/magnetic fields.

Thomson's "plum-pudding" model: electrons and protons randomly distributed in a positively charged cloud.

Rutherford's gold-foil (α-scattering) experiment (1911): He aimed α-particles at a thin gold foil. - Most α-particles passed straight through → atom is mostly empty space. - A few were deflected; very few bounced back → there is a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus containing most of the mass. - This overturned the plum-pudding model and gave the nuclear model of the atom.

✍️ Must-know (Rutherford activity questions): (1) Most pass undeflected because atoms are mostly empty space. (2) A small fraction deflect slightly because they pass near the small positive nucleus. (3) Only those hitting the nucleus head-on (rare) bounce ~180°. (5) Result overturned Thomson's model → atom has a tiny massive positive nucleus.

1.3.3 Discovery of the Neutron

  • James Chadwick (1932): when beryllium is irradiated with α-rays, a strongly penetrating radiation results; Chadwick showed it consists of neutral particles = neutrons.
  • Neutron mass mₙ = 1.67493 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, ≈ 1840× the mass of an electron, and ≈ the proton's mass, but no charge.
