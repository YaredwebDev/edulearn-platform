2.3 Phrasal Verbs: blow, boil, break

◌ Not started
Phrasal verb Meaning Example
blow away to impress greatly; to carry off by wind The results blew us away.
blow off to (allow to) be carried away by wind; to ignore He blew off the meeting.
blow out to extinguish (a flame); to burst She blew out the candles.
blow up to explode; to inflate; to become very angry A tyre blew up on the road.
boil down to to be ultimately a matter of It all boils down to hard work.
break in to enter by force; to interrupt Thieves broke in at night.
break out (war/fire/disease) to start suddenly A fire broke out in the warehouse.
break through to force a way through; to make a discovery Scientists broke through with a new vaccine.
break down to stop working; to collapse; to become upset The car broke down on the way.
break off to separate; to end abruptly She broke off the conversation.

✋ Trap: break down has several meanings (machine stops / someone becomes emotionally upset / negotiations fail / to analyse into parts). Let the context decide.
