7.3 Uses of Nuclear Radiation

◌ Not started

Medical applications: - External beam radiation (teletherapy): a beam from outside the body precisely aimed at a cancer site (e.g. gamma from a radioactive cobalt-60 source). - Internal radiation therapy: - Brachytherapy: placing a gamma/beta emitter (tube, wire, capsule, seeds, pellets) in/near the cancer. e.g. iodine-131 treats thyroid cancer and non-malignant thyroid disorders. - Systemic radiation therapy: the patient swallows or is injected with a radioactive substance that travels to find & kill cancer cells. - Nuclear medicine / tracers: injected radioactive sources act as tracers to image soft tissues such as blood vessels or kidneys (diagnosis). The radioactive source should emit gamma radiation (penetrates out of the body for detection). - Ethiopian context: Black Lion Specialised Hospital (AAU) has used radioisotopes for cancer treatment.

✋ Trap: for diagnostic imaging/tracing you want a gamma emitter (to escape the body and be detected). Iodine-131 (beta+gamma emitter) is used to treat thyroid conditions because the thyroid concentrates iodine.

Radioactive dating: using the decay of a radioactive isotope to find the age of objects/fossils/rocks. N = N₀e^(−λt). - Carbon-14 dating (C-14): used to date recent organic material/fossils (< ~50,000 yr), because living things maintain a constant ¹⁴C/¹²C ratio that drops after death. - Potassium-40 (K-40): used to date rocks older than ~100,000 years (long half-life). - Lucy/Dinknesh: her age was determined using radioactive dating (she is ~3.2 million years old).

✋ Trap: C-14 is suited to recent organic/fossil material; K-40/uranium-series (long half-lives) are used for very old rocks. Longer-half-life isotopes date older objects.
