11.2 Soil Management

◌ Not started
  • How we manage soil determines its exposure to erosion & degradation. Repeated cultivation compacts soil (particles pressed together) → rainwater doesn't enter → runoff → soil erosion. Bare soil (no crop/grass/vegetation) is easily washed away; complete plant removal at harvest or burning leaves the soil empty and causes nutrient depletion & fertility decline.
  • Agronomic measures for annual crops (objective: protect soil exposure to water/wind erosion by covering it):
  • Mulching = spreading organic materials (crop residues) or inorganic materials (plastic) on the soil surface. It absorbs the energy of raindrops & runoff, prevents direct exposure, reduces the eroding capacity of water. Dry crop residues/mulch also protect soil.
  • Mixed cropping — crops grown in mixture give better coverage than mono-cropping. E.g. maize + beans in Ethiopia: maize canopy protects against raindrops, beans add canopy coverage.
  • Contour cropping / contour cultivation / strip cropping — planting long strips/plots of different crops along the contour (opposite the slope). Different canopy strips reduce sediments transported down the slope, reduce runoff speed → less soil erosion. Contour cultivation = cultivation opposite the slope direction.
  • Improving soil organic matter / fertility: including legumes in cereal plantings, applying manure, compost or organic fertilisers. (Inorganic fertilisers increase fertility too, but more crop types increase organic matter.) More organic matter → better resistance to erosion & structural deterioration → higher fertility → better growth → better canopy → less erosion.
  • Liming = adding calcium- & magnesium-rich materials (e.g. limestone) to raise pH & reduce soil acidity. Ca & Mg replace acidic nutrients, raising pH. Farmers in acidic areas also add wood ash (reduces acidity somewhat). Alternative: plant acid-tolerant crops that yield reasonably in acidic soils — e.g. rice, cowpea, cassava, mango.
  • Management of saline soils: remove accumulated soluble salts by washing (leaching) with good-quality water; add organic fertilisers (compost, animal manure) to improve physical properties & productivity. Salt tolerance differs by plant: sorghum, barley, wheat relatively salt-tolerant; rice is sensitive.
