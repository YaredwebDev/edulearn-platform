1.4.1 Tag Questions

A tag question is a short question added to the end of a sentence to ask for confirmation. Rules: - Positive statement → negative tag: "Birds are free, aren't they?" - Negative statement → positive tag: "He can't fly, can he?" - Auxiliary + pronoun forms the tag (is/are/am, do/does/did, have/has, will/can/must...). - "I am late, aren't I?" — the special exception (never "am I not").

1.4.2 The Simple Present Tense (V1 / V1-s)

Used for: - Habitual / repeated actions: "She walks to school every day." - General truths & facts: "Water boils at 100°C." - Future with schedules/timetables: "The bus leaves at 7:00." - With adverbs of frequency: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never (before the main verb, after be). - Singular subject (he/she/it) → verb + -s/-es. Negative/question use do/does: "She doesn't work here." "Does she live in the city?"

1.4.3 The Present Continuous Tense (am/is/are + -ing)

Used for: - An action happening now: "I am reading." - A temporary situation: "She is staying with her aunt this month." - A definite future arrangement: "We are leaving tomorrow."

✋ Trap — state verbs: understand, believe, know, like, want, love, hate, see, hear are not usually used in the continuous. Say "I understand," not "I am understanding."

1.4.4 Wh- and Yes/No Questions

  • Wh-questions (who, what, where, why, when, which, how, whose) — falling intonation. "Where do you live?"
  • Yes/no questions (do/does/did, have/has, modals) — rising intonation. "Do you like the city?"

✍️ Must-know: Question formation needs the auxiliary: "Where do you live?" (not "Where you live?"). With be or a modal, the word order is Verb/Aux + Subject: "Is she at home?" "Can you help me?"
