1.2 Pure and Applied Fields of Biology

  • Pure biology — the study of how life functions in nature (behaviour, structure, reproduction, etc.).
  • Applied biology — the use of biological knowledge to manage life (gardening, nursery work, agriculture, plant disease control, forestry, poultry, etc.). Relationship is similar to anatomy and surgery.

Examples (Table 1.4):

  • Pure: Morphology, Taxonomy, Evolution, Cytology, Embryology, Physiology, Histology, Paleontology, Bacteriology, Entomology.
  • Applied: Agriculture, Biotechnology, Horticulture, Pathology, Forestry, Pharmacognosy, Poultry, Pisciculture, Apiculture, Agroforestry, Animal Husbandry, Sericulture.

Importance of biology in other branches of science

  1. Biotechnology — use of living organisms or their products for human welfare; involves genetic manipulation (Genetic Engineering); produced genetically modified (GM) crops and animals.
  2. Bioinformatics — acquisition, storage, analysis, and dissemination of biological data (DNA and amino-acid sequences) using computer programs.
  3. Genetic Engineering — extracting or synthesizing selected genes and inserting them into another organism → organism with a new combination of genes.
  4. Biomedical Engineering — application of engineering principles to biology and medicine to improve health.
  5. Environmental Management — observation of the environment and finding solutions to maintain the balance of nature.
  6. Forensic Science — application of biological knowledge (DNA fingerprints, blood typing) to criminal and civil law.
