5.1 Chemical Bonding

◌ Not started
  • Chemical bond: the force that holds atoms together to form molecules or compounds.
  • Most elements exist in a combined state because their atoms are unstable by themselves; only a few (noble gases) have independent existence and are highly stable.
  • Why do atoms bond? To achieve a stable electron configuration (usually a noble-gas configuration).

The Octet Rule (Gilbert Lewis):

Atoms tend to form compounds in ways that give them eight valence electrons — the electron configuration of a noble gas (except helium, which needs 2).

  • The valence electrons of all atoms are between 1 and 8.
  • Exceptions: hydrogen and helium need only 2 electrons; some elements can have more than 8.

Three ways atoms satisfy the octet rule: 1. Losing their own valence electrons (metals). 2. Gaining valence electrons from other elements (nonmetals). 3. Sharing their valence electrons with other atoms (nonmetals).

Example: Sodium (2,8,1) loses 1 electron → 2,8 (stable). Oxygen (2,6) gains/shares 2 electrons → 2,8 (octet).
