4.1 Cell Cycle

A cell cycle is a sequence of events that takes place in the parent cell as a means of distributing genetic material, thereby forming daughter cells.

Two main divisions of the cell cycle:

  1. Interphase (preparation)
  2. Cell division (nuclear division + cytoplasmic division)

Interphase (divided into three phases)

  • G₁ phase (First Gap): cell is metabolically active; accumulates building blocks of DNA and associated proteins; stores energy for chromosome replication.
  • S phase (Synthesis of DNA): DNA replication occurs.
  • G₂ phase (Second Gap): cell replenishes energy stores, synthesizes proteins for chromosome movement; some organelles are duplicated; final preparations before mitosis.

Cell division

  • Karyokinesis (nuclear division) — mitosis or meiosis.
  • Cytokinesis (cytoplasmic division) — separation of cytoplasm into daughter cells.
✍️ Must-know: A cell spends most of its time in interphase (growth + DNA synthesis). Cancer occurs when the cell-cycle controlling system fails and cells divide in an unregulated manner ("cell madness").

Cancer: occurs when the controlling factors over cell division and cell growth fail; cancer cells divide continuously at high density, may not communicate with healthy cells, may not carry out normal functions, and may not anchor securely → they can travel and spread.
