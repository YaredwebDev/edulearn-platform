7.6 Applications

Area of a triangle with vertices (x₁,y₁),(x₂,y₂),(x₃,y₃):

A = ½|x₁(y₂−y₃) + x₂(y₃−y₁) + x₃(y₁−y₂)|

Example: A(−3,4),B(0,1),C(−3,−2): A=½|(−3)(1+2)+0(−2−4)+(−3)(4−1)|=½|−9+0−9|=9. AB=BC=3√2, slopes of AB and BC are −1 and 1 → product −1 → isosceles right-angled triangle.

Line equations of triangle sides & right-angle check: find the slope of each side, check if any two multiply to −1.

Example: Triangle A(−1,1),B(1,3),C(3,1): AB: y=x+2 (m=1); BC: y=−x+4 (m=−1); AC: y=1 (m=0). m₁·m₂=−1 → right-angled at B. BC's y-intercept is 4.

✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Use the triangle area formula with absolute value. Use slopes to classify triangles (right if two slopes multiply to −1; isosceles if two sides equal).
