2.2 Ionic Bonding

◌ Not started

2.2.1 Lewis Symbols and Lewis Structures (recap used all unit)

  • A Lewis symbol shows an element's symbol plus one dot per valence electron placed around the four sides.
  • A Lewis structure shows how the valence electrons of several atoms are shared/transferred, with each shared pair drawn as a line (or two dots) between the bonded atoms.

✍️ Must-know example: Sodium (Group IA, one dot) easily loses one e⁻; chlorine (Group VIIA, seven dots) easily gains one. Transfer gives Na⁺ and Cl⁻ which attract ionically → NaCl.

2.2.2 Formation of Ionic Bonds — lattice energy

An ionic (electrovalent) bond results from the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions formed by electron transfer. A lattice is the regular 3-D arrangement of ions in an ionic solid. Lattice energy (U) is the energy released when gaseous ions come together to form one mole of a solid ionic crystal:

✍️ Must-know definition: lattice energy = energy released (exothermic) when 1 mol of the crystal forms from its free gaseous ions. U is larger (more exothermic, more negative) for ions of higher charge and smaller size because attraction F ∝ (q₁q₂)/r².

Born–Haber cycle (Hess's-law route to lattice energy). You are given the sublimation (ΔH_sub), bond-dissociation/half-molecule terms, ionisation energy (IE), electron affinity (EA), and the overall enthalpy of formation (ΔH_f°); solve for U.

Worked 1 — NaCl: - ΔfH°(NaCl) = −411 kJ mol⁻¹ - Sublimation Na(s)→Na(g) = +108; ½Cl₂ bond = +120; IE₁(Na) = +496; EA(Cl) = −349 - U = ΔfH° − [108 + 120 + 496 + (−349)] = −411 − 375 = −786 kJ mol⁻¹

Worked 2 — LiF: - IE + EA net = +192 (endothermic overall to make ions in gas) - Ion-pair in gas → solid releases U: ion-pair → gas −755; ion(s) → solid, U = −1050 kJ mol⁻¹; ΔfH°(LiF) = −617 kJ mol⁻¹.

💡 Note: Born–Haber is Hess's law applied to ionic solids: lattice energy cannot be measured directly, so we get it via the cycle.

Trends in lattice energy: - U ∝ (q₁ × q₂)/r; bigger/less-charged ions → smaller (less exothermic) U. Order: CaS > BaS (Ba²⁺ bigger), MgO > NaF (higher charges), LiCl > CsCl (Li⁺ smaller than Cs⁺). - Na⁺ radius 102 pm vs K⁺ 138 pm → Na⁺ attracts Cl⁻ more → NaCl has a larger lattice energy (stronger) than KCl. - MgO vs LiF (similar ion sizes): MgO is stronger because it has 2+ and 2− ions (higher charge) vs Li⁺/F⁻ 1+/1−.

2.2.3 Octet rule "exceptions" among ions

  • Fe forms Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺; Zn forms only Zn²⁺; Ga and Sn form ions by losing electrons from the highest principal quantum number (n) first, not lowest energy first.
  • Fe: [Ar]3d⁶4s² → Fe²⁺ = [Ar]3d⁶ (lose 4s²), Fe³⁺ = [Ar]3d⁵.
  • Zn²⁺ = [Ar]3d¹⁰ (its 4s² are lost); Ga³⁺ = [Ar]3d¹⁰ (loses the 4p then the 4s); Sn → Sn²⁺ (loses 5p²), Sn⁴⁺ = [Kr]4d¹⁰ (loses 5p² then 5s²).

✋ Trap: For transition & post-transition ions the electron that is lost first is from the highest shell (largest n), and the resulting stable ion commonly has the 18-electron noble-ish core configuration ns²np⁶nd¹⁰ — not necessarily a noble-gas octet.

2.2.4 Properties of ionic compounds (experimental):

  • Solids with high melting points (held by strong lattice attraction).
  • Do not conduct electricity when solid (ions are fixed in the lattice).
  • Conduct when molten (liquid) or dissolved in water because the ions become mobile.
  • Ionic substances dissolve well in water because water molecules (polar) separate and surround the ions.
