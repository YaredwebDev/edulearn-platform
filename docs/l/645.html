5.2 Heat Transfer Mechanisms

◌ Not started

Three mechanisms of heat transfer: 1. Conduction — heat transmitted through collisions between neighbouring atoms/molecules (mainly in solids). Rate depends on the medium and the conducting slab's cross-sectional area & length. 2. Convection — transfer of heat by the movement of fluid (liquids and gases) — hot fluid rises, cool fluid sinks. 3. Radiation — heat transfer via electromagnetic waves; does not need a medium (can travel through vacuum, e.g. heat from the Sun).

✋ Trap: radiation is the only mechanism that does not require a medium; conduction is the main mode in solids, convection in liquids and gases. Metals feel colder than wood at the same room temperature because metals conduct heat away from your hand faster.

Applications / reasoning: aerogel is an excellent thermal insulator; a metal rod in a fire conducts heat along it; a liquid transfers heat by convection; sitting by a fire you receive most heat by radiation. If air were as good a conductor as iron, heat would escape our bodies/losses would be huge. Insulating walls should be poor conductors, thick, and trap air.
