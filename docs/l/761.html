5.6 Magnetic Force on a Current-Carrying Wire

◌ Not started

A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field experiences a magnetic force (a wire is a collection of moving charges). The electric motor and moving-coil galvanometer depend on this.

Magnitude: F = ILB sin θ - I = current, L = length of wire in the field, B = magnetic field, θ = angle between the current direction and the field.

Direction (right-hand rule): fingers point in direction of B, thumb in the direction of conventional current; the palm faces/pushes in the direction of the force. - Force is always perpendicular to the plane containing the conductor and the field. - Force is greatest when the conductor is at right angles (90°) to the field.

Example 5.3: L = 25 cm = 0.25 m, B = 0.30 T, θ = 90°, I = 6.0 A. F = 6 × 0.25 × 0.3 × sin 90° = 0.45 N.

✍️ Must-know: F = ILB sin θ. Max when wire ⊥ field.
