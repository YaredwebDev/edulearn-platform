UNIT 3 · LESSON 6 OF 6 · GRADE 11 AGRICULTURE

🧮 Key facts to memorise

◌ Not started
  • Industrial crops classified into oil, fibre, sugar crops (Ethiopian context).
  • Sesame: Africa origin; lowland semi-arid ≤1200 m, 25–27 °C; sandy loam; sensitive to excess water; ~7.4 q/ha.
  • Noug: Ethiopia origin (largest producer); ~half of food oil; mid-altitude 1800–2000 m; clay/excess-moisture tolerant; parasitic weeds dodder & orobanche; ~8 q/ha.
  • Cotton: lint fibre; warm & drought-tolerant; high rainfall discolours lint; acid-treated seed; half of plants with open bolls = harvest; 6–7 months; ginning→clean→spin→weave.
  • Sisal: hard fibre from leaf (ropes/sacks/carpets); Central America; rain-fed & drought-resistant; vegetative via suckers/bulbils in nursery; decortication; 2–3 yr to first leaf harvest.
  • Sugar cane: 14–18 months to first harvest; ratoon crop (regeneration, smaller, 2–3×); molasses→ethanol; bagasse→paper/plastic/firewood; 30 cm cuttings with 2–3 buds, rows ~1.6 m; excess N delays maturity.
  • Ethiopian textile industry (2021): >50 garment + 14 textile factories; imports raw cotton.
  • 8 state sugar factories (Wonji-Shewa, Matahara, Finchawa, Kessem, Tendaho, Arjo, Omo Kuraz II & III); ~400,000 t/yr; demand > production (2023).

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) classify industrial crops into oil, fibre and sugar crops with examples; (2) describe sesame and noug (origin, adaptation, planting, weeds, harvesting, uses, yields); (3) describe cotton (uses, processing stages ginning→weaving, Ethiopian status) and sisal (uses, propagation, decortication, harvesting); and (4) describe sugar-cane production from planting to sugar, including ratoon cropping, molasses/bagasse uses, pest/disease control and the state factories? Re-answer the review too (noug & sesame = food-oil crops; sisal = hard fibre for carpets/sacks/ropes; newly planted cane matures in ~18 months — the textbook answer among 8/12/18/24 is 18; molasses → ethanol; ratoon = regeneration second harvest).
