2.7 Valence-Bond (VB) Theory and Orbital Overlap

◌ Not started

VB theory: a covalent bond forms when two atomic orbitals overlap and share the region of space between the two nuclei (electrons in the overlapping orbitals pair up).

Sigma (σ) vs pi (π) bonds: - σ bond: formed by head-on (end-to-end) overlap — s–s (H–H), s–p, or p–p along the internuclear axis. Sigma bonds are strong and can form between hybridised orbitals. - π bond: formed by sideways (lateral) overlap of two parallel p orbitals above and below the bonding axis. A π bond is weaker and cannot exist alone between two atoms. - Single bond = 1σ. Double bond = 1σ + 1π. Triple bond = 1σ + 2π.

✍️ Must-know: π bond = "sidewise overlap of two parallel p orbitals." Double/triple bonds always contain exactly one σ plus one or two π bonds.
