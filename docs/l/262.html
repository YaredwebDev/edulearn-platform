6.3 Conservation of Natural Resources

◌ Not started

6.3.1 Soil Fertility and Earthworms

  • Earthworms = "friends of farmers": they aerate the soil (burrowing), mix and improve soil structure, and their casts (waste) add humus and nutrients, increasing fertility and water-holding capacity.

6.3.2 Biofertilizers and Vermicompost

  • Biofertilizers: living organisms (e.g., Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Azospirillum, blue-green algae) that enhance soil fertility naturally.
  • Vermicompost: compost produced by earthworms digesting organic waste — a rich, eco-friendly fertilizer.
  • These are sustainable, eco-friendly alternatives to chemical fertilizers (which can cause pollution and soil degradation).

6.3.3 Crop Rotation and Legumes

  • Crop rotation: growing different crops in sequence on the same land.
  • Growing legumes (peas, beans, clover) in rotation fixes nitrogen (via Rhizobium), naturally replenishing soil fertility — reducing the need for synthetic nitrogen fertilizers.

6.3.4 Conservation of Natural Resources

  • Natural resources = water, soil, forests, minerals, biodiversity, air.
  • Conservation: careful, sustainable use and protection of natural resources for present and future generations.
  • Methods: reforestation, controlled grazing, terracing, water conservation, sustainable agriculture, recycling, protecting endangered species, establishing national parks.
