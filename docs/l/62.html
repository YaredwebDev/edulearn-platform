10.3 Soil degradation: causes & consequences

◌ Not started

Natural resource degradation = any change/disturbance of the environment that causes deterioration, destruction or depletion of resources (soil, trees, water, air). It can destroy ecosystems and cause extinction of wildlife.

Soil degradation = a long-term decrease in the productivity of soils (reduces capacity to support healthy crops). Three major forms: 1. Water & wind erosion 2. Chemical degradation (soil acidification, salinisation, pollution by toxic chemicals) 3. Physical degradation (soil compaction)

  • Soil erosion = washing away of surface/top soils by water & wind. Mainly by water in the highlands (high rainfall) and wind in the lowlands (low vegetation cover). Natural, but human activities accelerate it: removal of vegetation, intensive tillage, overgrazing, mining, construction. Effects: loss of the productive top soil → lower yields, higher production costs; eroded soil pollutes water bodies (lakes/rivers/streams); increases sediment in dams → reduces hydropower generation.
  • Chemical degradation = loss of plant nutrients, acidification, salinisation, fertility decline. Causes: deforestation, agricultural mismanagement, industrial pollution.
  • Soil acidification — common in Ethiopian highlands (high rainfall). Caused by leaching (removal of basic ions — K, Ca, Mg, Na — by water) or removal in crop harvest, and improper use of acid-forming mineral fertilisers → pH falls below 7. Most crops prefer pH 6.5–7.2. ~40% of Ethiopian soils affected by acidity.
  • Soil salinisation — excessive accumulation of soluble salts in surface soils, from salt-rich irrigation + insufficient drainage; common in lowlands (low rainfall, high evapotranspiration). As water evaporates, dissolved salts remain → white thin layer on the surface; high salt makes water move from roots back to soil → plant dehydration/yield decline/death; restricts cropping options. Especially common in the Ethiopian Rift Valley.
  • Physical degradation = deterioration of natural composition/structure → compacted soils (particles too closely packed). Causes: water/wind erosion, intensive (repeated) tillage (oxen & machinery), overgrazing, loss of cultivated land to urban expansion. Effects: reduced root growth, less water/air movement, reduced nutrient uptake, reduced microbial activity, reduced water-storage capacity, increased flooding → reduced plant growth & yields.
