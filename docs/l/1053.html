7.4 Phrasal Verbs (revision & practice)

  • Phrasal verbs are common in spoken English and informal writing, often replacing a more formal verb: "fill in" = complete.
  • Prepositions/adverbs (at, up, in) added to verbs (go, get) make phrasal verbs with their own meanings; they take all tenses: "They are putting up the aerial"; "She'll put on goggles"; "Ali put off the job."
  • Three groups:
  1. No object: "They sat down." "Look out!"
  2. Object in end-position: "He looked after the video."
  3. Object in mid- or end-position: "She put down the phone." / "She put the phone down."
  • ✋ Pronoun object in group 3 must be mid-position only: "Markos wrote them down" ✓; "Markos wrote down them" ✋.

Unit 7 list: go on, go in, go in to, go off, go out, go over, go through, go up, go with, go for, go down.

  • "The lights went off"; "Aster went in to the nursing school"; "I want to go over this plan"; "Which tie goes with my suit?"; "The game went on"; "I'll go for black coffee"; "The price of food went up"; "It goes off me" (makes me dislike it); "go out for a picnic"; "go down (come in) it's cold."
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: pronoun objects go in the middle ("put it down," "write them down"). Recognise whether a phrasal verb takes an object and where it goes.
