2.4 Equations of Motion with Constant Acceleration

◌ Not started

For uniformly accelerated motion (constant acceleration) along a straight line:

  1. v = v₀ + at
  2. v_av = (v₀ + v)/2
  3. s = v₀t + ½at²
  4. s = ((v₀ + v)/2)t
  5. v² = v₀² + 2as

where v₀ = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time, s = displacement.

Free fall

A freely falling object moves under the influence of gravity alone. All objects near Earth's surface fall with the same constant acceleration g ≈ 9.80 m/s² (downward) in the absence of air resistance.

Replace 'a' with 'g' and 's' with 'h' (height): - v = v₀ + gt - h = v₀t + ½gt² - v² = v₀² + 2gh

✍️ Must-know: g = 9.8 m/s² at Earth's surface, maximum on the surface and decreasing with altitude. For free fall use g and h in place of a and s.

Example 2.7: Car from rest, 5 s, 100 m. a = 2s/t² = 200/25 = 8 m/s².

Example 2.10: Mango falls 1 s. v = gt = 9.8 m/s; h = ½gt² = 4.9 m.
