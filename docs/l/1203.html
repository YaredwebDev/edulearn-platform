6.1 Sum of Interior Angles of a Convex Polygon

Definition: A polygon is a simple closed plane figure formed by three or more line segments joined end to end, no two in succession collinear. The line segments are sides, their common endpoints are vertices, and the angles inside are interior angles.

Polygon names: Triangle (3), Quadrilateral (4), Pentagon (5), Hexagon (6), Heptagon (7), Octagon (8), Nonagon (9), Decagon (10). A polygon with n sides is an "n-gon."

Convex vs concave:

  • Convex: every interior angle < 180° (vertices point outwards).
  • Concave: at least one interior angle > 180° (a vertex points inwards).

Sum of interior angles of an n-sided polygon = (n − 2) × 180°.

Reasoning: Drawing all diagonals from one vertex divides the polygon into (n − 2) triangles, each with 180°.

Examples:

  • Quadrilateral: (4−2)×180° = 360° (2 triangles).
  • Pentagon: (5−2)×180° = 540° (3 triangles).
  • Hexagon: (6−2)×180° = 720°.
  • If sum = 1,080°, then (n−2)×180 = 1080 → n−2 = 6 → n = 8 (octagon).
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Sum of interior angles of n-gon = (n−2)×180°.
