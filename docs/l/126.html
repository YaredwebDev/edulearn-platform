3.6 Types of Enzymes

◌ Not started

3.6.1 Structural classification

  • Simple protein (active) vs conjugated protein (holoenzyme).
  • Holoenzyme = protein part (apoenzyme, inactive) + non-protein part (cofactor).
  • Cofactor further splits into:
  • Prosthetic group — firmly attached (usually a metal ion).
  • Coenzyme — loosely attached (usually a vitamin B complex).
  • Apoenzyme + coenzyme/cofactor = holoenzyme (the active complex).

✍️ Must-know: holoenzyme = apoenzyme + cofactor/coenzyme + (prosthetic groups of metal-ion activators). Most enzymes are proteins; not all proteins are enzymes.

3.6.2 Basic (functional) classification — six classes (end in "–ase")

  1. Oxidoreductases — catalyse oxidation–reduction (electron transfer): A⁻ + B → A + B⁻ (A = oxidant, B = reductant).
  2. Transferases — transfer functional groups (e.g. methyl) from a donor to an acceptor molecule.
  3. Hydrolases — catalyse hydrolysis of various bonds.
  4. Lyases — cleave bonds by means other than hydrolysis/oxidation (e.g. two or more substrates in one reaction).
  5. Isomerases — convert a molecule from one isomer to another.
  6. Ligases — catalyse the joining of two molecules, with concomitant hydrolysis of the diphosphate bond in ATP.

✍️ Must-know (MCQ): an enzyme transferring methyl groups from donor to acceptor = Transferase. Suffix "–ase" marks an enzyme.
