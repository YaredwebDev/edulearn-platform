3.1 Elasticity and Plasticity

◌ Not started
  • All practical solid bodies can be deformed (stretched, compressed, bent) by a deforming force.
  • Elastic body/property (elasticity): if a body regains its original shape and size after the deforming force is removed, it is elastic; the deformation is elastic deformation. (Examples: rubber, metals, steel ropes.)
  • Plastic body/property (plasticity): if a body does NOT regain its original shape and size after the deforming force is removed, it is plastic; the deformation is plastic deformation (permanent, irreversible). (Examples: putty, potter's clay.)

Elastic limit: the maximum deforming force up to which a body retains its property of elasticity. Beyond the elastic limit, the body does not regain its original shape/size (it becomes plastic). All materials are elastic up to a certain deformation, then turn plastic.

Applications of elasticity: - Metallic machinery parts are never stressed beyond the elastic limit (otherwise permanent deformation). - The thickness of crane ropes is chosen from the elastic limit + factor of safety. - Bridges become unsafe after long use because of continuous alternating strains (loss of elastic strength). - Maximum height of a mountain can be estimated from the elastic behavior of Earth.

✍️ Must-know: Elasticity = regain original shape; plasticity = permanent deformation. Beyond the elastic limit a body becomes plastic.
