4.1 The Concept of Force and Newton's Laws of Motion

◌ Not started

Dynamics = the subdivision of mechanics dealing with motion in relation to the physical factor that causes motion — force. Force = an external agent that changes the state of rest or state of motion of a body. 1 N = 1 kg·m/s².

Forces are vectors. There are contact forces (push/pull, friction) and field/action-at-a-distance forces (gravity, electromagnetic).

The four fundamental forces in nature (in order): strong nuclear force, electromagnetic force, weak nuclear force, gravitational force.

Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia)

"An object at rest remains at rest, and an object in motion continues in motion along a straight line with constant speed, unless an external (unbalanced) force acts upon it." Inertia = the tendency of a body to oppose any change in its state of rest or uniform motion. Larger mass ⇒ larger inertia.

Applications: a passenger without a seat belt is thrown forward when a car suddenly stops; airbags deploy during a collision to reduce injury by spreading the force over a longer time/distance.

✋ Trap: the common exam trap is a misconceived reading: inertia is proportional to mass, not to weight or speed. Coin-on-a-card trick (flick the card, coin falls straight down) demonstrates inertia at rest.

Newton's Second Law

"When an unbalanced (net) force acts on an object of mass m, the object accelerates in the direction of the net force with acceleration directly proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to the mass." - F_net = ma (where m = mass, a = acceleration). - Net force = sum (resultant) of all forces. 1 N = 1 kg·m/s².

✍️ Must-know: a = F_net/m. For a fixed force, bigger mass ⇒ smaller acceleration; for a fixed mass, bigger force ⇒ bigger acceleration (verified by the dynamics-cart activity with spring scale).

Weight and gravitational force: weight W = mg. Near Earth's surface g ≈ 9.8 m/s². Weight is a force (measured in N), mass is a scalar property (kg). An object on the Moon weighs one-sixth of its Earth weight because g_moon = g_Earth/6.

✋ Trap: mass is intrinsic and unchanging; weight (mg) changes with g. Example 4.1: 1 kg block weighs 1×9.8 ≈ 9.8 N on Earth and ≈1.63 N on the Moon. Question: is weight the same everywhere? No. If gold were sold by weight you'd rather buy in Addis Ababa (or buy where g is larger and sell where g is smaller…, as discussed in review).

Normal force: the force with which two objects push each other perpendicular to the surface of contact. - Object on a horizontal surface: FN = mg (equals weight). - Object on an incline: FN = mg cos θ, and the component of weight parallel to the incline is mg sin θ.

✍️ Must-know: FN is not always equal to weight. On an incline FN = mg cos θ. The force tending to slide a block down an incline is mg sin θ.

Newton's Third Law

"For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction." Forces come in pairs, acting on two different bodies (you cannot cancel action–reaction pairs because they act on different objects). - Action & reaction are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction, but act on different bodies.

✋ Trap (very common): action–reaction pairs cannot be added/cancelled because they act on different objects. Give examples: as you walk, you push Earth back and Earth pushes you forward; a player kicking a ball; Earth attracts the Moon & the Moon attracts Earth; rocket/ball recoil.

Weight vs normal misconceptions: normal force supports the object on a surface and is perpendicular to it; weight is the gravitational pull and always acts downward (toward Earth's centre).
