2.6 Sorghum (Sorghum bicolor)

◌ Not started
  • Origin: Africa; staple in drier Africa. Stalk used for animal feed and construction (fences, houses).
  • Production: altitude ~500–1500 m; reasonable yields on soils too poor for other crops; well-drained fertile clay soils; well-adapted to dry areas; can stay dormant during dry periods and resume growth when moisture returns — called 'the camel crop'. Rotated with haricot bean, soybean, sesame, cotton.
  • Land prep: 2–3 cultivations.
  • Planting: broadcast or rows 0.5–1 m between rows, ~40 cm between plants; depth ~2.5 cm; usually March–May. Earthing up ~1 month after planting resists lodging.
  • Fertilisation: ~32 kg N + 10 kg P per ha.
  • Weed/pest/disease: striga (parasitic weed) is the major weed causing massive losses → hand weeding before seed set, plus crop rotation and resistant varieties. Also insects, birds and fungal smut. Control = integrated (rotation + resistant varieties + chemicals).
  • Harvesting: matures ~4 months; harvest when grains start drying (moisture ~30%). National average ≈ 23 quintals (2.3 t)/ha.

✋ Trap (altitudes & adaptation — frequent exam question): wheat & barley = highland (~1800–3000 / 2200–3000 m); teff & maize = mid-altitude (maize 500–2400); sorghum = lowland & moisture-deficit (500–1500 m). Maize has the highest yield/ha; teff has the largest area. Teff/maize/sorghum lack gluten; wheat contains gluten.
