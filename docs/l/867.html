5.1 Digestive System

5.1.1 Anatomy of the Digestive System

The digestive system is a tube running from the mouth to the anus, together with associated organs. Its functions: break down food (physical and chemical), absorb nutrients, and eliminate waste.

Main parts (alimentary canal): Mouth → Pharynx → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) → Large intestine (colon, rectum) → Anus.

Associated organs: Salivary glands, Liver, Gall bladder, Pancreas.

Teeth

  • Human teeth: incisors (cutting/biting), canines (tearing), premolars (crushing/grinding), molars (grinding/chewing).
  • Adult (permanent) dental formula: (2:1:2:3 / 2:1:2:3) × 2 = 32 teeth.
  • Milk (deciduous) teeth: (2:1:0:2 / 2:1:0:2) × 2 = 20 teeth.

5.1.2 The Mouth

  • Food is chewed (mastication) and mixed with saliva.
  • Saliva is produced by 3 pairs of salivary glands (parotid, submandibular, sublingual).
  • Saliva contains salivary amylase (ptyalin) which begins breaking starch into maltose, and mucus (lubrication).
  • Bolus = ball of chewed food formed by the tongue.
  • Deglutition (swallowing): the epiglottis closes the windpipe (trachea) so food goes to the oesophagus.

5.1.3 The Stomach

  • A J-shaped muscular bag.
  • Gastric juice contains:
  • Hydrochloric acid (HCl): pH 1.5–2.5; kills bacteria, activates pepsinogen, denatures proteins.
  • Pepsinogen → activated to pepsin (protein-digesting enzyme) by HCl.
  • Mucus: protects the stomach lining from HCl and pepsin.
  • Churns food into chyme (semi-liquid).
  • Absorbs limited amounts of water, alcohol, some drugs (aspirin).

5.1.4 Small Intestine

  • ~6–7 m long; main site of digestion and absorption.
  • Duodenum: first part; receives bile (from gall bladder) and pancreatic juice (from pancreas).
  • Jejunum and ileum: continue digestion and absorption.
  • Intestinal juice from intestinal glands contains enzymes (maltase, sucrase, lactase, peptidase, lipase).
  • The inner wall has villi and microvilli — finger-like projections that enormously increase the surface area for absorption.
  • Inside each villus: capillaries (absorb monosaccharides and amino acids) and a lacteal (absorbs fatty acids and glycerol).

Final digestion products:

  • Carbohydrates → monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose)
  • Proteins → amino acids
  • Lipids → fatty acids + glycerol

5.1.6 Absorption, Assimilation, Egestion

  • Absorption: digested products pass through the intestinal wall into blood/lymph.
  • Assimilation: absorbed nutrients are used by cells to build new materials (e.g., amino acids → body proteins).
  • Egestion (defecation): removal of undigested waste (faeces) via the anus.

5.1.7 Disorders of the Digestive System

  • Constipation: infrequent, difficult defecation; caused by low fibre/water.
  • Diarrhoea: frequent loose, watery stools; caused by infection (e.g., cholera, rotavirus); leads to dehydration.
  • Peptic ulcer: erosion of stomach/duodenum lining; caused by Helicobacter pylori bacteria, excess acid, stress, some drugs.
  • Appendicitis: inflammation of the appendix.
  • Malnutrition: deficiency or excess of nutrients (e.g., marasmus, kwashiorkor, vitamin deficiencies, obesity).
✍️ Must-know: Saliva starts starch; HCl (pH 1.5–2.5) + pepsin digest protein in the stomach; villi in the small intestine absorb nutrients (capillaries for sugars/amino acids, lacteals for fats).
