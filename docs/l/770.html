6.5 Mirrors and Lenses

◌ Not started

Plane Mirrors

A plane mirror has a flat reflective surface. Image characteristics: - Virtual (appears behind the mirror). - Same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front. - Laterally inverted (left ↔ right reversed). - Same size as the object. - Upright (erect).

Number of images formed by two plane mirrors inclined at angle θ: - n ≈ (360°/θ) − 1, if 360°/θ is an even integer. - n ≈ 360°/θ, if 360°/θ is an odd integer. - e.g., θ = 60° → 360/60 = 6 (even) → n = 6 − 1 = 5 images.

Uses of plane mirrors: looking glasses, kaleidoscope, telescope, sextant, periscope, deflecting light.

Spherical Mirrors

  • Concave mirror: curved inward (like a bowl of a spoon); converges light.
  • Convex mirror: curved outward (back of a spoon); diverges light.

Terms: center of curvature (C), radius of curvature (R), pole (P), principal axis, aperture, principal focus (F), focal length (f). - R = 2f (for small apertures); focus lies midway between pole and center of curvature.

Rules for ray diagrams: (1) ray to the pole reflects at the same angle; (2) parallel ray passes through (concave) or appears to come from (convex) the focus; (3) ray through C retraces its path; (4) ray through the focus returns parallel.

Concave mirror images (Table 6.2): | Object | Image | Size | Nature | |---|---|---|---| | At infinity | At F | Highly diminished | Real, inverted | | Beyond C | Between F and C | Diminished | Real, inverted | | At C | At C | Same size | Real, inverted | | Between C and F | Beyond C | Enlarged | Real, inverted | | At F | At infinity | Highly enlarged | Real, inverted | | Between P and F | Behind mirror | Enlarged | Virtual, erect |

Convex mirror images (Table 6.3): always virtual, erect, diminished; object at infinity → image at F; between infinity and P → image between P and F, diminished.

Uses: concave mirrors in torches, searchlights, headlights, shaving mirrors, dentist mirrors, solar furnaces; convex mirrors as rear-view (wing) mirrors in vehicles (erect, diminished, wide field of view).

Mirror formula and magnification: - 1/v + 1/u = 1/f - m = h'/h = −v/u

Sign conventions: u positive for real object (in front); v positive for real image (in front of mirror); f positive for concave, negative for convex; h' positive if upright, negative if inverted.

Example 6.5: Convex rear-view mirror, R = −3.00 m → f = −1.50 m; bus at u = +5.00 m. 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/1.50 − 1/5.00 → v = −1.15 m (behind mirror). m = −v/u = +0.23 → virtual, erect, smaller.

Example 6.6: Object 6 cm at 25 cm in front of concave mirror (f = 15 cm). 1/v = 1/15 − 1/25 → v = 37.5 cm (real image; screen placed there). h' = −(hv/u) = −(37.5×6/25) = −6.0 cm → inverted, enlarged.

Lenses

A lens = a curved piece of transparent material that refracts light predictably. - Convex (converging) lens: thicker in the middle than at the edges; converges light. f positive. - Concave (diverging) lens: thicker at the edges than in the middle; diverges light. f negative.

Terms: two centers of curvature (C₁, C₂), principal axis, optical center (O, ray through it is undeviated), aperture, two principal foci (F₁, F₂), focal length (f).

Rules for ray diagrams: (1) parallel ray passes through focus (convex) or appears to come from focus (concave); (2) ray through focus emerges parallel; (3) ray through optical center passes undeviated.

Convex lens images (Table 6.5): | Object | Image | Size | Nature | |---|---|---|---| | At infinity | At F₂ | Highly diminished | Real, inverted | | Beyond 2F₁ | Between F₂ and 2F₂ | Diminished | Real, inverted | | At 2F₁ | At 2F₂ | Same size | Real, inverted | | Between F₁ and 2F₁ | Beyond 2F₂ | Enlarged | Real, inverted | | At F₁ | At infinity | Highly enlarged | Real, inverted | | Between F₁ and O | Same side, enlarged | Enlarged | Virtual, erect |

Concave lens images (Table 6.6): always virtual, erect, diminished regardless of object position.

Lens formula and magnification: - 1/u + 1/v = 1/f - m = h'/h = −v/u

Sign conventions: f positive for converging (convex) lens, negative for diverging (concave); v positive for real image (behind lens), negative for virtual.

Example 6.7: Concave lens f = −15 cm, image at v = −10 cm. 1/u = 1/f − 1/v = −1/15 + 1/10 = 1/30 → u = 30 cm. m = −v/u = −(−10)/30 = +0.33 → erect, virtual, ⅓ size.

Example 6.8: Object 2 cm at 15 cm from convex lens (f = 10 cm). 1/v = 1/10 − 1/15 → v = +30 cm (real). h' = −(2×30/15) = −6.0 cm; m = −2 → inverted, enlarged.

✍️ Must-know: Mirror & lens formula both 1/f = 1/u + 1/v; m = h'/h = −v/u. Concave mirror / convex lens: concave mirror convex. Convex mirror & concave lens always give virtual, erect, diminished images. R = 2f.
