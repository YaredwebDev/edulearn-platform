⚡ Quick Revision Box (Unit 5)

  1. Octet rule: 8 valence electrons = stable (He needs 2).
  2. Octet satisfied by: lose (metals), gain (nonmetals), share (nonmetals).
  3. Ionic bond: metal + nonmetal, large ΔEN (≥ 2.0). Electrostatic attraction.
  4. Covalent bond: nonmetal + nonmetal, ΔEN < 0.5 nonpolar; 0.5–2.0 polar.
  5. Ionic: high m.p./b.p., conducts when molten/dissolved, hard/brittle, soluble in water, high density.
  6. Covalent: low m.p./b.p., poor conductor, gases/liquids, soluble in nonpolar solvents.
  7. Single, double, triple bonds (H–H, O=O, N≡N).
  8. Coordinate bond: one atom supplies both electrons (NH₄⁺, H₃O⁺, BF₃–NH₃, Al₂Cl₆).
  9. Metallic bond: electron sea model; delocalized electrons; good electrical/thermal conductivity.
  10. Polar molecule examples: H₂O, HCl, NH₃, SO₂, H₂S. Nonpolar: H₂, O₂, N₂, CO₂, CCl₄, CH₄.
