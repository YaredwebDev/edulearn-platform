6.19 🧮 Key Formulas & Facts to Memorise

◌ Not started
  • Alcohols: CnH2n+1OH; ethers R–O–R′; aldehydes R–CHO; ketones R–CO–R′; acids R–COOH; esters RCOOR′
  • 1° alcohol →(oxid.)→ aldehyde →(oxid.)→ carboxylic acid; 2° alcohol → ketone; 3° alcohol → no normal oxidation
  • Ethanol + H₂SO₄: 170 °C → ethene (dehydration); 140 °C → diethyl ether
  • Ethanol fermentation: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2CH₃CH₂OH + 2CO₂ (zymase); max ~12–15%; hydration of ethene: 573 K/60 atm/H₃PO₄
  • Esterification: RCOOH + R′OH → RCOOR′ + H₂O (H⁺/H₂SO₄)
  • Ester hydrolysis: acid → RCOOH + R′OH; base (saponification) → RCOO⁻ + R′OH
  • Carboxylic acids ≤ 4 C miscible with water; propanoic acid bp 141 °C (highest of the isomeric set)
  • Fatty acid mp: stearic 69 (saturated) vs oleic 13 (1 C=C) vs linoleic −5 (2 C=C) vs linolenic −11 (3 C=C) for 18-C chains
  • Fats = saturated (sp³) solid triglycerides; oils = unsaturated (sp² C) liquid triglycerides; hydrogenation (Ni/Pd) converts oil → fat
  • Williamson ether synthesis: R′X + R–O⁻ → R–O–R′ + X⁻

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) classify each functional group and give general formulas; (2) write IUPAC (and common) names and structures for alcohols, ethers, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters; (3) classify alcohols (mono/di/tri; 1°/2°/3°) and acids (mono/di/tri); (4) describe physical trends (bp/solubility) and explain them by H-bonding; (5) name the preparations (hydration, fermentation, Williamson, oxidation, esterification) and predict products of oxidation/dehydration/HX/esterification/hydrolysis/reduction; (6) use chemical tests to tell an aldehyde from a ketone and detect a carboxylic acid (litmus + Na₂CO₃ fizz); (7) draw a triglyceride and explain fat vs oil, hardening, and rancidity; and (8) answer textbook review items (e.g. which is least water-soluble — hexane; CH₃OH + CH₃COOH → ester; secondary alcohols give ketones; acetic acid from fermentation of ethanol; fats/oils are esters)?
