1.1 Scalars and Vectors

◌ Not started

A physical quantity is any number or set of numbers used for a quantitative description of a physical phenomenon. Physical quantities are divided into two groups: scalars and vectors.

  • Scalar quantity: a physical quantity that can be completely specified by a single number together with an appropriate unit of measurement. It has only magnitude (no direction).
  • Examples: time, distance, speed, length, volume, temperature, energy, power, mass, density.
  • Vector quantity: a physical quantity that requires both magnitude AND direction for its complete description.
  • Examples: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum, impulse, weight, electric field strength.

✍️ Must-know: Scalars have only magnitude; vectors have magnitude and direction. Velocity, force and displacement are vectors; speed, distance and temperature are scalars.
