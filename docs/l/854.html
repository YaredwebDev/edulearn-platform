2.9 Response in Plants

Tropism = bending toward (positive) or away from (negative) the direction of a unilateral (one-sided) stimulus, caused by unequal distribution of the growth hormone auxin (e.g., Indole Acetic Acid, IAA), resulting in unequal growth.

Auxin effects: produced at shoot and root tips; promotes shoot growth at moderate concentrations but inhibits root growth at the same concentrations; roots need very low auxin.

TropismStimulusResponse
PhototropismLightShoot bends toward light (positively phototropic); root bends away from light
HydrotropismWaterRoot grows toward water (positively hydrotropic)
GeotropismGravityRoot grows downward (positively geotropic); shoot grows upward (negatively geotropic)

Darwin's phototropism experiment: with unilateral light, the shoot tip (coleoptile) bends toward light; if the tip is removed or covered with opaque foil, no bending occurs. Darwin concluded "some influence" is transmitted from the tip; this is now known to be auxin, which moves to the shaded side, causing more growth there.

Clinostat: a rotating instrument that overcomes the effect of gravity, so a horizontally placed seedling grows straight.

✍️ Must-know: Root = positively geotropic and positively hydrotropic; Shoot = positively phototropic but negatively geotropic (and negatively hydrotropic).
