1.3 Phrasal Verbs: act, add, allow, back

◌ Not started

Phrasal verbs = verb + particle (preposition/adverb) whose meaning often differs from the verb alone.

Phrasal verb Meaning Example
act out to perform/express (feelings, a story) The pupils acted out the scene.
act on to take action because of (advice/information) She acted on the teacher's advice.
act up to behave badly; to malfunction The engine acted up on the highway.
add on to attach / include as an extra They added on a new room.
add up to to result in; to mean The figures add up to a big total.
aim at to be directed at; to intend for The campaign aims at young people.
allow for to take into account; to consider Allow for traffic when you plan the trip.
allow of to permit (something) The rules do not allow of any delay.
back away to move backward (in fear/surprise) He backed away from the angry dog.
back down to admit defeat; to withdraw a demand He refused to back down in the argument.
back up to support; to move in reverse; to make a copy Please back up my decision.

✋ Trap: back down = give in (lose a fight/argument); back up can mean support OR reverse a vehicle OR make a data copy — use context to pick the meaning.
