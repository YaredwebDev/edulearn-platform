13.4 Climate Change Adaptation Strategies in Ethiopia

◌ Not started

Adaptation = the adjustments farmers & communities make in response to actual or expected climate change, to cope with impacts (modify how crops are produced and animals reared). - Common adaptation practices: changing crop rotation to include more drought-adapted crops; intercropping/mixing legumes & cereals (reduces erosion & evaporative water loss); changing to crops better adapted to drought / lower water demand; changing the crop calendar (plant early or late with the onset of rainfall); using perennials (e.g. fruit crops) mixed with legumes/cereals; using animal breeds better adapted to heat/water stress with better feed-conversion efficiency; grazing-land enclosures (reduce degradation, boost forage, cut disease); using locally available non-conventional feeds (agro-industrial by-products). - Strategies used by Ethiopian farmers: - Expansion of perennial crops — e.g. enset & tree species that tolerate climate change; when field crops fail the farmer has food from enset or sells tree products; reduces risk of total yield loss. - Income diversification — off-farm activities (trading, paid labour) reduce risk of total income loss during climate change. - Change in crop varieties — drought-tolerant varieties (short maturity or efficient water use) reduce total-yield-loss risk. - Mixed cropping — more than one crop on the same land; crops differ in drought tolerance, so if one fails another still yields. - Selecting/breeding more suitable animals — replace low producers with fewer highly productive, disease-resistant, locally adapted breeds. - Improving management practices — animal production is the major source of methane (CH₄); managing manure (e.g. biogas production), improving animal nutrition, cut-and-carry feeding, limiting free grazing all reduce greenhouse-gas emissions. - Climate Smart Agriculture (CSA) = an integrated approach managing crop production, animal production & natural resources to address the changing climate. Three aims: ① increase sustainable productivity; ② reduce vulnerability to climate risks & improve adaptive capacity; ③ reduce greenhouse-gas emissions from agriculture. A combination of practices is usually needed. Practices map to objectives: productivity (input, water & soil management), adaptation (crop diversification, integrated crop-livestock, improved grazing), mitigation (planting trees/agroforestry); some meet more than one.

✍️ Key term: agroforestry = deliberate integration of trees/shrubs with crops & livestock in agricultural systems.
