6.2 Biotic and Abiotic Components

◌ Not started

Abiotic factors (non-living components that influence the distribution of organisms): - Energy: solar energy captured by chlorophyll during photosynthesis powers most ecosystems. Light can't penetrate deep in aquatic environments → most photosynthesis near the surface. Dark ecosystems are powered by bacteria deriving energy from oxidation of inorganic chemicals (e.g., hydrogen sulfide). - Temperature: affects metabolism. Few organisms are active near 0 °C; temperatures above 45 °C destroy the enzymes of most organisms. Most organisms function best within a specific temperature range. - Water: essential to all life. Terrestrial organisms face drying out (reptilian scales, waxy leaf coatings reduce water loss); aquatic organisms face water balance problems. - Nutrients: distribution of photosynthetic organisms depends on availability of inorganic nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus). Soil structure, pH, and nutrient content determine plant distribution. - Salinity, oxygen, currents, tides: important in aquatic ecosystems (oxygen dissolved in water is critical for fish; cold, fast-moving water has higher oxygen). - Wind: important on land; increases water loss by evaporation. - Natural disturbances: storms or fire play a role in distribution.

Biotic factors (living components that influence distribution): - Predators (kill prey) and herbivores (eat plants/algae). - Presence/absence of pollinators, food resources, parasites, pathogens, and competing organisms act as biotic limitations.

✍️ Must-know: Abiotic = non-living (energy, light, temperature, water, nutrients, salinity, wind); Biotic = living (predators, herbivores, parasites, decomposers, competitors).
