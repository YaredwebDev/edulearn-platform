4.4 Protein Synthesis

◌ Not started
  • Proteins = amino acids joined by peptide bonds. 20 naturally occurring amino acids; each protein differs in sequence. Protein synthesis involves DNA, mRNA, tRNA, rRNA, amino acids, enzymes and ribosomes.
  • mRNA transcribes information from DNA (enzyme: RNA polymerase); tRNA brings amino acids & translates mRNA codons; rRNA forms ribosomes, which translate the mRNA template into a polypeptide chain.
  • Central dogma of life: DNA → RNA (transcription) → protein (translation). Reverse transcription synthesizes DNA from an RNA template.

Transcription (in the nucleus): a section of DNA separates; RNA nucleotides base-pair with the DNA template to form mRNA (carrying triplets called codons); mRNA detaches and leaves the nucleus to the ribosomes. tRNA and rRNA are also transcribed.

  • Codon = a sequence of three nucleotides on mRNA encoding a specific amino acid (e.g., tryptophan = UGG; valine = GUG). With 4 bases × triplets there are 4³ = 64 codons for 20 amino acids.
  • Stop codons: UAG, UAA, UGA. Start/initiator codon: AUG.

Translation (at the ribosome, in the cytoplasm): tRNA, carrying a specific amino acid and an anticodon (three nucleotides complementary to the mRNA codon), pairs with the mRNA codon; amino acids are joined by peptide bonds into a growing polypeptide → protein. - Example: mRNA codon GUG pairs with tRNA anticodon CAC, bringing valine.

✍️ Must-know: protein synthesis = transcription + translation. If mRNA has A-A-U, the tRNA anticodon is U-U-A (complementary, using U not T). tRNA carries amino acids to the mRNA at the ribosome.
