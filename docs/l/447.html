6E.1 Adverbial clauses of comparison: comparatives & superlatives

  • As…as / not so…as to show similarity/equality: The banker collects as much money as the cashier; She is not so tall as her brother. (Negative form = not as…as.)
  • Comparative to show a difference between two persons/things; superlative to compare one against all others of its kind: His application was processed more quickly than he thought; She is the best participant of all.
  • General rules for forming comparatives/superlatives:
  • One-syllable adjectives: add -er/-est (small→smaller→smallest; large→larger→largest; big→bigger→biggest — double final consonant).
  • Most two-syllable adjectives: use more/less and most/least (thoughtful→more thoughtful→the most thoughtful).
  • Adverbs ending in -ly: use more/less & most/least (carefully→more carefully→most carefully).
  • Two-syllable adjectives ending in -y: change y→i + -er/-est (sleepy→sleepier→sleepiest; happy→happier→happiest).
  • Two-syllable adjectives ending in -er/-le/-or/-ow: add -er/-est (gentle→gentler→gentlest; narrow→narrower→narrowest).
  • Three-or-more-syllable adjectives: use more/less & most/least (intelligent→more intelligent→most intelligent).
  • Some adjectives have both forms (clever→cleverer/more clever; happy→happier/more happy).

6E.2 Conditional clauses (four types)

  • Zero conditional (general truth/fact): If + present → present: If you heat ice, it melts; If you mix water and electricity, you get a shock.
  • First conditional (probable, present/future): If + present → will + verb: If you study, you will pass the exam; If people eat too many sweets, they will get fat.
  • Second conditional (improbable/unreal present or future): If + past → would + verb: If I were you, I would accept the offer; If I were taller, I wouldn't wear high heels.
  • Third conditional (unreal/imagined past): If + had + pp → would have + pp: If she had not studied well, she would not have joined university; If you had told me about the meeting, I would have come.
  • unless = if…not: If you do not call me, I won't come = Unless you call me, I won't come.
  • Inversion (more formal): omit if and invert the auxiliary: If I had known you before, I could have told you → Had I known you before, I could have told you. (Use should in real/inversion of first conditional.)

6E.3 Tenses revision (incl. present perfect)

  • Revise all tenses: past simple (I applied), past perfect continuous (had been thinking), past perfect (had called), past continuous (was working; were still talking), present simple (Today is Thursday), present continuous (I am sitting), future (will make me crazy), present perfect (has just rung), present perfect continuous (have been dreaming), future perfect / continuous (will have been in school; will have been studying), and present perfect passive contexts.
  • Present perfect (from the Google article): has become, has grown, have never returned, has lived, has been interested, has added, hasn't changed, have become. Present perfect links a past action to now (often with since/for).
