3.1 Introduction — The Three Physical States of Matter

◌ Not started

Matter = anything that occupies space and has mass. It exists in three common states plus a fourth: - Solid: definite volume and definite shape; rigid, essentially incompressible; particles vibrate about fixed points. Metals, chalk, sand, stone. - Liquid: definite volume but no definite shape (takes container shape); slightly compressible; flows; particles vibrate and shift positions. Water, ethanol, mercury, bromine, oil. - Gas: neither definite volume nor definite shape; highly compressible (mostly empty space between particles); takes the container's volume and shape. Air, H₂, O₂, CO₂, N₂. - Plasma (4th state): an ionised gas — a gaseous mixture of positive ions and free electrons, formed at extremely high temperature (millions of °C) where atoms are stripped of electrons. No fixed shape/volume; less dense than solids/liquids.

Terminology: liquids and gases are fluids (they flow); solids and liquids are condensed states (much higher density than gases).

✍️ Must-know: Water's three states = steam (gas), water (liquid), ice (solid). Ice rain/precipitation vs liquid water differ in state; heating a solid usually melts it, further heating boils it.
