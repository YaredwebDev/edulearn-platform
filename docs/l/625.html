2.3 Algebraic (Component) Method of Addition of Vectors in 2-D

◌ Not started

Analytic methods give exact results (unlike graphical methods which are approximate).

Adding two collinear vectors (same/opposite line)

  • Same direction: magnitude = sum, direction unchanged. e.g. boat 0.50 m/s downstream plus river 0.30 m/s ⇒ resultant 0.8 m/s downstream. (Example 2.2)
  • Opposite directions (antiparallel): magnitude = difference of magnitudes, direction = that of the larger force. e.g. two men pushing a block with 200 N and 100 N in opposite directions ⇒ resultant 100 N toward the right (the larger force). (Example 2.3)
  • Upstream case (Example 2.3 boat): 0.50 − 0.30 = 0.20 m/s upstream.

✋ Trap: largest possible magnitude of resultant of two vectors = |A| + |B| (parallel); smallest = ||A| − |B|| (antiparallel). For |A|=13, |B|=9: largest = 22, smallest = 4.

Adding two perpendicular vectors

Use the Pythagorean theorem for magnitude and trigonometry (tan θ) for direction: - Resultant magnitude R = √(A² + B²) - tan θ = opposite/adjacent.

Ant example (Example 2.4): ant walks 40 cm South then 50 cm West ⇒ resultant displacement magnitude R = √(40²+50²) = √4100 ≈ 64 m; direction by tan θ = 40/50 giving θ South of West.

Trigonometry recall: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite/adjacent.

Components of a vector (resolution)

Component of a vector = projection of the vector on the coordinate axes. Resolution = breaking a vector into its components. - With the angle θ measured from the +x axis: Ax = A cos θ and Ay = A sin θ. (cosine → x, sine → y). - With the angle measured from the y axis, the roles swap: Ax = A sin θ, Ay = A cos θ.

✋ Trap: whether cos goes with x or y depends on which axis the angle is measured from — always draw the right triangle and check. Example 2.5: a force of 35 N at 40° above the −x axis ⇒ Fx = 35 cos 40° = 26.8 N and Fy = 35 sin 40° (toward +y).

Finding the resultant by the component method

  1. Resolve every vector into its x- and y-components.
  2. Sum the x-components to get Rx and sum the y-components to get Ry.
  3. Magnitude R = √(Rx² + Ry²).
  4. Direction tan θ = Ry/Rx.

✋ Quadrant attention (from textbook): - Rx and Ry both positive ⇒ θ above the +x axis. - Rx negative, Ry positive ⇒ θ above the −x axis. - Both negative ⇒ θ below the −x axis. - Rx positive, Ry negative ⇒ θ below the +x axis.

Unit Vectors

A unit vector has magnitude 1. Along the axes they are denoted î (x-axis) and ĵ (y-axis). Any vector is A = Ax î + Ay ĵ; its magnitude is |A| = √(Ax²+Ay²). A unit vector in the direction of A is Â = A/|A|.

e.g. a unit vector along F = (3î − 4ĵ) N has magnitude 5, so the unit vector is (3î − 4ĵ)/5.

✍️ Must-know (resultant by components): if R = A + B then Rx = Ax + Bx and Ry = Ay + By, R = √(Rx²+Ry²) and tan θ = Ry/Rx. Adding A = 4î+2ĵ and B = −9î+3ĵ ⇒ R = −5î+5ĵ, |R|=√50 ≈ 7 units, direction 45° above the −x axis (2nd quadrant).

Traditional Maresha worked example (Example 2.11): an oxen+farmer ploughing system. Farmer's force F1 = 150 N at 60° above horizontal and oxen force F2 = 1200 N at 40° from horizontal; the resultant horizontal pulling force is the sum of the two x-components. (Downward resultant vertical force must slightly exceed upward or the Maresha lifts out of the ground and can't plough.)
