6.5 Esters

◌ Not started

6.5.1 Structure, naming, occurrence

  • Esters = RCOOR′ — derivatives of carboxylic acids where the acid's –OH is replaced by an alkoxy group –OR′ (from an alcohol/phenol). General formula RCO₂R′.
  • Naming: two parts — first the part from the alcohol (alkyl group, ending "–yl"), then the part from the acid (ending "–ate"/"–oate"). Alcohol part comes first.
  • Ethanol + ethanoic acid → ethyl ethanoate (ethyl acetate).
  • Table examples: HCOOCH₃ methyl formate/methyl methanoate; CH₃COOCH₃ methyl acetate/methyl ethanoate; HCOOCH₂CH₃ ethyl formate/ethyl methanoate; CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ ethyl acetate/ethyl ethanoate; propyl acetate/propyl ethanoate.
  • Esters occur widely and give fruits/flowers their pleasant smell/flavour (apple = isoamyl isovalerate; pineapple = ethyl butyrate; banana & pear = isopentyl acetate; orange = octyl acetate; grape = methyl anthranilate). Fats, oils and waxes are esters.

✍️ Must-know naming skill: split the ester at the –COO– bond. Left part (on carbonyl carbon, e.g. CH₃CO–) comes from the acid; right part (–O–CH₂CH₃) comes from the alcohol. Name = [alcohol alkyl-yl] [acid-ate].

6.5.2 Physical properties of esters

  • Boiling points increase with molecular mass; branched esters boil lower than straight-chain isomers. Esters cannot hydrogen-bond with each other → lower boiling points than comparable acids/alcohols.
  • Solubility: low-molecular-mass esters are fairly soluble in water (they can H-bond to water via the carbonyl O); solubility falls with mass; all esters dissolve in organic solvents.
  • Odour: pleasant (opposite of the disagreeable odour of carboxylic acids) → used in perfumes and food flavourings.

6.5.3 Chemical reactions of esters

  • i. Hydrolysis (the most important reaction): ester + water → carboxylic acid + alcohol.
  • Acid-catalysed: RCOOR′ + H₂O → RCOOH + R′OH (reversible).
  • Base-catalysed = saponification: RCOOR′ + NaOH → RCOO⁻Na⁺ + R′OH (not reversible; used to make soaps). E.g. in digestion, enzymes hydrolyse fats.
  • ii. Reduction: esters reduced by LiAlH₄ to primary alcohols (two alcohols result: one from the acid part, one from the alcohol part). E.g. butanoic ester → butan-1-ol + methanol.
  • iii. Formation (esterification): acid + alcohol over acid catalyst → ester + water (see 6.4.4). Aspirin = acetylsalicylic acid made from salicylic acid + acetic acid.

6.5.4 Uses of esters

Pleasant-smelling (perfumes, food flavourings); low-mass esters are non-toxic liquids → solvents for oils, fats, nail polish, varnishes, paints, gums, resins; medicine (aspirin); clothing (polyesters/Dacron); plasticisers (octyl phthalate).
