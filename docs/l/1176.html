📖 GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS (quick definitions for revision)

Amplitude – the maximum deviation of a periodic function from its mean value (for sine/cosine, the height above/below the axis).

Angle of depression – the angle below the horizontal that an observer's line of vision is lowered to see an object.

Angle of elevation – the angle above the horizontal that an observer's line of vision is raised to see an object.

Angle of inclination – the anticlockwise angle from the positive x-axis to a line; its tangent equals the slope.

Antilogarithm – the number M such that log M = N; written antilog(N).

Apex – the vertex (top point) of a pyramid or cone not on the base.

Apothem – the perpendicular distance from the center of a regular polygon to a side.

Arc – a portion of the circumference of a circle (major > 180°, minor < 180°).

Arc length – the length of an arc, s = 2πr·θ/360°.

Asymptote – a line a graph approaches but never touches (x-axis for aˣ, y-axis for logₐx).

Axis of symmetry – the vertical line through the vertex of a parabola; also the line about which a figure is symmetric.

Base (of exponent) – the number being raised to a power in aⁿ.

Base (of logarithm) – the number a in logₐx.

Bijective (one-to-one correspondence) – a function that is both injective and surjective.

Central angle – an angle formed by two radii with vertex at the center of a circle.

Characteristic – the integer part of a common logarithm (the power of 10).

Chord – a line segment joining two points on a circle.

Circle – the locus of points in a plane equidistant from a fixed center.

Co-domain – the set of all possible outputs (B in f:A→B).

Co-terminal angles – angles in standard position that share the same terminal side (differ by multiples of 360° or 2π).

Common logarithm – logarithm to base 10, written log M.

Complementary angles – two angles whose sum is 90°.

Completing the square – rewriting ax²+bx+c as a(x+b/2a)² + (4ac−b²)/4a.

Cone – a pyramid with a circular base; right circular cone has its apex above the center.

Constant function – f(x)=b for all x; degree-0 polynomial (horizontal line).

Cyclic quadrilateral – a quadrilateral whose four vertices lie on a circle (opposite angles sum to 180°).

Degree (polynomial) – the highest exponent n of a polynomial.

Degree (angle) – 1/360 of a complete revolution.

Domain – the set of all inputs (x-values) for which a function is defined.

Exponent – the power to which a base is raised in aⁿ.

Exponential function – f(x)=aˣ, a>0, a≠1.

Factor theorem – f(c)=0 iff (x−c) is a factor of f(x).

Frustum – the part of a pyramid/cone between the base and a parallel cross-section.

Function – a relation in which no two distinct ordered pairs have the same first element.

Gradient (slope) – the ratio of vertical rise to horizontal run: m=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁).

Great circle – a cross-section of a sphere through its center (largest possible circle).

Hemisphere – one half of a sphere, divided by a great circle.

Inclination – see angle of inclination.

Injective (one-to-one) – each element of the domain maps to a distinct image.

Inscribed angle – an angle with vertex on the circle formed by two chords.

Inverse function – the function that reverses the mapping of f; y=logₐx is the inverse of y=aˣ.

Leading coefficient – the coefficient of the highest-degree term of a polynomial.

Linear function – f(x)=ax+b, a≠0; graph is a straight line.

Location theorem – if f(a) and f(b) have opposite signs, a zero lies between a and b.

Logarithm – the exponent to which a base must be raised to obtain a number: logₐc=b iff aᵇ=c.

Mantissa – the fractional part of a common logarithm.

Midpoint – the point halfway between two endpoints: ((x₁+x₂)/2,(y₁+y₂)/2).

Multiplicity (of a zero) – how many times a factor (x−c) appears in a polynomial.

Natural logarithm – logarithm to base e, written ln x.

One-to-one – see injective.

Onto (surjective) – every element of the co-domain has a pre-image.

Parabola – the U-shaped graph of a quadratic function.

Parallel lines – lines with the same slope (m₁=m₂).

Perpendicular lines – lines whose slopes multiply to −1 (m₁·m₂=−1).

Period – the interval after which a periodic function repeats (2π for sin/cos, π for tan).

Point-slope form – y−y₁=m(x−x₁).

Polynomial function – p(x)=aₙxⁿ+aₙ₋₁xⁿ⁻¹+…+a₀ with non-negative integer exponents.

Quadrantal angle – an angle that is an integral multiple of 90° (π/2).

Quadratic function – f(x)=ax²+bx+c, a≠0.

Radian – the angle subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius; π rad=180°.

Range – the set of all outputs of a function.

Rational Zero Test – method to find possible rational zeros p/q from factors of a₀ and aₙ.

Reciprocal functions – csc, sec, cot (reciprocals of sin, cos, tan).

Reference angle – the positive acute angle between the terminal side and the x-axis.

Relation – a set of ordered pairs.

Remainder theorem – the remainder of f(x)÷(x−c) is f(c).

Secant (geometry) – a line that intersects a circle at two points.

Sector – the region bounded by two radii and an arc.

Segment (circle) – the region bounded by a chord and an arc.

Slant height – the altitude of a lateral face of a pyramid/cone.

Slope-intercept form – y=mx+b.

Sphere – a solid bounded by a surface every point of which is equidistant from a center.

Supplementary angles – two angles whose sum is 180°.

Surjective – see onto.

Tangent (geometry) – a line that touches a circle at exactly one point.

Turning point (vertex) – the point where a graph changes direction.

Unit circle – a circle of radius 1 centered at the origin.

Vertex (parabola) – the turning point of a quadratic graph; the minimum or maximum.

Vertical line test – a graph is a function iff no vertical line cuts it more than once.

Zero of a function – a value c with f(c)=0 (a root of f(x)=0).
