UNIT 7 · LESSON 7 OF 7 · GRADE 11 PHYSICS

🧮 Key facts to memorise

◌ Not started
  • Nucleus = protons + neutrons; hydrogen-1 has no neutron.
  • ᴬzX: A = mass number, Z = atomic number, N = A − Z.
  • Nuclear radius R ≈ r₀A^(1/3), r₀ ≈ 1.2 fm (1 fm = 10⁻¹⁵ m).
  • Isotopes: same Z, different N (protium/deuterium/tritium).
  • Rutherford: most of the atom is empty space; +charge & mass concentrated in nucleus.
  • Strong force binds nucleons (~100× EM, 10³⁸× gravity); weak force → beta decay.
  • Mass defect Δm = [Zmp + (A−Z)mn] − m_nucleus; BE = Δm c²; 1 u ≈ 931.5 MeV; BEN = BE/A.
  • Radioactivity = spontaneous decay to stable nuclei.
  • α = ²He⁴ (+2e), high ionisation, low penetration; β = electron (±), moderate; γ = EM wave, low ionisation, high penetration.
  • α: ᴬzX → ᴬ⁻⁴ z₋₂Y + α; β⁻: ᴬzX → ᴬ z₊₁Y + β.
  • Gray (Gy) absorbed dose = J/kg; Sievert (Sv) biological dose.
  • A = λN; N = N₀e^(−λt); T½ = ln2/λ = 0.693/λ; 1 Bq = 1 decay/s.
  • Radiation protection: Time, Distance, Shielding.
  • Fission splits heavy nuclei (power & weapons); fusion joins light nuclei (Sun).
  • Fission by-products radioactive (nuclear waste); control rods absorb neutrons; moderator slows neutrons.
  • Uses: cobalt-60 teletherapy, iodine-131 thyroid treatment, gamma tracers, C-14 (recent) & K-40 (old rocks) dating.

✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) name the nucleus's particles, use ᴬzX notation, and give isotope definitions/examples; (2) describe Rutherford's experiment and the discovery of the proton & neutron; (3) explain strong vs weak nuclear forces and their strengths; (4) compute mass defect, binding energy and BEN (1 u ≈ 931.5 MeV) for given isotopes and explain nuclear stability; (5) define radioactivity and compare α, β, γ in charge, mass, ionising and penetrating power and write decay equations; (6) state radiation dangers and units (Gy, Sv) and safety precautions; (7) describe detectors (Geiger counter, scintillator) and use half-life/activity equations (A=λN, N=N₀e^(−λt), T½=0.693/λ); (8) explain medical uses (cobalt-60, iodine-131, gamma tracers) and radioactive dating (C-14 vs K-40); (9) contrast fission and fusion, balance nuclear equations, name reactor components and explain the chain reaction/critical mass; (10) explain fusion as the Sun's energy source; and (11) apply the Time–Distance–Shielding safety rules? Re-answer the end-of-unit questions (Rutherford vs Bohr models; radii of U-238/U-235; beta-minus & alpha decay equations for K-242 and Pu-239; comparing α & β emission; research vs power reactors; radiotherapy protection; iodine-131 orally).
