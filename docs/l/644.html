5.1 The Concept of Heat

◌ Not started
  • Temperature is a measure related to molecular motion; heat is energy in transit due to a temperature difference.
  • Heat = energy which is in transit from one body to another as a result of a difference in temperature. (Heat is not "stored" — it flows.)
  • Internal energy = the sum of the internal kinetic energy (translational, rotational, vibrational motion of molecules) and the internal potential energy (due to molecular attractive forces). It includes the energy of all chemical bonds. Internal energy increases with rising temperature and with changes of state (solid→liquid→gas).
  • Thermodynamic work = the quantity of energy transferred from one system to another (e.g., by a gas pushing a piston).
  • Heat and work are two ways of transferring energy between a system and its surroundings; both contribute to internal energy. Heat ≠ work, but heat can be converted into work and vice versa.

✋ Trap: heat is energy in transit due to a temperature difference — two systems at thermal equilibrium (equal temperature) do not exchange heat. Spontaneous heat flow is from hot to cold. You can't say one body "has more heat" than another; you compare internal energy and temperature.

Review-style reasoning: two equal-volume cylinders A and B of the same gas, A hotter: (a) A does not have "more heat" — heat is only in transit; (b) A has greater internal energy (higher average KE of molecules); (c) on contact, heat flows A→B; B's average molecular KE increases. If heat flows into a gas but internal energy stays the same, then the gas must be doing work (pushing the piston).
