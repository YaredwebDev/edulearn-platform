5.1 Magnet

◌ Not started
  • Lodestone = a naturally occurring magnetized form of the iron oxide mineral magnetite; discovered by the ancient Greeks; attracts iron objects.
  • Magnet = a material/object that produces a magnetic field, exerting a force that attracts iron/steel objects (and can attract or repel other magnets).

Types of magnets: 1. Permanent magnets — made of magnetic material (e.g., steel), do not lose their magnetism once magnetized. Used in speakers, phones, generators, TVs, sensors. - Demagnetize by: extreme temperatures, hammering (loosens atomic attraction), or striking one magnet with another. 2. Temporary magnets — magnetized only in the presence of a magnetic field; lose it when the field is removed (e.g., iron nails, paper-clips). 3. Electromagnets — a coil of wire wrapped around an iron core; current through the coil generates a magnetic field, whose strength is controlled by the current. Used in generators, motors, transformers, loudspeakers, MRI machines, magnetic locks.

Properties of magnets: - Iron filings cling most at the ends → these are the poles (North and South). - Magnetic poles always exist in pairs — cutting a magnet in two produces two new N–S pairs. (No isolated single pole/monopole has ever been observed.) - A freely suspended magnet points North–South. - Like poles repel; unlike poles attract. - Magnetic force is greater when the distance between magnets is smaller.

✍️ Must-know: Every magnet has a North and South pole; poles exist in pairs; like poles repel, unlike attract.
