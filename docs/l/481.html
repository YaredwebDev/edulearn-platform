2.4.1 Connectors of Addition

Show adding another point: and, also, besides, further, furthermore, too, moreover, in addition, then, another, of equal importance. - "He studies hard; moreover, he practises daily."

2.4.2 Connectors of Sequence (order of ideas/time)

at first, eventually, finally, first/firstly, in the end, in the first place, in the second place, lastly, later, next, second/secondly, to begin with. - "First, set your goal. Next, plan your time. Finally, reward yourself."

2.4.3 Adverbs of Frequency

Words that tell how often: always (100%), usually, often, sometimes, occasionally, rarely, never (0%). - Position: before the main verb ("She always studies") but after the verb be ("She is never late").

2.4.4 The Simple Past Tense (V2)

Used for completed actions in the past. - Regular: verb + -ed (play→played, study→studied, stop→stopped). - Irregular: memorise the forms (go→went, eat→ate, write→wrote, cut→cut). - Negative/question with did: "She didn't go." "Did they come?"

2.4.5 The Past Continuous Tense (was/were + -ing)

Used for an action in progress at a past time, often interrupted by a simple-past action: - "I was reading when she came." - Two simultaneous past actions: "While we were talking, it was raining."

✋ Trap: The shorter/interrupting action uses simple past; the longer ongoing action uses past continuous. "I was sleeping when the phone rang."
