10.9 🧮 Key facts to memorise

◌ Not started
  • Renewable (regenerate: forests, soils, water, biodiversity, air) vs non-renewable (oil, coal, minerals).
  • Water: 70% of crust; 97% ocean, ~2% fresh, ~1% frozen.
  • Ethiopia = "water tower of Africa"; 8 river basins + Rift Valley lake basin; big irrigation & hydropower potential.
  • Mineral soil ≈ 5% organic + 45% minerals + 25% water + 25% air. Soil stores carbon.
  • Soil degradation forms: erosion, chemical (acidification/salinisation), physical (compaction).
  • Acidification: highland, leaching of basic ions, pH<7, ~40% of Ethiopian soils, ideal 6.5–7.2.
  • Salinisation: lowland, salt-rich irrigation, poor drainage, Rift Valley.
  • Water pollution causes: industrial chemicals, organic waste, fertilisers (N/P), metals, chlorine, oil/pesticides, solids, bacteria/viruses.
  • Tropical forests >50% degraded since 1960s.
  • Endangered (risk of extinction) vs threatened (may become endangered).
  • Air: 78% N₂, 21% O₂ (99%). Pollution causes respiratory/heart/lung disease.
  • Mineral exploitation pollutes air/soil/water (heavy metals, radionuclides).
  • Review answers: resources that regenerate = Renewable natural resources (B); non-renewable = Coal & Natural gas (D, B and C); soils mitigate climate change because they store carbon (B); max population a resource can support = Carrying capacity (B); using resources for current & future needs = Sustainable use (B); soil degradation caused by = All (D) (erosion, acidification, compaction); forest degradation does NOT result in increase in biomass production (A); indicator of wildlife degradation = decline in population (A); water pollution caused by = All (D); major cause of forest degradation in Ethiopia = expansion of agricultural land (A).
