1.3 Status of Crop Production

◌ Not started
  • Crop production is determined by the crop type and the environment in which it is grown. Activities: choosing a crop species and variety, land preparation, planting at the right time, protecting from diseases/pests, and adopting productivity-raising techniques.
  • Modern agriculture uses: crop rotation, fallowing (leaving land free of crops for 2–3 seasons to let soil recover), soil conservation, machinery, agrochemicals, irrigation, biotechnology and genetic improvement / plant breeding.
  • The three Eras of agriculture in industrialised regions: 1. Mechanical Era (1930–1950) — machinery reduced labour; a farmer can operate a large farm. 2. Chemical Era (1950–1970) — agrochemicals control crop diseases, weeds and pests. 3. Biotechnology / Information-Technology Era (1970–present) — improved seeds, higher yields, pest/disease resistance.
  • Less-developed regions (e.g. sub-Saharan Africa): traditional, still reliant on draft animals → very low productivity.

Status of crop production in Ethiopia: - Ethiopia has about 51 million hectares of arable land, of which only about 20% is cultivated, mainly by smallholders. - Five major crops ≈ 75% of the country's cultivated area: teff, wheat, maize, sorghum and barley. These supply ≈ 64% of calories consumed. - Cereal production has risen (6.23 → 10.5 million ha; 4.53 → 16.5 million tonnes over 1961–2019) mainly from expanded area and some inputs, not from higher yield per hectare. - Principal factors for low yields in Ethiopia: soil degradation; traditional farming with little technology; uncertain/variable rainfall; very low irrigation; and low use of inputs (improved seeds, fertilisers, pesticides).

✍️ Must-know numbers: ~51 M ha arable land, only ~20% cultivated; 5 crops (teff, wheat, maize, sorghum, barley) = ~75% of cultivated area supplying ~64% of calories. Cereal gains came from area expansion, not yield.
