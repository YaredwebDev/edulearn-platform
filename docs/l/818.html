3.6 Lipids

◌ Not started
  • Insoluble in water but soluble in non-polar solvents (ether, chloroform, acetone, benzene).
  • Elements: C, H, O.
  • Fats = solid at room temperature; oils = liquid at room temperature.
  • True lipids = organic compounds formed by fatty acids + alcohol (glycerol).
  • Fatty acid general formula: CH₃(CH₂)ₙCOOH, containing the acidic carboxyl group (-COOH); long hydrocarbon tail (often 15 or 17 carbons).

Types of fatty acids: - Unsaturated fatty acids: contain one or more double bonds (C=C) → not maximum hydrogen; melt easily; mostly oils (plants); mono-unsaturated (one double bond) vs polyunsaturated (several). - Saturated fatty acids: no double bonds; solid at room temperature; animal fats.

Phospholipids: one fatty acid replaced by a phosphate group; the phosphate head is hydrophilic (water-loving), the two fatty-acid tails are hydrophobic (water-hating) → form a bilayer (cell membrane).

Functions of lipids: stored energy / major fuel store; insulation (adipose cells); structural components of cell membranes; precursors for vitamin D, bile acids, hormones (cortisol, aldosterone, progesterone, estrogen, testosterone); protective coatings (wax on skin, fur, feathers, fruit, leaves).
