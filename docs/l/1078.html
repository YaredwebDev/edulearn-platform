10.7 Writing

10.7.1 Re-arranging sentences of a paragraph

Put the sentences in logical order. Example (jojoba): start with the general statement (The jojoba is an example of a plant…), then describe it, then its value, then what scientists hope. Practise ordering:

  1. "The jojoba is an example of a plant that scientists are trying to domesticate."
  2. "The jojoba, a wild desert bush that grows in some parts of the United States and Mexico, is about 2 meters high."
  3. "Its fruit is 40–60 percent liquid wax, called jojoba oil, which is valuable."
  4. "This can be used in making cosmetics and high quality machine oils."
  5. "By domesticating the jojoba, scientists hope to change unproductive desert land into productive agricultural land."

10.7.2 Writing an Expository Text

  • An expository essay gives an account/explanation of something.
  • Developed using techniques: definition, illustration (example), cause & effect, classification, comparison, contrast, process description — the dominant technique categorises the essay.
  • Conclusion sentence = the last sentence; summarises/ties together the paragraph's ideas, re-states the topic in another way, connects supporting details to the topic. Use conclusion starters: in general, generally, therefore, thus, to sum up, to conclude, to summarize, to make the long story short, briefly, in brief, in short, finally.
  • Example paragraph: "Loneliness and Its Effects" (definition, belonging, isolation, effects — depression, hopelessness, low self-esteem, cardiovascular disease, weight loss, hormonal imbalance, immune-system inhibition).
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Expository essay = explanation using one dominant technique (definition, cause/effect, comparison, process…). A conclusion = summarise + re-state the main idea + give insight/advice. Use conclusion starters.

📝 Writing focus

  • Order jumbled sentences into a coherent paragraph.
  • Write an expository paragraph/essay (e.g., on doping, or a common drug in your locality) with a proper conclusion.
  • Punctuate unpunctuated paragraphs (capital letters, commas, full stops).
  • Choose the correct word from the confusing-words pairs.
  • Construct sentences with direct/indirect objects and transitive/intransitive verbs.
