1.1 Major Branches of Biology

◌ Not started

Biology is the study of living organisms and life processes. It is divided into three major branches:

Major branch What it studies Examples of sub-disciplines
Zoology (Animal biology) The study of animals Herpetology (reptiles), Ichthyology (fish), Mammalogy (mammals), Ornithology (birds), Entomology (insects)
Botany The study of plants Plant structure, properties, biochemical processes, classification, plant diseases, plant–environment interactions
Microbiology The study of microscopic organisms (microbes) that cannot be seen with the unaided eye Bacteriology (bacteria), Mycology (fungi); includes bacteria, archaea, protists, viruses, microscopic algae and fungi

Botany facts to remember: - Plants provide food, shelter, clothing, medicine, ornament, and tools. - Through photosynthesis, plants convert solar energy into chemical energy of food and release oxygen as a by-product — both essential for all life.

📌 Ethiopian examples in this unit: Capra walie (Waliya ibex) is an endemic wild animal of Ethiopia; Ruta chalepensis L. (Tena Adam) is a traditional herbal medicine of Ethiopia.

Fields of biology based on the structure studied (Table 1.1)

Field What it studies
Morphology External form and structure (e.g., shape, leaf texture)
Anatomy Internal bodily structure revealed by dissection (e.g., stomach, liver, heart)
Histology Details of tissue structure (e.g., parenchyma, connective tissue)
Cytology The study of cells (e.g., plant cell, nerve cell)
Cell biology Structure, function, and components of the cell (e.g., mitochondrion, ribosome, nucleus)
Molecular biology Structure and function of informational molecules (e.g., DNA, RNA)

Fields of biology concerned with life processes (Table 1.2)

Field What it studies
Physiology Normal functions of living organisms and their parts (e.g., photosynthesis, digestion)
Embryology Development of the embryo from zygote to form and shape (blastula, gastrula)
Ecology Interaction of organisms with their environment (food chain, biomass, biosphere)
Taxonomy Identification, nomenclature, and classification of organisms (e.g., human = Homo sapiens)
Paleontology Origin, growth, and structure of organisms of the past (fossils)
Evolution Change in species characteristics over generations by natural selection (e.g., Darwin's finches)
Genetics Heredity and variation (gene concept, Mendel's laws)
Exobiology Origin, evolution, distribution, and future of life in the Universe (life on Moon/Mars)

Relationship between biology and other sciences (Table 1.3)

Biology is linked to chemistry, physics, and other sciences. Examples: - Chemistry — structure of lipids and proteins in cell membranes; formation of oxyhaemoglobin; acid–base equilibrium (homeostasis); osmosis/diffusion. - Physics — cohesion and adhesion → surface tension and capillary action (transport of water in plants).

✍️ Must-know: Anatomy = internal structure (dissection); Morphology = external form; Histology = tissues; Cytology = cells.
