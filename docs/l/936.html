📖 GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS (quick definitions for revision)

Abiotic factor – a non-living component of an ecosystem (energy, light, temperature, water, nutrients, salinity).

Abstinence – avoiding sexual intercourse; the most effective method of birth control, also preventing STIs.

Active transport – movement of molecules/ions against a concentration gradient, using energy.

Adolescence – the period between puberty and adulthood when social learning takes place.

Algae – plant-like, photosynthetic protists; basis of aquatic food chains.

Angiosperm – a seed plant that makes flowers and fruits.

Autotroph (producer) – an organism that makes its own food (photosynthesis).

Balanced diet – a diet containing all nutrients in the correct amount and proportion.

Binary fission – asexual reproduction by division into two equal parts (bacteria).

Binomial nomenclature – the two-part naming system (genus + specific epithet).

Biome – a major terrestrial or aquatic life zone characterized by vegetation or physical environment.

Biosphere – the whole of the Earth's surface that contains living organisms.

Biotic factor – a living component of an ecosystem (predators, herbivores, parasites, competitors).

Budding – asexual reproduction by division into two unequal parts (yeast, hydra).

Cell – the smallest and basic unit of living things.

Cell membrane – thin selectively permeable structure controlling what enters/leaves a cell.

Cell theory – all organisms composed of cells; cells are the smallest unit of life; cells arise from pre-existing cells.

Cell wall – rigid structure (cellulose in plants) providing support; absent in animal cells.

Chloroplast – organelle where photosynthesis occurs; contains chlorophyll.

Cilia – short, hair-like organelles for locomotion.

Classification – grouping things based on their similarities.

Climax community – a stable community that undergoes little or no change.

Commensalism (+/0) – one species benefits; the other is neither helped nor harmed.

Community – the different species within an area and their interactions.

Competition (−/−) – individuals use a resource that limits the survival/reproduction of both.

Consumers (heterotrophs) – organisms that cannot make their own food.

Contractile vacuole – organelle that expels excess water (osmoregulation).

Corpus luteum – structure secreting estrogen and progesterone after ovulation.

Cytoplasm – thick fluid-filled region of the cell containing organelles.

Decomposer – organism (bacteria/fungi) that breaks down dead organic matter.

Deficiency disease – illness caused by a shortage of a particular nutrient.

Diffusion – movement of molecules from high to low concentration (passive).

Dichotomous key – a tool for identifying organisms using pairs of contrasting features.

Doping – the use of prohibited medications/drugs in competitive sports.

Ecology – the study of interactions among organisms and with their environment.

Ecosystem – the community of organisms plus the non-living environment; self-supporting.

Endemic disease – a disease-causing agent that exists permanently in a specific place/population.

Epidemic – a disease that occurs suddenly and spreads in a specific area/population.

Eukaryotic cell – a cell with a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

Excretion – the removal of metabolic wastes from the body.

Fertilization – the fusion of sperm and egg nuclei to form a zygote.

Fission – asexual reproduction by dividing into two or more parts.

Flagellum – a long, thread-like organelle enabling cell movement.

Fragmentation – asexual reproduction where a parent breaks into parts that become whole organisms.

Gamete – a sex cell (egg or sperm) produced by meiosis.

Genus – a taxon of similar species.

Golgi apparatus – organelle that collects, packages, and distributes molecules.

Grafting – artificial vegetative propagation combining a scion and a stock.

Gymnosperm – a seed plant that does not make flowers or fruits (conifers).

Habitat – the place where an organism lives.

Hemolysis – the bursting of animal cells (e.g., red blood cells) in a hypotonic solution.

Heterotroph (consumer) – an organism that cannot make its own food.

Homeostasis – the regulation of internal conditions to maintain stability.

Hypothesis – a proposed, testable scientific explanation.

Implantation – the sinking of an embryo into the lining of the uterus.

Infectious disease – a disease caused by a disease-causing organism.

Invertebrate – an animal without a backbone.

Isotonic solution – a solution with equal solute and water concentration to the cell.

Kingdom – the highest (broadest) rank in the Linnaean hierarchy.

Lysosome – organelle containing digestive enzymes.

Macronutrient – a nutrient needed in large quantities (carbohydrate, protein, fat).

Magnification – how many times larger an image is than the object (eyepiece × objective).

Malnutrition – insufficient, excessive, or imbalanced consumption of nutrients.

Meiosis – cell division producing gametes.

Menopause – the end of menstrual cycles (when all ovarian follicles are depleted).

Menstrual cycle – the approximately monthly changes in the uterus.

Micronutrient – a nutrient needed in small quantities (vitamins, minerals).

Mitochondria – the "powerhouse" organelles where respiration occurs.

Mitosis – cell division producing two identical daughter cells.

Mutualism (+/+) – an interaction benefiting both species.

Niche – the combination of the job an organism does and where it lives.

Non-infectious disease – a disease not caused by a pathogen (genetic, deficiency, etc.).

Nucleus – the largest organelle containing DNA; controls the cell.

Osmosis – the diffusion of water across a membrane from high to low water concentration.

Organ – a structure composed of several tissues with a special function.

Organelle – a distinct structure within a cell with a specific function.

Osmosis/Plasmolysis – shrinking of a cell when it loses water in a hypertonic solution.

Ovulation – the release of an egg from a ruptured follicle.

Pandemic – an epidemic spreading worldwide (e.g., COVID-19).

Parasitism (+/−) – one organism (parasite) benefits while the host is harmed.

Parthenogenesis – asexual reproduction in which an unfertilized egg develops.

Permafrost – a permanently frozen layer of soil (in tundra).

Pioneer species – the first organisms to colonize a new area.

Placenta – structure connecting embryo to mother for nutrient/gas exchange.

Population – a group of interbreeding organisms of the same species in the same area.

Predation (+/−) – one organism (predator) kills and eats another (prey).

Prokaryotic cell – a cell without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles.

Protoplasm/Cytoplasm – see cytoplasm.

Puberty – the time when secondary sex characteristics begin to develop.

Quadrant (quadrat) – a marked-off square area used to sample populations.

Reproduction – the process that makes more of the same kind of organism.

Resolution – the ability to distinguish between two separate points.

Ribosome – organelle that synthesizes proteins.

Scientific method – a series of steps guiding scientific investigation.

Secondary succession – re-colonization of an area after a disturbance.

Selectively permeable membrane – a membrane that chooses what can enter/leave.

Species – a group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring.

Sterilization – permanent prevention of gamete production/release (vasectomy/laparotomy).

Succession – the orderly, natural changes in an ecosystem's communities.

Taxis/Taxonomy – the science of naming, identifying, and classifying organisms.

Taxon – a group of organisms sharing a unique set of traits.

Tissue – a group of similar cells acting as a functional unit.

Vasectomy – male sterilization (cutting/sealing the vas deferens).

Vegetative propagation – asexual reproduction in plants (stolons, rhizomes, corms, tubers, bulbs, grafting, cutting).

Vertebrate – an animal with a backbone.

Vesicle – a small membranous sac that stores/transports materials.

Wasting – low weight-for-height; Stunting – low height-for-age; Underweight – low weight-for-age.

Zygote – the fertilized egg (result of fertilization).
