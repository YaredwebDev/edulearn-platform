9D.1 Semantic relationships between words

  • Synonyms = words with the same (or almost the same) meaning.
  • Antonyms = words with the opposite meaning.
  • Homophones = words that sound alike but have different meanings/spellings.
  • Give synonyms/antonyms/homophones for: weak, right, poor, holy, vain, rude. Replace underlined words with synonyms/antonyms in practice sentences (e.g. virtues, bold, gloomy, coward, dull, compulsory, etc.).

9D.2 Idiomatic expressions

  • An idiom is a combination of words whose meaning differs from the literal meaning of each word (e.g. pulling someone's leg = to try to fool someone, not to drag their leg). Idioms are figurative language.
  • Learn & use: kill two birds with one stone (accomplish two goals at once) · let the cat out of the bag (reveal a secret) · let bygones be bygones (forgive past quarrels) · don't put all your eggs in one basket (don't risk everything on one thing) · a bird in hand is worth two in the bush (what you have is worth more than uncertain promises) · hold your tongue (keep quiet).
  • Guess: tickled pink (very pleased), down in the dumps (sad), under the weather (unwell), raining cats and dogs (raining heavily), driving me up the wall (annoying me greatly), a piece of cake (very easy), just Greek to me (incomprehensible), keep your chin up (stay cheerful), in the same boat (in the same situation).

9D.3 Phrasal verbs with put

  • put across (communicate/express clearly) · put aside (ignore; save money) · put on (put on clothing; gain weight) · put (doubts) to bed (settle/dismiss) · put together (assemble/combine) · put up (place/raise; put up luggage overhead) · put back (postpone; return) · put away (imprison; store/save).
  • Examples: put across complex ideas; put our differences aside; put aside a few dollars; put on your jacket / put on weight; put doubts to bed; put together our plan; put up your luggage; the meeting has been put back; he was put away for ten years.
