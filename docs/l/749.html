4.8 Current, Voltage, and Ohm's Law

◌ Not started

Electric current = rate of flow of electric charge: I = ΔQ/Δt - SI unit: ampere (A); 1 A = 1 C/s. Subunits: 1 mA = 10⁻³ A, 1 µA = 10⁻⁶ A. - Conventional current flows from positive terminal to negative terminal (assumed flow of positive charge). Electron current flows from negative to positive. Conventional current is the standard. - Measured by an ammeter.

Potential difference (voltage) = work done to move a unit charge between two points: V = W/Q - SI unit: volt (V); 1 V = 1 J/C. Measured by a voltmeter. - A battery converts chemical energy into electric potential energy, setting charges in motion.

Resistance = how much a conductor opposes the flow of charge. SI unit: ohm (Ω) = V/A.

Ohm's law (Georg Simon Ohm): current through a conductor is proportional to the voltage across it (at constant temperature): V = IR or R = V/I - Ohmic materials (copper, aluminium, silver) have constant resistance and a linear I–V graph. - Non-ohmic materials have a nonlinear I–V relationship. - A rheostat is used to vary resistance in a circuit.

Resistivity: resistance depends on the material, length and cross-sectional area: R = ρ·L/A - ρ = resistivity (SI unit: Ω·m), a characteristic property of the material. - Conductivity is the reciprocal of resistivity.

Example 4.3: I = 0.5 A for 600 s → Q = 0.5 × 600 = 300 C.

Example 4.4: V = 12 V, q = 2 C → W = V·Q = 24 J.

Example 4.5: Bulb, 220 V, 1200 Ω → I = 220/1200 = 0.18 A.

Example 4.6: Heater 60 V, 4 A → R = 15 Ω; at 120 V, I = 120/15 = 8 A.

Example 4.7: R=26 Ω, L=1 m, d=0.3 mm → ρ = RA/L = R·πd²/4L = 1.84×10⁻⁶ Ω·m (manganese).

✍️ Must-know: I = Q/t; V = W/Q; V = IR; R = ρL/A. Birds on a single wire don't get shocked because there is no voltage difference across them.
