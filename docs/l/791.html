5.3 Mechanism of action of skeletal muscles

◌ Not started
  • Skeletal muscle fibers are large single cells formed by the fusion of many cells. Cytoplasm is mostly myofibrils of two filaments: thick filaments of myosin and thin filaments of actin, organised into contractile units called sarcomeres.
  • Actin & myosin are proteins in every muscle type; myosin is a molecular motor that converts chemical energy from ATP into mechanical energy to pull actin filaments → contraction.
  • Troponin = a complex of three regulatory proteins. In a relaxed muscle, tropomyosin blocks the myosin-binding site on actin (preventing contraction); myosin binding to actin forms cross-bridges → contraction.
  • Mechanism: (1) Muscle activation — a motor nerve sends an action potential to the neuromuscular junction, causing the sarcoplasmic reticulum to release calcium into the muscle cell. (2) Contraction — calcium binds troponin, allowing actin & myosin to bind (cross-bridges) using ATP. (3) Recharging — ATP is re-synthesized. (4) Relaxation — when nerve stimulation stops, calcium is pumped back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum, breaking actin–myosin links (relaxation fails if ATP runs out — rigor mortis).
  • Three types of muscle contraction: Isometric (no length change), Concentric (shortening), Eccentric (lengthening the muscle fibers).

✋ Review trap: the "three types of muscle contraction" are concentric, isometric and eccentric — NOT "centric". Calcium released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum drives contraction.
