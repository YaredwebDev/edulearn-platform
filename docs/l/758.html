5.3 The Earth's Magnetic Field and the Compass

◌ Not started

Earth's magnetic field (geomagnetic field) extends from Earth's interior into space; it causes a compass needle to rotate. - Origin: the movement of molten iron in the outer core generates the field. - Shape similar to a huge bar magnet tilted about 11° from the geographic North–South axis. - Earth has two sets of poles: geographic poles and magnetic poles. - The magnetic field lines point N→S, so what we call the magnetic North pole is actually the South pole of the bar magnet (the magnet's South pole is near Earth's North pole).

Compass: an instrument using a magnetized needle free to turn, used to find the direction of a magnetic field and for navigation.

Some animals (pigeons, bees, monarch butterflies, sea turtles, fish) detect magnetic fields for orientation.

✍️ Must-know: Earth's magnetic field is like a bar magnet tilted ~11°; its "magnetic North" is actually the bar magnet's South pole. Origin: molten iron in the outer core.
