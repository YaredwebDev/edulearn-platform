🔑 MASTER GRAMMAR & KEY-FACTS SHEET (keep for revision)

Tenses

  • Simple present = habitual actions, general truths, future schedules. Singular subject → verb + -s/-es; negative/question with do/does. Adverbs of frequency: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never.
  • Present continuous = is/am/are + -ing; action happening now; also a definite future plan ("She is coming tomorrow").
  • Simple past = V2 for completed past actions; regular -ed, irregular forms; negative/question with did.
  • Past continuous = was/were + -ing; an action in progress at a past time; often paired with simple past ("I was reading when she came").
  • Present perfect = have/has + V3; unfinished past action continuing to now; past action with present result; experience; no definite past time. Words: since, for, just, already, yet, ever, never.
  • Present perfect continuous = have/has + been + -ing; action started in past and still continuing/unfinished.
  • since = a moment in time ("since 2012"); for = a period of time ("for six years").

Tag questions

  • Positive statement → negative tag; negative statement → positive tag. "Birds are free, aren't they?" "He can't fly, can he?"
  • I am late, aren't I? (the exception) but "I am not late, am I?"

Question types

  • Wh-questions (who, what, where, why, when, which, how, whose) — falling intonation.
  • Yes/no questions (do/does/did, have/has, modals) — rising intonation.

Modal verbs

  • Ability: can. Permission: can/may/could. Possibility: could/may/might. Advice: should/ought to. Obligation: must/have to. Prohibition: mustn't. No obligation: don't have to/don't need to/need not. Future: will/shall. Offers/suggestions: shall.
  • must = obligation from the speaker's own view; have to = external/general law/rule; mustn't = not allowed (prohibition); don't have to / need not = not necessary.

Conditionals

  • Zero (facts/general truths): If + present, present. "If you heat ice, it melts."
  • Type 1 (probable future): If + present, will + V1. "If it rains, you will get wet."
  • Type 2 (unreal/unlikely present): If + past (were), would/could/might + V1. "If I were you, I would accept."
  • Type 3 (unreal past): If + had + V3, would/could/might + have + V3. "If I had studied, I would have passed."

Relative (adjectival) clauses

  • Pronouns: who (people, subject), whom (people, object), whose (possession), which/that (things), where (place), when (time).
  • Defining clauses give essential information — no commas. Non-defining clauses add extra information — set off by commas.

Connectors

  • Addition: and, also, besides, further, furthermore, too, moreover, in addition, then, another, of equal importance.
  • Sequence/time order: at first, eventually, finally, first/firstly, in the end, in the first place, in the second place, lastly, later, next, second/secondly, to begin with.

do / does / did

  • do with I, you, we, they (plural); does with he, she, it (singular); did for past (all). Used in affirmative ("They do their chores"), negative ("She doesn't speak French"), and questions ("Do the students...?").

🎯 MATRIC-PREP EXAM ESSENTIALS (what to drill for Grade 12)

  1. Vocabulary from every reading passage — the most useful resource. Master the contextual meaning + an example for each word in the per-unit lists.
  2. Tenses — the difference between present perfect and simple past is the #1 trap; also past continuous, present perfect continuous, and since/for.
  3. Conditionals — all four types (zero, 1, 2, 3) and "if I were / had I known."
  4. Modals of obligation — must vs have to vs mustn't vs don't have to/needn't.
  5. Relative clauses — who/whom/whose/which/that/where/when, defining vs non-defining (commas).
  6. Tag questions — especially the "I am → aren't I" exception and positive/negative pairing.
  7. Phrasal verbs — the themed lists in each unit (act/back, blow/break, come, close/clear, drop/dry/eat, count/crack/cut, etc.).
  8. Word formation — prefixes (ante, anti, en, dis, un, ex) and suffixes (-ate, -ise/ize, -ment, -tion, -ful, -ness).
  9. Synonyms & antonyms — a whole question type.
  10. Connectors — addition and sequence words for writing.
  11. Writing genres — paragraph, report, informal letter, descriptive essay, expository essay, polite agreement/disagreement.
  12. Pronunciation — rising/falling intonation for yes/no vs wh-questions.

⚠️ COMMON EXAM TRAPS (avoid these)

  • ✋ "I am think" / "I am believe" — state verbs don't take continuous. Use "I think," "I believe."
  • ✋ Tag question after "I am" → "aren't I?" not "am not I?"
  • ✋ "must not" vs "don't have to": mustn't = not allowed (prohibition); don't have to/needn't = not necessary. "I need not play" ≠ "I must not play."
  • ✋ Present perfect vs simple past: use simple past with a definite past time ("yesterday, ago, last week"); use present perfect with since/for/just/already/yet and no definite time.
  • ✋ "One of my friends are…" → singular verb: "One of my friends is…"
  • ✋ "I used to smoked" → "I used to smoke."
  • ✋ "She don't" → "She doesn't." "Do" with plural, "does" with singular.
  • ✋ Relative clause: don't use a comma in a defining clause ("The girl who is tall is my friend" — no commas); use commas in non-defining ("My father, who is over 60, works on the farm").
  • ✋ Modal + "to": "should to go" ✋ → "should go"; but "ought to go," "have to go," "need to go" are correct.

1.4 Grammar

1.4.1 Tag Questions

A tag question is a short question added to the end of a sentence to ask for confirmation. Rules:

  • Positive statement → negative tag: "Birds are free, aren't they?"
  • Negative statement → positive tag: "He can't fly, can he?"
  • Auxiliary + pronoun forms the tag (is/are/am, do/does/did, have/has, will/can/must...).
  • "I am late, aren't I?" — the special exception (never "am I not").

1.4.2 The Simple Present Tense (V1 / V1-s)

Used for:

  • Habitual / repeated actions: "She walks to school every day."
  • General truths & facts: "Water boils at 100°C."
  • Future with schedules/timetables: "The bus leaves at 7:00."
  • With adverbs of frequency: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never (before the main verb, after be).
  • Singular subject (he/she/it) → verb + -s/-es. Negative/question use do/does: "She doesn't work here." "Does she live in the city?"

1.4.3 The Present Continuous Tense (am/is/are + -ing)

Used for:

  • An action happening now: "I am reading."
  • A temporary situation: "She is staying with her aunt this month."
  • A definite future arrangement: "We are leaving tomorrow."
Note: ✋ Trap — state verbs: understand, believe, know, like, want, love, hate, see, hear are not usually used in the continuous. Say "I understand," not "I am understanding."

1.4.4 Wh- and Yes/No Questions

  • Wh-questions (who, what, where, why, when, which, how, whose) — falling intonation. "Where do you live?"
  • Yes/no questions (do/does/did, have/has, modals) — rising intonation. "Do you like the city?"
✍️ Must-know: ✍️ Must-know: Question formation needs the auxiliary: "Where do you live?" (not "Where you live?"). With be or a modal, the word order is Verb/Aux + Subject: "Is she at home?" "Can you help me?"

📝 Writing focus

  • Build a set of related sentences into a cohesive paragraph about city vs rural life, using connecting words (and, but, because, however, also) and the unit's vocabulary.
Note: ✅ End-of-unit self-check: Can you (1) compare city and rural life in your own words, (2) define all 9+ vocabulary words with a sentence, (3) use 10+ phrasal verbs (act/add/allow/back), (4) write correct tag questions (including "I am → aren't I?"), (5) use simple present and present continuous correctly, (6) form wh- and yes/no questions?

2.4 Grammar

2.4.1 Connectors of Addition

Show adding another point: and, also, besides, further, furthermore, too, moreover, in addition, then, another, of equal importance.

  • "He studies hard; moreover, he practises daily."

2.4.2 Connectors of Sequence (order of ideas/time)

at first, eventually, finally, first/firstly, in the end, in the first place, in the second place, lastly, later, next, second/secondly, to begin with.

  • "First, set your goal. Next, plan your time. Finally, reward yourself."

2.4.3 Adverbs of Frequency

Words that tell how often: always (100%), usually, often, sometimes, occasionally, rarely, never (0%).

  • Position: before the main verb ("She always studies") but after the verb be ("She is never late").

2.4.4 The Simple Past Tense (V2)

Used for completed actions in the past.

  • Regular: verb + -ed (play→played, study→studied, stop→stopped).
  • Irregular: memorise the forms (go→went, eat→ate, write→wrote, cut→cut).
  • Negative/question with did: "She didn't go." "Did they come?"

2.4.5 The Past Continuous Tense (was/were + -ing)

Used for an action in progress at a past time, often interrupted by a simple-past action:

  • "I was reading when she came."
  • Two simultaneous past actions: "While we were talking, it was raining."
Note: ✋ Trap: The shorter/interrupting action uses simple past; the longer ongoing action uses past continuous. "I was sleeping when the phone rang."

8.4 Grammar: Present Perfect & Present Perfect Continuous

8.4.1 The Present Perfect Tense (have/has + past participle/V3)

Used for:

  • An action that started in the past and continues to now: "I have lived here since 2010."
  • A finished action with a result in the present: "I have lost my keys" (so I can't enter now).
  • Experience (no definite time): "She has been to Asmara."
  • With since (a point in time) and for (a period): "since 2015," "for three years."

Forms: Positive "I have studied." Negative "I haven't studied." Question "Have you studied?"

8.4.2 The Present Perfect Continuous Tense (have/has + been + -ing)

Used for an action that began in the past and is still continuing/unfinished, emphasising the duration:

  • "I have been playing basketball for three hours."
  • "It has been raining all morning."

8.4.3 Present Perfect vs Simple Past (comparison)

Present PerfectSimple Past
Unfinished action continuing to now: "I've known Julie for ten years."Finished action: "I knew Julie for ten years (but we lost touch)."
Finished action with a present result: "I've lost my keys!"Finished action with no present result: "I lost my keys yesterday."
Experience (no time): "My brother has been to Asmara three times."Finished past action: "My great-grandmother went to Asmara three times."
With unfinished time (this week, today): "I've seen John this week."With finished time (last week, yesterday): "I saw John last week."
Note: ✋ Trap: Use the simple past with a definite past time (yesterday, last week, in 2010, ago). Use the present perfect with since, for, just, already, yet, ever, never and no definite past time. "I have seen John last week" is WRONG → "I saw John last week."

This unit compares the present perfect (introduced in Unit 8) with the simple past in detail.

9.4.1 Forms of the Simple Past

  • Positive: V2 — "He played football." (played/chopped/studied = regular; cut/ate = irregular.)
  • Negative: did + not + V1 — "He didn't play football."
  • Question: Did + subject + V1 — "Did he play football?"

9.4.2 Forms of the Present Perfect

  • Positive: have/has + V3 — "I have studied hard." / "She has played volleyball."
  • Negative: haven't/hasn't + V3 — "I haven't studied hard."
  • Question: Have/Has + subject + V3 — "Have you studied?"

9.4.3 Choosing between them

Present PerfectSimple Past
Unfinished action from past to now: "I've known Julie for ten years (and still do)."Finished action: "I knew Julie for ten years (then we lost touch)."
Experience / finished action with a result in the present: "I've lost my keys!"Finished action with no result now: "I lost my keys yesterday."
With unfinished time (this week, today): "I've seen John this week."With finished time (last week, yesterday): "I saw John last week."
Note: ✋ Trap: The moment there is a definite past time (yesterday, last week, in 2010, ... ago), switch from present perfect to simple past.

9.4.4 Prepositions since / for

  • since + a point in time: "since 2010," "since morning."
  • for + a period of time: "for five years," "for a long time."

10.1 Reading: The Roles of Computers and the Internet in Our Lives

(the core "story"/text of the unit)

The passage gives advantages and disadvantages of computers and the internet.

Advantages:

  • A huge store of easily available information at a click of the mouse.
  • Students can submit neat, legible assignments and use software applications, coloured graphs and diagrams; spelling/grammar errors are largely avoided.
  • Knowledge spreads: anyone can publish on the internet; research students need not labour through manuscripts; universities, websites and queries can be consulted online — saving time, labour and money.
  • E-forums and conferences allow healthy discussion.
  • It builds a global family (international communities on social networks) instead of narrow community/caste groups.
  • People can be brought together quickly to protest, fight for rights, or garner support; doctors, lawyers and teachers can be consulted online.

Disadvantages:

  • A negative counter-point exists for each positive aspect.
  • People spend hours chatting or surfing and neglect family — less communicative, more dependent on the computer.
  • Children (esp. in developed countries) may spend ~20 hours a week playing computer games.
  • Online games often show violence and aggressiveness; children may accept violence as normal behaviour.
  • It can ring the death knell on good habits: reading is dying, communicative skills deteriorate, and health suffers from long hours at the screen.
  • Children can easily get in touch with negative groups.

The key message: "It depends on our wisely making use of a computer to turn it into a boon instead of a bane."

This unit revises four tenses. Master their positive, negative and question forms.

TenseFormFunctionExample

Past continuouswas/were + -ingAction in progress for a longer time in the pastI was playing basketball the whole evening.

Choosing between present perfect simple and continuous:

  • Present perfect simple focuses on a completed result/experience: "I have written three letters."
  • Present perfect continuous focuses on the duration/ongoing action: "I have been writing letters all morning."

Time clues:

  • Simple past: yesterday, last week, in 2010, ago.

  • Present perfect: since, for, just, already, yet, ever, never.
  • Present perfect continuous: for, since, all morning/day, lately.

📖 GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS (quick definitions for revision)

Adjective – a word that modifies or gives additional information about a noun.

Adverb of frequency – a word that tells how often an action happens (always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never).

Antonym – a word with the opposite meaning (fast/slow, safe/dangerous).

Article – a word used before a noun: a, an (indefinite), the (definite).

Bane – a source of harm or ruin (opposite of boon).

Boon – a benefit; a blessing (opposite of bane).

Collocation – words that naturally go together (heavy traffic, strong argument, rich culture).

Conditional sentence – an if-clause + a main clause; types zero, 1, 2, 3.

Connectors of addition – words that add another point (and, also, besides, moreover, furthermore, in addition).

Connectors of sequence – words that order ideas or time (first, next, then, finally, later).

Defining (essential) clause – a relative clause that gives essential information; takes NO commas.

Descriptive essay – an essay that describes a person, place, community or thing with rich detail.

Discourse markers – linking words (because, however, therefore, moreover).

Equality – giving everyone the same things so all can enjoy full, healthy lives.

Equity – understanding and giving people what they need to enjoy healthy lives (tailored to need).

Expository essay – an essay that explains something (definition, cause/effect, comparison, process).

First conditional – If + present, will + V1 (probable future).

Gerund – an -ing verb used as a noun (reading, gardening, helping).

Justice – the collective responsibility of a free and just society to protect rights for every individual; every person receives their due.

Modal verb – an auxiliary adding meaning such as ability, permission, obligation (can, may, must, should, etc.).

Non-defining (non-essential) clause – a relative clause giving extra information; set off by commas.

Past continuous – was/were + -ing (action in progress at a past time).

Past participle (V3) – the third form of a verb used with have/has/been.

Phrasal verb – a verb + particle whose meaning often differs from the verb alone (give up, cut down).

Polite agreement / disagreement – phrases to agree or disagree courteously (I'm afraid I disagree...).

Prefix – a word part added at the beginning that modifies meaning (un-, dis-, anti-, ante-, en-, ex-).

Present continuous – am/is/are + -ing (action now; definite future plan).

Present perfect – have/has + V3 (unfinished action; result; experience; since/for/just/already/yet).

Present perfect continuous – have/has + been + -ing (action started in past and continuing to now).

Second conditional – If + past (were), would/could/might + V1 (unlikely/unreal present).

Simple past – V2 / did + V1 (completed past actions).

Simple present – V1 / V1-s (habits, general truths, schedules).

State verb – a verb not usually used in continuous forms (think, believe, know, want, like).

Suffix – a word part added at the end that changes word class (-ate, -ise/-ize, -ment, -tion).

Synonym – a word with the same or similar meaning (big/large, end/finish).

Tag question – a short question added to confirm information (Birds are free, aren't they?).

Third conditional – If + had + V3, would/could/might + have + V3 (impossible past).

Topic sentence – the sentence that carries the main idea of a paragraph.

Wh-questions – questions beginning with who, what, where, why, when, which, how, whose.

Word formation – creating new words using prefixes and suffixes.

Yes/no questions – questions that can be answered "yes" or "no" (with do/does/did/have/modals).

Zero conditional – If + present, present (general truths/facts).

📝 GRAMMAR CHEAT-SHEET (one-line reminders)

  • Tag questions: positive → negative tag, and vice-versa; "I am → aren't I?"
  • Simple present: V1-s for he/she/it; negatives/questions with do/does.
  • Present continuous: am/is/are + -ing.
  • Simple past: V2; did + V1 for negative/question.
  • Past continuous: was/were + -ing.
  • Present perfect: have/has + V3; since (point) / for (period).
  • Present perfect continuous: have/has + been + -ing.
  • Modals of obligation: must (own view) / have to (external) / mustn't (prohibited) / don't have to & need not (not necessary).
  • Zero conditional: if + present, present (facts).
  • 1st conditional: if + present, will + V1 (probable).
  • 2nd conditional: if + past (were), would + V1 (unlikely).
  • 3rd conditional: if + had + V3, would have + V3 (impossible past).
  • Relative pronouns: who (people), which (things), that (either), whose (possession), where (place), when (time).
  • Defining clause: no commas; non-defining clause: commas.
  • since = point in time; for = period of time.
